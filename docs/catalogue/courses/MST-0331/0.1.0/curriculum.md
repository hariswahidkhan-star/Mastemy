## Learning outcomes

1. Explain safety management systems and the specialist's role
2. Apply risk assessment, hazard control and program management
3. Apply training, incident management and performance measurement

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-0331-Q0001** (single, Select ONE) A leading indicator of safety performance is best illustrated by:

- A. Percentage of planned safety inspections completed on time **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: completed inspections are a proactive leading measure.
- B. Number of lost-time injuries last year  
  _Rationale:_ Injury counts are lagging indicators.
- C. Total workers' compensation paid  
  _Rationale:_ Claims cost is a lagging outcome.
- D. Days since the last recordable injury  
  _Rationale:_ That is a lagging, after-the-fact measure.

**MST-0331-Q0002** (single, Select ONE) Rating a risk on consequence alone is flawed because it ignores:

- A. How likely the harm is to occur **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: risk combines consequence and likelihood.
- B. The colour of the hazard sign  
  _Rationale:_ Sign colour is not a risk dimension.
- C. The manager's job title  
  _Rationale:_ Job title is irrelevant to risk rating.
- D. The time of day only  
  _Rationale:_ Time alone is not the missing dimension.

**MST-0331-Q0003** (multiple, Select TWO) Select TWO components of a plan-do-check-act safety management system.

- A. Setting objectives and planning controls (Plan) **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: planning objectives and controls is the Plan stage.
- B. Checking performance through audits and metrics (Check) **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: auditing and measuring is the Check stage.
- C. Ignoring incident data  
  _Rationale:_ Ignoring data is not part of PDCA.
- D. Hiding nonconformances from auditors  
  _Rationale:_ Concealment is not part of a management system.
- E. Removing all records  
  _Rationale:_ Records are retained, not removed.
