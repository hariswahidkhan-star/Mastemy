## Learning outcomes

1. Explain the business and legal structures of an architecture practice
2. Apply financial management and risk-management principles to a firm
3. Describe professional, ethical and legal responsibilities of the architect
4. Explain contracts and agreements governing professional practice

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1269-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A firm's net multiplier primarily measures:

- A. Revenue generated per unit of direct labour cost **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the net multiplier relates net revenue to direct labour, a core firm financial metric.
- B. The number of projects in the pipeline  
  _Rationale:_ Pipeline count is a workload measure, not the net multiplier.
- C. The square footage of a building  
  _Rationale:_ Building area is a project metric, unrelated to the net multiplier.
- D. The client's credit rating  
  _Rationale:_ Client creditworthiness is separate from the firm's net multiplier.

**MST-1269-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) The level of skill and care a reasonably prudent architect would exercise is the:

- A. Standard of care **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the standard of care defines the architect's professional duty.
- B. Statute of repose  
  _Rationale:_ A statute of repose limits the time to bring a claim.
- C. Scope of services  
  _Rationale:_ Scope defines deliverables, not the care standard.
- D. Fee basis  
  _Rationale:_ Fee basis concerns compensation, not duty of care.

**MST-1269-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO are appropriate ways for a firm to manage professional-liability risk?

- A. Carrying professional liability (E&O) insurance **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: E&O insurance is a standard liability-management tool.
- B. Documenting decisions and communications clearly **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: good documentation reduces and defends against liability.
- C. Omitting a scope of services to stay flexible  
  _Rationale:_ An undefined scope increases ambiguity and liability.
- D. Ignoring code requirements to meet a deadline  
  _Rationale:_ Ignoring code raises liability and violates professional duty.
