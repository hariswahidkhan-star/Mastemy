## Learning outcomes

1. Benefits management foundations
2. Identifying and mapping benefits
3. Measuring benefits

5. Reviewing and sustaining benefits

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Selection items cannot demonstrate facilitating a live benefits workshop; practice comes through instructor-led simulations.

## Integrative case

A company invested in a new CRM expecting higher retention. A year on, nobody can say whether it worked. The learner must separate outputs from benefits, build a benefits map with measures and baselines, assign benefit owners, and run a post-implementation review, then recommend actions to realise and sustain the intended value.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1761-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Which best distinguishes an output from a benefit?

- A. An output is what is delivered; a benefit is the measurable improvement it enables **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: outputs are deliverables, benefits are the value realised from them.
- B. They are two words for the same thing  
  _Rationale:_ They are distinct concepts in benefits management.
- C. An output is always financial, a benefit never is  
  _Rationale:_ Both can be financial or non-financial.
- D. A benefit is delivered before the output  
  _Rationale:_ Benefits are realised after and because of outputs.

**MST-1761-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO are needed to measure a benefit credibly? (Select TWO.)

- A. A baseline of the current state **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: without a baseline you cannot show change.
- B. A defined measure and target with a date **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a measurable target makes realisation verifiable.
- C. A promise that it will be 'much better'  
  _Rationale:_ Unmeasurable claims cannot be verified.
- D. The sponsor's personal opinion only  
  _Rationale:_ Opinion is not an objective measure.

**MST-1761-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A benefit owner is typically:

- A. A business role accountable for realising the benefit after delivery **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: benefit ownership usually sits with the business, not the project team.
- B. The project manager, who owns it forever  
  _Rationale:_ PMs usually hand benefit ownership to the business.
- C. Whoever happens to be free that week  
  _Rationale:_ Ownership must be deliberate and accountable.
- D. Nobody; benefits owners are unnecessary  
  _Rationale:_ Unowned benefits are rarely realised.
