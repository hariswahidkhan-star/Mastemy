## Learning outcomes

1. Explain the causes and global consequences of the First World War
2. Analyse the interwar crises, including depression and the rise of dictatorships
3. Evaluate the Second World War and its reshaping of the global order
4. Explain the Cold War and the process of decolonisation
5. Assess globalisation, technological change and economic integration since 1945
6. Evaluate contemporary challenges, from new powers to climate and migration

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2108-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why do historians treat the two world wars as connected but not a single inevitable conflict?

- A. The 1919 settlement and interwar choices shaped the second war, but different decisions could have altered outcomes **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the wars are linked through contingent decisions, not a predetermined single event.
- B. Because the wars happened in the same year  
  _Rationale:_ They were decades apart.
- C. Because they involved none of the same countries  
  _Rationale:_ Many of the same states fought; the point is about inevitability.
- D. Because there was no connection at all  
  _Rationale:_ There were real connections; the caution is against inevitability.

**MST-2108-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO factors most directly drove decolonisation after 1945? (Select TWO.)

- A. Weakened European powers after the Second World War **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the war drained imperial capacity to hold colonies.
- B. Organised nationalist movements demanding independence **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: sustained anti-colonial movements pressed for and won independence.
- C. The invention of the printing press  
  _Rationale:_ A much earlier technology, unrelated.
- D. The fall of the Roman Empire  
  _Rationale:_ An ancient event, irrelevant to 20th-century decolonisation.

**MST-2108-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A commentator argues that globalisation is 'entirely new and benefits everyone equally'. What is the flaw?

- A. Global connection has a long history and its gains and costs are distributed unevenly **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: globalisation is not wholly new and produces winners and losers, not uniform benefit.
- B. Globalisation does not exist  
  _Rationale:_ It is a real process; the issue is the claim about it.
- C. Trade began only in the 21st century  
  _Rationale:_ Long-distance trade is ancient.
- D. No one benefits from globalisation  
  _Rationale:_ Some benefit substantially; the point is uneven distribution.
