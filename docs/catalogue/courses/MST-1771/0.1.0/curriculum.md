## Learning outcomes

1. Explain the chain of infection and principles of infection prevention
2. Apply standard and transmission-based precautions
3. Explain decontamination, sterilisation and the clinical environment
4. Describe surveillance, outbreak management and antimicrobial stewardship

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every course outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Hands-on operation, the quality of tools and live outputs, and professional judgement are taught by video demonstration and model-answer analysis; the certificate assessment uses single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only and never requires uploads, recorded work, submitted code or peer review.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1771-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Hand hygiene is considered the single most effective action against healthcare-associated infection mainly because it:

- A. Interrupts transmission of microbes between patients and surfaces **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: hand hygiene breaks the transmission link of the chain of infection.
- B. Kills microbes inside the patient's bloodstream  
  _Rationale:_ Hand hygiene acts on the hands, not systemically inside the patient.
- C. Replaces the need for any other precaution  
  _Rationale:_ It is essential but does not replace PPE or other precautions.
- D. Sterilises the skin permanently  
  _Rationale:_ Hand hygiene reduces transient flora temporarily; it does not sterilise skin permanently.**MST-1771-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) A reusable surgical instrument that enters sterile tissue must be processed to what level before reuse?

- A. Sterilisation, removing all microbial life including spores **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: instruments entering sterile tissue are critical items and require sterilisation.
- B. Low-level disinfection only  
  _Rationale:_ Low-level disinfection is insufficient for items entering sterile tissue.
- C. Cleaning with detergent only  
  _Rationale:_ Cleaning removes soil but does not render a critical item safe for sterile-tissue use.
- D. Wiping with alcohol at the bedside  
  _Rationale:_ Alcohol wiping does not achieve sterilisation of a critical instrument.**MST-1771-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO actions directly support breaking the chain of infection during a diarrhoeal outbreak? (Select TWO)

- A. Isolating or cohorting affected residents **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: isolation/cohorting limits the reservoir and transmission routes.
- B. Reinforcing hand hygiene with soap and water **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: hand hygiene interrupts transmission, and soap and water is preferred for some spore-forming causes.
- C. Stopping all cleaning to avoid disturbing surfaces  
  _Rationale:_ Enhanced cleaning is needed in an outbreak; stopping cleaning would worsen spread.
- D. Sharing equipment between affected and unaffected residents  
  _Rationale:_ Sharing equipment spreads infection and must be avoided.
