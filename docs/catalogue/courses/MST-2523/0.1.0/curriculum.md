## Learning outcomes

1. Distinguish provisions from other liabilities and accruals
2. Apply the recognition criteria for a provision
3. Distinguish legal and constructive obligations
4. Measure a provision using best estimate, expected value and present value
5. Account for contingent liabilities and contingent assets through disclosure
6. Apply the standard to common cases such as onerous contracts, restructurings and warranties

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2523-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Which of the following is one of the recognition criteria for a provision under IAS 37?

- A. A present obligation arising from a past event that is probable and can be reliably estimated **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a provision requires a present obligation, probable outflow and a reliable estimate.
- B. A possible obligation that might arise from a future event  
  _Rationale:_ That describes a contingent liability, which is disclosed, not provided for.
- C. Any expense management expects to incur next year  
  _Rationale:_ Expected future expenses are not provisions without a present obligation.
- D. A future operating loss the entity forecasts  
  _Rationale:_ Provisions are not recognised for future operating losses.

**MST-2523-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO situations are accounted for by disclosure rather than recognition under IAS 37? (Select TWO.)

- A. A contingent liability where an outflow is possible but not probable **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: possible-but-not-probable obligations are disclosed as contingent liabilities.
- B. A contingent asset where an inflow is probable but not virtually certain **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: probable (not virtually certain) inflows are disclosed as contingent assets, not recognised.
- C. A warranty obligation that is probable and reliably measurable  
  _Rationale:_ That meets the criteria for a provision and is recognised.
- D. A decommissioning obligation that already exists and is estimable  
  _Rationale:_ That is recognised as a provision, not merely disclosed.

**MST-2523-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A company wants to recognise a provision for expected operating losses of a division next year. Why is this not permitted under IAS 37?

- A. Future operating losses do not arise from a past obligating event, so there is no present obligation to provide for **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: there is no present obligation, so no provision is recognised.
- B. Operating losses are always recognised as contingent assets  
  _Rationale:_ They are neither provisions nor contingent assets.
- C. Provisions can only be made for tax  
  _Rationale:_ Provisions are not limited to tax.
- D. The loss must instead be capitalised as an asset  
  _Rationale:_ An expected loss is not an asset.
