## Learning outcomes

1. Explain energy-management principles and the ISO 50001 cycle
2. Collect and analyse energy data to build a baseline and KPIs
3. Identify and quantify energy-efficiency opportunities
4. Evaluate projects using savings, payback and simple financials
5. Sustain savings through monitoring, targeting and behaviour

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1175-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) What is the difference between power measured in kW and energy measured in kWh?

- A. Power is the rate of use; energy is power used over time **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: kW is instantaneous rate; kWh is kW multiplied by hours of use.
- B. They are the same unit  
  _Rationale:_ They measure different things: rate versus accumulated quantity.
- C. kWh is the rate and kW is the total  
  _Rationale:_ This reverses the definitions.
- D. kW measures cost and kWh measures carbon  
  _Rationale:_ Both are energy/power units, not cost or carbon measures.

**MST-1175-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Before comparing two months' energy consumption, which TWO normalisations are usually appropriate? (Select TWO.)

- A. Adjust for production output or occupancy **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: energy per unit of output removes volume effects.
- B. Adjust for weather (e.g. heating/cooling degree-days) **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: weather strongly drives heating and cooling energy.
- C. Ignore any differences and compare raw totals  
  _Rationale:_ Raw totals confound real efficiency with volume and weather.
- D. Convert kWh into kW to compare  
  _Rationale:_ That mixes rate and quantity and is not a normalisation.

**MST-1175-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A measure costs 10,000 and saves 4,000 per year. What is its simple payback period?

- A. 2.5 years **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: simple payback = cost / annual saving = 10,000 / 4,000 = 2.5 years.
- B. 4 years  
  _Rationale:_ This inverts the ratio; payback is cost divided by annual saving.
- C. 0.4 years  
  _Rationale:_ This divides saving by cost; payback is the reverse.
- D. 10 years  
  _Rationale:_ This ignores the annual saving figure entirely.
