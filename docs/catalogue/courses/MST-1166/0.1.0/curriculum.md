## Learning outcomes

1. Compare transport modes and their cost, speed and reliability trade-offs
2. Plan freight using Incoterms, documentation and carrier selection
3. Design routes and loads to reduce cost and improve service
4. Manage transportation cost, rates and freight audit
5. Use transportation KPIs and visibility to control performance

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1166-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A shipment is low value per kilogram, not time-critical, and moves across an ocean. Which mode is usually most appropriate?

- A. Ocean freight **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: for low value density and non-urgent cross-ocean moves, sea freight offers the lowest cost per unit.
- B. Air freight  
  _Rationale:_ Air is fast but far costlier, unjustified for low-value, non-urgent goods.
- C. Expedited road only  
  _Rationale:_ Road cannot cross an ocean and expedited service adds needless cost.
- D. Courier parcel  
  _Rationale:_ Parcel courier suits small urgent items, not bulk low-value ocean cargo.

**MST-1166-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Under Incoterms, which TWO points are defined by the chosen term? (Select TWO.)

- A. Where risk of loss or damage transfers from seller to buyer **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: each Incoterm fixes the point where risk passes.
- B. Which party arranges and pays for carriage to a point **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: Incoterms allocate transport cost and arrangement responsibilities.
- C. The retail selling price of the goods  
  _Rationale:_ Incoterms do not set the product's market price.
- D. The product's quality specification  
  _Rationale:_ Quality specs are defined in the contract of sale, not by Incoterms.

**MST-1166-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Two carriers have the same average transit time, but one varies far more day to day. Why does the lower-variability carrier often give better service?

- A. Reliability (low variability) improves on-time performance and planning **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: consistent transit times let customers and planners meet windows; averages hide reliability.
- B. A higher average always means better service  
  _Rationale:_ The averages are equal here; variability is the differentiator.
- C. Variability has no effect on on-time delivery  
  _Rationale:_ High variability directly causes missed delivery windows.
- D. Lower variability always means lower cost  
  _Rationale:_ Reliability and cost are related but not identical; the benefit here is service.
