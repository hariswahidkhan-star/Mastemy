## Learning outcomes

1. Describe the properties of acids and bases
2. Use the pH scale to classify solutions as acidic, neutral or alkaline
3. Explain how indicators show whether a solution is acidic or alkaline
4. Describe neutralisation and write simple word equations for it
5. Give everyday examples of acids, bases and neutralisation
6. Follow laboratory safety and use only dilute, teacher-approved solutions

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance. Any experiment ideas are optional and require adult supervision; they are not assessed.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2857-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A solution has a pH of 2. How is it best described?

- A. Strongly acidic **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a low pH well below 7 means strongly acidic.
- B. Neutral  
  _Rationale:_ Neutral is pH 7; pH 2 is acidic.
- C. Strongly alkaline  
  _Rationale:_ Alkaline solutions have pH above 7; pH 2 is acidic.
- D. Pure water  
  _Rationale:_ Pure water is pH 7, not pH 2.

**MST-2857-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO observations suggest a solution is an acid? (Select TWO.)

- A. It turns blue litmus paper red **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: acids turn blue litmus red.
- B. It has a pH below 7 **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: acids have a pH less than 7.
- C. It turns red litmus paper blue  
  _Rationale:_ That indicates a base/alkali, not an acid.
- D. It has a pH of exactly 7  
  _Rationale:_ pH 7 is neutral, not acidic.

**MST-2857-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) What are the products when a dilute acid reacts with a base in neutralisation?

- A. A salt and water **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: acid + base -> salt + water.
- B. Only a gas  
  _Rationale:_ Neutralisation forms a salt and water, not just a gas.
- C. A stronger acid  
  _Rationale:_ Neutralisation reduces acidity; it does not make a stronger acid.
- D. Nothing new forms  
  _Rationale:_ New products, a salt and water, are formed.
