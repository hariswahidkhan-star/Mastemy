## Learning outcomes

1. Explain the theory of evolution by natural selection
2. Describe how variation arises in populations
3. Explain how adaptations improve survival and reproduction
4. Interpret evidence for evolution such as fossils
5. Explain how species change over long timescales
6. Discuss examples such as antibiotic resistance

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2873-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Natural selection favours individuals that:

- A. Are better suited to survive and reproduce **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: better-suited individuals survive and pass on their genes.
- B. Choose to change their own genes  
  _Rationale:_ Individuals cannot choose to change their genes.
- C. Are always the largest  
  _Rationale:_ Being largest is not always an advantage.
- D. Never produce any offspring  
  _Rationale:_ Those that do not reproduce pass on no genes.

**MST-2873-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) Which provides direct evidence that species have changed over long periods?

- A. Fossils in rock layers **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: fossils show how organisms changed over time.
- B. The colour of modern cars  
  _Rationale:_ Cars are not biological evidence of evolution.
- C. Daily weather records  
  _Rationale:_ Weather does not show species changing over time.
- D. A single photograph of one animal  
  _Rationale:_ One photo cannot show change across long timescales.

**MST-2873-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO factors drive evolution by natural selection? (Select TWO.)

- A. Variation within a population **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: variation provides the differences selection acts on.
- B. A selection pressure such as predators or disease **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: selection pressures determine which variants survive.
- C. All individuals being identical  
  _Rationale:_ Without variation, selection cannot act.
- D. Organisms deciding to improve themselves  
  _Rationale:_ Evolution is not driven by conscious choice.
