## Learning outcomes

1. Describe PMO types, mandates and the value a PMO delivers
2. Apply governance, methodology and portfolio-management practices through a PMO
3. Evaluate PMO services, metrics and performance reporting
4. Analyse PMO maturity, change and stakeholder engagement

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-0130-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Executives complain the PMO produces many status reports but adds little value. Which change best addresses the root problem?

- A. Tie PMO metrics to business outcomes and decisions they inform **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: linking metrics to outcomes and decisions makes the PMO valuable rather than a report factory.
- B. Produce more frequent reports  
  _Rationale:_ More of a low-value activity does not add value.
- C. Add more templates for teams to fill in  
  _Rationale:_ Added overhead without outcome linkage does not solve the problem.
- D. Stop measuring anything  
  _Rationale:_ Abandoning metrics removes the PMO's ability to show value.

**MST-0130-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) A young company with few projects and low process maturity wants a PMO. Which operating model usually fits best initially?

- A. A supportive PMO providing templates, coaching and light governance **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a supportive model suits low maturity, building capability before heavier control.
- B. A controlling PMO enforcing strict compliance immediately  
  _Rationale:_ Heavy control on immature teams tends to create resistance and overhead.
- C. A directive PMO that runs every project centrally  
  _Rationale:_ Full central control is rarely appropriate for a small, immature portfolio.
- D. No PMO because the company is small  
  _Rationale:_ The scenario establishes the company wants a PMO; the question is the model.

**MST-0130-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Designing portfolio prioritisation for the PMO, which TWO practices make selection defensible? (Select TWO)

- A. Scoring initiatives against explicit, weighted strategic criteria **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: transparent weighted criteria make prioritisation defensible.
- B. Considering capacity and interdependencies, not just individual value **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: capacity and dependencies shape what can actually be delivered.
- C. Ranking strictly by the seniority of the requesting executive  
  _Rationale:_ Seniority-based ranking is not a defensible portfolio method.
- D. Approving every proposed project to avoid conflict  
  _Rationale:_ Approving everything defeats prioritisation.
- E. Hiding the criteria so decisions cannot be questioned  
  _Rationale:_ Opacity undermines defensibility.
