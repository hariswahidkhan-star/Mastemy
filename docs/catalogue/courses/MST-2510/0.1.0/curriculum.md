## Learning outcomes

1. Apply the five-step model to identify revenue from a contract with a customer
2. Identify distinct performance obligations within a contract
3. Determine and allocate the transaction price, including variable consideration
4. Decide whether revenue is recognised over time or at a point in time
5. Account for common features such as contract modifications, warranties and principal-versus-agent situations
6. Prepare the core disclosures that explain revenue to users

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2510-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) In the five-step revenue model, what must an entity do immediately after identifying the contract with a customer?

- A. Identify the separate performance obligations in the contract **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: step 2 is identifying the distinct performance obligations.
- B. Recognise the entire transaction price as revenue  
  _Rationale:_ Recognition is the final step, only as obligations are satisfied.
- C. Determine the entity's income tax on the contract  
  _Rationale:_ Tax is outside the revenue model.
- D. Write off any expected contract costs  
  _Rationale:_ Cost treatment is not the next step in the model.

**MST-2510-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO factors indicate that a performance obligation is satisfied over time rather than at a point in time? (Select TWO.)

- A. The customer simultaneously receives and consumes the benefits as the entity performs **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: this is one of the over-time criteria.
- B. The entity's performance creates an asset with no alternative use and the entity has an enforceable right to payment for work completed to date **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: this combination is an over-time criterion.
- C. The customer pays the full price in advance  
  _Rationale:_ Advance payment does not by itself determine timing of recognition.
- D. The contract includes a fixed price  
  _Rationale:_ A fixed price is unrelated to whether control transfers over time.

**MST-2510-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A retailer earns a fixed fee for arranging sales of a manufacturer's goods and never controls the goods. How should it most likely recognise revenue?

- A. As an agent, recognising only the net fee for arranging the sale **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: without control of the goods the retailer is an agent and recognises the net commission.
- B. As a principal, recognising the gross selling price of the goods  
  _Rationale:_ Gross (principal) treatment requires control of the goods before transfer.
- C. Not at all, because arranging sales is never revenue  
  _Rationale:_ Arranging sales for a fee is revenue; the question is gross vs net.
- D. Only when the manufacturer is paid  
  _Rationale:_ Timing is driven by satisfying the obligation, not the manufacturer's receipt.
