## Learning outcomes

1. Explain health-and-safety management principles and the duty of care
2. Identify hazards and assess risk using a structured method
3. Apply the hierarchy of controls to reduce risk
4. Describe the elements of a health-and-safety management system
5. Investigate incidents and drive corrective and preventive action

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Hands-on operation of the tools and workflows taught, the quality of live outputs, and professional judgement on the job are taught by video demonstration and model-answer analysis; the certificate assessment uses single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only and never requires uploads, recorded work, graded prompts or peer review.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1171-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Which control sits highest in the hierarchy of controls for a hazardous solvent?

- A. Eliminate the solvent by redesigning the process to not need it **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: elimination is the most effective, highest-order control.
- B. Provide respirators to workers  
  _Rationale:_ PPE is the lowest-order control, used last.
- C. Put up a warning sign  
  _Rationale:_ Signage is an administrative control, lower than elimination.
- D. Write a safe-work procedure  
  _Rationale:_ Procedures are administrative controls, below elimination and substitution.

**MST-1171-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) A risk rating uses likelihood and severity. Which TWO statements are correct? (Select TWO.)

- A. Raising either likelihood or severity raises the risk rating **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: risk is a function of both factors.
- B. A low-likelihood but catastrophic event can still rate as high risk **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: high severity can drive a high rating despite low likelihood.
- C. Only likelihood determines the risk rating  
  _Rationale:_ Severity is equally part of the rating.
- D. Severity is irrelevant once likelihood is known  
  _Rationale:_ Severity is essential to the rating.

**MST-1171-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) An incident investigation concludes 'the worker was careless'. Why is this inadequate?

- A. It stops at individual blame and ignores system causes that could prevent recurrence **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: effective investigation finds systemic causes and preventive actions.
- B. It is the ideal root cause for most incidents  
  _Rationale:_ Individual blame is rarely the true root cause and prevents learning.
- C. It satisfies preventive-action requirements  
  _Rationale:_ Blame alone yields no preventive action.
- D. It removes the need for corrective action  
  _Rationale:_ Corrective and preventive action are still required.
