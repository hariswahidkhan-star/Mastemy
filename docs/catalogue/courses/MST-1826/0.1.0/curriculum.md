## Learning outcomes

1. Explain negotiation types, styles and the value of preparation
2. Prepare using interests, BATNA, ZOPA and targets
3. Create value through interest-based bargaining
4. Claim value with anchoring, concessions and tactics
5. Close agreements and handle difficult tactics and relationships

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Integrative case

A small agency must renew a contract with a key client who is demanding a price cut. Prepare the negotiation: clarify interests on both sides, set a target and walkaway using BATNA, plan how to expand the deal beyond price, and run the conversation to a durable agreement that protects the relationship.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1826-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) What does your BATNA represent in a negotiation?

- A. Your best alternative if no agreement is reached **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: BATNA is your best option away from the table and sets your walkaway power.
- B. The maximum you are willing to pay  
  _Rationale:_ That is closer to your reservation price, not your BATNA.
- C. The other side's opening offer  
  _Rationale:_ BATNA is your own alternative, not their offer.
- D. The midpoint between both positions  
  _Rationale:_ BATNA is independent of the other party's position.

**MST-1826-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO moves help create value (integrative bargaining) rather than just claim it? (Select TWO.)

- A. Trade issues each side values differently **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: logrolling across differing priorities expands the pie.
- B. Ask questions to uncover the other side's interests **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: understanding interests reveals mutual-gain options.
- C. Make the first demand as extreme as possible  
  _Rationale:_ Extreme anchoring is a value-claiming, not value-creating, move.
- D. Refuse to share any information  
  _Rationale:_ Withholding all information blocks mutual-gain trades.

**MST-1826-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A counterpart opens with an extreme anchor. What is a sound response?

- A. Decline to counter near the anchor and re-anchor with your own justified number **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: resetting with a reasoned counter avoids being pulled toward their anchor.
- B. Immediately split the difference with their anchor  
  _Rationale:_ Splitting from an extreme anchor concedes too much.
- C. Accept it to preserve goodwill  
  _Rationale:_ Accepting an extreme anchor sacrifices your interests.
- D. End the negotiation at once  
  _Rationale:_ An aggressive anchor is a tactic to manage, not an automatic dealbreaker.
