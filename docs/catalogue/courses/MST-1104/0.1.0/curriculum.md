## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1104-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A sound content strategy starts by defining:

- A. How many posts to publish each week  
  _Rationale:_ Volume is an output decision, not the starting point.
- B. Who the audience is and what business goal the content serves **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: audience and purpose anchor every later content decision.
- C. Which colours to use in graphics  
  _Rationale:_ Visual choices follow strategy, not the other way round.
- D. The social platforms competitors use  
  _Rationale:_ Competitor channels are input, not the foundation of your strategy.

**MST-1104-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) 'Content atomisation' (repurposing) primarily means:

- A. Deleting old content to start fresh  
  _Rationale:_ Atomisation reuses content; it does not delete it.
- B. Breaking one substantial piece into many smaller formats for different channels **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a pillar piece is broken down and reshaped for multiple channels.
- C. Writing every piece completely from scratch  
  _Rationale:_ That is the opposite of repurposing.
- D. Publishing the same article on ten sites verbatim  
  _Rationale:_ Duplicating verbatim risks duplicate-content issues and is not atomisation.

**MST-1104-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO are meaningful content metrics tied to goals rather than vanity? (Select TWO)

- A. Conversions or leads generated from a piece **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: conversions tie content directly to a business goal.
- B. Engaged time or scroll depth on a page **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: engagement depth indicates whether content actually held attention.
- C. Raw page views with no context  
  _Rationale:_ Unqualified views are a classic vanity metric.
- D. Number of words published this month  
  _Rationale:_ Word count measures effort, not value delivered.
