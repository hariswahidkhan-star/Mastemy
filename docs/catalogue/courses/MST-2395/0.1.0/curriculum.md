## Learning outcomes

1. Explain the four-stroke and two-stroke operating cycles and their thermodynamic basis
2. Describe the main engine components and their functions
3. Analyse air-fuel mixing, combustion and the role of ignition timing
4. Interpret engine performance maps for power, torque and specific fuel consumption
5. Explain emissions formation and the principal after-treatment technologies
6. Diagnose common combustion and mechanical faults from symptoms

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2395-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) During which stroke of a four-stroke petrol engine does the spark plug normally fire to initiate combustion?

- A. At the end of the intake stroke  
  _Rationale:_ Firing during intake would ignite an incomplete, uncompressed charge.
- B. Near the end of the compression stroke **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Ignition occurs shortly before top dead centre on compression so peak pressure arrives just after TDC on the power stroke.
- C. During the exhaust stroke  
  _Rationale:_ The exhaust stroke expels burned gas; igniting then does no useful work.
- D. At bottom dead centre of the power stroke  
  _Rationale:_ That is far too late; the useful expansion would already be lost.

**MST-2395-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) An engine runs with lambda = 1.0. What does this indicate about the air-fuel mixture?

- A. The mixture is exactly stoichiometric **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Lambda = 1.0 means the actual air-fuel ratio equals the stoichiometric ratio.
- B. The mixture is rich with excess fuel  
  _Rationale:_ A rich mixture gives lambda below 1.0.
- C. The mixture is lean with excess air  
  _Rationale:_ A lean mixture gives lambda above 1.0.
- D. There is no fuel being injected  
  _Rationale:_ Zero fuel would not produce a defined lambda of 1.0.

**MST-2395-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) A technician observes engine knock under load. Select TWO changes that would typically reduce the tendency to knock.

- A. Retard the ignition timing **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Later ignition lowers peak in-cylinder pressure and temperature, reducing end-gas auto-ignition.
- B. Use a higher-octane fuel **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Higher octane resists auto-ignition, directly reducing knock under load.
- C. Increase the compression ratio further  
  _Rationale:_ Raising compression increases end-gas pressure and temperature, worsening knock.
- D. Lean the mixture significantly at high load  
  _Rationale:_ Leaning at high load raises combustion temperatures and tends to increase knock.
- E. Fit larger diameter wheels  
  _Rationale:_ Wheel size does not affect in-cylinder combustion conditions.
