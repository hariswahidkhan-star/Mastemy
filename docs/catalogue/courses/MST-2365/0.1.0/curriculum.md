## Learning outcomes

1. Describe how building energy use is distributed across HVAC, lighting and plug loads
2. Explain how HVAC systems and their controls affect energy and comfort
3. Interpret building management system data and key performance indicators
4. Apply measurement and verification to confirm savings from measures
5. Relate the building envelope and ventilation to heating and cooling demand
6. Plan a continuous commissioning and optimisation routine

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2365-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) In a typical commercial office, which end use is usually the single largest share of energy?

- A. HVAC (heating, ventilation and air conditioning) **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ HVAC is typically the dominant energy end use in offices.
- B. Exterior signage  
  _Rationale:_ Signage is a minor load.
- C. Elevators  
  _Rationale:_ Vertical transport is usually a small share.
- D. Vending machines  
  _Rationale:_ Plug loads like vending are minor compared with HVAC.

**MST-2365-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO practices help confirm that an energy-saving measure actually delivered savings? (Select TWO.)

- A. Establishing a measured baseline before the change **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ A baseline is essential to attribute savings.
- B. Comparing adjusted post-measure consumption against that baseline **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Measurement and verification compares against the adjusted baseline.
- C. Assuming the vendor's brochure figure is the realised saving  
  _Rationale:_ Brochure figures are not verified savings.
- D. Repainting the plant room  
  _Rationale:_ Cosmetic work does not verify savings.

**MST-2365-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A BMS trend shows a zone's heating valve and cooling coil both active at the same time. What does this most likely indicate?

- A. A control fault causing simultaneous heating and cooling that wastes energy **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Simultaneous heating and cooling is a classic controls fault wasting energy.
- B. Optimal comfort control working as designed  
  _Rationale:_ Fighting heating and cooling is wasteful, not optimal.
- C. That the building envelope is perfectly sealed  
  _Rationale:_ Envelope sealing is unrelated to this control conflict.
- D. That the meter is broken  
  _Rationale:_ The trend reflects control state, not a meter fault.
