## Learning outcomes

1. Explain the core disciplines of aerospace engineering and how they combine in a flight vehicle
2. Describe the physics of flight: lift, drag, thrust and weight, and how they balance
3. Relate atmospheric and space environments to vehicle design choices
4. Outline the systems-engineering lifecycle used to develop aerospace vehicles
5. Identify major career paths and the role of standards, safety and certification
6. Communicate design trade-offs clearly to non-specialist stakeholders

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2169-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) In steady level cruise, how do the four forces acting on an aircraft relate?

- A. Lift balances weight and thrust balances drag **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: in steady level flight the opposing force pairs are in balance.
- B. Thrust must always exceed lift  
  _Rationale:_ Thrust opposes drag, not lift; this confuses the force pairs.
- C. Weight is eliminated once airborne  
  _Rationale:_ Weight always acts; it is balanced by lift, not removed.
- D. Drag only acts during landing  
  _Rationale:_ Drag acts whenever the vehicle moves through air.

**MST-2169-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO are systems-engineering activities used across an aerospace vehicle's lifecycle? (Select TWO.)

- A. Defining requirements and running trade studies **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: requirements and trade studies are core systems-engineering activities.
- B. Verification and validation against requirements **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: V&V confirms the design meets its requirements.
- C. Choosing the paint colour for marketing  
  _Rationale:_ Cosmetic choices are not systems-engineering lifecycle activities.
- D. Ignoring stakeholder needs to save time  
  _Rationale:_ Systems engineering is driven by stakeholder requirements.

**MST-2169-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why do engineers favour lightweight high-strength materials in aircraft structures?

- A. They improve the strength-to-weight ratio, aiding efficiency and payload **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a better strength-to-weight ratio is the key design driver.
- B. Because lighter always means cheaper  
  _Rationale:_ Advanced lightweight materials are often more expensive, not cheaper.
- C. Because weight has no effect on flight  
  _Rationale:_ Weight is one of the four forces and strongly affects performance.
- D. Because heavier materials cannot be machined  
  _Rationale:_ Machinability is not the reason; the driver is strength-to-weight.
