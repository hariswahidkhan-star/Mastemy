## Learning outcomes

1. Explain the legal issues relating to the formation of commercial contracts
2. Describe the fundamentals of contract terms and specifications
3. Compare the main types of commercial agreement used in procurement
4. Explain how documents and schedules form part of a contract

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1263-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A supplier displays goods with a price in a catalogue. In contract law this is normally treated as:

- A. An offer  
  _Rationale:_ A catalogue price is generally not an offer capable of acceptance.
- B. An invitation to treat **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: catalogues and displays are usually invitations to treat, inviting offers.
- C. Acceptance  
  _Rationale:_ Acceptance responds to an offer; a catalogue precedes any offer.
- D. Consideration  
  _Rationale:_ Consideration is the value exchanged, not the display itself.

**MST-1263-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) Breach of which type of term generally entitles the injured party to terminate and claim damages?

- A. A warranty  
  _Rationale:_ Breach of warranty usually gives damages only, not termination.
- B. A condition **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a condition is a fundamental term; its breach allows termination and damages.
- C. A representation  
  _Rationale:_ A representation is a pre-contract statement, not a contract term.
- D. An innominate term  
  _Rationale:_ An innominate term's remedy depends on the seriousness of the breach.

**MST-1263-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO documents would typically appear in a contract's order of precedence?

- A. The signed form of agreement **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the form of agreement is normally the top-ranking contract document.
- B. The agreed specification/schedules **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the specification and schedules form part of the contract and are ranked within the precedence.
- C. A supplier's internal marketing brochure  
  _Rationale:_ Marketing material is not usually a contract document.
- D. An unrelated employee handbook  
  _Rationale:_ An employee handbook is internal HR policy, not part of the commercial contract.
