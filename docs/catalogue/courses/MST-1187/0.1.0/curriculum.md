## Learning outcomes

1. Explain how AI supports investment research without replacing judgement
2. Use AI to summarise filings, news and research efficiently
3. Verify AI-generated figures and claims against primary sources
4. Support portfolio analysis and scenario questions with AI responsibly
5. Manage compliance, bias and conflict-of-interest risks when using AI

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1187-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) An AI summary states a company's quarterly revenue grew 12%. Before putting this in a client-facing note, what should the analyst do?

- A. Trace the figure back to the primary filing and confirm the period and number  **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: figures must be verified against primary sources before client use.
- B. Publish it because the summary sounded authoritative  
  _Rationale:_ Authoritative tone does not guarantee the number is correct or current.
- C. Round it up to make the story stronger  
  _Rationale:_ Altering figures to improve a narrative is misleading and improper.
- D. Assume the model never makes numerical errors  
  _Rationale:_ Models can misstate or carry stale figures; verification is required.

**MST-1187-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO are appropriate uses of AI in investment research? (Select TWO.)

- A. Drafting a first-pass summary of a long filing for an analyst to check  **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: summarisation to be verified is a sound, time-saving use.
- B. Generating scenario questions to structure an analyst's own thinking  **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: AI can help frame scenarios the analyst then evaluates.
- C. Issuing an automated buy recommendation directly to clients with no review  
  _Rationale:_ Unreviewed AI recommendations to clients carry serious compliance and accuracy risk.
- D. Fabricating figures when the source is unavailable  
  _Rationale:_ Fabricating data is never acceptable.

**MST-1187-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) An analyst presents an AI-generated two-scenario comparison to the portfolio manager as 'what will happen' to a stock. Why is this framing wrong?

- A. Scenarios are illustrative assumptions, not predictions of actual outcomes  **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: scenario outputs rest on assumptions and must not be sold as forecasts.
- B. AI scenarios are always exactly correct  
  _Rationale:_ Scenarios depend on assumptions and are not guaranteed outcomes.
- C. Portfolio managers cannot read scenario analysis  
  _Rationale:_ PMs use scenarios routinely; the issue is the misleading framing.
- D. Comparisons should never be shown to a PM  
  _Rationale:_ Comparisons are useful when framed honestly as scenarios.
