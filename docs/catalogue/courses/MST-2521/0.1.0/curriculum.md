## Learning outcomes

1. Determine the elements of cost recognised for an item of property, plant and equipment
2. Apply the cost model and the revaluation model for subsequent measurement
3. Account for depreciation, useful lives and residual values
4. Account for subsequent expenditure, componentisation and major inspections
5. Account for revaluations, including the revaluation surplus and reversals
6. Account for derecognition and prepare the required disclosures

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2521-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Which cost is included in the initial cost of an item of property, plant and equipment?

- A. The costs directly attributable to bringing the asset to the location and condition for its intended use **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: directly attributable costs are capitalised.
- B. The cost of staff training unrelated to the asset  
  _Rationale:_ General training is not a directly attributable cost of the asset.
- C. Administrative overheads of the whole entity  
  _Rationale:_ General administration is not capitalised into the asset.
- D. Future repair costs expected over the asset's life  
  _Rationale:_ Future repairs are expensed when incurred, not capitalised at recognition.

**MST-2521-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO statements about the revaluation model in IAS 16 are correct? (Select TWO.)

- A. A revaluation increase is generally recognised in other comprehensive income and accumulated in a revaluation surplus **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: increases go to OCI/revaluation surplus (except reversing a prior decrease).
- B. If the revaluation model is used, revaluations must be kept sufficiently up to date **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: revaluations must be regular enough that carrying amount does not differ materially from fair value.
- C. Revaluation increases are always recognised in profit or loss  
  _Rationale:_ Increases go to OCI unless reversing a previous decrease recognised in P&L.
- D. Only one asset in a class may be revalued  
  _Rationale:_ The whole class must be revalued, not a single item.

**MST-2521-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A company changes the estimated useful life of a machine from 10 to 7 years partway through its life. How is this change accounted for?

- A. Prospectively, by depreciating the remaining carrying amount over the revised remaining life **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a change in estimate is applied prospectively.
- B. Retrospectively, by restating prior-year depreciation  
  _Rationale:_ A change in estimate is not applied retrospectively.
- C. By writing the asset off immediately  
  _Rationale:_ The asset is not written off; depreciation is simply re-spread.
- D. By leaving depreciation unchanged  
  _Rationale:_ The revised life changes future depreciation.
