## Learning outcomes

1. Plot and read points in all four quadrants
2. Find the midpoint and length of a line segment
3. Find the gradient and equation of a straight line
4. Determine whether lines are parallel or perpendicular
5. Represent and add vectors in component form
6. Find the magnitude of a vector

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2828-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) What is the midpoint of the points (2, 4) and (6, 8)?

- A. (4, 6) **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the midpoint averages the coordinates, ((2+6)/2, (4+8)/2) = (4, 6).
- B. (8, 12)  
  _Rationale:_ That adds the coordinates instead of averaging them.
- C. (4, 4)  
  _Rationale:_ The y-coordinate should be (4+8)/2 = 6, not 4.
- D. (2, 2)  
  _Rationale:_ That subtracts and halves; the midpoint averages the values.

**MST-2828-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Select TWO statements that are true about parallel lines. (Select TWO.)

- A. They have equal gradients **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: parallel lines rise at the same rate, so their gradients are equal.
- B. They never meet **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: parallel lines stay the same distance apart and never intersect.
- C. They have gradients that multiply to -1  
  _Rationale:_ That describes perpendicular lines, not parallel ones.
- D. They always pass through the origin  
  _Rationale:_ Parallel lines can have any y-intercepts.

**MST-2828-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A vector has components (3, 4). What is its magnitude?

- A. 5 **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: magnitude is the square root of (3 squared + 4 squared) = square root of 25 = 5.
- B. 7  
  _Rationale:_ That adds the components; magnitude uses Pythagoras on them.
- C. 12  
  _Rationale:_ 12 is 3 x 4, which is not the magnitude.
- D. 25  
  _Rationale:_ 25 is the sum of the squares; the magnitude is its square root, 5.
