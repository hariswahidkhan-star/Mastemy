## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-0384-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) The axial normal stress in a bar of cross-sectional area A carrying axial force F is:

- A. F divided by A **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: normal stress sigma = F/A for uniform axial loading.
- B. F multiplied by A  
  _Rationale:_ Multiplying force by area does not give stress; units would be wrong.
- C. A divided by F  
  _Rationale:_ This is the inverse of stress and has the wrong units.
- D. F divided by the length of the bar  
  _Rationale:_ Stress depends on area, not on the bar's length.

**MST-0384-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) Fatigue failure in a machine component is primarily associated with:

- A. Repeated (cyclic) loading over many cycles **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: fatigue arises from fluctuating stresses applied repeatedly.
- B. A single static overload applied once  
  _Rationale:_ That is static failure, not fatigue.
- C. Loads well below any stress whatsoever  
  _Rationale:_ Fatigue still requires fluctuating stress, not zero stress.
- D. Only thermal expansion with no mechanical load  
  _Rationale:_ Fatigue is driven by cyclic mechanical stress, not expansion alone.

**MST-0384-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO statements about static failure theories for ductile materials are generally correct?

- A. The distortion-energy (von Mises) theory is commonly used for ductile materials **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: von Mises is a standard ductile-failure criterion.
- B. The maximum-shear-stress theory is a conservative ductile criterion **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: Tresca (max-shear) is a common, more conservative ductile theory.
- C. Ductile failure theories are based solely on the material's color  
  _Rationale:_ Color is irrelevant to failure theory.
- D. Ductile materials never yield before fracture  
  _Rationale:_ Ductile materials characteristically yield before fracture.
- E. Failure theories require no knowledge of the stress state  
  _Rationale:_ The stress state is essential input to any failure theory.
