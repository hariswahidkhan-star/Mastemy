## Learning outcomes

1. Calculate the mean, median, mode and range of a data set
2. Choose an appropriate average for a situation
3. Represent data using suitable charts and graphs
4. Interpret data to draw and justify conclusions
5. Describe probability using words and fractions
6. Calculate probabilities for simple equally likely events

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2822-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) What is the mean of 4, 6, 8 and 2?

- A. 5 **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the total is 20 and there are 4 values, so 20 divided by 4 is 5.
- B. 6  
  _Rationale:_ 6 is a value in the list, not the mean; the mean is 5.
- C. 8  
  _Rationale:_ 8 is the largest value, not the mean.
- D. 20  
  _Rationale:_ 20 is the total; the mean divides the total by how many values there are.

**MST-2822-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Select TWO statements that correctly describe probability. (Select TWO.)

- A. A probability of 0 means the event is impossible **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: 0 means it cannot happen.
- B. A probability of 1 means the event is certain **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: 1 means it will definitely happen.
- C. A probability can be greater than 1  
  _Rationale:_ Probabilities range from 0 to 1 only.
- D. A probability of 0.5 means the event is impossible  
  _Rationale:_ 0.5 means an even chance, not impossible.

**MST-2822-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A fair six-sided die is rolled. What is the probability of rolling a number greater than 4?

- A. 2/6 **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the numbers greater than 4 are 5 and 6, so 2 of the 6 outcomes.
- B. 4/6  
  _Rationale:_ That counts numbers up to and including 4, not greater than 4.
- C. 1/6  
  _Rationale:_ There are two outcomes greater than 4, not one.
- D. 6/6  
  _Rationale:_ Not every roll is greater than 4, so it is not certain.
