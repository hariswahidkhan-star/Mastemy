## Integrative case

Diagnose a retail investor's decisions over a volatile year: name the biases behind holding losers and selling winners, show how framing and loss aversion drove them, explain the herding that fed a bubble, and recommend simple rules that reduce the damage.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1730-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) An investor holds onto losing stocks too long but sells winners too quickly. This is the:

- A. Disposition effect **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the disposition effect describes exactly this pattern.
- B. Anchoring bias  
  _Rationale:_ Anchoring is fixating on a reference number.
- C. Confirmation bias  
  _Rationale:_ Confirmation bias is seeking agreeing evidence.
- D. Availability bias  
  _Rationale:_ Availability is overweighting easily recalled events.

**MST-1730-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select ALL that apply) Which two statements about loss aversion are correct? (Select TWO) (Select TWO)

- A. People tend to feel losses more strongly than equivalent gains **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: this asymmetry defines loss aversion.
- B. It can lead investors to avoid crystallising a loss **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: aversion to losses delays selling losers.
- C. It means people enjoy losses more than gains  
  _Rationale:_ People dislike losses; they do not enjoy them.
- D. It proves markets are always perfectly efficient  
  _Rationale:_ It is evidence of behaviour inconsistent with pure efficiency.

**MST-1730-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why can obvious mispricing sometimes persist in markets?

- A. There are real limits to arbitrage, such as cost and risk **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: frictions stop arbitrage from instantly correcting prices.
- B. Prices are always exactly correct  
  _Rationale:_ If so, mispricing could not exist by definition.
- C. No investor ever notices mispricing  
  _Rationale:_ Some do notice; acting on it is constrained.
- D. Regulators set every price  
  _Rationale:_ Market prices are not set by regulators.
