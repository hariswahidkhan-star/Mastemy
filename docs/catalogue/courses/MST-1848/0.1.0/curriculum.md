## Learning outcomes

1. Recognize and read HSK 1-level vocabulary and basic characters/pinyin
2. Apply basic Chinese grammar: word order, particles and measure words
3. Demonstrate listening comprehension of simple everyday Chinese
4. Understand simple everyday expressions for common situations

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: This preparation assesses HSK 1 listening and reading knowledge through MCQ/MR only; it does not assess productive speaking or handwriting performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1848-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) In a basic Chinese sentence, what is the usual word order?

- A. Subject - verb - object **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: basic Chinese follows subject-verb-object order.
- B. Verb - subject - object  
  _Rationale:_ This is not the basic Chinese order.
- C. Object - subject - verb  
  _Rationale:_ This is not the standard order for simple sentences.
- D. There is no word order in Chinese  
  _Rationale:_ Chinese relies heavily on word order for meaning.

**MST-1848-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why do tone marks matter when learning pinyin at HSK 1?

- A. Different tones can give the same syllable completely different meanings **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: tone distinguishes meaning for otherwise identical syllables.
- B. Tones are purely decorative and carry no meaning  
  _Rationale:_ Tones are meaningful, not decorative.
- C. Tone marks replace the need to learn vocabulary  
  _Rationale:_ Tones do not replace vocabulary knowledge.
- D. Tones only matter in writing, never in speech  
  _Rationale:_ Tones are especially important in speech.

**MST-1848-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Select TWO sentences that are grammatically correct at HSK 1 level.

- A. A number is followed by a measure word before the noun **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: Chinese requires a measure word between a number and a noun.
- B. A simple statement places the subject before the verb **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: subject precedes the verb in a basic statement.
- C. A number is placed directly before a noun with no measure word  
  _Rationale:_ This omits the required measure word.
- D. The object always comes before the subject  
  _Rationale:_ This violates basic word order.
- E. Verbs are conjugated for person like in English  
  _Rationale:_ Chinese verbs are not conjugated for person.
