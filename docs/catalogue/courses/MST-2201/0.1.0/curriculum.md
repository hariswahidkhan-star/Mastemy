## Learning outcomes

1. Describe the properties and uses of concrete, steel, timber and masonry
2. Explain how key materials are specified, tested and controlled for quality
3. Compare common construction methods for foundations, framing and envelope
4. Relate material choice to durability, cost, buildability and sustainability
5. Identify common material defects and their causes
6. Read a material specification and check a delivery against it

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2201-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A concrete cylinder fails its 28-day strength test. Before blaming the mix design, what is the most important thing to check first?

- A. Whether the samples were made, cured and tested correctly to the standard **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: sampling, curing and testing errors are a common cause of apparent failures.
- B. The colour of the aggregate  
  _Rationale:_ Aggregate colour does not determine strength results.
- C. Whether it rained anywhere that month  
  _Rationale:_ General weather is not the first thing to verify.
- D. The brand of the mixer truck  
  _Rationale:_ Truck brand is not a controlling factor for test validity.

**MST-2201-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO properties make timber attractive as a structural material? (Select TWO.)

- A. A high strength-to-weight ratio **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: timber is strong relative to its weight.
- B. Renewability when sustainably sourced **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: responsibly sourced timber is renewable.
- C. Immunity to moisture and decay  
  _Rationale:_ Timber is susceptible to moisture and decay without protection.
- D. Non-combustibility  
  _Rationale:_ Timber is combustible; it is not non-combustible.

**MST-2201-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why is proper curing critical for in-situ concrete?

- A. It keeps moisture available so the cement can continue to hydrate and gain strength **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: curing maintains hydration and strength development.
- B. It makes the concrete a darker colour  
  _Rationale:_ Appearance is not the purpose of curing.
- C. It removes the need for any reinforcement  
  _Rationale:_ Curing does not replace reinforcement.
- D. It allows the formwork to stay on forever  
  _Rationale:_ Curing is unrelated to leaving formwork permanently.
