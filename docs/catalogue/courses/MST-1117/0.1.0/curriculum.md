## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1117-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A seller behaves as a 'trusted advisor' primarily when they:

- A. Always agree with whatever the customer says  
  _Rationale:_ Blind agreement is not advice; it adds no value.
- B. Prioritise the customer's long-term interest, even when it slows a deal **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: putting the customer's interest first is what builds trust.
- C. Hide information that might delay a sale  
  _Rationale:_ Withholding information destroys trust.
- D. Push the highest-margin product regardless of fit  
  _Rationale:_ Self-serving recommendations undermine the advisor role.

**MST-1117-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) A stakeholder power map in an enterprise account is used to:

- A. Rank stakeholders only by job title  
  _Rationale:_ Title alone does not capture influence or interest.
- B. Understand who influences the decision and how they view the solution **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a power map captures influence and stance to guide engagement.
- C. Decide the contract price  
  _Rationale:_ Pricing is separate from mapping stakeholders.
- D. Replace the need to talk to stakeholders  
  _Rationale:_ A map guides, but does not replace, real engagement.

**MST-1117-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO belong in a useful enterprise account plan? (Select TWO)

- A. Specific account objectives tied to the customer's goals **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: objectives linked to customer goals keep the plan relevant.
- B. Identified whitespace for future expansion **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: whitespace shows where value can grow in the account.
- C. A promise to avoid ever discussing risks  
  _Rationale:_ Ignoring risks weakens, not strengthens, a plan.
- D. A single contact who owns the whole relationship alone  
  _Rationale:_ Relying on one contact is a risk, not a plan strength.
