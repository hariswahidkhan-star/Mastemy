## Learning outcomes

1. Define inventories and identify the costs included in their cost
2. Apply the measurement rule of the lower of cost and net realisable value
3. Apply permitted cost formulas such as FIFO and weighted average
4. Account for the write-down of inventory and its reversal
5. Explain the treatment of inventories for a manufacturer, including overhead allocation
6. Prepare the disclosures required for inventories

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Integrative case

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2518-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) How does IAS 2 require inventories to be measured?

- A. At the lower of cost and net realisable value **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: inventories are measured at the lower of cost and NRV.
- B. At the higher of cost and net realisable value  
  _Rationale:_ It is the lower, not the higher, of the two.
- C. Always at selling price  
  _Rationale:_ Selling price is not the measurement basis.
- D. At replacement cost in all cases  
  _Rationale:_ Replacement cost is not the general measurement rule.

**MST-2518-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO cost formulas are permitted by IAS 2 for interchangeable inventory items? (Select TWO.)

- A. First-in, first-out (FIFO) **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: FIFO is permitted.
- B. Weighted average cost **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: weighted average cost is permitted.
- C. Last-in, first-out (LIFO)  
  _Rationale:_ LIFO is not permitted under IAS 2.
- D. Next-in, first-out (NIFO)  
  _Rationale:_ NIFO is not a permitted cost formula.

**MST-2518-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A retailer writes a product line down to net realisable value, then the selling price recovers above original cost. By how much may the write-down be reversed?

- A. Only up to the original cost; inventory is never carried above cost **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a reversal is limited so the carrying amount does not exceed original cost.
- B. Up to the new, higher selling price  
  _Rationale:_ Inventory cannot be carried above cost even if selling price exceeds it.
- C. The write-down can never be reversed  
  _Rationale:_ Reversals are permitted when NRV recovers.
- D. Up to double the original cost  
  _Rationale:_ There is no basis for carrying above cost.
