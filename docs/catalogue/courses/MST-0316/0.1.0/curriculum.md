## Learning outcomes

1. Explain auditing principles, types and the auditor's role
2. Apply audit planning, preparation and process understanding
3. Conduct audits, gather evidence and report findings
4. Apply audit follow-up, corrective action and quality tools

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-0316-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) An audit finding must always be supported by:

- A. Objective evidence linked to a specific requirement **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: findings rest on objective evidence against requirements.
- B. The auditor's personal opinion  
  _Rationale:_ Opinion is not sufficient for a finding.
- C. A rumor from another department  
  _Rationale:_ Hearsay is not objective evidence.
- D. The auditee's job title  
  _Rationale:_ A title is irrelevant to a finding.

**MST-0316-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) Before closing a corrective action, the auditor should:

- A. Verify the action actually resolved the root cause **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: verification confirms the cause was addressed.
- B. Close it as soon as a form is submitted  
  _Rationale:_ A submitted form does not prove resolution.
- C. Assume the auditee fixed it  
  _Rationale:_ Assumption is not verification.
- D. Skip follow-up to save time  
  _Rationale:_ Follow-up is part of the audit process.

**MST-0316-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Select TWO responsibilities that belong to the auditor.

- A. Gathering and evaluating objective evidence **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: evidence gathering is an auditor duty.
- B. Reporting findings clearly and accurately **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: accurate reporting is an auditor duty.
- C. Implementing the corrective fixes personally  
  _Rationale:_ Fixing is the auditee's responsibility, not the auditor's.
- D. Deciding employee discipline  
  _Rationale:_ Discipline is not the auditor's role.
- E. Setting the organization's prices  
  _Rationale:_ Pricing is unrelated to auditing.
