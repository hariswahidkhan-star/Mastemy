## Learning outcomes

1. Explain ecosystem structure, biodiversity and ecosystem services
2. Analyse human impacts such as pollution, land-use change and resource use
3. Describe biogeochemical cycles including carbon, nitrogen and water
4. Evaluate renewable and non-renewable energy and their trade-offs
5. Assess sustainability and the management of common resources
6. Interpret environmental data and reason about uncertainty

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Integrative case

A fishing town faces declining catches and algal blooms: students link nutrient pollution to the nitrogen cycle, assess land-use and energy choices, apply sustainability and common-resource management ideas, and interpret monitoring data with its uncertainty to propose a management plan.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2841-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Which of the following is an example of an ecosystem service?

- A. Wetlands filtering and purifying water **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: water purification is a regulating ecosystem service.
- B. A factory manufacturing plastic bottles  
  _Rationale:_ That is industrial production, not an ecosystem service.
- C. A government passing a new law  
  _Rationale:_ Legislation is not an ecosystem service.
- D. A car engine burning fuel  
  _Rationale:_ That is not a service provided by an ecosystem.

**MST-2841-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) Fertiliser running off farmland causes algal blooms and low-oxygen 'dead zones' in a lake. Which cycle is most directly disrupted? (Select ONE)

- A. The nitrogen (and nutrient) cycle **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: excess nitrogen and phosphorus drive eutrophication.
- B. The rock cycle  
  _Rationale:_ The rock cycle is not the main driver of algal blooms.
- C. The day-night cycle  
  _Rationale:_ That is unrelated to nutrient pollution.
- D. The cycle of the Moon's phases  
  _Rationale:_ Lunar phases do not cause algal blooms.

**MST-2841-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Select TWO genuine trade-offs or limits of renewable energy sources. (Select TWO)

- A. Some sources are intermittent and depend on weather or time of day **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: solar and wind vary with conditions, needing storage or backup.
- B. Building the infrastructure still has material and land-use impacts **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: manufacture and siting carry real environmental costs.
- C. They release more carbon dioxide in use than coal does  
  _Rationale:_ Renewables generally emit far less carbon in use than coal.
- D. They can never generate any usable electricity  
  _Rationale:_ Renewables do generate substantial usable electricity.
