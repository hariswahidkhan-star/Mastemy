## Learning outcomes

1. Explain Lean and Six Sigma concepts, history and the White Belt role
2. Apply the DMAIC improvement framework at an awareness level
3. Apply basic Lean waste identification and process thinking
4. Explain how to support improvement teams and projects

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1741-Q0001** (single, Select ONE) In DMAIC, the phase where the team confirms the problem and project goal is:

- A. Define **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: Define establishes the problem, goal and scope.
- B. Improve  
  _Rationale:_ Improve implements solutions, after the problem is understood.
- C. Control  
  _Rationale:_ Control sustains the gains at the end.
- D. Analyze  
  _Rationale:_ Analyze finds causes, after Define and Measure.

**MST-1741-Q0002** (single, Select ONE) A White Belt's typical contribution to an improvement project is to:

- A. Support the team and help collect data at an awareness level **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: White Belts support teams and basic activities, not lead advanced analysis.
- B. Lead the statistical analysis  
  _Rationale:_ That is a higher belt's role.
- C. Approve the project charter alone  
  _Rationale:_ Charters are approved by sponsors/champions.
- D. Replace the Black Belt  
  _Rationale:_ White Belts do not lead projects.

**MST-1741-Q0003** (multiple, Select TWO) Select TWO of the eight wastes (DOWNTIME) in Lean.

- A. Waiting **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: waiting is one of the eight wastes.
- B. Overproduction **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: overproduction is one of the eight wastes.
- C. Value-added processing  
  _Rationale:_ Value-added work is not waste.
- D. Customer satisfaction  
  _Rationale:_ Satisfaction is an outcome, not a waste.
- E. Meeting the specification  
  _Rationale:_ Meeting spec is not a waste.
