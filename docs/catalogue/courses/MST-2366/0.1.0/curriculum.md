## Learning outcomes

1. Explain how hydrogen is produced and the meaning of common colour labels
2. Describe hydrogen storage, transport and safety challenges
3. Compare hydrogen and other alternative fuels for different end uses
4. Assess energy losses along hydrogen and synthetic-fuel value chains
5. Identify realistic near-term applications versus overstated ones
6. Relate alternative fuels to decarbonisation of hard-to-electrify sectors

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2366-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) What distinguishes so-called green hydrogen from grey hydrogen?

- A. Green hydrogen is made by electrolysis using low-carbon electricity, grey from unabated fossil reforming **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ The colour labels describe the production route and its emissions.
- B. Green hydrogen is a different element from grey hydrogen  
  _Rationale:_ Both are the same element; the labels describe production.
- C. Green hydrogen is simply dyed a different colour  
  _Rationale:_ The labels are metaphors for production, not actual colour.
- D. Grey hydrogen is always cleaner than green  
  _Rationale:_ Grey hydrogen from unabated reforming is higher-emission.

**MST-2366-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO are genuine drawbacks of using hydrogen as an energy carrier? (Select TWO.)

- A. Significant energy losses when converting power to hydrogen and back **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Round-trip conversion losses are a major drawback.
- B. Low density requiring compression or liquefaction for storage and transport **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Hydrogen's low density makes storage and transport demanding.
- C. It cannot be produced from water at all  
  _Rationale:_ Electrolysis produces hydrogen from water.
- D. It is heavier than air and pools at ground level  
  _Rationale:_ Hydrogen is lighter than air and disperses upward.

**MST-2366-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) For which application is hydrogen generally a weaker choice than direct electrification?

- A. Powering a typical passenger car commute **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Battery-electric cars avoid hydrogen's round-trip losses for everyday driving.
- B. Feedstock for low-carbon ammonia production  
  _Rationale:_ Hydrogen is a strong fit as chemical feedstock.
- C. Some high-temperature industrial heat  
  _Rationale:_ Hard-to-electrify heat is a candidate for hydrogen.
- D. Long-term chemical energy storage in industry  
  _Rationale:_ That is a plausible hydrogen use case.
