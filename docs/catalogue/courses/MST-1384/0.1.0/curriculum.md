## Learning outcomes

1. Frame AI as a business capability, not just a technology
2. Identify where AI creates durable competitive advantage
3. Set priorities, governance and risk appetite for AI
4. Allocate investment and talent for responsible AI adoption
5. Lead organisational change and manage AI-related risk

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1384-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) An executive is choosing between AI initiatives. Which best signals a durable advantage rather than hype?

- A. It leverages proprietary data or a hard-to-copy workflow to create lasting value **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: defensible value usually rests on assets competitors cannot easily copy.
- B. It uses the newest model announced this month  
  _Rationale:_ Newness is not a moat.
- C. It lets the firm say 'we use AI' in press releases  
  _Rationale:_ Marketing language is not strategic advantage.
- D. It copies exactly what a competitor just launched  
  _Rationale:_ Copying rarely creates durable advantage.

**MST-1384-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO moves show responsible executive leadership of AI? (Select TWO.)

- A. Define risk appetite and governance before scaling AI **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: governance set early prevents avoidable harm.
- B. Assign clear accountability for AI outcomes **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: accountability ensures oversight and response.
- C. Deploy widely first and consider governance later  
  _Rationale:_ Deferring governance invites unmanaged risk.
- D. Treat all AI risk as the vendor's problem  
  _Rationale:_ The organisation retains accountability for its use of AI.

**MST-1384-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why do many AI initiatives cost more than the initial model or licence suggests?

- A. Data, integration, talent and ongoing operation add substantial cost **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: total cost of ownership extends well beyond the model itself.
- B. Models and licences are the only real cost  
  _Rationale:_ Operating and data costs are often larger.
- C. AI has no ongoing operating cost  
  _Rationale:_ Running AI in production has recurring costs.
- D. Talent is never needed for AI  
  _Rationale:_ Skilled people are usually essential.
