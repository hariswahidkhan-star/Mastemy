## Learning outcomes

1. Communication foundations
2. Writing that gets read
3. Speaking and presenting
4. Listening and interpersonal skills
5. Difficult and cross-context communication

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Selection items cannot demonstrate live delivery; speaking and writing skills are practised through instructor-facilitated exercises and review.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1828-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) For a busy executive deciding quickly, how should a recommendation report usually be structured?

- A. Lead with the recommendation, then provide supporting detail **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: putting the conclusion first serves a reader who decides quickly.
- B. Save the recommendation for the final paragraph  
  _Rationale:_ Burying the recommendation forces the reader to hunt for the point.
- C. Omit the recommendation and present only data  
  _Rationale:_ A recommendation report should state a clear recommendation.
- D. Use the longest possible sentences to sound thorough  
  _Rationale:_ Dense prose slows the reader; clarity and brevity serve the decision.

**MST-1828-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select ALL that apply) Which behaviours demonstrate active listening? (Select TWO)

- A. Paraphrasing what you heard to confirm understanding **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: paraphrasing checks and signals understanding.
- B. Asking a clarifying question before responding **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: clarifying questions deepen understanding.
- C. Planning your rebuttal while the other person talks  
  _Rationale:_ Rehearsing a reply means you are not truly listening.
- D. Interrupting to finish their sentences  
  _Rationale:_ Interrupting disrupts the speaker and signals impatience.

**MST-1828-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why is email often a poor channel for delivering sensitive, emotionally charged news?

- A. It strips tone and nonverbal cues, so the message is easily misread **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: without tone and body language, sensitive messages are easily misinterpreted.
- B. Email cannot contain long text  
  _Rationale:_ Email can hold long text; the issue is missing tone and interaction.
- C. Email is always slower than a letter  
  _Rationale:_ Speed is not the relevant drawback for sensitive news.
- D. Sensitive topics are never appropriate to discuss  
  _Rationale:_ They can be discussed; the point is choosing a richer channel.
