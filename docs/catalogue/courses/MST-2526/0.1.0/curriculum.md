## Learning outcomes

1. Explain the objective of the first-time adoption standard and the opening IFRS statement of financial position
2. Identify the date of transition and the first IFRS reporting period
3. Apply the general principle of retrospective application of IFRS
4. Apply the mandatory exceptions to retrospective application
5. Apply the optional exemptions available on first-time adoption
6. Prepare the reconciliations required in the first IFRS financial statements

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Integrative case

A company reporting under local GAAP is adopting IFRS for the first time and must set its transition date, build an opening IFRS statement of financial position, decide which optional exemptions (such as deemed cost) to use, apply the mandatory exceptions, and prepare the equity and profit reconciliations required in its first IFRS financial statements.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2526-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) What must an entity prepare at its date of transition to IFRS?

- A. An opening IFRS statement of financial position **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: an opening IFRS statement of financial position is prepared at the transition date.
- B. A final local-GAAP statement with no changes  
  _Rationale:_ The opening balance sheet is restated to IFRS.
- C. Only a cash-flow statement  
  _Rationale:_ A full opening IFRS statement of financial position is required, not just cash flows.
- D. A tax return for the transition year  
  _Rationale:_ A tax return is not the IFRS 1 requirement.

**MST-2526-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO features characterise the IFRS 1 approach to first-time adoption? (Select TWO.)

- A. IFRS are generally applied retrospectively as if they had always been used **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the general principle is retrospective application.
- B. Certain mandatory exceptions and optional exemptions modify full retrospective application **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: IFRS 1 provides mandatory exceptions and optional exemptions.
- C. Comparative figures may be left under previous GAAP  
  _Rationale:_ Comparatives are restated to IFRS, not left under previous GAAP.
- D. No reconciliations are required from previous GAAP  
  _Rationale:_ Reconciliations of equity and profit are required.

**MST-2526-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A first-time adopter applies the deemed-cost exemption, treating it as compulsory for all its assets. Why is this a misunderstanding?

- A. Deemed cost is an optional exemption the entity may choose, not a mandatory requirement **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: deemed cost is an optional exemption, not compulsory.
- B. Deemed cost must be applied to every asset by law  
  _Rationale:_ It is optional, not compulsory.
- C. Deemed cost is a mandatory exception that cannot be avoided  
  _Rationale:_ It is an optional exemption, not a mandatory exception.
- D. Deemed cost only applies to liabilities  
  _Rationale:_ It applies to certain assets, and it is optional.
