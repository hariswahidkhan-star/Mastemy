## Learning outcomes

1. Sustainable supply chain foundations
2. Measuring footprint
3. Sustainable sourcing
4. Circular economy and logistics
5. Reporting and strategy

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Selection items cannot demonstrate running a full lifecycle assessment or supplier audit; practice comes through instructor-led exercises.

## Integrative case

A manufacturer faces customer pressure to cut its supply-chain emissions. The learner must map impacts across the triple bottom line, account for Scope 1-3 emissions and find hotspots, set supplier standards, apply circular-economy and green-logistics ideas, and set credible targets, then recommend a sustainability roadmap that avoids greenwashing.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1808-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Scope 3 emissions are:

- A. Indirect emissions across the value chain, such as purchased goods and transport **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: Scope 3 covers upstream and downstream value-chain emissions.
- B. Direct emissions from owned sources  
  _Rationale:_ Those are Scope 1 emissions.
- C. Emissions from purchased electricity  
  _Rationale:_ Those are Scope 2 emissions.
- D. Emissions that do not need to be reported  
  _Rationale:_ Scope 3 is increasingly expected in disclosure.

**MST-1808-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO make a sustainability claim credible rather than greenwashing? (Select TWO.)

- A. A specific, measurable target with a baseline and date **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: measurable, time-bound targets can be verified.
- B. Evidence and third-party verification of progress **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: verified evidence distinguishes real progress from spin.
- C. Vague wording like 'eco-friendly' with no data  
  _Rationale:_ Unsubstantiated adjectives are classic greenwashing.
- D. Highlighting one green feature while hiding larger impacts  
  _Rationale:_ Selective disclosure is a form of greenwashing.

**MST-1808-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A circular economy aims primarily to:

- A. Keep materials and products in use and design out waste **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: circularity retains value and eliminates waste by design.
- B. Maximise single-use products for convenience  
  _Rationale:_ Single-use runs counter to circularity.
- C. Recycle only, with no change to design  
  _Rationale:_ Recycling alone is not a full circular economy.
- D. Move production to the cheapest location  
  _Rationale:_ Cost relocation is unrelated to circularity.
