## Learning outcomes

1. Explain how copyright applies to AI training data and generated outputs
2. Distinguish authorship, ownership and licensing questions in generative AI
3. Identify infringement and plagiarism risks in AI-assisted work
4. Apply fair-use and licensing reasoning to common AI use cases
5. Recommend governance practices that reduce IP risk in an organisation

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1373-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A designer prompts an image tool and ships the result. In many jurisdictions, what is the most accurate statement about copyright in a purely AI-generated image?

- A. Protection may be limited or unavailable where no human authorship is present **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: several jurisdictions require human authorship for copyright to subsist.
- B. The image is automatically fully copyrighted to the prompter in every jurisdiction  
  _Rationale:_ Overstated: rules on AI authorship vary and often require human creative contribution.
- C. The tool vendor always owns the copyright regardless of the terms  
  _Rationale:_ Ownership depends on the tool's terms, not an automatic vendor rule.
- D. AI images are always in the public domain worldwide  
  _Rationale:_ There is no universal rule placing all AI output in the public domain.

**MST-1373-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO practices most reduce copyright risk when a team uses generative AI for client work? (Select TWO.)

- A. Keep records of prompts, sources and tool licences used **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: provenance records support licensing and defend against claims.
- B. Review outputs for substantial similarity to known works **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: similarity review catches likely infringement before release.
- C. Assume all generated output is original and skip review  
  _Rationale:_ Skipping review leaves infringement undetected.
- D. Remove all human involvement to avoid liability  
  _Rationale:_ Removing human review increases, not decreases, risk.

**MST-1373-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why can the data used to train a generative model raise copyright questions?

- A. Training data may include works still protected by third-party copyright **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: protected works in training data are the core of many disputes.
- B. Training always destroys the original works  
  _Rationale:_ Training does not destroy sources; copies may persist.
- C. Only data older than 100 years is ever used  
  _Rationale:_ Models commonly train on recent, in-copyright material.
- D. Copyright never applies to anything on the internet  
  _Rationale:_ Material online is frequently still in copyright.
