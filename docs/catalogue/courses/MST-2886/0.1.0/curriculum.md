## Learning outcomes

1. Construct a clear, arguable thesis for an academic essay
2. Build logical arguments supported by evidence
3. Address counterarguments fairly and effectively
4. Use persuasive and rhetorical techniques appropriately
5. Cite sources and avoid plagiarism
6. Revise writing for coherence, cohesion and style

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2886-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) What makes a thesis statement 'arguable'?

- A. Reasonable people could disagree with it **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: an arguable thesis takes a debatable position.
- B. It states an obvious fact everyone accepts  
  _Rationale:_ An undebatable fact is not arguable.
- C. It is the longest sentence in the essay  
  _Rationale:_ Length does not make a thesis arguable.
- D. It avoids taking any position  
  _Rationale:_ Taking no position is the opposite of arguable.

**MST-2886-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why should a persuasive essay address counterarguments?

- A. It strengthens your case by answering likely objections **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: rebutting objections makes an argument more convincing.
- B. It lets you avoid giving any evidence  
  _Rationale:_ You still need evidence for your own claims.
- C. It is only there to fill up space  
  _Rationale:_ Counterarguments serve a real persuasive purpose.
- D. It proves the other side is always right  
  _Rationale:_ You address objections in order to answer them, not concede.

**MST-2886-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO practices help you use sources responsibly? (Select TWO.)

- A. Citing where each idea or quotation came from **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: citation credits the source and avoids plagiarism.
- B. Putting another writer's exact words in quotation marks **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: quotation marks signal borrowed wording.
- C. Copying a passage and changing one word  
  _Rationale:_ Near-copying without credit is still plagiarism.
- D. Presenting a source's idea as entirely your own  
  _Rationale:_ Claiming others' ideas as your own is plagiarism.
