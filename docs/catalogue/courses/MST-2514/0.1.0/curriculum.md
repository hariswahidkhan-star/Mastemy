## Learning outcomes

1. Apply the control model of power, exposure to variable returns, and the link between them
2. Identify the parent and the boundary of the group
3. Explain the consolidation procedures at a conceptual level
4. Account for non-controlling interests within consolidated equity
5. Apply the investment entity exception
6. Explain loss of control and the required accounting

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Integrative case

A diversified group controls one entity through a majority stake, another through contractual rights despite holding under half the votes, and holds investments through a fund that may be an investment entity; determine the group boundary under the control model, outline the consolidation procedures and non-controlling-interest presentation, and explain the accounting when the group later loses control of one subsidiary.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2514-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Under IFRS 10's control model, an investor controls an investee only when it has all of which elements?

- A. Power over the investee, exposure to variable returns, and the ability to use its power to affect those returns **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: all three elements of the control model must be present.
- B. More than 50% of the voting shares in every case  
  _Rationale:_ A majority of votes is neither always necessary nor sufficient for control.
- C. A seat on the board and nothing more  
  _Rationale:_ Board representation alone does not establish control.
- D. A legal contract to purchase the investee within a year  
  _Rationale:_ A future purchase contract is not the control model.

**MST-2514-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO procedures are part of preparing consolidated financial statements? (Select TWO.)

- A. Eliminating intra-group balances, transactions and unrealised profits in full **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: full elimination of intra-group items is a core procedure.
- B. Presenting non-controlling interests within equity, separately from the owners of the parent **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: NCI is presented within consolidated equity, shown separately.
- C. Replacing the subsidiary's revenue with the dividends the parent received  
  _Rationale:_ Consolidation combines line items, it does not substitute dividends for revenue.
- D. Measuring the subsidiary using the equity method  
  _Rationale:_ Subsidiaries are consolidated line by line, not equity-accounted.

**MST-2514-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A parent holds 45% of an entity's voting rights, while the remaining 55% is held by thousands of dispersed shareholders who rarely vote together. Why might the parent still control the entity?

- A. Its holding may be large enough relative to the dispersed holdings to give de facto control over the relevant activities **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: de facto control can exist where other holdings are widely dispersed.
- B. Control is automatic for any holding above 40%  
  _Rationale:_ There is no automatic 40% threshold.
- C. Control depends only on legal ownership of a majority of shares  
  _Rationale:_ Control turns on power over returns, which can exist below 50%.
- D. A 45% holding can never give control  
  _Rationale:_ De facto control can arise below 50%.
