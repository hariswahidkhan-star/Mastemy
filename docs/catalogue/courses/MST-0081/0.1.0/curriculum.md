## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-0081-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) The actuarial present value of a whole life insurance of 1 payable at the end of the year of death is denoted:

- A. A_x **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: A_x is the standard symbol for the APV of a whole life insurance.
- B. a_x  
  _Rationale:_ Lowercase a with a double-dot denotes an annuity, not an insurance.
- C. l_x  
  _Rationale:_ l_x is the number of lives surviving to age x in a life table.
- D. q_x  
  _Rationale:_ q_x is the one-year probability of death, not an APV.

**MST-0081-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) Under the equivalence principle, the benefit premium is set so that:

- A. The APV of premiums equals the APV of benefits at issue **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the equivalence principle equates the APV of premiums and benefits.
- B. Premiums always exceed benefits by a 20% margin  
  _Rationale:_ That describes a loading, not the pure equivalence principle.
- C. The reserve is zero at every duration  
  _Rationale:_ Reserves are generally positive after issue.
- D. Premiums equal the sum insured  
  _Rationale:_ Premiums are far smaller than the sum insured in general.

**MST-0081-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Select TWO statements that are true about a net premium policy reserve.

- A. It is prospectively the APV of future benefits minus the APV of future premiums **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: this is the prospective reserve definition.
- B. It is generally zero at issue under the equivalence principle **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: at issue the APVs are equal, so the reserve is zero.
- C. It must always be negative  
  _Rationale:_ Reserves are typically non-negative after issue.
- D. It ignores mortality entirely  
  _Rationale:_ Reserves depend on mortality assumptions.
- E. It equals the sum insured at every duration  
  _Rationale:_ The reserve builds gradually and does not equal the sum insured until maturity/death.
