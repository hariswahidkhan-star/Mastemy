## Learning outcomes

1. Explain natural selection and the mechanisms of evolutionary change
2. Describe speciation, phylogeny and evidence for common descent
3. Explain population growth, regulation and community interactions
4. Describe energy flow and nutrient cycling in ecosystems
5. Relate biodiversity and conservation to ecosystem function
6. Interpret ecological and evolutionary data to reason about change over time

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1977-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Which of the following is required for natural selection to drive evolutionary change in a population?

- A. Heritable variation in traits that affect survival or reproduction **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: selection needs heritable, fitness-affecting variation.
- B. Individuals changing their own genes through effort during life  
  _Rationale:_ Acquired changes are not inherited in this way.
- C. A constant, unchanging environment  
  _Rationale:_ Selection does not require a static environment.
- D. Equal reproductive success for every individual  
  _Rationale:_ Differential reproduction is the engine of selection.

**MST-1977-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO processes can change allele frequencies in a population? (Select TWO.)

- A. Genetic drift **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: random sampling changes frequencies, especially in small populations.
- B. Gene flow between populations **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: migration introduces or removes alleles.
- C. Using an organ more during an individual's life  
  _Rationale:_ Use and disuse do not alter inherited allele frequencies.
- D. Counting the population  
  _Rationale:_ Observation does not change allele frequencies.

**MST-1977-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Roughly how much of the energy at one trophic level is typically available to the next level up?

- A. About 10 percent **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: roughly 10 percent transfers; the rest is lost mainly as heat.
- B. About 90 percent  
  _Rationale:_ Most energy is lost, not transferred upward.
- C. 100 percent  
  _Rationale:_ Energy transfer between levels is far from complete.
- D. 0 percent  
  _Rationale:_ Some energy does transfer, which supports higher levels.
