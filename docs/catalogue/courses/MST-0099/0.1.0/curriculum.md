## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-0099-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Internal audit’s role in enterprise risk management is best described as:

- A. Providing independent assurance over the effectiveness of risk management **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: internal audit gives independent assurance on risk management effectiveness.
- B. Owning and accepting all enterprise risks  
  _Rationale:_ Management owns risk; audit provides assurance, not ownership.
- C. Setting the organization’s risk appetite alone  
  _Rationale:_ Risk appetite is set by the board/management, not audit alone.
- D. Replacing the risk function entirely  
  _Rationale:_ Audit assures risk management; it does not replace it.

**MST-0099-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) To preserve objectivity, internal auditors providing assurance over a process should generally avoid:

- A. Having designed or operated the control they are now auditing **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: auditing one’s own work impairs objectivity.
- B. Reading the control documentation  
  _Rationale:_ Reviewing documentation is a normal audit activity.
- C. Interviewing process owners  
  _Rationale:_ Interviews are a standard, acceptable audit technique.
- D. Reporting findings to the audit committee  
  _Rationale:_ Reporting to the audit committee supports independence.

**MST-0099-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Select TWO inputs that support risk-based audit planning.

- A. The organization’s risk assessment and risk register **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the risk register helps prioritize audit effort by risk.
- B. Input from management and the board on key concerns **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: stakeholder input informs where assurance is most needed.
- C. Auditing areas strictly in alphabetical order  
  _Rationale:_ Alphabetical order ignores risk.
- D. Choosing engagements by coin flip  
  _Rationale:_ Random selection is not risk-based.
- E. Avoiding any area that is considered high risk  
  _Rationale:_ High-risk areas should be prioritized, not avoided.
