## Learning outcomes

1. Explain the Earth-system processes behind major natural hazards
2. Distinguish hazard, vulnerability, exposure and risk
3. Analyse how earthquakes, volcanoes, floods and storms develop
4. Interpret hazard and risk maps and monitoring data
5. Evaluate preparedness, early warning and mitigation strategies
6. Assess how human choices change disaster risk

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Integrative case

A coastal city on an active margin faces earthquake, tsunami and storm-surge threats: students analyse the Earth-system causes, distinguish hazard from risk using exposure and vulnerability, interpret hazard maps and monitoring data, and evaluate early-warning and preparedness measures to cut the risk.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2843-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) What is the difference between a natural 'hazard' and 'risk'?

- A. A hazard is the threatening process; risk also depends on exposure and vulnerability of people and property **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: risk combines the hazard with who and what is exposed and how vulnerable they are.
- B. They are identical terms  
  _Rationale:_ They are distinct concepts in disaster science.
- C. A hazard only exists after people are harmed  
  _Rationale:_ A hazard can exist whether or not anyone is harmed.
- D. Risk is the process and hazard is the harm  
  _Rationale:_ This reverses the definitions.

**MST-2843-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) A powerful undersea earthquake at a subduction zone can trigger a tsunami. What is the most direct cause of the tsunami? (Select ONE)

- A. Sudden vertical displacement of the sea floor pushing the water column **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the abrupt sea-floor movement displaces the water, forming the wave.
- B. Strong winds blowing across the ocean surface  
  _Rationale:_ That drives ordinary waves, not this tsunami.
- C. The Moon pulling the tide higher that day  
  _Rationale:_ Tides are not the cause of a tsunami.
- D. Warm surface water evaporating quickly  
  _Rationale:_ Evaporation does not generate a tsunami.

**MST-2843-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Select TWO actions that genuinely reduce disaster risk for a flood-prone town. (Select TWO)

- A. Installing an early-warning system with clear evacuation routes **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: timely warning and evacuation reduce harm.
- B. Restoring wetlands and improving drainage to absorb floodwater **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: natural and engineered drainage lower flood impact.
- C. Building more homes directly on the floodplain  
  _Rationale:_ This increases exposure and therefore risk.
- D. Removing all monitoring to save money  
  _Rationale:_ Removing monitoring raises risk by cutting warning time.
