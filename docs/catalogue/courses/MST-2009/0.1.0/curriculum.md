## Learning outcomes

1. Describe the structure and function of the major biomolecules
2. Explain enzyme catalysis and kinetics
3. Outline central metabolic pathways and their regulation
4. Relate protein structure to function and folding
5. Describe information flow from gene to protein
6. Explain how AI predicts protein structure and function

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2009-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) In Michaelis-Menten kinetics, what does the Km value represent?

- A. The substrate concentration at which the reaction rate is half of Vmax **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: Km is the substrate concentration at half-maximal rate.
- B. The maximum possible reaction rate  
  _Rationale:_ That is Vmax, not Km.
- C. The total amount of enzyme present  
  _Rationale:_ Km is a concentration parameter, not enzyme quantity.
- D. The rate constant for product release only  
  _Rationale:_ Km combines several rate constants, not just product release.

**MST-2009-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO statements about protein structure are correct? (Select TWO.)

- A. The primary structure is the linear sequence of amino acids **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: primary structure is the residue sequence.
- B. Hydrogen bonding stabilises secondary-structure elements **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: alpha-helices and beta-sheets are held by hydrogen bonds.
- C. The quaternary structure applies to every single protein  
  _Rationale:_ Only multi-subunit proteins have quaternary structure.
- D. Peptide bonds are easily broken by weak intermolecular forces  
  _Rationale:_ Peptide bonds are strong covalent bonds.

**MST-2009-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why should an AI-predicted protein structure be treated as a hypothesis?

- A. The prediction may be wrong for novel folds and still needs experimental checks **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: predictions guide but do not replace experimental validation.
- B. Predicted structures are always experimentally proven  
  _Rationale:_ Prediction is not experimental proof.
- C. Proteins have no measurable structure  
  _Rationale:_ Structures are measured by crystallography, cryo-EM and NMR.
- D. Confidence scores guarantee correctness  
  _Rationale:_ High confidence reduces but does not eliminate risk of error.
