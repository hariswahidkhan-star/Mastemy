## Learning outcomes

1. Plan UX research methods and study design
2. Recruit participants and conduct research sessions
3. Run usability evaluation and expert reviews
4. Analyse findings and communicate recommendations

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Integrative case

A UX researcher must evaluate a checkout flow with poor conversion. They must define research questions, choose and justify methods, recruit representative users, run usability tests and a heuristic review, then analyse and prioritise findings into recommendations the product team can act on.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1135-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) The first step in planning UX research should be to:

- A. Define the research questions and decisions they inform **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: questions drive method choice and study design.
- B. Book the most expensive eye-tracking lab  
  _Rationale:_ Tools follow the question, not the reverse.
- C. Write the final report  
  _Rationale:_ The report comes after the study.
- D. Pick participants at random with no screening  
  _Rationale:_ Recruiting follows from the questions.

**MST-1135-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO practices keep a usability session valid? (Select TWO.)

- A. Give realistic tasks and let the participant think aloud **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: task-based observation yields usability evidence.
- B. Avoid leading questions that bias responses **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: neutral facilitation protects validity.
- C. Tell the participant the correct path when they hesitate  
  _Rationale:_ That contaminates the finding.
- D. Record only your own opinions, not participant behaviour  
  _Rationale:_ Findings must come from observed behaviour.

**MST-1135-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A severity rating on a usability finding helps teams by:

- A. Prioritising which problems to fix first **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: severity guides prioritisation of fixes.
- B. Guaranteeing the bug count reaches zero  
  _Rationale:_ Severity rates impact, not a guarantee.
- C. Recruiting the participants  
  _Rationale:_ Unrelated to recruiting.
- D. Selecting the research method  
  _Rationale:_ Method is chosen earlier.
