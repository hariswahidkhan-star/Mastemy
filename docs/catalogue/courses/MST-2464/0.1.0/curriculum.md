## Learning outcomes

1. Explain the role, duties and accountability of a board of directors
2. Distinguish governance from management and respect the boundary
3. Compose and structure an effective board and its committees
4. Run board processes that enable oversight without overreach
5. Map stakeholders and balance their legitimate but competing interests
6. Handle governance in crisis, conflict and succession situations

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2464-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A board begins directing weekly operational decisions during a rough quarter. What is the governance problem?

- A. The board is crossing the governance-management boundary, undermining oversight and management accountability **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: boards govern and oversee; running operations erodes both oversight and management's accountability.
- B. Boards should always run operations in a downturn  
  _Rationale:_ This inverts the board's role.
- C. There is no boundary between governance and management  
  _Rationale:_ The boundary is central to governance.
- D. Operational involvement strengthens independence  
  _Rationale:_ It compromises, not strengthens, oversight.

**MST-2464-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO features most strengthen a board's oversight capability? (Select TWO.)

- A. A genuinely independent audit committee **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: independent audit oversight strengthens financial oversight.
- B. A skills matrix matched to the company's strategic risks **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: relevant skills let the board oversee the real risks.
- C. Combining the chair and CEO roles in one person  
  _Rationale:_ Combining the roles can weaken independent oversight.
- D. Agendas filled entirely with management presentations  
  _Rationale:_ This crowds out oversight and challenge.
- E. Avoiding any director turnover  
  _Rationale:_ Permanent membership can erode independence and fresh challenge.

**MST-2464-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) An activist investor demands immediate board seats and a strategy reversal. What is the most defensible governance response?

- A. Engage substantively, test the arguments on their merits, and act in the interests of the company as a whole **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: boards should engage constructively and decide in the company's overall interest, not capitulate or stonewall.
- B. Reject all demands automatically to protect incumbents  
  _Rationale:_ Reflexive rejection is not sound governance.
- C. Concede every demand immediately to avoid a fight  
  _Rationale:_ Capitulation ignores the board's duty to all shareholders.
- D. Refuse any communication with the investor  
  _Rationale:_ Stonewalling tends to escalate and ignores legitimate points.
