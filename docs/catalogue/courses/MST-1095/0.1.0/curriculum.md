## Integrative case

A founder is convinced a new scheduling app will sell because colleagues said 'that sounds useful'. Design a discovery programme: frame falsifiable hypotheses, recruit the right participants, run behaviour-focused interviews and observations, synthesise the evidence, and decide whether there is genuine problem-solution fit or only polite encouragement.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1095-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A user says 'I would definitely pay for this' when shown a mock-up. Why should a researcher treat this with caution?

- A. Stated future purchase intent is a weak predictor of actual behaviour **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: people routinely overstate willingness to pay for hypothetical products.
- B. Mock-ups are never allowed in interviews  
  _Rationale:_ Mock-ups are a valid stimulus; the issue is the type of claim, not the artefact.
- C. The user must be lying  
  _Rationale:_ The user may be sincere; intent simply does not reliably predict action.
- D. Payment questions are illegal in research  
  _Rationale:_ There is no such prohibition; the concern is predictive validity.

**MST-1095-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which practices strengthen the validity of a customer discovery study? (Select TWO)

- A. Recruiting participants who match the real target segment **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a representative sample makes findings generalisable to the intended users.
- B. Asking about specific past behaviour rather than hypothetical futures **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: recalled behaviour is a better evidence base than speculation.
- C. Interviewing only enthusiastic supporters of the idea  
  _Rationale:_ This introduces confirmation bias and inflates apparent demand.
- D. Stopping after the first interview that confirms the hypothesis  
  _Rationale:_ One confirming case is anecdote, not validation.

**MST-1095-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) During synthesis a team keeps only the three quotes that support launching the product and discards contradicting notes. What error is this?

- A. Confirmation bias in analysis **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: selectively retaining supportive evidence distorts the conclusion.
- B. Correct prioritisation of signal  
  _Rationale:_ Discarding disconfirming data is not prioritisation; it hides risk.
- C. Thematic saturation  
  _Rationale:_ Saturation is reaching no new themes, not ignoring inconvenient ones.
- D. Triangulation  
  _Rationale:_ Triangulation combines sources; here evidence is being suppressed.
