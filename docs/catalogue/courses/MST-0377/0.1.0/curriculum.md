## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-0377-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) For a resistor obeying Ohm's law, if the voltage across it doubles while resistance is constant, the current through it:

- A. Doubles **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: I = V/R, so with R fixed, doubling V doubles I.
- B. Halves  
  _Rationale:_ Current rises with voltage when resistance is fixed; it does not fall.
- C. Stays the same  
  _Rationale:_ With R fixed, current is proportional to voltage, so it changes.
- D. Quadruples  
  _Rationale:_ Ohm's law is linear, so current scales by the same factor as voltage.

**MST-0377-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) A net force acts on a body of constant mass. According to Newton's second law, the body's acceleration is:

- A. Directly proportional to the net force **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a = F/m, so acceleration scales with net force at constant mass.
- B. Inversely proportional to the net force  
  _Rationale:_ Acceleration rises, not falls, with net force.
- C. Independent of the net force  
  _Rationale:_ Newton's second law ties acceleration directly to net force.
- D. Zero whenever a force is applied  
  _Rationale:_ A nonzero net force produces a nonzero acceleration.

**MST-0377-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO statements about an ideal gas are correct?

- A. At fixed temperature, pressure and volume are inversely related **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: this is Boyle's law, PV = constant at fixed T and n.
- B. At fixed pressure, volume is proportional to absolute temperature **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: this is Charles's law using absolute temperature.
- C. Ideal-gas calculations use gauge pressure  
  _Rationale:_ Ideal-gas law uses absolute pressure, not gauge.
- D. Ideal-gas calculations use Celsius temperature directly  
  _Rationale:_ They require absolute (Kelvin or Rankine) temperature.
- E. An ideal gas has strong intermolecular attractive forces  
  _Rationale:_ The ideal-gas model assumes negligible intermolecular forces.
