## Learning outcomes

1. Distinguish mixtures from pure substances
2. Explain dissolving in terms of a solute spreading through a solvent
3. Describe factors that affect how fast and how much solute dissolves
4. Choose separation methods such as filtering, evaporation and sieving
5. Explain that dissolving is a physical change that can be reversed
6. Follow safety rules: only mix safe, food-safe materials with adult supervision

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance. Any experiment ideas are optional and require adult supervision; they are not assessed.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2852-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) When salt fully dissolves in water and you cannot see it, what has happened to it?

- A. Its tiny particles have spread out evenly through the water **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the solute particles spread through the solvent; the salt is still there.
- B. The salt has been destroyed  
  _Rationale:_ Dissolving does not destroy the solute.
- C. The salt turned into water  
  _Rationale:_ The salt is still salt, just spread out in the water.
- D. The salt floated away as a gas  
  _Rationale:_ The salt stays dissolved in the liquid, it does not become a gas.

**MST-2852-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO methods could separate salt dissolved in water? (Select TWO.)

- A. Letting the water evaporate to leave the salt behind **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: evaporation removes the water and leaves the salt.
- B. Boiling off the water with an adult's help to collect the salt **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: heating evaporates the water, leaving the salt.
- C. Filtering the solution through paper  
  _Rationale:_ Filtering cannot separate a dissolved solute; it passes through the paper.
- D. Using a magnet  
  _Rationale:_ Salt is not magnetic, so a magnet will not separate it.

**MST-2852-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Which change would make sugar dissolve FASTER in a cup of water?

- A. Using warm water and stirring **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: higher temperature and stirring both speed up dissolving.
- B. Using ice-cold water and not stirring  
  _Rationale:_ Cold, still water dissolves sugar more slowly.
- C. Adding more sugar at once  
  _Rationale:_ Adding more sugar does not make a given amount dissolve faster.
- D. Covering the cup with a lid  
  _Rationale:_ A lid does not speed up dissolving.
