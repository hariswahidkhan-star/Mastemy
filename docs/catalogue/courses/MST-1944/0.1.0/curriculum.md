## Learning outcomes

1. Explain how fear and greed affect trading decisions under stress
2. Identify common cognitive biases that distort trading judgement
3. Recognise and interrupt tilt, revenge trading and overtrading
4. Build pre-market routines and checklists that support discipline
5. Journal emotions alongside trades to reveal behavioural patterns
6. Use reviews to change one habit at a time

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1944-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) After two consecutive losses a trader doubles position size to 'win it back fast'. What behaviour is this?

- A. Revenge trading driven by the urge to recover losses **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: increasing size to recoup losses emotionally is classic revenge trading.
- B. A disciplined, planned response  
  _Rationale:_ It abandons the plan in response to emotion, the opposite of discipline.
- C. Correct application of position sizing  
  _Rationale:_ Sizing up after losses increases risk rather than controlling it.
- D. A hedging strategy  
  _Rationale:_ There is no hedge; it is an emotional escalation of risk.

**MST-1944-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO habits most directly support trading discipline? (Select TWO.)

- A. Following a written pre-market checklist before trading **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: checklists reduce impulsive, emotion-driven decisions.
- B. Setting a rule to stop trading after a defined daily loss **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a hard stop-loss limit protects against tilt.
- C. Increasing size whenever a trade feels certain  
  _Rationale:_ Acting on a feeling of certainty invites overconfidence bias.
- D. Avoiding any journaling so losses are not dwelt on  
  _Rationale:_ Skipping journaling removes the feedback that builds discipline.

**MST-1944-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A trader only reads news that agrees with an open long position and ignores bearish signals. Which bias is this?

- A. Confirmation bias **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: seeking only confirming information while ignoring disconfirming evidence is confirmation bias.
- B. Recency bias  
  _Rationale:_ Recency bias is over-weighting the most recent events, not filtering by agreement.
- C. Anchoring  
  _Rationale:_ Anchoring is fixating on a reference number, not selective reading.
- D. Survivorship bias  
  _Rationale:_ Survivorship bias concerns ignoring failures in a sample, not one-sided news reading.
