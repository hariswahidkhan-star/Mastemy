## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1075-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why is capturing tasks in an external system better than keeping them in memory?

- A. It frees mental capacity and reduces the risk of forgetting commitments **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: offloading open loops reduces mental load and missed tasks.
- B. It guarantees tasks complete themselves  
  _Rationale:_ Capture organises work; it does not complete it.
- C. It removes the need to ever prioritise  
  _Rationale:_ Prioritisation is still needed after capture.
- D. It makes every task equally urgent  
  _Rationale:_ Capture does not change urgency; prioritisation does.

**MST-1075-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) In an urgent/important matrix, which TWO quadrants most deserve proactive scheduling? (Select TWO)

- A. Important and not urgent (plan and schedule) **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: important-but-not-urgent work needs deliberate scheduling or it is crowded out.
- B. Important and urgent (do now) **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: important-and-urgent work must be handled promptly.
- C. Not important and urgent (minimise or delegate)  
  _Rationale:_ These are candidates to delegate or reduce, not to proactively schedule.
- D. Not important and not urgent (eliminate)  
  _Rationale:_ These should be eliminated, not scheduled.

**MST-1075-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) What does research on multitasking suggest about switching between demanding tasks?

- A. Switching carries a cognitive cost that lowers overall effectiveness **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: task switching incurs a cost and reduces effective output on demanding work.
- B. It reliably doubles total output  
  _Rationale:_ Multitasking does not double output; it usually reduces it.
- C. It has no measurable effect  
  _Rationale:_ There is a measurable switching cost.
- D. It improves accuracy on complex tasks  
  _Rationale:_ Switching tends to reduce accuracy on complex tasks.
