## Learning outcomes

1. Describe construction project management and the built-environment delivery lifecycle
2. Apply contract administration and risk practices across construction phases
3. Evaluate stakeholder, scope and change management on construction projects
4. Analyse claims, disputes and project close-out in construction

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-0129-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) An owner wants to transfer the risk of uncertain excavation quantities on a project where ground conditions are poorly known. Which contract approach best fits?

- A. A measured/unit-price arrangement so payment follows actual quantities **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: unit-price contracting suits uncertain quantities by paying for measured work.
- B. A pure lump-sum contract with no remeasurement  
  _Rationale:_ A lump sum forces the contractor to price unknowns, inflating bids and inviting claims.
- C. Refusing any contract until all quantities are known  
  _Rationale:_ Full certainty before contracting is rarely feasible and delays the project.
- D. A handshake agreement to decide later  
  _Rationale:_ Unstructured arrangements create disputes.

**MST-0129-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) A contractor submits a delay claim. What evidence most strengthens a well-founded extension-of-time claim?

- A. A baseline programme and records showing the delaying event's impact on the critical path **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: critical-path impact evidenced against a baseline supports a defensible delay claim.
- B. A general assertion that the job 'felt slow'  
  _Rationale:_ Unsupported assertions do not substantiate a claim.
- C. The contractor's desired profit margin  
  _Rationale:_ Desired margin is irrelevant to entitlement.
- D. Weather being pleasant throughout  
  _Rationale:_ Good weather would not support a delay claim.

**MST-0129-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) A design change arrives mid-construction. Which TWO steps are essential to proper change control? (Select TWO)

- A. Documenting the change and its cost/time impact in a change order **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: changes must be documented with cost and schedule impact.
- B. Obtaining authorised approval before proceeding **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: authorised approval is required before executing the change.
- C. Starting the new work immediately on a verbal instruction  
  _Rationale:_ Acting on an undocumented verbal instruction undermines change control.
- D. Hiding the change from the owner to avoid delay  
  _Rationale:_ Concealment is improper and creates disputes.
- E. Assuming the subcontractor will absorb the cost silently  
  _Rationale:_ Unagreed cost absorption invites claims.
