## Learning outcomes

1. Describe oscillations and travelling waves with their key parameters
2. Apply superposition, interference and standing-wave conditions
3. Explain reflection, refraction and the behaviour of light at interfaces
4. Analyse image formation by mirrors and thin lenses
5. Explain diffraction and interference as evidence for the wave nature of light
6. Relate the Doppler effect and resonance to real observations

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2032-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A wave on a string has its tension quadrupled while mass per unit length is unchanged. The wave speed

- A. doubles **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: speed is proportional to the square root of tension, so quadrupling tension doubles the speed.
- B. quadruples  
  _Rationale:_ Speed scales with the square root of tension, not linearly.
- C. halves  
  _Rationale:_ Increasing tension increases, not decreases, the speed.
- D. is unchanged  
  _Rationale:_ Wave speed depends on tension.

**MST-2032-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO observations are direct evidence that light behaves as a wave? (Select TWO.)

- A. A double-slit experiment produces an interference pattern of bright and dark fringes **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: interference is a wave phenomenon.
- B. A diffraction grating spreads white light into a spectrum **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: diffraction demonstrates the wave nature of light.
- C. The photoelectric effect ejects electrons above a threshold frequency  
  _Rationale:_ The photoelectric effect is evidence for the particle (photon) nature of light.
- D. Light travels in straight lines through a uniform medium  
  _Rationale:_ Rectilinear propagation is consistent with both ray and wave models and is not distinctive evidence.

**MST-2032-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) An object is placed beyond the focal point of a converging lens. The image formed is

- A. real, inverted and on the opposite side of the lens **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: an object outside the focal length of a converging lens forms a real, inverted image.
- B. virtual, upright and larger  
  _Rationale:_ That occurs only when the object is inside the focal length.
- C. virtual and on the same side as the object  
  _Rationale:_ A real image forms on the opposite side for this geometry.
- D. at infinity  
  _Rationale:_ An image at infinity requires the object at the focal point.
