## Learning outcomes

1. Apply Newton's laws to analyse forces and motion in one and two dimensions
2. Use work, energy and the work-energy theorem to solve motion problems
3. Apply conservation of momentum and energy to collisions and systems of particles
4. Analyse rotational motion, torque and angular momentum
5. Set up and interpret simple harmonic and central-force (orbital) motion
6. Choose the most efficient conservation principle for a given mechanics problem

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2029-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A hockey puck slides at constant velocity across frictionless ice. What is the net force on it?

- A. Zero, because constant velocity means no acceleration **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: Newton's first and second laws require zero net force when acceleration is zero.
- B. A forward force equal to its weight  
  _Rationale:_ No forward force is needed to maintain constant velocity.
- C. A force in the direction of motion that decreases over time  
  _Rationale:_ Constant velocity means zero acceleration and therefore zero net force.
- D. A net upward force  
  _Rationale:_ Vertical forces balance; the net force is zero in all directions.

**MST-2029-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO quantities are always conserved for an isolated system of colliding particles, regardless of whether the collision is elastic or inelastic? (Select TWO.)

- A. Total linear momentum **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: momentum is conserved for an isolated system in every collision.
- B. Total mechanical energy  
  _Rationale:_ Mechanical energy is conserved only in elastic collisions.
- C. Total mass of the system **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: mass is conserved in classical collisions.
- D. Total kinetic energy  
  _Rationale:_ Kinetic energy is lost to heat and deformation in inelastic collisions.

**MST-2029-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A solid disk and a hoop of equal mass and radius are released from rest down the same incline. Which reaches the bottom first and why?

- A. The disk, because it has a smaller moment of inertia so more energy goes into translation **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a smaller moment of inertia leaves more energy for translational kinetic energy.
- B. The hoop, because its mass is at the rim  
  _Rationale:_ The hoop's larger moment of inertia slows its translation.
- C. They tie, because mass and radius are equal  
  _Rationale:_ Moment of inertia differs by shape even at equal mass and radius.
- D. The hoop, because it rolls without slipping  
  _Rationale:_ Both roll without slipping; the difference is the moment of inertia.
