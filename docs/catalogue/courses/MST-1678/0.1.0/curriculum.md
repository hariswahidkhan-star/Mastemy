## Learning outcomes

1. Explain social engineering and why it works
2. Recognise phishing, spear phishing and related techniques
3. Identify vishing, smishing and physical social engineering
4. Apply verification habits and resist pressure tactics
5. Report and respond to suspected social-engineering attempts

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Integrative case

An employee receives a convincing message appearing to come from a senior manager, urgently requesting a payment change, followed by a phone call that reinforces it. Recognise the manipulation tactics, verify through the right channel, resist the pressure, and report it so others are protected.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1678-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) An urgent email from a 'manager' asks you to change a supplier's bank details immediately. What is the safest action?

- A. Verify the request out of band using a known contact method before acting **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: out-of-band verification defeats business email compromise.
- B. Change the details right away to avoid delay  
  _Rationale:_ Acting on urgency without verification is the attacker's goal.
- C. Reply to the email to confirm  
  _Rationale:_ Replying may just reach the attacker who controls the thread.
- D. Ignore it entirely and tell no one  
  _Rationale:_ Ignoring without reporting leaves others exposed.

**MST-1678-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO are common psychological triggers used in social engineering? (Select TWO.)

- A. A false sense of urgency **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: urgency pushes targets to act without thinking.
- B. Apparent authority, such as posing as a manager **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: authority discourages questioning the request.
- C. Offering to slow the process down for careful review  
  _Rationale:_ Attackers avoid giving time for scrutiny.
- D. Encouraging the target to verify independently  
  _Rationale:_ Attackers discourage independent verification.

**MST-1678-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why is caller ID not sufficient proof of a caller's identity?

- A. Caller ID can be spoofed to display any number **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: displayed numbers can be forged, so they do not prove identity.
- B. Caller ID is always blank  
  _Rationale:_ Caller ID often shows a number; the issue is it can be faked.
- C. Phones cannot display numbers  
  _Rationale:_ Phones do display numbers; they simply can be spoofed.
- D. Only mobiles can be spoofed  
  _Rationale:_ Spoofing is not limited to mobile phones.
