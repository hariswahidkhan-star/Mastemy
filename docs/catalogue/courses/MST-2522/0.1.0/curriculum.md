## Learning outcomes

1. Explain the objective of impairment testing and when it is required
2. Identify indicators of impairment
3. Measure recoverable amount as the higher of fair value less costs of disposal and value in use
4. Apply value-in-use calculations at a conceptual level
5. Identify cash-generating units and allocate goodwill to them
6. Account for impairment losses, their allocation and reversals

- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2522-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) How is the recoverable amount of an asset defined under IAS 36?

- A. The higher of its fair value less costs of disposal and its value in use **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: recoverable amount is the higher of the two measures.
- B. The lower of its fair value less costs of disposal and its value in use  
  _Rationale:_ It is the higher, not the lower, of the two.
- C. Its original cost less accumulated depreciation  
  _Rationale:_ That is carrying amount, not recoverable amount.
- D. Its insured value  
  _Rationale:_ Insured value is not the measure of recoverable amount.

**MST-2522-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO statements about impairment losses under IAS 36 are correct? (Select TWO.)

- A. Goodwill is tested for impairment at least annually, irrespective of indicators **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: goodwill (and certain intangibles) must be tested at least annually.
- B. An impairment loss on goodwill is not reversed in a later period **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: goodwill impairment losses are never reversed.
- C. Impairment losses may only ever be recognised at disposal  
  _Rationale:_ Impairment is recognised when recoverable amount is below carrying amount, not only at disposal.
- D. An impairment loss on a machine can never be reversed  
  _Rationale:_ Non-goodwill impairment losses can be reversed if recoverable amount recovers.

**MST-2522-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) An entity recognised an impairment loss on goodwill two years ago. The cash-generating unit's performance has since recovered strongly. May the goodwill impairment be reversed?

- A. No; an impairment loss recognised on goodwill is never reversed **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: goodwill impairment losses are not reversed under any circumstances.
- B. Yes, in full, because the unit has recovered  
  _Rationale:_ Goodwill impairments cannot be reversed.
- C. Yes, but only up to half the original loss  
  _Rationale:_ No partial reversal of goodwill impairment is permitted.
- D. Only if the directors approve  
  _Rationale:_ Director approval does not permit a goodwill reversal.
