## Learning outcomes

1. Explain the purpose and monthly cadence of the S&OP process
2. Build and reconcile demand plans with forecasts and assumptions
3. Develop supply plans and test them against capacity and constraints
4. Balance demand and supply and quantify financial trade-offs
5. Run the executive S&OP review and measure process maturity

- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1167-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) What is the primary purpose of the S&OP process?

- A. To align demand, supply and finance into one agreed, decision-ready plan **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: S&OP reconciles functions into a single balanced plan with executive decisions.
- B. To produce the most accurate statistical forecast possible  
  _Rationale:_ Forecasting feeds S&OP but is not its purpose; alignment and decisions are.
- C. To replace the annual budget  
  _Rationale:_ S&OP informs but does not replace the budget; it is a rolling operating plan.
- D. To schedule individual production jobs on the floor  
  _Rationale:_ That is detailed scheduling, downstream of S&OP's aggregate planning.

**MST-1167-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) A demand plan exceeds rough-cut capacity in month 3. Which TWO options could legitimately close the gap? (Select TWO.)

- A. Build inventory in earlier months while capacity is available **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: pre-building uses spare early capacity to meet the later peak.
- B. Add overtime or a temporary shift in month 3 **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: temporarily increasing capacity is a valid way to meet the peak.
- C. Ignore the constraint and keep the plan unchanged  
  _Rationale:_ An infeasible plan is not a plan; the constraint must be addressed.
- D. Lower the forecast to match capacity without reason  
  _Rationale:_ Forcing the forecast down to fit capacity hides the real demand signal.

**MST-1167-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A monthly S&OP meeting ends with a good discussion but no recorded decisions or owners. What is the main problem?

- A. Without owned, documented decisions the process does not drive action **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: S&OP exists to produce accountable decisions, not just discussion.
- B. The meeting was simply too short  
  _Rationale:_ Length is not the issue; the missing output is decisions and ownership.
- C. Forecast accuracy must have been too low  
  _Rationale:_ Accuracy is separate from the failure to record decisions.
- D. Finance should not attend S&OP meetings  
  _Rationale:_ Finance is a core participant; excluding it would weaken S&OP.
