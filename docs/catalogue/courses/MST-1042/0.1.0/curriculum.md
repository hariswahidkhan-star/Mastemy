## Learning outcomes

1. Build relevant project cash flows on an incremental basis
2. Apply the time value of money and discounting
3. Compute and interpret NPV and IRR
4. Estimate and apply the cost of capital (WACC)
5. Apply payback, profitability index and ranking rules
6. Run sensitivity, scenario and risk analysis on a decision

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Integrative case

A company is choosing between two mutually exclusive plant investments with different lives and cash-flow shapes. The analyst must build incremental cash flows, discount at WACC, reconcile a conflict between NPV and IRR, run sensitivity on the key driver, and recommend a decision to the investment committee.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1042-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) When NPV and IRR give conflicting rankings for two mutually exclusive projects, the sound decision rule is to:

- A. Follow NPV, because it measures value added in currency terms **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: NPV is theoretically superior for mutually exclusive choices.
- B. Follow IRR, because a higher percentage is always better  
  _Rationale:_ IRR can mislead with scale and timing differences.
- C. Average the two rankings  
  _Rationale:_ Averaging has no theoretical basis.
- D. Pick the shorter payback  
  _Rationale:_ Payback ignores cash flows after the cut-off.

**MST-1042-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO items are relevant to a project's incremental cash flows? (Select TWO.)

- A. Additional working capital the project requires **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: incremental working capital is a relevant cash flow.
- B. Opportunity cost of using an owned building **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: forgone use is a relevant cost.
- C. Money already spent on a feasibility study  
  _Rationale:_ That is a sunk cost and irrelevant.
- D. Allocated head-office overhead that will not change  
  _Rationale:_ Unchanged allocations are not incremental.

**MST-1042-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) WACC is used in capital budgeting as the:

- A. Discount rate reflecting the blended required return of debt and equity **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: WACC is the hurdle/discount rate for average-risk projects.
- B. Rate of inflation expected over the project  
  _Rationale:_ WACC is not an inflation measure.
- C. Statutory tax rate  
  _Rationale:_ Tax enters via the after-tax cost of debt, not as WACC itself.
- D. Project's internal rate of return  
  _Rationale:_ IRR is an output, not the discount rate.
