## Learning outcomes

1. Structure a credible business case for an AI initiative
2. Estimate costs, benefits and total cost of ownership
3. Quantify and risk-adjust expected returns honestly
4. Define metrics and a measurement plan for AI value
5. Communicate a go/no-go recommendation to decision-makers

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1385-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A proposal reports ROI using only the best-case benefit. Why is this misleading?

- A. Expected value should reflect probability and risk, not just the best case **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: honest ROI risk-adjusts benefits rather than assuming the upside.
- B. Best-case figures are always the right basis  
  _Rationale:_ Best-case alone overstates expected returns.
- C. Risk never affects returns  
  _Rationale:_ Risk directly affects expected value.
- D. ROI should ignore probability  
  _Rationale:_ Ignoring probability inflates the estimate.

**MST-1385-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO cost categories are most often missed in an AI business case? (Select TWO.)

- A. Ongoing inference, hosting and monitoring costs **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: recurring operating costs are frequently underestimated.
- B. Data preparation, integration and maintenance **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: data and integration work is a major hidden cost.
- C. The one-time model licence fee  
  _Rationale:_ Licence fees are usually the most visible, not missed, cost.
- D. The cost already printed in the vendor quote  
  _Rationale:_ Quoted costs are visible, not the missed ones.

**MST-1385-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why should a business case present a sensitivity range rather than a single ROI number?

- A. It shows how the outcome depends on uncertain assumptions **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a range communicates honest uncertainty to decision-makers.
- B. A single number is always more accurate  
  _Rationale:_ A single number hides the underlying uncertainty.
- C. Ranges are only for scientists  
  _Rationale:_ Ranges are standard in sound business cases.
- D. Uncertainty should be hidden from leadership  
  _Rationale:_ Hiding uncertainty leads to poor decisions.
