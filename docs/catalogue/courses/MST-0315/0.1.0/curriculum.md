## Learning outcomes

1. Explain quality management systems and the quality engineer's role
2. Apply product and process design quality methods
3. Apply statistical methods, control and measurement systems
4. Explain quality assurance, auditing and continuous improvement

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-0315-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A quality engineer sees points varying within the control limits with no pattern. This indicates:

- A. Common-cause variation inherent to the process **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: in-control variation with no pattern is common cause.
- B. A special cause requiring immediate reaction  
  _Rationale:_ No signal means no special cause to chase.
- C. That the chart is broken  
  _Rationale:_ Normal variation does not mean a broken chart.
- D. That inspection should stop  
  _Rationale:_ In-control status does not call for stopping monitoring.

**MST-0315-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) For a recurring defect, the most effective quality action is to:

- A. Perform root-cause analysis and address the underlying cause **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: solving the root cause prevents recurrence.
- B. Inspect harder and scrap more  
  _Rationale:_ More inspection does not remove the cause.
- C. Ignore it if customers have not complained  
  _Rationale:_ Recurring defects need action regardless.
- D. Blame the operator without analysis  
  _Rationale:_ Blame without analysis misses the cause.

**MST-0315-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Select TWO categories in the cost of quality.

- A. Prevention costs **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: prevention is a cost-of-quality category.
- B. Failure costs **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: internal and external failure are cost-of-quality categories.
- C. Marketing travel costs  
  _Rationale:_ Travel is not a cost-of-quality category.
- D. Office rent  
  _Rationale:_ Rent is overhead, not cost of quality.
- E. Executive bonuses  
  _Rationale:_ Bonuses are not a cost-of-quality category.
