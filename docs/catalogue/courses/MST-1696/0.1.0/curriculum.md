## Learning outcomes

1. What bookkeeping is
2. Recording day-to-day transactions
3. Cash, bank and control
4. VAT/sales tax and payroll basics
5. Period-end and reporting

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Selection items cannot demonstrate a full set of books; recording, reconciling and reporting are taught through instructor-built worked examples.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1696-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why should a small business keep its money in a separate business bank account?

- A. So business transactions can be recorded accurately and separated from personal ones **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: separation keeps the books accurate and simplifies reconciliation and reporting.
- B. Because personal accounts charge higher interest  
  _Rationale:_ Interest rates are not the reason; clean recordkeeping is.
- C. So the owner never has to record transactions  
  _Rationale:_ Transactions must still be recorded regardless of the account used.
- D. Because tax is not owed on a business account  
  _Rationale:_ The account type does not change tax owed.

**MST-1696-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select ALL that apply) Which can cause a bank reconciliation difference between the cash book and the bank statement? (Select TWO)

- A. An unpresented cheque not yet cleared by the bank **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a cheque recorded but not yet cleared creates a timing difference.
- B. Bank charges not yet recorded in the cash book **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: charges on the statement but not in the books cause a difference.
- C. The chart of accounts being alphabetised  
  _Rationale:_ Ordering the chart of accounts does not affect the reconciliation.
- D. Using a different font on the invoice  
  _Rationale:_ Formatting has no effect on recorded balances.

**MST-1696-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A cafe collects sales tax from customers on behalf of the tax authority. How should this collected tax be treated in the books?

- A. As a liability owed to the tax authority, not as income **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: collected sales tax is money owed onward, recorded as a liability.
- B. As additional sales revenue  
  _Rationale:_ It is not the business's income; it is collected on behalf of the authority.
- C. As an expense of the business  
  _Rationale:_ Collected tax is not an expense; it is a liability to remit.
- D. It does not need to be recorded  
  _Rationale:_ It must be recorded so the amount owed to the authority is tracked.
