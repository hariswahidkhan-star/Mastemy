## Learning outcomes

1. Explain the purpose of budgets and forecasts and how they differ
2. Build a simple operating budget from drivers and assumptions
3. Choose an appropriate budgeting approach for a given situation
4. Produce and update a rolling forecast as actuals arrive
5. Analyse variances and distinguish volume, price and efficiency effects
6. Communicate budget assumptions and risks transparently

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2486-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) What best distinguishes a forecast from a budget?

- A. A forecast is an updated expectation of what will happen; a budget is an agreed plan and target **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: forecasts update with reality, budgets are the agreed plan.
- B. A forecast is always lower than a budget  
  _Rationale:_ Direction is not what defines the difference.
- C. A budget covers one month and a forecast covers one week  
  _Rationale:_ Period length does not define them.
- D. They are interchangeable terms  
  _Rationale:_ They serve different purposes.

**MST-2486-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO are features of zero-based budgeting? (Select TWO.)

- A. Every cost must be justified from a zero base each cycle **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: nothing is assumed from prior spend.
- B. It can surface and challenge legacy spending **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a key benefit is exposing embedded costs.
- C. It simply adds a fixed percentage to last year  
  _Rationale:_ That describes incremental budgeting.
- D. It removes the need for any assumptions  
  _Rationale:_ Assumptions are still required.

**MST-2486-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Actual sales matched budget in units but revenue was below budget. The most likely cause is:

- A. An adverse price or discount variance despite on-target volume **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: same volume, lower revenue points to realised price.
- B. A favourable volume variance  
  _Rationale:_ Volume was on budget, not favourable.
- C. A cash timing difference  
  _Rationale:_ Revenue, not cash, is being compared.
- D. A depreciation change  
  _Rationale:_ Depreciation does not drive sales revenue.
