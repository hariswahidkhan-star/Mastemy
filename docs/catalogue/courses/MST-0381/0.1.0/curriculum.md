## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-0381-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) For a roadway, stopping sight distance generally:

- A. Increases as design speed increases **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: higher speed means longer braking and reaction distance.
- B. Decreases as design speed increases  
  _Rationale:_ Faster vehicles need more, not less, distance to stop.
- C. Is independent of design speed  
  _Rationale:_ Stopping distance depends strongly on speed.
- D. Depends only on pavement color  
  _Rationale:_ Pavement color is not a governing factor in stopping distance.

**MST-0381-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) Superelevation on a horizontal curve is primarily provided to:

- A. Help counteract the lateral (centripetal) demand of vehicles on the curve **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: banking offsets part of the lateral force needed to turn.
- B. Increase the posted speed limit automatically  
  _Rationale:_ Superelevation is a design feature, not a legal speed change.
- C. Drain water away from adjacent property only  
  _Rationale:_ Its primary purpose is lateral balance, not just drainage.
- D. Eliminate the need for any side friction  
  _Rationale:_ It reduces but does not eliminate reliance on side friction.

**MST-0381-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO inputs most directly influence required pavement structural thickness?

- A. The anticipated traffic loading (e.g., equivalent axle loads) **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: heavier/more traffic requires greater structural capacity.
- B. The strength/stiffness of the subgrade soil **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: weaker subgrade requires a thicker structural section.
- C. The color of the lane markings  
  _Rationale:_ Lane-marking color does not affect structural thickness.
- D. The names of the adjacent streets  
  _Rationale:_ Street names have no structural relevance.
- E. The posted speed limit alone  
  _Rationale:_ Speed limit by itself does not set structural thickness.
