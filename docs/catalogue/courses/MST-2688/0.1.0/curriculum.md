## Learning outcomes

1. Explain in plain terms what artificial intelligence is and how it is being applied in customer service
2. Describe the main categories of AI use case relevant to customer service and the tasks each suits
3. Judge which customer service tasks are and are not appropriate for current AI tools
4. Identify data, bias, privacy and reliability risks specific to AI in customer service
5. Explain why human oversight and validation remain essential for customer service AI outputs
6. Distinguish realistic near-term customer service capabilities from speculative or overstated claims

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2688-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A vendor tells a customer service organisation that its AI will 'replace agents entirely'. Why is that claim misleading for current AI?

- A. Current AI automates specific tasks but cannot take on the full judgement, accountability and context of agents **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: today's systems are narrow and task-specific; they support rather than wholly replace skilled people.
- B. Because AI is always less accurate than people on every task  
  _Rationale:_ Overstated: AI can match or beat people on some narrow tasks; the real limit is scope, judgement and accountability.
- C. Because regulations forbid any use of AI in the field  
  _Rationale:_ Inaccurate: most uses are permitted with appropriate controls; the claim's problem is overreach, not legality.
- D. Because AI only works in laboratories, never in real settings  
  _Rationale:_ Inaccurate: AI is deployed in real settings; the issue is the breadth of the 'replace entirely' claim.

**MST-2688-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO are realistic near-term uses of AI in customer service? (Select TWO.)

- A. Drafting suggested replies for a support agent to review and send **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: drafting suggested replies for a support agent to review and send is an established, task-specific application today.
- B. Routing and summarising incoming requests to the right team **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: routing and summarising incoming requests to the right team is a realistic current application with human oversight.
- C. Resolving every complex complaint with no human escalation path  
  _Rationale:_ Overclaim: this presumes general autonomy that current AI does not reliably provide.
- D. Guaranteeing every customer is always satisfied automatically  
  _Rationale:_ Overclaim: this treats a speculative future capability as if it were available now.

**MST-2688-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why must a customer service team validate AI outputs before relying on them for a decision that affects a person?

- A. AI can produce confident but wrong outputs, and chat data can be incomplete and models can confidently give wrong answers, so a person must check them in context **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: models can err and reflect data flaws; accountable human review protects the people affected.
- B. Validation is only needed while the model is new; once deployed it no longer makes mistakes  
  _Rationale:_ Incorrect: performance can drift and inputs change, so ongoing checking is required.
- C. Because AI outputs are copyrighted and cannot be used without review  
  _Rationale:_ Not the issue here: the concern is reliability and harm, not copyright.
- D. Validation is unnecessary if the vendor reports high accuracy  
  _Rationale:_ Incorrect: headline accuracy does not tell you how errors fall on individual people.
