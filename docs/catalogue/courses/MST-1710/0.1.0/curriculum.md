## Learning outcomes

1. Explain core risk concepts, categories and the risk management process
2. Identify and describe risks using structured techniques
3. Assess and prioritise risks by likelihood and impact
4. Select and design appropriate risk responses and controls
5. Monitor, report and govern risk using registers and appetite statements

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1710-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) What is the difference between inherent and residual risk?

- A. Inherent is before controls; residual is after controls are applied **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: residual risk remains once controls take effect.
- B. Inherent is financial; residual is operational  
  _Rationale:_ Both terms apply to any risk category.
- C. Residual risk is always zero after treatment  
  _Rationale:_ Controls reduce, rarely eliminate, risk.
- D. Inherent risk only applies to compliance risks  
  _Rationale:_ It applies to all risks.

**MST-1710-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) A risk of a key supplier failing is rated high-likelihood, high-impact. Which TWO responses fit a 'treat' and a 'transfer' strategy? (Select TWO.)

- A. Qualify a second supplier to reduce dependency **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: dual sourcing treats the risk by lowering likelihood/impact.
- B. Take out business-interruption insurance **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: insurance transfers part of the financial consequence.
- C. Remove the risk from the register  
  _Rationale:_ Deleting the record does not change the exposure.
- D. Accept it with no action and no monitoring  
  _Rationale:_ That is tolerate without oversight, not treat or transfer.

**MST-1710-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why should a risk statement separate the cause from the risk and the effect?

- A. So responses can target the driver and the consequence precisely **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: clear structure enables targeted, measurable treatment.
- B. Because standards forbid describing effects  
  _Rationale:_ Effects are part of a good risk statement.
- C. To make the register shorter  
  _Rationale:_ Clarity, not brevity, is the aim.
- D. So every risk scores the same  
  _Rationale:_ Structure does not equalise scores.
