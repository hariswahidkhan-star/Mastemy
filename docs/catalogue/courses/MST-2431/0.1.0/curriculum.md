## Learning outcomes

1. Describe the three phases of production and the key crew roles
2. Plan a shoot with a script breakdown, shot list and storyboard
3. Apply shot sizes, camera movement and the basics of visual language
4. Light and record clean picture and sound on location
5. Edit footage into a coherent sequence using continuity and pacing
6. Finish a short video with sound, titles and correct export settings

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2431-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Recording 'room tone' on a location shoot is important because it:

- A. Provides ambient sound the editor can use to smooth and fill gaps in dialogue **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: room tone gives continuous ambience for seamless audio edits.
- B. Improves the picture's dynamic range  
  _Rationale:_ Room tone is audio, not picture.
- C. Replaces the need for a boom microphone  
  _Rationale:_ It supplements, not replaces, dialogue recording.
- D. Sets the camera's white balance  
  _Rationale:_ That is unrelated to audio.

**MST-2431-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO shots are typically needed to cut together smoothly within a continuity sequence? (Select TWO.)

- A. A wide (establishing) shot showing spatial relationships **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the wide establishes geography for the cut.
- B. Matching coverage such as a medium or close-up on the action **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: matched coverage lets the editor cut on action.
- C. A single unbroken take with no other coverage  
  _Rationale:_ No coverage limits editing options.
- D. Random unrelated B-roll with no matching action  
  _Rationale:_ Unmatched footage breaks continuity.

**MST-2431-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) The '180-degree rule' in filmmaking exists mainly to:

- A. Keep consistent screen direction so the audience is not spatially confused **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: staying on one side of the action line preserves screen direction.
- B. Ensure correct exposure  
  _Rationale:_ It is about spatial continuity, not exposure.
- C. Guarantee smooth audio  
  _Rationale:_ It concerns camera placement, not sound.
- D. Limit a scene to 180 seconds  
  _Rationale:_ It has nothing to do with duration.
