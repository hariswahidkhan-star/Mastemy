## Learning outcomes

1. Add two groups together to find how many altogether
2. Take away from a group to find how many are left
3. Use number bonds to 10 to help add and subtract
4. Count on and count back to solve simple problems
5. Write addition and subtraction number sentences with + , - and =
6. Check an answer by counting the objects

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Modules

### M01 Adding to 10 (25% (design weight), design weight)

- Worked applications: (1) Combine two groups of counters and count the total; (2) Solve 'I have 5 sweets and get 3 more'
- Common misconception addressed: Starting the count again from 1 instead of counting on from the first number
- Module check: 10 items / 10 min, threshold 75%

### M02 Taking away to 10 (25% (design weight), design weight)

- Worked applications: (1) Cross out 4 of 9 cakes and count what is left; (2) Solve 'There were 8 birds and 2 flew away'
- Common misconception addressed: Counting the crossed-out objects as part of the answer

## Integrative case

Run a pretend party where you add plates of cakes together to feed everyone and take some away as guests eat them, writing a number sentence for each change and checking it is right.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2811-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) What is 7 + 5?

- A. 12 **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: counting on 5 from 7 reaches 12.
- B. 11  
  _Rationale:_ That is only counting on 4; counting on 5 from 7 gives 12.
- C. 13  
  _Rationale:_ That is counting on 6; the answer to 7 + 5 is 12.
- D. 2  
  _Rationale:_ Adding makes the number bigger, not smaller.

**MST-2811-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Select TWO number sentences that equal 10. (Select TWO.)

- A. 6 + 4 **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: 6 and 4 is a number bond to 10.
- B. 8 + 2 **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: 8 and 2 is a number bond to 10.
- C. 5 + 3  
  _Rationale:_ 5 + 3 equals 8, not 10.
- D. 7 + 1  
  _Rationale:_ 7 + 1 equals 8, not 10.

**MST-2811-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) There were 15 apples and 6 were eaten. How many are left?

- A. 9 **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: counting back 6 from 15 leaves 9.
- B. 21  
  _Rationale:_ That is adding; 'eaten' means we take away.
- C. 11  
  _Rationale:_ That is taking away only 4; taking away 6 leaves 9.
- D. 6  
  _Rationale:_ 6 is the number eaten, not the number left.
