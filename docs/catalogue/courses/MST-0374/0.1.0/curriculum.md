## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-0374-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A mixer receives 100 kg/h of a stream and 50 kg/h of a second stream at steady state with no reaction. What is the mass flow of the single outlet?

- A. 150 kg/h **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: at steady state with no reaction, mass in equals mass out, so 100 + 50 = 150 kg/h.
- B. 50 kg/h  
  _Rationale:_ This ignores the first inlet stream.
- C. 100 kg/h  
  _Rationale:_ This ignores the second inlet stream.
- D. 5000 kg/h  
  _Rationale:_ Mass flows add; they are not multiplied.

**MST-0374-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) For an ideal gas held at constant temperature, if the absolute pressure doubles, the volume will:

- A. Halve **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: at constant temperature, pressure and volume are inversely proportional (Boyle's law).
- B. Double  
  _Rationale:_ Volume falls, not rises, when pressure increases at constant temperature.
- C. Stay the same  
  _Rationale:_ Volume changes inversely with pressure at constant temperature.
- D. Quadruple  
  _Rationale:_ The relationship is inverse and linear, not a fourfold increase.

**MST-0374-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO changes increase the frictional pressure drop for fully developed flow in a given pipe?

- A. Increasing the volumetric flow rate **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: higher velocity increases frictional pressure drop.
- B. Increasing the pipe length **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: pressure drop is proportional to length for fully developed flow.
- C. Increasing the pipe diameter  
  _Rationale:_ A larger diameter lowers velocity and pressure drop.
- D. Lowering the fluid velocity  
  _Rationale:_ Lower velocity reduces pressure drop.
- E. Removing all fittings and valves  
  _Rationale:_ Fewer fittings reduce, not increase, losses.
