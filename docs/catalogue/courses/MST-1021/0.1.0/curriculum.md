## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1021-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A business buys equipment for cash. How does the accounting equation respond?

- A. One asset (equipment) rises and another asset (cash) falls, so the equation still balances **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: swapping one asset for another leaves total assets, and the equation, unchanged.
- B. Assets rise and liabilities rise by the same amount  
  _Rationale:_ No liability is involved in a cash purchase.
- C. Equity falls with no change to assets  
  _Rationale:_ Equity is unaffected by swapping one asset for another.
- D. The equation no longer balances  
  _Rationale:_ The equation always balances under double entry.

**MST-1021-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) Under the accrual basis, when is revenue recognised?

- A. When it is earned, regardless of when cash is received **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: accrual accounting recognises revenue when earned.
- B. Only when cash is received  
  _Rationale:_ That is the cash basis, not accrual.
- C. At the start of the financial year  
  _Rationale:_ Timing follows the earning of revenue, not the calendar start.
- D. When the invoice is deleted  
  _Rationale:_ Deleting an invoice is not a recognition event.

**MST-1021-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO statements about the financial statements are correct? (Select TWO)

- A. Net profit from the income statement increases retained earnings in equity **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: profit flows into retained earnings on the balance sheet.
- B. The cash flow statement explains the change in the cash balance over the period **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: it reconciles opening and closing cash across three activity types.
- C. A profitable company always has a positive cash balance  
  _Rationale:_ Profit and cash differ; a profitable firm can still be short of cash.
- D. The balance sheet reports performance over a period  
  _Rationale:_ The balance sheet is a point-in-time snapshot, not a period measure.
