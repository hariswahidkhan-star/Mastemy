## Learning outcomes

1. State Newton's three laws of motion and give an example of each
2. Use free-body diagrams to identify the forces acting on an object
3. Apply F = ma to calculate force, mass or acceleration
4. Explain inertia and why seatbelts and headrests matter
5. Identify action-reaction pairs and distinguish them from balanced forces
6. Apply safe, controlled methods when measuring motion

- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance. Any experiment ideas are optional and require adult supervision; they are not assessed.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2854-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A 3 kg object accelerates at 2 m/s2. What resultant force acts on it?

- A. 6 N **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: F = ma = 3 kg x 2 m/s2 = 6 N.
- B. 1.5 N  
  _Rationale:_ That divides mass by acceleration; F = ma gives 6 N.
- C. 5 N  
  _Rationale:_ That adds mass and acceleration; F = ma multiplies them to give 6 N.
- D. 0 N  
  _Rationale:_ A non-zero acceleration means a non-zero resultant force.

**MST-2854-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO statements correctly describe Newton's third law? (Select TWO.)

- A. Forces always occur in pairs that are equal in size and opposite in direction **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: this is the core of the third law.
- B. The two forces in a pair act on different objects **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: action and reaction act on different bodies.
- C. The two forces act on the same object and cancel out  
  _Rationale:_ Third-law pairs act on different objects, so they do not cancel.
- D. The reaction force is always smaller than the action force  
  _Rationale:_ The paired forces are equal in size.

**MST-2854-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why does a passenger lurch forward when a car brakes suddenly?

- A. Inertia keeps the passenger moving forward until a force stops them **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: by Newton's first law the body continues in motion until acted on by a force.
- B. The car pushes the passenger forward  
  _Rationale:_ The car decelerates; the passenger continues due to inertia.
- C. Gravity increases during braking  
  _Rationale:_ Gravity is unchanged; inertia explains the motion.
- D. The seatbelt pushes them forward  
  _Rationale:_ The seatbelt provides the backward force that stops them.
