## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1068-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Your BATNA in a negotiation is:

- A. Your best alternative if no agreement is reached **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: BATNA is your best option away from the table and sets your walk-away.
- B. The first offer you plan to make  
  _Rationale:_ That is your opening anchor, not your alternative.
- C. The midpoint between both opening offers  
  _Rationale:_ That is a possible settlement point, not an alternative.
- D. The other side's reservation price  
  _Rationale:_ Their reservation price is a separate concept from your BATNA.

**MST-1068-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) Trading issues that each side values differently is a technique for:

- A. Creating value so both sides can gain **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: trading across differing priorities expands the pie for mutual gain.
- B. Claiming a fixed amount at the other's expense  
  _Rationale:_ That is distributive claiming, not value creation.
- C. Avoiding preparation  
  _Rationale:_ Trading effectively still requires preparation.
- D. Hiding your interests permanently  
  _Rationale:_ Value creation relies on some sharing of interests.

**MST-1068-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO statements about preparing to negotiate are correct? (Select TWO)

- A. Knowing your reservation value tells you when to walk away **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the reservation point defines the worst deal you would accept.
- B. Understanding the other side's interests helps you find trades **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: their interests reveal possible value-creating exchanges.
- C. A strong BATNA weakens your position at the table  
  _Rationale:_ A strong BATNA strengthens your position.
- D. Preparation matters only in large, formal negotiations  
  _Rationale:_ Preparation improves outcomes in everyday negotiations too.
