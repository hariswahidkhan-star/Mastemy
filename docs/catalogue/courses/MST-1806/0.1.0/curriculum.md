## Learning outcomes

1. Construction project context
2. Planning and scheduling
3. Cost and procurement
4. Quality, safety and risk
5. Delivery and handover

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Selection items cannot demonstrate running a live site; hands-on practice belongs on a construction project.

## Integrative case

A commercial fit-out is running late and over budget with a disputed delay claim. The learner must review the delivery method and programme, re-plan the critical path and phasing, tighten cost control and the contract position, strengthen safety and quality, and plan handover, then recommend a recovery strategy.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1806-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A lump-sum (fixed-price) construction contract primarily places the cost risk of quantity changes on:

- A. The contractor **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: in a lump-sum contract the contractor carries the risk of their estimated quantities.
- B. The client alone  
  _Rationale:_ Lump-sum shifts quantity risk largely to the contractor, not the client.
- C. The designer  
  _Rationale:_ Designers are not the cost-risk holder in a lump-sum contract.
- D. The local authority  
  _Rationale:_ The authority is not a party to the commercial risk allocation.

**MST-1806-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO are effective site safety controls? (Select TWO.)

- A. A task-specific risk assessment and method statement **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: planning the safe method controls the hazard before work starts.
- B. Physical controls such as edge protection and exclusion zones **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: engineering/physical controls reduce exposure directly.
- C. Relying on workers' experience with no documented controls  
  _Rationale:_ Undocumented reliance is not a control.
- D. Adding safety signage after an incident only  
  _Rationale:_ Reactive signage alone is not proactive control.

**MST-1806-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) For a contractor to be entitled to an extension of time for a delay, the delay must generally be:

- A. Caused by an event that is the client's risk and that affects the critical path **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: entitlement typically requires a relevant, client-risk event on the critical path.
- B. Any delay at all, regardless of cause  
  _Rationale:_ Not all delays carry entitlement to an extension.
- C. A delay the contractor themselves caused  
  _Rationale:_ Contractor-caused delay does not earn an extension of time.
- D. A delay to a task with large float  
  _Rationale:_ Delay to a non-critical task usually does not extend completion.
