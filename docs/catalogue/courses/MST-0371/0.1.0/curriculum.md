## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-0371-Q0001** (single-answer-mcq, Select ONE) A simply supported beam carries a central point load. The maximum bending moment occurs:

- A. At mid-span, under the load **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: for a central point load on a simple beam, the maximum moment is at mid-span.
- B. At the supports  
  _Rationale:_ At simple supports the bending moment is zero.
- C. Uniformly along the beam  
  _Rationale:_ The moment varies along the span; it is not uniform.
- D. At the quarter points only  
  _Rationale:_ The maximum for this case is at mid-span, not the quarter points.

**MST-0371-Q0002** (single-answer-mcq, Select ONE) Two cash flows are compared using present worth at an interest rate i. Doubling i will generally:

- A. Reduce the present worth of future cash flows **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a higher discount rate lowers the present worth of future amounts.
- B. Increase the present worth of future cash flows  
  _Rationale:_ Higher discounting decreases, not increases, present worth.
- C. Leave present worth unchanged  
  _Rationale:_ Present worth is sensitive to the interest rate.
- D. Only affect the first year  
  _Rationale:_ All future cash flows are discounted more heavily.

**MST-0371-Q0003** (multiple-answer-selection, Select TWO) Which TWO statements about applying Bernoulli's equation to pipe flow are correct?

- A. It assumes energy is conserved along a streamline **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: Bernoulli expresses conservation of energy along a streamline.
- B. Real pipe flow requires adding head-loss terms **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: friction losses must be included for real flow.
- C. It applies unchanged to highly viscous, turbulent flow with losses  
  _Rationale:_ Ideal Bernoulli ignores losses, so it does not apply unchanged.
- D. It requires the fluid to be compressible  
  _Rationale:_ Standard Bernoulli assumes incompressible flow.
- E. Velocity has no effect on pressure  
  _Rationale:_ Bernoulli explicitly links velocity and pressure.
