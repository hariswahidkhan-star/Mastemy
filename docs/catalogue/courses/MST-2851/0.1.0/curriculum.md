## Learning outcomes

1. Describe atoms as the tiny building blocks of all matter
2. Name the three subatomic particles and where they are found in an atom
3. Explain that an element is made of only one kind of atom
4. Read basic information from an element's box, including its symbol
5. Distinguish elements from compounds in simple terms
6. Appreciate that models of the atom are simplified pictures, not photographs

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance. Any experiment ideas are optional and require adult supervision; they are not assessed.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2851-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) What is an element?

- A. A substance made of only one kind of atom **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: an element contains just one type of atom.
- B. Any liquid that can be poured  
  _Rationale:_ State of matter does not define an element.
- C. A mixture of several different atoms  
  _Rationale:_ That describes a compound or mixture, not an element.
- D. Anything that glows  
  _Rationale:_ Glowing has nothing to do with being an element.

**MST-2851-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO particles are found inside the nucleus of an atom? (Select TWO.)

- A. Protons **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: protons are in the nucleus.
- B. Neutrons **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: neutrons are in the nucleus.
- C. Electrons  
  _Rationale:_ Electrons move around the nucleus, not inside it.
- D. Photons  
  _Rationale:_ Photons are particles of light, not parts of an atom's nucleus.

**MST-2851-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why do scientists use models to show atoms?

- A. Atoms are far too small to photograph, so simplified pictures help us understand them **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: models are useful simplified pictures of things too small to see directly.
- B. Because atoms do not really exist  
  _Rationale:_ Atoms do exist; models just help us picture them.
- C. Because models are exactly what atoms look like  
  _Rationale:_ Models are simplifications, not exact images.
- D. To make chemistry harder  
  _Rationale:_ Models are meant to make ideas clearer, not harder.
