## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1090-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) The clearest difference between engagement and satisfaction is that engagement reflects:

- A. How content people are with pay and perks  
  _Rationale:_ That is closer to satisfaction with conditions.
- B. People's commitment and willingness to give discretionary effort **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: engagement is about effort and commitment, not just contentment.
- C. How many people attend social events  
  _Rationale:_ Attendance is not a measure of engagement.
- D. Whether the office has good facilities  
  _Rationale:_ Facilities relate to conditions, not engagement.

**MST-1090-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) The most common reason engagement surveys fail to improve anything is that:

- A. The questions are too short  
  _Rationale:_ Question length is rarely the core failure.
- B. Results are collected but no visible action follows **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: inaction after surveys erodes trust and change.
- C. Too few people are allowed to respond  
  _Rationale:_ Low response is a data issue, not the main failure of impact.
- D. The survey is run too rarely to matter  
  _Rationale:_ Frequency helps, but acting on results matters more.

**MST-1090-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO everyday manager behaviours most support engagement? (Select TWO)

- A. Recognising good work specifically and promptly **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: timely, specific recognition reinforces effort.
- B. Creating safety for people to raise concerns **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: psychological safety lets people contribute and flag issues.
- C. Reserving all feedback for the annual review  
  _Rationale:_ Delaying feedback weakens engagement.
- D. Taking credit for the team's results  
  _Rationale:_ Claiming others' credit undermines trust and engagement.
