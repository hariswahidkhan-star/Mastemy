## Learning outcomes

1. Identify the main idea and key details of a text
2. Answer literal questions using evidence from the text
3. Make simple inferences from clues in a passage
4. Sequence the events of a story or explanation
5. Use context to work out the meaning of new words
6. Summarise a short passage in their own words

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2877-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) What is the 'main idea' of a paragraph?

- A. The most important point the paragraph is about **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the main idea is the central point.
- B. The longest sentence in the paragraph  
  _Rationale:_ Length does not make a sentence the main idea.
- C. The first word of the paragraph  
  _Rationale:_ A single word is not the main idea.
- D. Any small detail you find interesting  
  _Rationale:_ An interesting detail is not the same as the main idea.

**MST-2877-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) A story says 'Sam pulled on his coat and grabbed an umbrella.' What can you infer about the weather?

- A. It is probably cold or rainy **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a coat and umbrella are clues to cold or wet weather.
- B. It is a hot sunny day  
  _Rationale:_ People rarely need a coat and umbrella on a hot day.
- C. Sam is going swimming  
  _Rationale:_ Nothing suggests swimming.
- D. It is night-time  
  _Rationale:_ The clues point to weather, not time of day.

**MST-2877-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO strategies help you find the meaning of a new word in a passage? (Select TWO.)

- A. Read the sentences around the word for clues **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: context clues help reveal meaning.
- B. Look for a familiar part inside the word **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: known roots or parts give hints.
- C. Skip the whole paragraph  
  _Rationale:_ Skipping removes the context you need.
- D. Change the word to one you like better  
  _Rationale:_ Swapping the word changes the author's meaning.
