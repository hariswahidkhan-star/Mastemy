## Learning outcomes

1. Construct clear, reasoned arguments on a motion
2. Identify and evaluate evidence and reasoning
3. Recognise common logical fallacies
4. Rebut opposing arguments effectively
5. Structure and deliver a persuasive speech in a debate
6. Reflect critically on their own reasoning and biases

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2888-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) What is a 'logical fallacy'?

- A. A flaw in reasoning that makes an argument unsound **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a fallacy is a reasoning error.
- B. A fact that has been proven true  
  _Rationale:_ A proven fact is not a fallacy.
- C. A polite way to agree with someone  
  _Rationale:_ Agreement is not a fallacy.
- D. The title of a debate motion  
  _Rationale:_ A motion title is not a fallacy.

**MST-2888-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) Which response best rebuts an opponent's argument?

- A. Showing that their evidence does not actually support their claim **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: strong rebuttal challenges the reasoning or evidence.
- B. Saying their haircut looks silly  
  _Rationale:_ That attacks the person, not the argument.
- C. Repeating your own point more loudly  
  _Rationale:_ Volume is not rebuttal.
- D. Agreeing with everything they said  
  _Rationale:_ Agreement does not rebut the point.

**MST-2888-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO elements make up a well-built argument? (Select TWO.)

- A. A clear claim stating your position **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a claim states what you argue.
- B. Evidence that supports the claim **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: evidence backs the claim up.
- C. A loud and angry tone of voice  
  _Rationale:_ Tone is not a structural element of an argument.
- D. A personal insult aimed at the other side  
  _Rationale:_ Insults weaken rather than build an argument.
