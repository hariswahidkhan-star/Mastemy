## Learning outcomes

1. Explain the drivers and goals of finance transformation
2. Design a finance operating model and shared-service structure
3. Standardise and automate finance processes
4. Define service levels, metrics and governance for shared services
5. Manage change and risk through a transformation

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1060-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A common mistake in finance transformation is to:

- A. Automate a broken process instead of standardising and fixing it first **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: automating waste entrenches it; standardise first.
- B. Standardise processes before automating them  
  _Rationale:_ Standardising first is the recommended approach, not a mistake.
- C. Define clear service levels  
  _Rationale:_ Clear SLAs are good practice, not a mistake.
- D. Involve stakeholders in change  
  _Rationale:_ Engaging stakeholders is good practice.

**MST-1060-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO are typical goals of moving finance work into a shared-service centre? (Select TWO.)

- A. Standardised, consistent processing across the organisation **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: shared services drive standardisation and consistency.
- B. Lower unit cost through scale and efficiency **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: scale and efficiency reduce processing cost.
- C. Letting every local office invent its own process  
  _Rationale:_ Shared services reduce, not increase, local variation.
- D. Removing all performance measurement  
  _Rationale:_ Shared services rely on SLAs and metrics.

**MST-1060-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Good shared-service metrics focus primarily on:

- A. Service outcomes and quality, not just activity volume **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: outcome and quality metrics reflect real performance.
- B. Only the number of transactions processed  
  _Rationale:_ Volume alone is a vanity metric missing quality.
- C. The number of staff dismissed  
  _Rationale:_ Headcount cuts are not a service-quality metric.
- D. Avoiding any targets  
  _Rationale:_ Targets and SLAs are essential to shared services.
