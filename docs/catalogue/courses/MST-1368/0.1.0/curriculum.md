## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1368-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why is a complete AI use-case inventory foundational to governance?

- A. You cannot govern or risk-assess systems you do not know exist **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: visibility of all use cases, including shadow AI, enables control.
- B. Inventories make models more accurate  
  _Rationale:_ Inventory is about oversight, not accuracy.
- C. It replaces the need for any policy  
  _Rationale:_ Policy and inventory are complementary.
- D. It certifies the organisation  
  _Rationale:_ Inventory does not certify anything.

**MST-1368-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO practices make governance proportionate rather than bureaucratic? (Select TWO.)

- A. Tier use cases by risk and apply controls accordingly **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: higher-risk cases get more scrutiny.
- B. Place review gates at key lifecycle stages **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: gates focus oversight where decisions are made.
- C. Require identical heavy review for every trivial use  
  _Rationale:_ That is bureaucratic and unsustainable.
- D. Avoid assigning any owners  
  _Rationale:_ Accountability requires named owners.

**MST-1368-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A governance dashboard reports 'number of committee meetings held'. Why is that a weak metric?

- A. It measures activity, not whether AI risk is actually reduced **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: outcome metrics matter more than activity counts.
- B. Meetings are never useful  
  _Rationale:_ Meetings can be useful; the metric is just weak.
- C. It is too hard to count  
  _Rationale:_ It is easy to count but not meaningful.
- D. It certifies compliance  
  _Rationale:_ It certifies nothing.
