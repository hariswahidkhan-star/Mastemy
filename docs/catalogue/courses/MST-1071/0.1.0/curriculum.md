## Learning outcomes

1. Analyse audience and purpose to set a clear message
2. Structure a talk with a strong opening, body and close
3. Design slides and visuals that support, not compete
4. Deliver with effective voice, pace and body language
5. Manage nerves and recover from mistakes
6. Handle questions, feedback and difficult audiences

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Integrative case

A team lead must deliver a 10-minute decision pitch to sceptical executives. They must define the single message, structure a hook-driven talk, cut a cluttered slide deck, plan delivery and nerve management, and prepare for hostile questions, then defend the plan to a communications coach.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1071-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) The most important early step in preparing a talk is to:

- A. Define the single core message the audience should remember **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a clear takeaway drives every other choice.
- B. Choose a slide template  
  _Rationale:_ Design follows message, not the reverse.
- C. Write the full script word for word  
  _Rationale:_ Scripting precedes the message risks losing focus.
- D. Decide what to wear  
  _Rationale:_ Not the primary preparation step.

**MST-1071-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO practices improve slide effectiveness? (Select TWO.)

- A. Limit each slide to one idea with minimal text **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: one idea per slide aids comprehension.
- B. Use a visual or chart that makes the point faster than words **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the right visual supports the message.
- C. Fill the slide with the full speaker script  
  _Rationale:_ Dense text competes with the speaker.
- D. Read every slide aloud verbatim  
  _Rationale:_ That bores the audience and adds no value.

**MST-1071-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) When asked a question you cannot answer, the most credible response is to:

- A. Acknowledge you do not know and commit to follow up **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: honesty preserves credibility.
- B. Invent a plausible-sounding answer  
  _Rationale:_ Bluffing risks being caught and losing trust.
- C. Dismiss the question as irrelevant  
  _Rationale:_ That can alienate the audience.
- D. Repeat your earlier slide until time runs out  
  _Rationale:_ Evasion damages credibility.
