## Learning outcomes

1. Define AGI and related terms and explain why they are contested
2. Describe the gap between today's narrow AI and general intelligence
3. Explain the main drivers of AI progress and their limits
4. Interpret capability benchmarks critically and cautiously
5. Reason about uncertain long-range scenarios without overclaiming
6. Discuss safety, alignment and governance as genuine open questions

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2676-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why is it misleading to describe 'AGI' as a single, precisely defined milestone that will clearly be reached on a known date?

- A. AGI is a contested concept with no agreed definition or test, and expert timelines genuinely disagree **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the term is used differently by different people and its arrival is deeply uncertain.
- B. Because AGI has already been achieved and dated  
  _Rationale:_ Inaccurate: there is no agreed evidence of general intelligence today.
- C. Because AGI is impossible and will never be discussed  
  _Rationale:_ Overstated: feasibility and timing are open, not closed, questions.
- D. Because only one lab is allowed to define it  
  _Rationale:_ Inaccurate: no single body sets the definition.

**MST-2676-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO statements about AI capability benchmarks are accurate? (Select TWO.)

- A. A high benchmark score can overstate real-world ability if the test is narrow or leaked into training data **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: benchmarks measure specific tasks and can be gamed or contaminated.
- B. Benchmarks measure narrow tasks and do not prove general understanding **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: performance on a test is not the same as general intelligence.
- C. A benchmark score settles whether a system is generally intelligent  
  _Rationale:_ Incorrect: no single score demonstrates general intelligence.
- D. Benchmarks are irrelevant and tell us nothing  
  _Rationale:_ Overstated: they are useful but limited and must be read carefully.

**MST-2676-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A commentator states a specific year by which AGI 'will definitely' arrive. How should this be treated?

- A. As one uncertain opinion among many; timelines are contested and should carry explicit uncertainty **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: honest discussion of the future attaches confidence levels rather than certainty.
- B. As established fact because an expert said it  
  _Rationale:_ Incorrect: expert disagreement is wide; authority is not certainty.
- C. As impossible, since no change ever happens  
  _Rationale:_ Overstated: progress is real; its timing is what is uncertain.
- D. As irrelevant because the future cannot be discussed at all  
  _Rationale:_ Incorrect: it can be discussed, carefully and with caveats.
