## Integrative case

A fast-growing firm has a founder who is CEO and chair, a board that rubber-stamps decisions, and no risk oversight. Apply governance fundamentals: separate governance from management, assess board independence and committees, clarify accountability and disclosure, strengthen risk and control, and connect tone from the top to culture.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1838-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) What best distinguishes corporate governance from management?

- A. Governance directs and oversees the organisation; management runs it day to day **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the board governs and holds management to account.
- B. They are identical functions  
  _Rationale:_ They are distinct, with different roles and accountabilities.
- C. Governance means performing daily operations  
  _Rationale:_ That is management's role, not governance.
- D. Management sets strategy and governance executes it  
  _Rationale:_ This reverses the typical relationship.

**MST-1838-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which features generally strengthen board effectiveness? (Select TWO)

- A. A sufficient number of independent directors **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: independence supports objective oversight of management.
- B. Specialised committees such as audit and remuneration **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: committees allow focused, expert oversight.
- C. Having the CEO single-handedly control all board decisions  
  _Rationale:_ Concentrated control undermines independent oversight.
- D. Avoiding any disclosure to shareholders  
  _Rationale:_ Transparency is a core governance expectation.

**MST-1838-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A board reviews risks once at year-end and assumes it is covered. Why is this inadequate?

- A. Risk oversight must be ongoing, not a one-off annual checklist **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: risks evolve continuously and require regular oversight.
- B. Risk only needs review when something goes wrong  
  _Rationale:_ Reactive-only oversight fails to prevent issues.
- C. Boards have no role in risk  
  _Rationale:_ Risk oversight is a core board responsibility.
- D. Annual review is the maximum ever required  
  _Rationale:_ Effective oversight is continuous, not capped at annual.
