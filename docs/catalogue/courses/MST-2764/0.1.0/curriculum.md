## Learning outcomes

1. Explain synthetic biology as the engineering of biological systems
2. Describe the design-build-test-learn cycle in bioengineering
3. Explain genetic parts, circuits and the idea of standardisation conceptually
4. Describe common applications in medicine, materials and sustainability
5. Reason about biosafety, biosecurity and containment principles
6. Assess ethical, regulatory and societal considerations responsibly

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2764-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why is the design-build-test-learn (DBTL) cycle emphasised in synthetic biology?

- A. Biological systems are complex and unpredictable, so iterative cycles are needed to refine designs **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: DBTL handles biology's variability through repeated design, measurement and learning.
- B. Because biological designs always work perfectly on the first attempt  
  _Rationale:_ They rarely do; iteration via DBTL is precisely why the cycle matters.
- C. Because DBTL avoids any need for measurement  
  _Rationale:_ The 'test' stage is measurement; it is central, not avoided.
- D. Because DBTL replaces biology with pure software  
  _Rationale:_ DBTL guides engineering of real biological systems, not their replacement.

**MST-2764-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO are responsible principles when working in or reviewing synthetic biology? (Select TWO.)

- A. Applying appropriate biosafety and containment measures **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: containment and biosafety protect people and the environment.
- B. Considering biosecurity to prevent misuse **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: biosecurity guards against deliberate misuse.
- C. Publishing step-by-step protocols for creating dangerous agents  
  _Rationale:_ This is unsafe and irresponsible; it enables harm.
- D. Skipping oversight when a project 'feels' low risk  
  _Rationale:_ Oversight should not be skipped on gut feeling; governance applies regardless.

**MST-2764-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) What does treating a gene as a standardised 'part' aim to achieve conceptually?

- A. A reusable, characterised building block that can be combined into larger designs more predictably **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: standardisation and abstraction aim to make biological engineering more modular and reusable.
- B. A guarantee that the part behaves identically in every organism  
  _Rationale:_ Context-dependence means parts do not always behave identically across hosts.
- C. A way to remove all unpredictability from biology  
  _Rationale:_ Standardisation reduces but cannot remove biological unpredictability.
- D. A legal patent that prevents any study of the gene  
  _Rationale:_ Standardisation is an engineering idea, not a prohibition on study.
