## Learning outcomes

1. Explain estimate classes and their expected accuracy
2. Build a bottom-up cost estimate from quantities and rates
3. Apply allowances for overhead, risk and contingency
4. Evaluate tenders on price and non-price criteria
5. Communicate estimate assumptions and basis of estimate

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1149-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A sponsor is told a project will cost 'exactly 4.2 million' based on a concept-stage estimate. Why is this statement misleading?

- A. A concept-stage estimate carries a wide accuracy range, not an exact figure  **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: early-class estimates should be stated as a range reflecting their uncertainty.
- B. Concept estimates are always more accurate than detailed ones  
  _Rationale:_ Accuracy improves, not worsens, as design matures.
- C. Estimates should never include a number  
  _Rationale:_ Estimates give numbers; the issue is implying false precision.
- D. 4.2 million is too low for any project  
  _Rationale:_ The absolute value is not the point; the implied exactness is.

**MST-1149-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO practices help avoid awarding a tender to an artificially low bid? (Select TWO.)

- A. Normalise tenders to a common scope before comparing prices  **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: normalisation exposes gaps and qualifications hidden in a low price.
- B. Evaluate non-price criteria such as programme and quality  **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: weighted non-price criteria guard against buying on headline price alone.
- C. Always pick the lowest number regardless of exclusions  
  _Rationale:_ That is exactly the behaviour that leads to accepting an unsound bid.
- D. Ignore the basis-of-estimate and qualifications  
  _Rationale:_ Qualifications often reveal why a bid is low; ignoring them is risky.

**MST-1149-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) An estimator lists a risk of 'adverse weather' and raises the priced rates to cover it, then also adds the same weather risk into the contingency line. What is the error?

- A. The same risk is counted twice, overstating the estimate  **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a risk should be covered once, either in rates or in contingency, not both.
- B. Weather can never be an estimating risk  
  _Rationale:_ Weather is a legitimate risk; the error is double-counting it.
- C. Contingency should always be zero  
  _Rationale:_ Contingency covers identified residual risk; it is not meant to be zero.
- D. Rates should never include risk  
  _Rationale:_ Rates can carry risk; the error is counting the same risk again in contingency.
