## Learning outcomes

1. Why ethics matters
2. Fundamental principles
3. Threats and safeguards
4. Conflicts and independence
5. Resolving dilemmas

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses conceptual knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess a disciplinary determination or a binding ruling on a real ethical case.

## Integrative case

Work through a dilemma where a manager pressures an accountant to delay recording a liability: identify the principles and threats at stake, judge whether independence is compromised, apply an ethical decision framework, and choose a defensible course of action including escalation.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1727-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) An accountant feels pressured by a manager to misstate results. The primary threat to ethical behaviour here is:

- A. Intimidation **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: pressure from a superior is an intimidation threat.
- B. Self-review  
  _Rationale:_ Self-review involves reviewing one's own prior work.
- C. Familiarity  
  _Rationale:_ Familiarity arises from close relationships over time.
- D. Advocacy  
  _Rationale:_ Advocacy involves promoting a client's position.

**MST-1727-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select ALL that apply) Which two are fundamental principles of professional accounting ethics? (Select TWO) (Select TWO)

- A. Integrity **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: integrity is a fundamental principle.
- B. Objectivity **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: objectivity is a fundamental principle.
- C. Maximising personal bonus  
  _Rationale:_ Personal gain is not an ethical principle.
- D. Loyalty to management above all  
  _Rationale:_ Blind loyalty can conflict with integrity and objectivity.

**MST-1727-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Independence 'in appearance' matters because:

- A. A reasonable observer must also perceive the accountant as unbiased **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: perceived independence protects public trust.
- B. Only the accountant's private intentions count  
  _Rationale:_ Appearance to others matters, not just intentions.
- C. Appearance is irrelevant if no law is broken  
  _Rationale:_ Appearance affects trust even where nothing illegal occurs.
- D. It guarantees a higher fee  
  _Rationale:_ Independence is about trust, not fees.
