## Learning outcomes

1. Explain the supervisor's safety responsibilities and leadership role
2. Apply hazard recognition and control at the work-team level
3. Apply safety communication, training and incident response
4. Explain compliance, documentation and continuous improvement

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-0332-Q0001** (single, Select ONE) A supervisor's pre-task safety briefing is most effective when it:

- A. Covers the specific hazards and controls for today's actual task **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a task-specific briefing addresses the real hazards workers will face.
- B. Repeats a generic script unrelated to the task  
  _Rationale:_ Generic scripts miss the real hazards.
- C. Is skipped when the crew is experienced  
  _Rationale:_ Experience does not remove task-specific hazards.
- D. Only lists company awards  
  _Rationale:_ Awards are not hazard controls.

**MST-0332-Q0002** (single, Select ONE) A job hazard analysis is weakened when the supervisor:

- A. Signs it off without observing the actual task steps **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a valid JHA is based on the real task, not a desk guess.
- B. Breaks the job into steps  
  _Rationale:_ Breaking the job into steps is correct practice.
- C. Identifies a hazard per step  
  _Rationale:_ Identifying hazards per step is correct practice.
- D. Assigns a control per hazard  
  _Rationale:_ Assigning controls is correct practice.

**MST-0332-Q0003** (multiple, Select TWO) Select TWO supervisor actions that strengthen a positive safety culture.

- A. Recognising workers who report hazards **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: recognition encourages reporting.
- B. Acting quickly on reported concerns **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: timely action builds trust.
- C. Punishing workers for reporting near-misses  
  _Rationale:_ Punishment suppresses reporting.
- D. Hiding incidents from the crew  
  _Rationale:_ Concealment harms culture.
- E. Skipping all briefings  
  _Rationale:_ Skipping briefings weakens safety.
