## Learning outcomes

1. Explain how M&A fits a corporate growth and portfolio strategy
2. Screen and evaluate acquisition targets against a strategic thesis
3. Understand valuation, deal structure and the sources of synergy
4. Run disciplined due diligence to test the investment thesis
5. Plan integration to realise value and avoid common destruction traps
6. Recognise why many deals fail and how to improve the odds

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2465-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Deal sponsors justify a high price mainly by large projected revenue synergies. What is the most prudent corporate-development stance?

- A. Treat revenue synergies as the least reliable value and avoid paying for them upfront **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: revenue synergies are notoriously hard to realise and should not be prepaid into the price.
- B. Revenue synergies are the most certain source of value  
  _Rationale:_ They are generally the least certain.
- C. Synergies should never influence valuation  
  _Rationale:_ Reliable cost synergies legitimately inform value.
- D. Paying for projected synergies always improves returns  
  _Rationale:_ Overpaying for uncertain synergies destroys value.

**MST-2465-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO practices most improve the odds that an acquisition creates value? (Select TWO.)

- A. Setting and respecting a disciplined walk-away price **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: walk-away discipline prevents value-destroying overpayment.
- B. Planning integration before the deal closes **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: early integration planning is strongly linked to realised value.
- C. Winning the deal regardless of price to avoid losing to a rival  
  _Rationale:_ Winning at any price destroys value.
- D. Deferring all integration thinking until a year after close  
  _Rationale:_ Late integration loses momentum and value.
- E. Hiding diligence findings from the board  
  _Rationale:_ Concealing risks undermines sound decisions.

**MST-2465-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Post-close, an acquirer leaves the target entirely alone for 18 months 'to avoid disruption'. What is the most likely consequence?

- A. The planned synergies and strategic rationale go largely unrealised **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: value from most deals requires active, timely integration, not indefinite autonomy.
- B. Value is automatically preserved by non-interference  
  _Rationale:_ Non-interference typically forfeits synergy value.
- C. Integration is unnecessary if the target is profitable  
  _Rationale:_ Standalone profit does not deliver the deal thesis.
- D. Delaying integration always improves retention  
  _Rationale:_ Prolonged ambiguity often harms retention.
