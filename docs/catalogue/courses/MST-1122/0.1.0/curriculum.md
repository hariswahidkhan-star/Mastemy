## Learning outcomes

1. Explain what a brand is and the value it creates
2. Research the market and find a usable insight
3. Define a distinctive, defensible position
4. Set brand strategy and architecture
5. Design coherent visual and verbal identity systems

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1122-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why is a logo not the same as a brand?

- A. A brand is the overall perception and relationship; the logo is one identifier of it **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the brand is the perception; the logo only signals it.
- B. A logo is more important than the brand  
  _Rationale:_ The logo is a part, not the whole.
- C. A brand only exists once there is a logo  
  _Rationale:_ Perception exists independent of a logo.
- D. They are interchangeable terms  
  _Rationale:_ They are distinct concepts.

**MST-1122-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO are hallmarks of strong positioning? (Select TWO.)

- A. It is distinctive versus competitors **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: positioning must differentiate.
- B. It is relevant to a defined target audience **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: positioning must matter to a specific audience.
- C. It tries to appeal to everyone  
  _Rationale:_ Appealing to everyone means standing for nothing.
- D. It copies the market leader  
  _Rationale:_ Copying removes distinctiveness.

**MST-1122-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A company wants its guidelines to actually produce consistent output. The guidelines should be treated as:

- A. Rules a team applies consistently, with clear examples **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: guidelines only work when applied as rules with examples.
- B. Decoration for the brand book  
  _Rationale:_ Unapplied guidelines produce no consistency.
- C. Suggestions each person can ignore  
  _Rationale:_ Optional rules produce inconsistency.
- D. A one-time exercise never revisited  
  _Rationale:_ Guidelines need to be maintained and used.
