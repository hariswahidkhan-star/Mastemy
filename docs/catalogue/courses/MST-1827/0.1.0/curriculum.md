## Learning outcomes

1. What leadership is
2. Self-awareness and trust
3. Direction, goals and delegation
4. Communication and feedback
5. Leading change and growth

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Selection items cannot demonstrate real leadership behaviour; practice comes through instructor-facilitated role-play and reflection.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1827-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A new leader keeps making every decision themselves and the team stops offering ideas. Which problem does this best illustrate?

- A. Failing to delegate and empower, which reduces ownership **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: hoarding decisions removes the team's ownership and initiative.
- B. Too much psychological safety  
  _Rationale:_ The issue is centralised decisions, not excess safety.
- C. Setting goals that are too clear  
  _Rationale:_ Clear goals are helpful; the problem is lack of delegation.
- D. Giving too much feedback  
  _Rationale:_ The scenario is about decisions, not feedback volume.

**MST-1827-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select ALL that apply) Which behaviours help build trust with a new team? (Select TWO)

- A. Following through on commitments you make **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: reliability is a foundation of trust.
- B. Listening to understand before responding **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: genuine listening signals respect and builds trust.
- C. Claiming credit for the team's wins  
  _Rationale:_ Taking others' credit erodes trust quickly.
- D. Changing your story depending on the audience  
  _Rationale:_ Inconsistency undermines credibility and trust.

**MST-1827-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) When giving difficult feedback, why is it more effective to describe specific behaviour and its impact rather than label the person?

- A. It keeps the conversation about changeable actions and reduces defensiveness **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: focusing on behaviour and impact invites change without attacking identity.
- B. It avoids giving any feedback at all  
  _Rationale:_ It is a way of giving feedback clearly, not avoiding it.
- C. It guarantees the person will never be upset  
  _Rationale:_ It reduces defensiveness but cannot guarantee a reaction.
- D. Labels are always more motivating  
  _Rationale:_ Personal labels tend to raise defensiveness, not motivation.
