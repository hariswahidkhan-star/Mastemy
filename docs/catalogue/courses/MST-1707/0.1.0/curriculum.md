## Learning outcomes

1. What equity is
2. How shares are issued and traded
3. Valuation basics
4. Risk and diversification
5. Market behaviour

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; this is general financial education, not investment advice, and it does not assess suitability of any specific stock for an individual.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1707-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A company issues new shares in an IPO and investors later trade those shares on an exchange. The company receives cash:

- A. Only at the IPO (primary issuance) **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the issuer is paid at primary issuance, not on later trades.
- B. Every time the share changes hands  
  _Rationale:_ Secondary trades are between investors; the company is not paid.
- C. Only when the price rises  
  _Rationale:_ Price moves do not pay the company.
- D. When dividends are received  
  _Rationale:_ Dividends flow from the company to holders, not the reverse.

**MST-1707-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select ALL that apply) Which two risks can diversification across many unrelated stocks reduce or remove? (Select TWO) (Select TWO)

- A. A single company's product failure **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: firm-specific risk is diversifiable.
- B. A fraud at one issuer **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: idiosyncratic events are diversifiable.
- C. A broad market crash  
  _Rationale:_ Systematic (market) risk is not diversifiable.
- D. A rise in economy-wide interest rates  
  _Rationale:_ Economy-wide factors affect all stocks and are systematic.

**MST-1707-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Total return on a stock over a year is best described as:

- A. Price change plus dividends received, relative to the starting price **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: total return combines capital gain and income.
- B. Only the change in share price  
  _Rationale:_ That ignores dividend income.
- C. Only the dividend yield  
  _Rationale:_ That ignores capital gains or losses.
- D. The company's reported net profit  
  _Rationale:_ Company profit is not the investor's return.
