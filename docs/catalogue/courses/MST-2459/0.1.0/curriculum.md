## Learning outcomes

1. Articulate the strategic role of the chief executive distinct from operational management
2. Set and communicate a clear enterprise strategy and strategic priorities
3. Allocate capital and attention across the portfolio to create value
4. Build and lead a top team that executes the strategy
5. Read the external environment and reposition the enterprise when it shifts
6. Hold the organisation accountable for strategic outcomes without micromanaging

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Integrative case

A long-standing manufacturer's core market is shrinking while a new CEO inherits a loyal but operationally focused top team: set three enterprise priorities, reallocate capital away from the declining core, reshape the executive team, and present the strategy to a sceptical board.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2459-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A newly appointed CEO is spending most of each week in operational reviews. What is the strongest argument that this is a strategic problem?

- A. The CEO's unique contribution is direction-setting and capital allocation, which operational immersion crowds out **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: only the CEO can set enterprise direction and allocate capital, so that work must not be crowded out.
- B. Operational reviews are never useful for a CEO  
  _Rationale:_ Too absolute; some operational insight is valuable.
- C. CEOs should delegate all decisions to the top team  
  _Rationale:_ Direction and capital decisions cannot be fully delegated.
- D. The board prefers CEOs who avoid operations  
  _Rationale:_ Board preference is not the underlying reason.

**MST-2459-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO actions most directly express the CEO's capital-allocation responsibility? (Select TWO.)

- A. Shifting investment from a declining unit to a priority growth business **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: reallocating capital toward priorities is core CEO work.
- B. Deciding to divest a non-core business unit **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: divestiture reallocates capital out of a non-core business.
- C. Approving the office catering vendor  
  _Rationale:_ This is an operational purchasing decision, not capital allocation.
- D. Editing the wording of a press release  
  _Rationale:_ Communications editing is not capital allocation.
- E. Scheduling the quarterly all-hands meeting  
  _Rationale:_ Scheduling is administrative, not capital allocation.

**MST-2459-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A CEO announces a clear three-priority strategy but a year later nothing has changed. What is the most likely strategic cause rather than an execution excuse?

- A. Budgets, headcount and incentives were never realigned to the three priorities **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: strategy that is not reflected in resource allocation does not change behaviour.
- B. The priorities were communicated only once  
  _Rationale:_ Repetition helps but is secondary to resource realignment.
- C. The market moved faster than expected  
  _Rationale:_ Possible but not the most likely internal cause.
- D. Employees disliked the new logo  
  _Rationale:_ Irrelevant to strategic follow-through.
