## Learning outcomes

1. Read the income statement, balance sheet and cash-flow statement
2. Compute and interpret core profitability, liquidity and leverage ratios
3. Spot earnings-quality red flags in reported numbers
4. Interpret earnings reports and management guidance
5. Apply multiples and a simplified intrinsic model to gauge value
6. Combine valuation with timing to form a trade thesis

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1942-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A company reports rising net income but falling operating cash flow and surging receivables. What does this most likely signal?

- A. A possible earnings-quality concern worth investigating **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: profit without matching cash and ballooning receivables is a classic red flag to probe.
- B. A guaranteed accounting fraud  
  _Rationale:_ It is a warning sign to investigate, not proof of fraud.
- C. Strong, high-quality earnings  
  _Rationale:_ Cash lagging profit is the opposite of high quality.
- D. An irrelevant difference that traders ignore  
  _Rationale:_ The cash-versus-profit gap is exactly what fundamental traders examine.

**MST-1942-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO are valid uses of valuation multiples for a trader? (Select TWO.)

- A. Comparing a company's P/E against its industry peers **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: relative valuation across comparable peers is a core use.
- B. Flagging when a multiple is far from its own history **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: comparing a multiple to its historical range is a legitimate signal.
- C. Treating a low P/E as an automatic buy signal in isolation  
  _Rationale:_ A low multiple can reflect real problems; it is not an automatic buy.
- D. Using a multiple as proof a stock must rise this week  
  _Rationale:_ Valuation says nothing precise about short-term timing.

**MST-1942-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) In a simplified DCF, a trader lowers the long-term growth assumption. What happens to the estimated intrinsic value, all else equal?

- A. It decreases **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: lower expected future cash-flow growth reduces the present value.
- B. It increases  
  _Rationale:_ Lower growth reduces, not increases, discounted value.
- C. It stays exactly the same  
  _Rationale:_ Growth is a direct input to the cash-flow projection.
- D. It becomes impossible to calculate  
  _Rationale:_ The model still computes; the value is simply lower.
