## Learning outcomes

1. Identify and measure angles using a protractor
2. Use angle facts on a straight line and around a point
3. Apply properties of angles in triangles and quadrilaterals
4. Use angles formed by parallel lines
5. Classify triangles and quadrilaterals by their properties
6. Solve multi-step problems using angle reasoning

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2821-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) The angles in a triangle are 50 degrees and 60 degrees. What is the third angle?

- A. 70 degrees **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: angles in a triangle sum to 180, and 180 - 50 - 60 = 70.
- B. 80 degrees  
  _Rationale:_ That would make the sum 190, which is too much.
- C. 110 degrees  
  _Rationale:_ That is 180 minus only one angle; both must be subtracted.
- D. 60 degrees  
  _Rationale:_ That would make the sum 170, not 180.

**MST-2821-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Select TWO facts that are always true. (Select TWO.)

- A. Angles on a straight line add up to 180 degrees **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a straight line is a half turn, 180 degrees.
- B. Angles around a point add up to 360 degrees **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a full turn around a point is 360 degrees.
- C. Every triangle has a right angle  
  _Rationale:_ Only right-angled triangles do; many triangles have none.
- D. All angles in a quadrilateral are equal  
  _Rationale:_ Only some quadrilaterals, like squares, have equal angles.

**MST-2821-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Two angles sit on a straight line. One is 125 degrees. What is the other?

- A. 55 degrees **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: angles on a straight line sum to 180, and 180 - 125 = 55.
- B. 65 degrees  
  _Rationale:_ 180 - 125 is 55, not 65.
- C. 235 degrees  
  _Rationale:_ That adds the angles; they should total 180.
- D. 125 degrees  
  _Rationale:_ Equal angles would total 250, not 180.
