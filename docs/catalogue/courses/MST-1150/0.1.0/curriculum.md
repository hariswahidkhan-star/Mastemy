## Learning outcomes

1. Explain the role of the quantity surveyor across a project
2. Apply a standard method of measurement to take off quantities
3. Prepare and price a bill of quantities
4. Value work in progress and manage interim payments
5. Prepare a final account and manage variations commercially

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1150-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) During an interim valuation, a contractor claims payment for steel delivered to site but not yet installed. Under a typical standard contract, how is this usually treated?

- A. It may be paid as materials on site, subject to the contract's conditions  **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: unfixed materials properly on site are commonly payable if contract conditions are met.
- B. It can never be included until the steel is fixed in place  
  _Rationale:_ Many contracts allow payment for materials on site before fixing.
- C. It must be paid in full regardless of any conditions  
  _Rationale:_ Payment is subject to conditions such as protection and title.
- D. It is treated as a variation  
  _Rationale:_ Delivery of planned materials is not a variation.

**MST-1150-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO are core reasons for using a standard method of measurement? (Select TWO.)

- A. It makes quantities comparable between different estimators and tenderers  **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: common rules allow like-for-like comparison of bills.
- B. It sets consistent rules for what to include, exclude and deduct  **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the method defines measurement conventions so takes-off are consistent.
- C. It removes the need to read the drawings  
  _Rationale:_ Measurement still requires reading the drawings carefully.
- D. It guarantees the project cannot exceed budget  
  _Rationale:_ Consistent measurement does not by itself control out-turn cost.

**MST-1150-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A take-off measures a concrete slab at its full gross area and ignores a large opening the rules say must be deducted. What is the result?

- A. The quantity of concrete is overstated  **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: failing to deduct the opening inflates the measured volume and the priced cost.
- B. The quantity is unaffected because openings are never deducted  
  _Rationale:_ The method's rules require the deduction; ignoring it is an error.
- C. The unit rate changes automatically  
  _Rationale:_ The unit rate is unaffected; the quantity is wrong.
- D. The slab becomes a variation  
  _Rationale:_ A measurement error is not a variation.
