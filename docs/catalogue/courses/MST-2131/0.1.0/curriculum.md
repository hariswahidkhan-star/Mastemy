## Learning outcomes

1. Define pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics and distinguish the two
2. Describe absorption, distribution, metabolism and excretion (ADME) at a conceptual level
3. Explain receptor concepts: agonists, antagonists, affinity and efficacy
4. Interpret basic dose-response relationships and the therapeutic index
5. Summarise major drug classes by mechanism rather than by brand
6. Explain common sources of drug interactions and adverse effects as educational concepts

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2131-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Pharmacokinetics is best described as what the:

- A. Body does to the drug **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: pharmacokinetics covers absorption, distribution, metabolism and excretion.
- B. Drug does to the body  
  _Rationale:_ That describes pharmacodynamics, not pharmacokinetics.
- C. Prescriber does to the chart  
  _Rationale:_ This is unrelated to the definition.
- D. Pharmacy does to the stock  
  _Rationale:_ This is unrelated to the definition.

**MST-2131-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO factors describe how a receptor antagonist behaves? (Select TWO.)

- A. It binds the receptor **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: an antagonist has affinity and binds the receptor.
- B. It produces no activation on its own **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a pure antagonist has affinity but no efficacy.
- C. It always increases the receptor's natural response  
  _Rationale:_ That describes an agonist, not an antagonist.
- D. It has high efficacy by definition  
  _Rationale:_ A pure antagonist has zero efficacy.
- E. It permanently destroys the receptor  
  _Rationale:_ Antagonists block but do not inherently destroy receptors.

**MST-2131-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A drug that reduces the oral availability of a medicine because the liver metabolises much of it before it reaches circulation illustrates:

- A. The first-pass effect **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: first-pass hepatic metabolism lowers the fraction reaching systemic circulation.
- B. Zero-order excretion  
  _Rationale:_ This describes an elimination kinetics concept, not first-pass loss.
- C. Receptor desensitisation  
  _Rationale:_ That is a pharmacodynamic adaptation, not a metabolism concept.
- D. Protein-binding displacement  
  _Rationale:_ That is a distribution interaction, not first-pass metabolism.
