## Learning outcomes

1. Describe the business continuity management lifecycle and good practice
2. Apply analysis techniques to understand the organisation and its priorities
3. Evaluate design and implementation of continuity solutions
4. Analyse validation, embedding and ongoing management of continuity

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Knowledge-practice forms use MCQ/MR only; official item formats not reproducible in this format are listed in the exam-version record.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-0120-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A firm determines it can tolerate at most 24 hours before a service's disruption becomes unacceptable. In BCM terms this 24-hour limit is the:

- A. Maximum tolerable period of disruption (MTPD) **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the MTPD is the outer limit before impact is unacceptable.
- B. Office lease term  
  _Rationale:_ The lease term is unrelated to disruption tolerance.
- C. Annual leave allowance  
  _Rationale:_ Leave allowance is irrelevant to MTPD.
- D. Marketing campaign length  
  _Rationale:_ This is not a BCM concept.

**MST-0120-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why should a continuity solution be chosen to meet the minimum acceptable level of service rather than full normal capacity?

- A. It balances recovery capability against cost and resources **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: solutions are sized to the minimum acceptable level to be proportionate.
- B. Full capacity is always illegal  
  _Rationale:_ Full capacity is not illegal; it is just costlier than needed.
- C. Minimum service means doing nothing  
  _Rationale:_ Minimum acceptable service still delivers critical output.
- D. Cost is never a factor  
  _Rationale:_ Cost is explicitly a design factor.

**MST-0120-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO activities best embed business continuity into an organisation's culture? (Select TWO)

- A. Ongoing staff awareness and training **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: awareness embeds continuity in daily behaviour.
- B. Regular exercises involving real teams **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: participation builds shared ownership.
- C. Keeping the plan secret from staff  
  _Rationale:_ Secrecy prevents embedding.
- D. A one-off email years ago  
  _Rationale:_ A single stale message does not embed culture.
- E. Punishing anyone who mentions risk  
  _Rationale:_ Suppressing risk talk harms the culture.
