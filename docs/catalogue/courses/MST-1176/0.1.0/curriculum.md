## Learning outcomes

1. Describe the main renewable technologies and their resource basis
2. Assess sites, resources and energy yield at a screening level
3. Navigate permitting, grid connection and stakeholder requirements
4. Build a basic project financial model and understand PPAs
5. Outline project delivery, risks and the development-to-operation path

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1176-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A 10 MW solar plant runs at a 20% capacity factor. Roughly how much energy does it generate in a year (8,760 hours)?

- A. About 17,520 MWh **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: 10 MW x 8,760 h x 0.20 = 17,520 MWh.
- B. About 87,600 MWh  
  _Rationale:_ This omits the capacity factor; real output is 20% of the maximum.
- C. About 10 MWh  
  _Rationale:_ This confuses capacity in MW with annual energy in MWh.
- D. About 2,000 MWh  
  _Rationale:_ This miscalculates; multiply capacity by hours and by the capacity factor.

**MST-1176-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO factors can hard-limit the size or viability of a renewable project regardless of resource quality? (Select TWO.)

- A. Available grid-connection capacity at the point of connection **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: limited interconnection capacity caps how much can be exported.
- B. Permitting and land-use constraints  
  _Rationale:_ Permitting can delay or block, but it is listed separately; still, it is a real constraint.
- C. Environmental or planning permission being refused **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a refused permit can stop the project entirely.
- D. The colour of the solar panels  
  _Rationale:_ Panel colour does not affect project viability.

**MST-1176-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) What is the main benefit to a developer of a fixed-price power purchase agreement (PPA) compared with selling on the merchant market?

- A. Revenue certainty that supports financing **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a fixed-price PPA stabilises revenue, which lenders favour for financing.
- B. Guaranteed higher total revenue than merchant sales  
  _Rationale:_ A PPA trades upside for certainty; it is not always higher revenue.
- C. Removal of all construction risk  
  _Rationale:_ A PPA addresses revenue, not construction risk.
- D. No need for a grid connection  
  _Rationale:_ A grid connection is still required to deliver the energy.
