## Learning outcomes

1. Define key pathophysiology concepts: aetiology, pathogenesis, clinical manifestations and sequelae
2. Explain cellular responses to injury including adaptation, reversible injury and cell death
3. Describe the inflammatory response and the stages of tissue healing
4. Relate common disease mechanisms to the signs and symptoms they produce
5. Distinguish acute from chronic disease processes using representative examples
6. Explain how disturbances in one system produce downstream effects in others

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2130-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) An increase in the SIZE of individual cells in response to greater workload is called:

- A. Hypertrophy **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: hypertrophy is an increase in cell size.
- B. Hyperplasia  
  _Rationale:_ Hyperplasia is an increase in the number of cells.
- C. Atrophy  
  _Rationale:_ Atrophy is a decrease in cell size or number.
- D. Metaplasia  
  _Rationale:_ Metaplasia is a change from one cell type to another.

**MST-2130-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO are cardinal signs of acute inflammation? (Select TWO.)

- A. Redness **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: redness (rubor) results from increased blood flow.
- B. Heat **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: heat (calor) accompanies increased local blood flow.
- C. Permanent loss of the organ  
  _Rationale:_ Organ loss is not a cardinal sign of acute inflammation.
- D. Reduced blood supply to the area  
  _Rationale:_ Acute inflammation increases, not reduces, local blood flow.
- E. Formation of malignant cells  
  _Rationale:_ Malignancy is not a sign of acute inflammation.

**MST-2130-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Which process describes programmed, orderly cell death that does not usually provoke inflammation?

- A. Apoptosis **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: apoptosis is regulated cell death that typically avoids an inflammatory response.
- B. Coagulative necrosis  
  _Rationale:_ Necrosis is uncontrolled death that often triggers inflammation.
- C. Hyperplasia  
  _Rationale:_ Hyperplasia is increased cell number, not cell death.
- D. Caseous necrosis  
  _Rationale:_ Caseous necrosis is a form of necrosis, not programmed death.
