## Learning outcomes

1. Explain Africa's place in early human history and its environmental diversity
2. Describe ancient and classical African states and trade networks
3. Analyse the great medieval and early modern African kingdoms and empires
4. Evaluate the causes and consequences of the Atlantic and other slave trades
5. Explain the scramble for Africa, colonial rule and resistance
6. Assess decolonisation and the challenges and achievements of independent states

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2106-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why do historians reject the claim that 'Africa had no history before European contact'?

- A. Africa had many states, cities, trade networks and written and oral records long before contact **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: abundant evidence documents complex pre-contact African societies.
- B. Because Africa was uninhabited until then  
  _Rationale:_ Africa is central to human origins and was long inhabited.
- C. Because only Europeans kept records  
  _Rationale:_ African societies kept oral and written records of their own.
- D. Because nothing changed before contact  
  _Rationale:_ There was extensive change; the myth erases it.

**MST-2106-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO underpinned the wealth of major West African empires such as those in the Sahel? (Select TWO.)

- A. Control of trans-Saharan trade routes **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: taxing and controlling trade built imperial wealth.
- B. Trade in gold and salt **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: gold and salt were key commodities.
- C. Transatlantic steamship lines  
  _Rationale:_ Steamships are a much later technology.
- D. Industrial coal mining  
  _Rationale:_ Not a basis of these empires' wealth.

**MST-2106-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A survey course covers Africa only through the Atlantic slave trade and colonialism. What is the historiographical problem?

- A. It reduces a long, diverse history to episodes of external disruption and erases African agency **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: this framing omits deep history, states and internal dynamics, distorting the record.
- B. Those topics are unimportant  
  _Rationale:_ They matter greatly; the problem is making them the whole story.
- C. Africa has no other documented history  
  _Rationale:_ There is extensive other history to cover.
- D. Slavery and colonialism had no lasting effects  
  _Rationale:_ They had major effects; the issue is exclusivity of focus.
