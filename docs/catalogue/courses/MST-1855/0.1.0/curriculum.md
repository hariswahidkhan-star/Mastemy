## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1855-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) In standard written German, common nouns are always:

- A. Capitalized (written with an initial capital letter) **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: German capitalizes all nouns.
- B. Written entirely in lowercase  
  _Rationale:_ German nouns take an initial capital, not all-lowercase.
- C. Written in italics  
  _Rationale:_ Italicizing nouns is not a German spelling rule.
- D. Preceded by an apostrophe  
  _Rationale:_ German nouns are not marked by a preceding apostrophe.

**MST-1855-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) How many grammatical cases does standard German have?

- A. Four (nominative, accusative, dative, genitive) **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: German uses four cases.
- B. Two  
  _Rationale:_ German has four cases, not two.
- C. Three  
  _Rationale:_ German has four cases, not three.
- D. Six  
  _Rationale:_ Six cases is characteristic of some other languages, not standard German.

**MST-1855-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO of the following are grammatical genders of German nouns?

- A. Masculine (der) **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: masculine is one of the three German genders.
- B. Neuter (das) **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: neuter is one of the three German genders.
- C. Partitive  
  _Rationale:_ 'Partitive' is not a German gender.
- D. Animate  
  _Rationale:_ 'Animate' is not a German grammatical gender category.
- E. Dual  
  _Rationale:_ 'Dual' is a number, not a German gender.
