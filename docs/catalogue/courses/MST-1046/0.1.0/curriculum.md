## Learning outcomes

1. Explain how procurement decisions flow through to financial statements and cash flow
2. Evaluate supplier payment terms and their working-capital impact
3. Design purchase-to-pay controls that prevent duplicate and fraudulent payments
4. Analyse early-payment discounts and dynamic discounting trade-offs
5. Build a supplier-payment strategy aligned to liquidity and risk goals

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1046-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A supplier offers 2/10 net 30. What is the approximate annualised cost of NOT taking the discount?

- A. About 37% - the 2% saved over the 20 extra days annualises to a high implied rate **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: 2/98 over 20 days annualised is roughly 37%, usually worth taking if cash allows.
- B. About 2% - the discount is only 2% so the cost is 2%  
  _Rationale:_ This ignores that the 2% is earned over only 20 days, not a full year.
- C. About 24% - a flat month-based estimate  
  _Rationale:_ This understates the annualised rate; the correct basis uses 2/98 over 20 days.
- D. Zero - there is never a cost to paying later  
  _Rationale:_ Forgoing the discount has a real implied financing cost.

**MST-1046-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO controls most directly reduce the risk of duplicate supplier payments? (Select TWO.)

- A. System blocking of invoices with a matching supplier, amount and invoice number **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: duplicate detection on key fields stops repeat payments.
- B. Three-way match of PO, goods receipt and invoice before payment **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: matching prevents paying for goods not ordered or received, including re-keyed duplicates.
- C. Paying every invoice immediately on receipt  
  _Rationale:_ Speed without matching increases duplicate and fraud risk.
- D. Letting one person raise, approve and pay invoices  
  _Rationale:_ Lack of segregation enables both error and fraud.

**MST-1046-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Extending supplier payment terms from 30 to 60 days will, all else equal, most directly:

- A. Increase days payable outstanding and free up working capital short term **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: paying later raises DPO and retains cash, though it may strain suppliers.
- B. Reduce the company's revenue  
  _Rationale:_ Payment terms affect payables and cash, not revenue directly.
- C. Immediately improve gross margin  
  _Rationale:_ Timing of payment does not change the cost of goods purchased.
- D. Eliminate the need for cash-flow forecasting  
  _Rationale:_ Forecasting remains essential regardless of terms.
