## Learning outcomes

1. Describe the main medical imaging modalities and the physical principle behind each
2. Compare modalities by what tissues and questions they suit
3. Explain basic image-formation concepts such as contrast and resolution
4. Summarise radiation-safety and justification principles conceptually
5. Describe standard anatomical views and orientation in images
6. Explain the educational role of imaging in a diagnostic pathway, confirmed by clinicians

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2135-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Which imaging modality uses a strong magnetic field and radio waves rather than ionising radiation?

- A. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: MRI uses magnetic fields and radiofrequency pulses, not ionising radiation.
- B. Computed tomography (CT)  
  _Rationale:_ CT uses ionising X-rays.
- C. Plain radiography (X-ray)  
  _Rationale:_ X-ray uses ionising radiation.
- D. PET scanning  
  _Rationale:_ PET uses radioactive tracers, i.e. ionising radiation.

**MST-2135-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO statements about ultrasound are correct? (Select TWO.)

- A. It uses high-frequency sound waves **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: ultrasound forms images from reflected sound waves.
- B. It does not use ionising radiation **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: ultrasound is a non-ionising modality.
- C. It is the best modality for imaging dense bone interiors  
  _Rationale:_ Sound does not penetrate dense bone well; X-ray/CT suit bone.
- D. It always requires an intravenous contrast injection  
  _Rationale:_ Most ultrasound is performed without injected contrast.
- E. It exposes the patient to a high radiation dose  
  _Rationale:_ Ultrasound delivers no ionising radiation.

**MST-2135-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) The ALARA principle in imaging safety stands for keeping radiation dose:

- A. As Low As Reasonably Achievable **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: ALARA means minimising dose while still answering the clinical question.
- B. As Large As Reasonably Allowed  
  _Rationale:_ ALARA is about minimising, not maximising, dose.
- C. Always Linked to A Referral  
  _Rationale:_ This is not the meaning of ALARA.
- D. Averaged Low Across Repeat Attempts  
  _Rationale:_ This is not the definition of ALARA.
