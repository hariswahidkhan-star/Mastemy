## Learning outcomes

1. Why business ethics matters
2. Ethical frameworks for decisions
3. Conflicts of interest and integrity
4. Fairness, respect and the workplace
5. Accountability and speaking up
6. Ethical culture and governance

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Selection items cannot demonstrate hands-on performance of the skill; applied practice comes through instructor-facilitated exercises and model-answer analysis.

## Integrative case

A sales manager is offered a lavish holiday by a supplier just before a contract decision, while a junior reports being pressured to hide a defect. Work the situation ethically: separate legal from ethical, apply decision frameworks, identify the conflict of interest, protect the person speaking up, and recommend an accountable, culture-consistent course of action.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1099-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A practice is fully legal in a jurisdiction but would damage trust and harm customers if known publicly. What does this illustrate?

- A. Legality and ethics are related but not identical **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: an action can be legal yet still be unethical.
- B. If it is legal, it is automatically ethical  
  _Rationale:_ Law sets a floor, not the whole of ethical obligation.
- C. Ethics only concerns illegal acts  
  _Rationale:_ Many ethical issues arise entirely within the law.
- D. Public opinion has no bearing on ethics  
  _Rationale:_ A publicity test is a recognised ethical heuristic.

**MST-1099-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which situations should normally be disclosed as potential conflicts of interest? (Select TWO)

- A. Approving a contract for a company your spouse owns **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a personal financial tie to the decision is a classic conflict.
- B. Hiring a close friend into a role you supervise **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the personal relationship can compromise impartial judgement.
- C. Recommending a vendor purely on documented performance  
  _Rationale:_ An arm's-length, evidence-based recommendation is not a conflict.
- D. Declining a supplier's routine product brochure  
  _Rationale:_ Receiving public marketing material is not a conflict of interest.

**MST-1099-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) An employee notices financial figures being misstated but says nothing because 'reporting isn't my responsibility'. What accountability principle is being neglected?

- A. A duty to raise concerns through a safe reporting channel **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: accountability includes speaking up about serious wrongdoing.
- B. The right to remain anonymous forever  
  _Rationale:_ Anonymity is a protection, not a reason to stay silent.
- C. The need to confront the wrongdoer directly and alone  
  _Rationale:_ Safe channels exist precisely so direct confrontation is not required.
- D. The principle that only managers have ethical duties  
  _Rationale:_ Ethical responsibility is not limited to managers.
