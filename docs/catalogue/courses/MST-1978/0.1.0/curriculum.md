## Learning outcomes

1. Describe the structure and diversity of bacteria, archaea, viruses and fungi
2. Explain microbial growth, metabolism and methods of control
3. Describe microbial genetics and horizontal gene transfer
4. Explain host-microbe interactions and the basis of infection
5. Describe antimicrobial agents and the rise of resistance
6. Apply aseptic reasoning and interpret basic microbiology results

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1978-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) The Gram stain separates bacteria mainly by the thickness and location of which cell-envelope component?

- A. The peptidoglycan layer **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: Gram-positive cells have a thick peptidoglycan layer that retains the stain.
- B. The nuclear membrane  
  _Rationale:_ Bacteria have no nuclear membrane.
- C. The mitochondrial cristae  
  _Rationale:_ Bacteria lack mitochondria.
- D. The endoplasmic reticulum  
  _Rationale:_ Bacteria lack an endoplasmic reticulum.

**MST-1978-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO are mechanisms of horizontal gene transfer in bacteria? (Select TWO.)

- A. Conjugation through a pilus **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: conjugation transfers DNA through direct cell-to-cell contact.
- B. Transduction by a bacteriophage **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a phage can carry bacterial DNA between cells.
- C. Mitosis  
  _Rationale:_ Bacteria do not divide by mitosis.
- D. Meiosis  
  _Rationale:_ Bacteria do not undergo meiosis.

**MST-1978-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why are antibiotics ineffective against viral infections?

- A. Antibiotics target bacterial structures and processes that viruses lack **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: viruses lack the bacterial targets that antibiotics act on.
- B. Viruses are simply too large for antibiotics  
  _Rationale:_ Viruses are smaller, not larger, than bacteria.
- C. Viruses have thick peptidoglycan walls  
  _Rationale:_ Viruses have no peptidoglycan wall.
- D. Antibiotics only work at high temperature  
  _Rationale:_ Temperature is not the reason.
