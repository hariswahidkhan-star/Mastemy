## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1854-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Which definite article correctly precedes the masculine singular noun 'libro'?

- A. el **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: 'el libro' uses the masculine singular definite article.
- B. la  
  _Rationale:_ 'la' is feminine singular and does not agree with 'libro'.
- C. los  
  _Rationale:_ 'los' is masculine plural and does not agree with a singular noun.
- D. las  
  _Rationale:_ 'las' is feminine plural and does not agree with 'libro'.

**MST-1854-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) The Spanish preterite (preterito indefinido) is primarily used to express:

- A. Completed actions in the past **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the preterite expresses completed past actions.
- B. Ongoing actions happening right now  
  _Rationale:_ That is the present or present progressive, not the preterite.
- C. Future intentions  
  _Rationale:_ Future intentions use future or 'ir a' constructions, not the preterite.
- D. Habitual present routines  
  _Rationale:_ Habitual present uses the present tense, not the preterite.

**MST-1854-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO statements about Spanish noun-adjective agreement are correct?

- A. Adjectives generally agree in gender with the noun **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: adjectives match masculine/feminine gender.
- B. Adjectives generally agree in number with the noun **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: adjectives match singular/plural number.
- C. Adjectives never change their form  
  _Rationale:_ Spanish adjectives inflect for gender and number.
- D. Adjectives must always come before the noun  
  _Rationale:_ Descriptive adjectives usually follow the noun.
- E. Definite articles do not change with gender  
  _Rationale:_ Spanish articles change with gender and number.
