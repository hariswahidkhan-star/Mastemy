## Learning outcomes

1. Explain the fraud triangle and common occupational fraud schemes
2. Assess fraud risk across financial processes
3. Design preventive and detective anti-fraud controls
4. Recognise red flags and analyse data for anomalies
5. Build a fraud response and whistleblowing framework

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1051-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) The three elements of the classic fraud triangle are:

- A. Pressure, opportunity and rationalisation **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the fraud triangle is pressure, opportunity and rationalisation.
- B. Budget, forecast and variance  
  _Rationale:_ These are planning terms, not the fraud triangle.
- C. Assets, liabilities and equity  
  _Rationale:_ These are accounting elements, not fraud drivers.
- D. Plan, do, check  
  _Rationale:_ This is a quality cycle, not the fraud triangle.

**MST-1051-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO measures best help prevent and deter occupational fraud? (Select TWO.)

- A. A confidential whistleblowing channel with no retaliation **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: tips are a leading detection source and a strong deterrent.
- B. Segregation of duties over cash and vendor data **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: removing opportunity prevents many schemes.
- C. Removing all approval requirements to speed work  
  _Rationale:_ This increases opportunity for fraud.
- D. Keeping fraud policies secret from staff  
  _Rationale:_ Visible policies deter; secrecy weakens deterrence.

**MST-1051-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A sudden vendor whose bank details match an employee's account is best described as:

- A. A red flag warranting investigation **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: matching employee and vendor bank details is a classic fraud red flag.
- B. Normal and requiring no action  
  _Rationale:_ This pattern should be investigated, not ignored.
- C. Proof the controls are working  
  _Rationale:_ It signals a potential control failure.
- D. Irrelevant to fraud risk  
  _Rationale:_ It is directly relevant to fraud risk.
