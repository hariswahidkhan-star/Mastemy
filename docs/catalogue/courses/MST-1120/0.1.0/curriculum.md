## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1120-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) The 'service-recovery paradox' describes how a customer can end up more loyal when:

- A. A problem never occurs at all  
  _Rationale:_ The paradox is specifically about recovery after a failure.
- B. A problem is resolved quickly and well after it occurs **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: excellent recovery can leave a customer more loyal than if nothing had gone wrong.
- C. The company blames the customer for the issue  
  _Rationale:_ Blaming the customer damages loyalty.
- D. The complaint is ignored to avoid conflict  
  _Rationale:_ Ignoring complaints reduces loyalty.

**MST-1120-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) Customer Effort Score (CES) is most appropriate for measuring:

- A. Overall emotional loyalty to the brand  
  _Rationale:_ That is closer to what NPS attempts to capture.
- B. How easy it was to get a specific task done **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: CES captures the effort required for a specific interaction.
- C. The company's annual revenue growth  
  _Rationale:_ CES is a customer metric, not a financial one.
- D. How many features a product has  
  _Rationale:_ Feature count is unrelated to effort score.

**MST-1120-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO make a customer journey map genuinely useful? (Select TWO)

- A. It captures the customer's emotions at each step **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: emotions reveal where the experience succeeds or fails.
- B. It is built from real customer research, not assumptions **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: grounding the map in research keeps it honest.
- C. It documents only internal system steps  
  _Rationale:_ An internal-process view misses the customer's experience.
- D. It is drawn once and never revisited  
  _Rationale:_ A static map quickly goes stale.
