## Learning outcomes

1. Explain what copywriting is and how it differs from other writing
2. Research the audience, product and voice before writing
3. Write clear, persuasive headlines and body copy
4. Apply persuasion principles and structure to drive action
5. Edit, test and adapt copy across formats and channels

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1872-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why do strong copywriters translate features into benefits?

- A. Readers act on what the product does for them, not the spec itself **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: benefits connect the feature to the reader's desired outcome.
- B. Features are always irrelevant  
  _Rationale:_ Features matter as proof, but benefits drive action.
- C. Benefits make copy longer  
  _Rationale:_ Benefits are about relevance, not length.
- D. It lets you avoid mentioning the product  
  _Rationale:_ Benefits are still tied to the product.

**MST-1872-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO traits make a call to action more effective? (Select TWO.)

- A. It is specific about the next action **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: specificity reduces friction and uncertainty.
- B. It states the value the reader gets by acting **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: tying the action to value increases motivation.
- C. It is as vague as possible to appeal to everyone  
  _Rationale:_ Vagueness lowers response, not raises it.
- D. It is hidden to avoid seeming pushy  
  _Rationale:_ A hidden call to action gets fewer conversions.

**MST-1872-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A headline lists five benefits in one long sentence. What is the main weakness?

- A. It lacks focus, so no single benefit lands with the reader **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a headline should earn attention with one clear idea.
- B. Headlines should never mention benefits  
  _Rationale:_ Headlines can lead with a benefit; the issue is focus.
- C. It is too short to be useful  
  _Rationale:_ The problem is cramming too much, not brevity.
- D. Benefits do not belong in copy  
  _Rationale:_ Benefits are central to persuasive copy.
