## Learning outcomes

1. Understand the idea of a limit informally
2. Interpret the derivative as a rate of change and a gradient
3. Differentiate simple polynomial functions
4. Find gradients and equations of tangents
5. Find and classify stationary points
6. Understand integration as the reverse of differentiation and as area

- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2827-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) What is the derivative of f(x) = x squared?

- A. 2x **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: by the power rule, the derivative of x squared is 2x.
- B. x  
  _Rationale:_ That halves the power incorrectly; the derivative is 2x.
- C. 2  
  _Rationale:_ 2 is the derivative of 2x, not of x squared.
- D. x cubed  
  _Rationale:_ That raises the power; differentiation lowers it by one.

**MST-2827-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Select TWO statements that correctly describe a derivative. (Select TWO.)

- A. It gives the gradient of the curve at a point **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the derivative is the slope of the tangent at that point.
- B. It describes the rate of change of the function **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the derivative measures how fast the output changes.
- C. It always gives the area under the curve  
  _Rationale:_ Area under a curve is found by integration, not differentiation.
- D. It is the same as the original function  
  _Rationale:_ The derivative is generally a different function.

**MST-2827-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) At a stationary point of a curve, what is the value of the gradient?

- A. 0 **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a stationary point is where the gradient, dy/dx, equals zero.
- B. 1  
  _Rationale:_ A gradient of 1 is a sloping tangent, not stationary.
- C. Undefined  
  _Rationale:_ The gradient is defined and equal to zero at a stationary point.
- D. The largest value on the curve  
  _Rationale:_ The gradient, not the y-value, is zero at a stationary point.
