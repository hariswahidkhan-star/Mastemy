## Learning outcomes

1. Define evaluation criteria for AI vendors and products
2. Assess vendor claims, demos and benchmarks critically
3. Evaluate data handling, security and compliance terms
4. Weigh total cost, lock-in and exit options
5. Recognise risk, bias and oversight gaps in vendor offerings

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1396-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A vendor shows a demo with 99% accuracy. What should you do before relying on that figure?

- A. Ask how, on what data and for which task the figure was measured **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: accuracy claims are meaningless without the task, data and method behind them.
- B. Accept it because the demo looked convincing  
  _Rationale:_ A demo is not evidence of real-world performance.
- C. Assume it applies to your data unchanged  
  _Rationale:_ Performance usually drops on different, real data.
- D. Ignore accuracy and focus only on price  
  _Rationale:_ Capability still matters alongside cost.

**MST-1396-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO checks most protect you when evaluating an AI vendor's data handling? (Select TWO.)

- A. Confirm where data is stored, processed and who can access it **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: knowing data location and access is central to risk.
- B. Review the contract terms on data reuse and deletion **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: reuse and deletion terms define real data control.
- C. Rely on the vendor's marketing promise of security  
  _Rationale:_ Marketing claims are not enforceable protections.
- D. Assume compliance because the vendor is well known  
  _Rationale:_ Brand size does not guarantee compliant data handling.

**MST-1396-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why does vendor lock-in matter in an AI purchase?

- A. It can make switching costly or impractical later, weakening your leverage **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: lock-in raises future cost and reduces bargaining power.
- B. It guarantees lower long-term costs  
  _Rationale:_ Lock-in usually raises long-term cost and risk.
- C. It only affects the vendor, not the buyer  
  _Rationale:_ Lock-in primarily constrains the buyer.
- D. It is irrelevant once a contract is signed  
  _Rationale:_ Exit options matter throughout the relationship.
