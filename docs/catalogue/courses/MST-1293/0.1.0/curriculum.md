## Learning outcomes

1. Apply telecommunications/ICT distribution design principles, codes and standards
2. Design structured cabling, pathways, spaces and grounding/bonding systems
3. Design for data centres, outside plant, and specialised distribution systems
4. Apply project coordination, documentation and professional practice

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1293-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) What is the key distinction between a code and a standard in ICT distribution design?

- A. Codes are typically adopted law/mandatory; standards are often voluntary best-practice **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: codes carry the force of law when adopted, while many standards are voluntary consensus documents.
- B. Standards are always legally mandatory and codes are optional  
  _Rationale:_ This reverses the usual relationship.
- C. They mean exactly the same thing  
  _Rationale:_ They are distinct: enforceability and origin differ.
- D. Neither has any bearing on design  
  _Rationale:_ Both codes and standards directly shape compliant design.

**MST-1293-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) A proposed horizontal copper cabling run exceeds the recognised maximum channel length. What is the correct design response?

- A. Redesign so the run stays within the recognised length limit (e.g. relocate the telecommunications room) **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: staying within recognised length limits is required to ensure performance and compliance.
- B. Install it as-is and assume it will still pass  
  _Rationale:_ Exceeding limits risks failing performance requirements.
- C. Remove grounding to save length  
  _Rationale:_ Grounding/bonding is a safety requirement, unrelated to and not a fix for length limits.
- D. Use the exceeded length because fibre rules allow it  
  _Rationale:_ Fibre has different limits; they do not justify exceeding the copper channel limit.

**MST-1293-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Select TWO practices that support a well-coordinated ICT infrastructure design.

- A. Coordinating pathways and spaces with mechanical, electrical and structural trades **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: multi-trade coordination avoids conflicts and rework.
- B. Producing clear, standards-referenced documentation **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: coordinated, standards-referenced documentation supports correct installation and handover.
- C. Designing in isolation from other building systems  
  _Rationale:_ Isolated design causes conflicts with other trades.
- D. Omitting grounding and bonding to reduce cost  
  _Rationale:_ Grounding/bonding is a safety requirement and cannot be omitted.
- E. Leaving length and fill limits undocumented  
  _Rationale:_ Undocumented limits lead to non-compliant installs.
