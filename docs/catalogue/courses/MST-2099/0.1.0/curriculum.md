## Learning outcomes

1. Explain how historians periodise world history and why period boundaries are interpretive, not natural
2. Trace the emergence of early states, agriculture and writing across multiple regions
3. Analyse how trade, migration and empire connected distant societies before 1500
4. Explain the causes and global consequences of early modern connections, 1450-1750
5. Evaluate the industrial, political and imperial transformations of the long 19th century
6. Assess the forces shaping the contemporary world since 1900, including decolonisation and globalisation

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2099-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A student argues that societies without writing were 'not yet civilised'. Why do historians reject this framing?

- A. 'Civilisation' describes forms of social complexity, not a moral rank, and oral societies were complex in other ways **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the term is descriptive, and writing is one feature among many, not a universal threshold of worth.
- B. Because all societies eventually developed writing  
  _Rationale:_ Many societies remained non-literate; the point is the ranking itself is flawed.
- C. Because writing was invented only once  
  _Rationale:_ Writing arose independently in several regions; this does not address the value judgement.
- D. Because civilisation requires cities, which all societies had  
  _Rationale:_ Not all societies were urban; the error is treating complexity as a single ladder.

**MST-2099-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO developments most directly connected distant societies across Afro-Eurasia before 1500? (Select TWO.)

- A. Long-distance trade networks such as the Silk Roads and Indian Ocean routes **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: these routes moved goods, people and ideas between regions.
- B. The spread of universalising religions along those routes **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: faiths like Buddhism and Islam travelled with merchants and migrants.
- C. Transatlantic plantation agriculture  
  _Rationale:_ That belongs to the post-1500 Atlantic world.
- D. Industrial railway networks  
  _Rationale:_ Railways are a 19th-century development.

**MST-2099-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why do historians treat the boundary between 'medieval' and 'early modern' as an interpretation rather than a fixed date?

- A. Periodisation is a tool for analysis; change was gradual and uneven across regions **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: labels are imposed by historians to organise continuous change, and fit regions differently.
- B. Because no sources survive from the transition  
  _Rationale:_ Abundant sources survive; the issue is interpretive framing.
- C. Because the calendar changed at that point everywhere  
  _Rationale:_ Calendar reforms were local and are not the basis of the division.
- D. Because all historians agree on the exact year  
  _Rationale:_ They do not agree, which is precisely why it is interpretive.
