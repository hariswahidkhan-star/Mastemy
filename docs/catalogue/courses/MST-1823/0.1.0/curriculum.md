## Learning outcomes

1. Explain what strategy is and how it differs from goals and tactics
2. Analyse the external environment and industry structure
3. Analyse internal resources, capabilities and competitive advantage
4. Formulate strategy using positioning and growth frameworks
5. Translate strategy into execution, metrics and review

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1823-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Which statement best describes a strategy rather than a goal?

- A. How the firm will win: the distinctive choices it makes to create advantage **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: strategy is the integrated set of choices about how to compete and win.
- B. A target to grow revenue by 20% next year  
  _Rationale:_ That is a goal, not how the firm will achieve it.
- C. A list of this quarter's tasks  
  _Rationale:_ Those are tactics, not strategy.
- D. The company's mission statement  
  _Rationale:_ A mission states purpose, not the competitive approach.

**MST-1823-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Using VRIO, which TWO conditions must a resource meet to be a source of sustained competitive advantage? (Select TWO.)

- A. It is valuable to customers or cost position **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: value is the first VRIO test.
- B. It is rare and hard for rivals to imitate **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: rarity and inimitability sustain the advantage.
- C. It is listed on the balance sheet  
  _Rationale:_ Accounting recognition is not a VRIO criterion.
- D. It is the cheapest resource available  
  _Rationale:_ Low cost alone does not confer advantage.

**MST-1823-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A company pursues both the lowest price and the most premium differentiation at once and loses money. This is best described as:

- A. Being 'stuck in the middle' without a clear competitive position **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: failing to commit to a generic strategy erodes advantage.
- B. A successful blue-ocean strategy  
  _Rationale:_ This is a failure of positioning, not a new uncontested market.
- C. Effective cost leadership  
  _Rationale:_ Cost leadership requires a disciplined low-cost focus.
- D. A sound focus strategy  
  _Rationale:_ Focus targets a niche, not everything at once.
