## Learning outcomes

1. Plan governed social content against a brief and brand rules
2. Use ChatGPT to draft content that respects voice and compliance limits
3. Produce on-brand visuals in Canva tied to the approved copy
4. Schedule and publish through Buffer with an approval gate
5. Operate the pipeline with review, disclosure and performance governance

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Selection items cannot demonstrate production coding/operational ability; that is taught through instructor-built projects and walkthroughs.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-0790-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A ChatGPT-drafted post makes a specific performance claim about the product. Before scheduling, you should:

- A. Verify the claim is substantiated and compliant, or remove it **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: unsubstantiated claims are a brand and legal risk.
- B. Schedule it because it is engaging  
  _Rationale:_ Engagement does not excuse an unsupported claim.
- C. Add an emoji to soften the claim  
  _Rationale:_ Cosmetic edits do not make a claim substantiated.
- D. Publish and correct later if challenged  
  _Rationale:_ Publishing first and fixing later accepts avoidable risk.

**MST-0790-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO conditions should a post meet before Buffer publishes it? (Select TWO.)

- A. It has passed the human approval gate **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: approval is the control that keeps non-compliant posts off the channel.
- B. Any required disclosure is present **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: disclosures are often legally required and must be on the post.
- C. It has the most hashtags of the week  
  _Rationale:_ Hashtag count is not a quality or compliance control.
- D. It was the fastest draft to produce  
  _Rationale:_ Speed of drafting says nothing about whether it should publish.

**MST-0790-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) What should a channel visual include to be accessible?

- A. Descriptive alt text and sufficient contrast **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: alt text and contrast make the visual usable by more people.
- B. The maximum amount of text that will fit  
  _Rationale:_ Dense text harms readability and accessibility.
- C. Only decorative imagery with no description  
  _Rationale:_ Undescribed imagery excludes screen-reader users.
- D. A watermark across the whole image  
  _Rationale:_ A heavy watermark reduces legibility, not improves access.
