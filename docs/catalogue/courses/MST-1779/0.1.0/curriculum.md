## Learning outcomes

1. Explain the structure, roles and context of healthcare management
2. Apply principles of leading, managing people and teams in health settings
3. Explain operations, quality, finance and resource management in healthcare
4. Describe strategy, change and governance in health organisations

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every course outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Hands-on operation, the quality of tools and live outputs, and professional judgement are taught by video demonstration and model-answer analysis; the certificate assessment uses single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only and never requires uploads, recorded work, submitted code or peer review.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1779-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A manager focuses on budgets, schedules and keeping processes running smoothly, while leadership is more concerned with:

- A. Setting direction and motivating people toward change **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: leadership emphasises vision, direction and influencing people, complementing management's control functions.
- B. Only approving expenditure  
  _Rationale:_ Approving spend is a management control activity, not the essence of leadership.
- C. Recording attendance  
  _Rationale:_ Attendance recording is an administrative task, not leadership.
- D. Avoiding any change to keep stability  
  _Rationale:_ Leadership typically drives change rather than avoiding it.**MST-1779-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) A clinic sees many patients but still has long waits because they queue at one overloaded step. The manager should first address the:

- A. Bottleneck step that limits overall flow **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the constraining step governs throughput, so improving flow starts there.
- B. Steps that already have spare capacity  
  _Rationale:_ Improving steps that are not the constraint will not improve overall flow.
- C. Total number of patients seen per day  
  _Rationale:_ High activity is not the same as good flow; the bottleneck is the issue.
- D. Colour of the waiting-room signage  
  _Rationale:_ Cosmetic changes do not relieve a capacity bottleneck.**MST-1779-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO belong to clinical governance rather than purely corporate governance? (Select TWO)

- A. Monitoring clinical audit and patient outcomes **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: clinical audit and outcomes are core clinical governance activities.
- B. Acting on patient safety incidents and complaints **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: learning from safety incidents and complaints is part of clinical governance.
- C. Approving the organisation's annual financial statements  
  _Rationale:_ Financial statement approval is corporate governance.
- D. Setting the board's conflict-of-interest register  
  _Rationale:_ The conflict-of-interest register is a corporate governance control.
