## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1113-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A valid A/B test of an email should change:

- A. Several elements at once to save time  
  _Rationale:_ Changing several elements makes the cause of any difference unclear.
- B. One variable at a time between the versions **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: isolating one variable lets you attribute the result.
- C. The audience as well as the content  
  _Rationale:_ Changing the audience confounds the test.
- D. The send time and the subject line together  
  _Rationale:_ Two changes at once prevent a clean comparison.

**MST-1113-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) Permission-based (opt-in) email matters mainly because it:

- A. Guarantees every recipient will buy  
  _Rationale:_ Consent does not guarantee purchase.
- B. Improves engagement and protects deliverability and trust **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: willing recipients engage more and keep sender reputation healthy.
- C. Removes the need to measure results  
  _Rationale:_ Measurement is still required.
- D. Lets you ignore unsubscribe requests  
  _Rationale:_ Honouring unsubscribes is a legal and ethical must.

**MST-1113-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO belong in a strong welcome flow for new subscribers? (Select TWO)

- A. A clear statement of what to expect and how often **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: setting expectations reduces later unsubscribes and complaints.
- B. An early, relevant first value or offer **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: delivering early value builds engagement with new subscribers.
- C. An immediate hard upsell with no context  
  _Rationale:_ A cold hard sell at hello tends to increase unsubscribes.
- D. A long gap before any contact at all  
  _Rationale:_ Delaying first contact loses the new subscriber's attention.
