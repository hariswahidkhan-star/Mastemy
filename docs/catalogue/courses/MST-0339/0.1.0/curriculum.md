## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-0339-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Under California wage-and-hour rules, a nonexempt employee works a 10-hour day. How is daily overtime generally treated differently from the federal standard?

- A. California generally requires overtime after 8 hours in a workday, not only after 40 in a week **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: California applies daily overtime after 8 hours, which federal law does not require.
- B. California never requires overtime if weekly hours stay under 40  
  _Rationale:_ Incorrect: daily overtime can apply even when the weekly total is under 40.
- C. California applies the same 40-hour-only rule as federal law  
  _Rationale:_ Incorrect: California adds a daily overtime standard beyond the weekly federal rule.
- D. California prohibits any work beyond 8 hours per day  
  _Rationale:_ Incorrect: longer days are permitted with appropriate overtime, not prohibited.

**MST-0339-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) A California employer wants to classify a worker as an independent contractor. Which framework is most associated with that determination in California?

- A. An 'ABC'-style test that presumes employee status unless specific conditions are met **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: California commonly applies an ABC-style test that presumes employment unless the criteria are satisfied.
- B. A rule that any worker who signs a contractor agreement is automatically a contractor  
  _Rationale:_ Incorrect: a signed agreement does not by itself establish contractor status.
- C. A rule based solely on whether the worker is paid by invoice  
  _Rationale:_ Incorrect: payment method alone does not determine classification.
- D. A rule that part-time workers are always contractors  
  _Rationale:_ Incorrect: part-time status does not determine contractor classification.

**MST-0339-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Select TWO characteristics of California's Fair Employment and Housing Act (FEHA) that commonly differ from the federal baseline.

- A. It applies to employers with fewer employees than some federal thresholds **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: FEHA's coverage reaches smaller employers than some federal statutes.
- B. It recognizes protected categories beyond the minimum federal list **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: California recognizes additional protected characteristics beyond the federal baseline.
- C. It eliminates the need for any anti-harassment training  
  _Rationale:_ Incorrect: California expands, rather than eliminates, harassment-prevention training duties.
- D. It removes all recordkeeping obligations for employers  
  _Rationale:_ Incorrect: FEHA does not remove recordkeeping obligations.
- E. It applies only to public-sector employers  
  _Rationale:_ Incorrect: FEHA applies broadly to private and public employers.
