## Learning outcomes

1. Explain what a food chain shows
2. Identify producers, consumers and decomposers
3. Describe the role of the Sun as an energy source
4. Build a simple food chain and food web
5. Explain predator and prey relationships
6. Predict what happens when part of a food web changes

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2865-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) In a food chain, what is a plant usually called?

- A. A producer **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: plants make their own food, so they are producers.
- B. A predator  
  _Rationale:_ Predators hunt other animals; plants do not.
- C. A decomposer  
  _Rationale:_ Decomposers break down dead things; plants make food.
- D. Prey  
  _Rationale:_ Prey is an animal that is eaten; a plant is a producer.

**MST-2865-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) Where does the energy in almost every food chain begin?

- A. The Sun **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the Sun's energy lets plants make food that starts the chain.
- B. The soil alone  
  _Rationale:_ Soil gives minerals, but the energy starts with the Sun.
- C. The Moon  
  _Rationale:_ The Moon does not give the energy plants use to grow.
- D. The wind  
  _Rationale:_ Wind moves air but is not the energy source for food chains.

**MST-2865-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO are examples of consumers in a pond? (Select TWO.)

- A. A frog **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a frog eats other living things, so it is a consumer.
- B. A heron **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a heron eats fish and frogs, so it is a consumer.
- C. Pond algae  
  _Rationale:_ Algae make their own food, so they are producers.
- D. Sunlight  
  _Rationale:_ Sunlight is energy, not a consumer.
