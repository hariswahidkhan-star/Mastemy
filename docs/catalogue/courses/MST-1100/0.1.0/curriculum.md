## Learning outcomes

1. Foundations of teaching adults
2. Designing a video curriculum
3. On-camera delivery
4. Engagement without a live room
5. Assessing learning fairly
6. Improving instruction

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Selection items cannot demonstrate hands-on performance of the skill; applied practice comes through instructor-facilitated exercises and model-answer analysis.

## Integrative case

A subject expert is asked to turn their knowledge into a video course but their first attempt is a single 90-minute monologue with no checks. Coach them to redesign it: define outcomes, chunk and sequence content, script for on-camera clarity, build retrieval and misconception-handling into the flow, and align fair MCQ assessment to the stated outcomes.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1100-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A trainer says 'I covered the whole topic in the video, so the learners must have learned it.' What is the flaw?

- A. Covering content does not guarantee that learning occurred **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: learning is evidenced by what learners can do, not by what was presented.
- B. Videos cannot teach anything  
  _Rationale:_ Video is an effective medium; coverage alone is the issue.
- C. Topics should never be covered fully  
  _Rationale:_ Thorough coverage is fine; the error is equating it with learning.
- D. Learners always learn everything shown  
  _Rationale:_ This is precisely the mistaken assumption being flagged.

**MST-1100-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which techniques help manage cognitive load in a video lesson? (Select TWO)

- A. Breaking a long topic into short, focused segments **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: chunking reduces the load on working memory.
- B. Removing redundant on-screen text that duplicates narration **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: cutting redundancy avoids the split-attention effect.
- C. Packing every related detail into one continuous video  
  _Rationale:_ This overloads learners and reduces retention.
- D. Speaking as fast as possible to save time  
  _Rationale:_ Excessive pace increases load and harms comprehension.

**MST-1100-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A quiz item asks about a statistic that never appeared in the lesson. Which assessment principle does this violate?

- A. Alignment of assessment to what was taught and to the outcomes **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: fair assessment tests the stated outcomes, not untaught trivia.
- B. The need for multiple-answer items  
  _Rationale:_ Format is not the issue; alignment is.
- C. The rule that quizzes must be timed  
  _Rationale:_ Timing is unrelated to this flaw.
- D. The requirement to randomise options  
  _Rationale:_ Randomisation does not address testing untaught content.
