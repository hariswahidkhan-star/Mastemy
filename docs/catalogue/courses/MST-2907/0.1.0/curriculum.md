## Learning outcomes

1. Explain why fairness and bias matter in AI systems
2. Describe how personal data is collected and why privacy matters
3. Explain the importance of transparency and consent
4. Identify who can be affected by an automated decision
5. Discuss accountability for AI mistakes
6. Reason through an AI ethics dilemma using simple principles

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2907-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why can an AI system still be unfair even though 'a computer' made the decision?

- A. Because it learns from data that may contain human bias, which it can repeat or amplify **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: AI can inherit and scale bias present in its data.
- B. Computers are never involved in unfairness  
  _Rationale:_ AI can be unfair when its data or design is biased.
- C. Because computers dislike certain people  
  _Rationale:_ Computers have no feelings; bias comes from data and design.
- D. Because fairness is impossible to think about  
  _Rationale:_ Fairness can and should be reasoned about.

**MST-2907-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO principles help make an AI system more trustworthy? (Select TWO.)

- A. Transparency about how decisions are made **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: being able to explain decisions builds trust.
- B. Clear accountability when something goes wrong **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: knowing who is responsible is essential.
- C. Collecting as much personal data as possible with no consent  
  _Rationale:_ Excess data without consent harms privacy and trust.
- D. Hiding the system's mistakes from users  
  _Rationale:_ Concealing errors reduces trust and accountability.

**MST-2907-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A hospital uses AI to help prioritise patients and it makes a harmful error. Who should be accountable?

- A. The people and organisation who chose, built and deployed the system remain responsible **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: humans and organisations stay accountable for AI they deploy.
- B. No one, because the AI decided  
  _Rationale:_ Deploying an AI does not remove human accountability.
- C. Only the patients affected  
  _Rationale:_ The affected people are harmed, not responsible.
- D. The computer hardware itself  
  _Rationale:_ Hardware is not a responsible party; the people who deploy it are.
