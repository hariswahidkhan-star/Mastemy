## Learning outcomes

1. Distinguish reactive, preventive, predictive and proactive maintenance strategies
2. Build preventive-maintenance plans with appropriate tasks and intervals
3. Plan and schedule maintenance work, including resources and parts
4. Use a CMMS and work-order workflow to control maintenance
5. Measure maintenance performance and improve plan effectiveness

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Integrative case

A maintenance planner at a food plant must cut unplanned downtime. Rank assets by criticality, build PM plans with defensible intervals, plan and schedule a week of work against limited labour, run the work orders through the CMMS, and report schedule compliance and the PMs to retire.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1159-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) In maintenance management, what is the key difference between planning and scheduling a job?

- A. Planning defines what work and how; scheduling defines who does it and when **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: planning is the scope, method, parts and labour estimate; scheduling assigns it to people and a time.
- B. Planning assigns the date; scheduling lists the parts  
  _Rationale:_ This reverses the two activities.
- C. They are the same activity with different names  
  _Rationale:_ They are distinct steps with different owners and outputs.
- D. Planning is only for emergencies  
  _Rationale:_ Planning applies mainly to proactive, non-emergency work.

**MST-1159-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO situations most justify a predictive (condition-based) strategy over fixed-interval PM? (Select TWO.)

- A. The failure gives measurable warning signs before it occurs **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a detectable P-F interval is what makes condition monitoring effective.
- B. The asset is critical and downtime is costly **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: higher consequence justifies the cost of monitoring to avoid surprise failure.
- C. The failure is sudden with no detectable precursor  
  _Rationale:_ With no warning signal, condition monitoring cannot give useful lead time.
- D. The asset is cheap, redundant and low-consequence  
  _Rationale:_ Low consequence usually favours run-to-failure, not predictive maintenance.

**MST-1159-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A team completed 18 of 24 scheduled jobs this week. What is the schedule compliance, and what does a persistently low value suggest?

- A. 75%; the schedule is being broken by reactive work or poor planning **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: 18/24 = 75%, and chronic low compliance points to interruptions or unrealistic schedules.
- B. 75%; the team simply needs to work faster  
  _Rationale:_ Low compliance is usually a planning/interruption problem, not effort.
- C. 133%; the schedule was exceeded  
  _Rationale:_ You cannot complete more than were scheduled here; the ratio is 18/24.
- D. 25%; a quarter of work was done  
  _Rationale:_ 25% is the non-completion share, not schedule compliance.
