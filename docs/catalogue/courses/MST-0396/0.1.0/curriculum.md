## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-0396-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A speaker says 'The meeting is on Tuesday... sorry, I mean Thursday.' The answer box asks for the day. What is correct?

- A. Thursday **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the self-correction replaces Tuesday with Thursday, the final stated answer.
- B. Tuesday  
  _Rationale:_ Tuesday was corrected and is no longer the intended answer.
- C. Tuesday and Thursday  
  _Rationale:_ Only the corrected day is the answer; listing both is wrong.
- D. Neither  
  _Rationale:_ The corrected day Thursday is clearly the intended answer.

**MST-0396-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) A completion answer must be 'ONE WORD AND/OR A NUMBER'. The spoken answer is 'twenty pounds'. What should be written if only the amount is required and the symbol is pre-printed?

- A. 20 **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a number counts as allowed, and the currency symbol is already printed, so '20' fits the limit.
- B. twenty pounds  
  _Rationale:_ That is two words and exceeds a one-word/number limit.
- C. twenty  
  _Rationale:_ Writing the word omits the numeric amount expected and may mis-match the pre-printed symbol.
- D. 20 pounds  
  _Rationale:_ Adding 'pounds' exceeds the limit when the symbol is pre-printed.

**MST-0396-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO strategies most help in Section 4 (a lecture)?

- A. Using signpost language to anticipate upcoming answers **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: lectures are heavily signposted, aiding prediction.
- B. Reading ahead to the next questions while listening **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: Section 4 runs without a mid-section pause, so reading ahead is essential.
- C. Waiting for the answer to be repeated  
  _Rationale:_ Answers are usually said once; waiting risks missing them.
- D. Writing full sentences for every point  
  _Rationale:_ Full sentences are too slow and unnecessary.
- E. Ignoring the question wording  
  _Rationale:_ Question wording guides what to listen for.
