## Learning outcomes

1. Explain cardiac conduction and the ECG waveform components
2. Apply a systematic rate and rhythm analysis method
3. Measure and interpret intervals and axis
4. Recognise common rhythm abnormalities
5. Identify patterns suggesting ischaemia and infarction
6. Apply interpretation to artefact, limits and escalation

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Integrative case

A monitoring technician is handed a 12-lead trace with a borderline rate, a wide QRS and possible ST changes on a noisy baseline. Using a systematic method they must work through rate, rhythm, intervals, axis and ST analysis, separate artefact from signal, and decide what to escalate to the clinician.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1781-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) On a standard ECG at 25 mm/s, one large (5 mm) box represents:

- A. 0.20 seconds **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: one large box = 0.20 s; one small box = 0.04 s.
- B. 0.04 seconds  
  _Rationale:_ That is one small box.
- C. 1.0 second  
  _Rationale:_ That is five large boxes.
- D. 0.5 seconds  
  _Rationale:_ That is 2.5 large boxes.

**MST-1781-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO features together best support sinus rhythm? (Select TWO.)

- A. A P wave before every QRS **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: sinus rhythm has a P before each QRS.
- B. A consistent, upright P wave in lead II **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: upright P in II reflects sinus-node origin.
- C. Absent P waves with an irregular baseline  
  _Rationale:_ That suggests atrial fibrillation.
- D. A rate fixed at exactly 60  
  _Rationale:_ Sinus rhythm spans a range, not a fixed rate.

**MST-1781-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A trace shows a shifting, fuzzy baseline with normal-looking QRS complexes buried in noise. The best first step is to:

- A. Check leads and repeat the recording to reduce artefact **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: resolve quality before interpreting.
- B. Diagnose ventricular fibrillation  
  _Rationale:_ That would over-read artefact.
- C. Report the rhythm as normal without review  
  _Rationale:_ Quality must be confirmed first.
- D. Increase the paper speed to 50 mm/s and re-measure rate  
  _Rationale:_ Speed change does not fix baseline artefact.
