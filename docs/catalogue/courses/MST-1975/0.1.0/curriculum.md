## Learning outcomes

1. Explain DNA structure, replication and the organisation of genomes
2. Describe transcription, translation and the central dogma of molecular biology
3. Apply Mendelian and non-Mendelian inheritance to predict genotype and phenotype
4. Explain how mutations arise and how they affect gene products
5. Describe gene regulation in prokaryotes and eukaryotes
6. Interpret pedigrees and simple crosses to reason about inheritance

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1975-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A single base substitution changes a codon so it specifies a different amino acid. This mutation is best described as:

- A. Missense **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a missense mutation changes one amino acid in the product.
- B. Silent  
  _Rationale:_ A silent mutation would not change the amino acid.
- C. Nonsense  
  _Rationale:_ A nonsense mutation creates a premature stop codon.
- D. Frameshift  
  _Rationale:_ A frameshift results from insertions or deletions not divisible by three.

**MST-1975-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO statements about DNA replication are correct? (Select TWO.)

- A. Each new double helix contains one parental and one newly made strand **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: replication is semi-conservative.
- B. DNA polymerase synthesises new strands in the 5' to 3' direction **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: nucleotides are added to the 3' end of the growing strand.
- C. Replication copies only one of the two strands  
  _Rationale:_ Both strands serve as templates.
- D. RNA primers are never involved  
  _Rationale:_ Primers are required to start synthesis.

**MST-1975-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Two heterozygous parents (Aa x Aa) are crossed for a simple dominant trait. What fraction of offspring is expected to show the recessive phenotype?

- A. 1/4 **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: only aa individuals (1 in 4) show the recessive phenotype.
- B. 1/2  
  _Rationale:_ Half are heterozygotes, which show the dominant phenotype.
- C. 3/4  
  _Rationale:_ Three-quarters show the dominant phenotype, not the recessive one.
- D. 0  
  _Rationale:_ aa offspring are expected at a frequency of 1/4.
