## Learning outcomes

1. Explain how corporate income tax is computed from accounting profit
2. Distinguish permanent and temporary differences
3. Apply the concept of taxable profit, allowances and reliefs in a jurisdiction-aware way
4. Account for current and deferred tax at a conceptual level
5. Recognise tax compliance obligations and the limits of generic guidance

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1052-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A permanent difference between accounting and taxable profit is one that:

- A. Never reverses in a future period **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: permanent differences (e.g. disallowed entertaining) do not reverse.
- B. Always reverses next year  
  _Rationale:_ That describes a temporary difference.
- C. Creates a deferred tax balance  
  _Rationale:_ Temporary, not permanent, differences create deferred tax.
- D. Only affects cash flow, never profit  
  _Rationale:_ Permanent differences affect the tax charge in the period.

**MST-1052-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO statements about deferred tax are correct? (Select TWO.)

- A. It arises from temporary differences between carrying and tax base amounts **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: deferred tax reflects temporary differences.
- B. It is an accounting recognition, not an immediate cash payment **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: deferred tax is a book provision, distinct from cash tax paid.
- C. It is the same as the current tax actually paid this year  
  _Rationale:_ Current tax, not deferred tax, is the amount payable now.
- D. It only ever appears for companies making losses  
  _Rationale:_ Deferred tax arises for profitable companies too.

**MST-1052-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Because tax rules vary by country, generic tax guidance should be treated as:

- A. A conceptual starting point that requires local professional advice to apply **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: jurisdiction-specific rules mean generic guidance is not a substitute for local advice.
- B. A complete filing solution for any country  
  _Rationale:_ Generic guidance cannot replace jurisdiction-specific rules.
- C. Legally binding everywhere  
  _Rationale:_ Tax law is jurisdiction-specific and not set by a course.
- D. Irrelevant to real filings  
  _Rationale:_ Concepts are useful, but local application is essential.
