## Learning outcomes

1. Control exposure using the aperture, shutter speed and ISO triangle
2. Choose focal length and aperture to shape depth of field and perspective
3. Compose photographs using framing, balance and point of view
4. Use natural and artificial light directionally and for quality
5. Manage focus, white balance and basic camera settings deliberately
6. Apply a non-destructive editing workflow to finish an image

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2429-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Opening the aperture from f/8 to f/2.8 (keeping exposure constant) will:

- A. Reduce depth of field, so less of the scene is in sharp focus **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: wider apertures (smaller f-number) shrink depth of field.
- B. Increase depth of field  
  _Rationale:_ Wider apertures reduce, not increase, depth of field.
- C. Freeze motion by itself  
  _Rationale:_ Motion is controlled by shutter speed.
- D. Change the white balance  
  _Rationale:_ Aperture does not set white balance.

**MST-2429-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO adjustments can each let you use a faster shutter speed in dim light? (Select TWO.)

- A. Opening the aperture to a smaller f-number **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a wider aperture admits more light, allowing a faster shutter.
- B. Raising the ISO **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: higher ISO increases sensitivity, allowing a faster shutter.
- C. Closing the aperture to f/22  
  _Rationale:_ That reduces light and forces a slower shutter.
- D. Lowering the ISO to its minimum  
  _Rationale:_ Lower ISO needs more light, not less.

**MST-2429-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) 'Quality of light' being described as 'soft' usually means the light is:

- A. Diffused, producing gradual shadow transitions and gentle contrast **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: soft light gives gradual, low-contrast shadow edges.
- B. Very bright and directional with hard-edged shadows  
  _Rationale:_ That describes hard light.
- C. Always coloured blue  
  _Rationale:_ Softness is about diffusion, not colour.
- D. Only possible with studio flash  
  _Rationale:_ Soft light occurs in nature too, e.g. overcast.
