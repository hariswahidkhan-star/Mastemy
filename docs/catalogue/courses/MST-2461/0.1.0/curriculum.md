## Learning outcomes

1. Describe a business model as an interlocking value, delivery and capture system
2. Map an existing business model and diagnose its weaknesses
3. Generate alternative models using patterns and customer-job analysis
4. Design value propositions aligned to customer jobs, pains and gains
5. Test business-model assumptions with low-cost experiments before scaling
6. Manage the transition and cannibalisation risk of a new model

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2461-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A team wants to launch a new subscription model immediately at full scale. What is the strongest business-model-innovation objection?

- A. The riskiest assumptions should be tested cheaply before committing to full scale **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: testing key assumptions first reduces the cost of being wrong.
- B. New models should never be subscription-based  
  _Rationale:_ Subscriptions can be excellent; the issue is testing.
- C. Scaling first always lowers unit cost enough to justify risk  
  _Rationale:_ Scale does not de-risk unvalidated assumptions.
- D. Business models cannot be tested in advance  
  _Rationale:_ They can be tested with cheap experiments.

**MST-2461-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO elements belong to the value-capture side of a business model? (Select TWO.)

- A. The revenue model and pricing mechanism **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: pricing and revenue model capture value.
- B. The cost structure that determines margin **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: cost structure governs how much captured value is retained.
- C. The onboarding tutorial design  
  _Rationale:_ Onboarding is delivery, not value capture.
- D. The brand's colour palette  
  _Rationale:_ Visual identity is not value capture.
- E. The warehouse layout  
  _Rationale:_ Operations layout is delivery, not capture.

**MST-2461-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) An experiment shows weak demand for a new offering, but the team reinterprets the data as positive and proceeds. What is the core failure?

- A. Confirmation bias is overriding honest reading of disconfirming evidence **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: ignoring disconfirming evidence defeats the purpose of testing.
- B. The experiment should have been larger before any decision  
  _Rationale:_ Size is secondary to interpreting the signal honestly.
- C. Experiments are not useful for business models  
  _Rationale:_ Experiments are central to model innovation.
- D. Weak demand always means the market is wrong  
  _Rationale:_ Blaming the market ignores the evidence.
