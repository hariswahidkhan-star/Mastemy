## Learning outcomes

1. Describe the environmental foundations of early civilisations in Mesopotamia, Egypt, the Indus and China
2. Explain how writing, law and administration developed to manage complex societies
3. Analyse religion, kingship and monumental architecture as expressions of political power
4. Compare the classical Mediterranean worlds of Greece and Rome
5. Explain economic, social and gender structures in selected ancient societies
6. Evaluate how archaeologists and historians reconstruct the ancient past from fragmentary evidence

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2100-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why is the claim 'Hammurabi's code tells us how ordinary Babylonians actually lived' unsafe?

- A. Law codes often state ideals and penalties, not the frequency or reality of everyday behaviour **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: prescriptive sources show what authorities wanted, which may differ from practice.
- B. Because the code is a forgery  
  _Rationale:_ Its authenticity is not in question; the issue is how to read a legal source.
- C. Because Babylonians could not read  
  _Rationale:_ Literacy levels are not the point; prescription versus practice is.
- D. Because the code only covers kings  
  _Rationale:_ It covers many social ranks; the caution is about ideals versus reality.

**MST-2100-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO features make the Indus Valley cities distinctive among early civilisations? (Select TWO.)

- A. Standardised urban planning with grid streets and drainage **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: uniform planning and sanitation are hallmarks of Indus cities.
- B. A script that remains undeciphered **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the Indus script has not been read, limiting textual history.
- C. Large pyramidal royal tombs  
  _Rationale:_ Monumental royal tombs are characteristic of Egypt, not the Indus.
- D. A surviving epic literature in translation  
  _Rationale:_ No readable Indus literature survives.

**MST-2100-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A historian relies only on Roman senatorial writers to describe the empire's enslaved population. What is the main problem?

- A. The sources are elite and external to the group described, so they are partial and biased **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: perspective and survival bias shape what elite texts record about subordinated people.
- B. The writers lived too early to know  
  _Rationale:_ They were contemporaries; the issue is whose viewpoint survives.
- C. Latin cannot describe social class  
  _Rationale:_ Language is not the barrier; representation is.
- D. Enslaved people left no archaeological trace  
  _Rationale:_ Material evidence exists and can supplement texts.
