## Learning outcomes

1. Identify the complete set of financial statements and the overall presentation requirements
2. Apply the concepts of going concern, accrual basis and materiality to presentation
3. Distinguish current and non-current classification in the statement of financial position
4. Present profit or loss and other comprehensive income appropriately
5. Explain the role and content of the notes, including accounting policies
6. Apply the requirements for comparatives, consistency and offsetting

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2517-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Which of the following is part of a complete set of financial statements?

- A. A statement of cash flows for the period **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a statement of cash flows is one of the required statements.
- B. A five-year share-price chart  
  _Rationale:_ A share-price chart is not a required financial statement.
- C. The chief executive's biography  
  _Rationale:_ Management biographies are not financial statements.
- D. A list of the entity's customers  
  _Rationale:_ A customer list is not a required statement.

**MST-2517-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO underlying concepts does IAS 1 require in preparing financial statements? (Select TWO.)

- A. The accrual basis of accounting **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: financial statements (other than cash-flow information) are prepared on the accrual basis.
- B. The going concern basis, unless the entity intends or needs to cease trading **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: statements are prepared on a going concern basis unless that is inappropriate.
- C. The cash basis of accounting for all items  
  _Rationale:_ The accrual basis, not the cash basis, is required.
- D. Recognising income only when dividends are declared  
  _Rationale:_ Income recognition follows accrual principles, not dividend declaration.

**MST-2517-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A company presents a single-line 'other income' that hides a large, unusual gain. Why can this breach presentation requirements?

- A. Material items should be disclosed separately so users can understand performance; aggregating a material item can impair fair presentation **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: materiality requires separate presentation of significant items.
- B. All income must be presented on one line  
  _Rationale:_ That is the opposite of the requirement.
- C. Gains are never allowed to be presented  
  _Rationale:_ Gains are presented; the issue is hiding a material one.
- D. Only losses need to be disclosed separately  
  _Rationale:_ Both material gains and losses warrant separate disclosure.
