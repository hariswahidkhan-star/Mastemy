## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1091-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) The riskiest-assumption test should target the belief that:

- A. Is easiest and cheapest to confirm  
  _Rationale:_ Ease is not the criterion; risk to the whole idea is.
- B. Would most damage the idea if it turned out to be false **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: testing the most dangerous assumption first saves wasted effort.
- C. The founder is most confident about  
  _Rationale:_ High confidence does not mean high risk to test.
- D. Investors find most interesting  
  _Rationale:_ Investor interest is not the basis for choosing the first test.

**MST-1091-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) A customer interview question is 'leading' when it:

- A. Asks about a past behaviour in detail  
  _Rationale:_ Asking about real past behaviour is good practice.
- B. Signals the answer the founder hopes to hear **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: leading questions nudge the interviewee toward a desired answer.
- C. Is open-ended and neutral  
  _Rationale:_ Open, neutral questions are the opposite of leading.
- D. Explores the customer's current workaround  
  _Rationale:_ Exploring workarounds reveals real needs; it is not leading.

**MST-1091-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO are signs of a 'vanity metric' rather than real traction? (Select TWO)

- A. A number that always goes up and never informs a decision **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a metric that cannot change a decision adds no learning.
- B. Total registered sign-ups with no active use **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: sign-ups without activation can overstate demand.
- C. Repeat purchases by the same customers  
  _Rationale:_ Repeat purchase is a meaningful sign of value.
- D. Week-on-week retention of active users  
  _Rationale:_ Retention is a strong, decision-relevant metric.
