## Learning outcomes

1. Describe common energy stores such as kinetic, gravitational, elastic and chemical
2. Explain that energy can be transferred and is conserved overall
3. Relate the speed and mass of an object to its kinetic energy
4. Describe how height gives an object gravitational potential energy
5. Explain simply how friction transfers energy to the surroundings
6. Apply safe practice when investigating moving objects

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance. Any experiment ideas are optional and require adult supervision; they are not assessed.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2850-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A ball is dropped from a shelf. Which energy change mainly happens as it falls?

- A. Gravitational potential energy transfers to kinetic energy **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: as it falls it loses height energy and gains movement energy.
- B. Kinetic energy turns into height energy  
  _Rationale:_ That is what happens going up, not falling down.
- C. Energy is created as it falls  
  _Rationale:_ Energy is transferred, not created.
- D. No energy change occurs  
  _Rationale:_ There is a clear transfer from height to movement energy.

**MST-2850-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO things increase a moving trolley's kinetic energy? (Select TWO.)

- A. Making it move faster **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: greater speed means more kinetic energy.
- B. Adding more mass to it **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: greater mass means more kinetic energy at the same speed.
- C. Painting it a brighter colour  
  _Rationale:_ Colour does not affect kinetic energy.
- D. Stopping it completely  
  _Rationale:_ A stopped trolley has no kinetic energy.

**MST-2850-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A toy car rolling on carpet slows down and stops. Where did its movement energy go?

- A. It was transferred to heat and sound by friction **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: friction transfers the kinetic energy to the surroundings as heat and sound.
- B. It was destroyed  
  _Rationale:_ Energy is not destroyed, only transferred.
- C. It turned into extra mass  
  _Rationale:_ The car's mass does not change.
- D. It disappeared into nothing  
  _Rationale:_ The energy spreads to the surroundings; it is conserved.
