## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-0087-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) The fundamental insurance ratemaking equation states that the rate should cover:

- A. Expected losses, expenses and a provision for profit/contingencies **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: rates fund losses, expenses and a profit/contingency load.
- B. Only paid losses from last month  
  _Rationale:_ Rates are prospective and must cover expenses and profit too.
- C. Investment income alone  
  _Rationale:_ Investment income is one consideration, not the whole rate.
- D. The competitor’s advertised price  
  _Rationale:_ Competitor pricing is a business factor, not the actuarial equation.

**MST-0087-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) An exposure base for ratemaking should ideally be:

- A. Proportional to expected loss and practical to measure **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a good exposure base varies with risk and is easy to administer.
- B. Unrelated to the risk being insured  
  _Rationale:_ An unrelated base fails to track loss potential.
- C. Impossible to audit  
  _Rationale:_ Auditability is a desirable, not undesirable, property.
- D. Fixed at 1 for every policy  
  _Rationale:_ A constant base cannot differentiate risk.

**MST-0087-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Select TWO methods commonly used to estimate unpaid claim liabilities.

- A. The chain-ladder (loss development) method **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: chain-ladder develops losses to ultimate using development factors.
- B. The Bornhuetter-Ferguson method **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: Bornhuetter-Ferguson blends expected losses with reported development.
- C. Choosing the reserve that matches the budget target  
  _Rationale:_ Reserves are estimated from data, not set to hit a budget.
- D. Using the CEO’s preferred round number  
  _Rationale:_ Reserve estimation is actuarial, not arbitrary.
- E. Ignoring development of recent accident years  
  _Rationale:_ Recent years need development; ignoring it understates reserves.
