## Learning outcomes

1. Explain why and when a business or asset is valued
2. Describe the main valuation approaches and when each is appropriate
3. Apply simple multiples-based valuation and interpret the result
4. Outline discounted cash flow mechanics and the role of the discount rate
5. Identify the key assumptions and sensitivities that drive a valuation
6. Communicate a valuation range and its limitations honestly

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Integrative case

A manager evaluating a small bolt-on acquisition must triangulate a defensible value using multiples and a simple DCF, then present a range and its key sensitivities to the board. This is general business education, not investment advice.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2489-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why do a market multiple and a DCF often give different values for the same business?

- A. They rest on different assumptions, so each is a cross-check rather than a single truth **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: triangulating methods is the point; divergence prompts scrutiny.
- B. One method is always wrong by definition  
  _Rationale:_ Neither is inherently wrong.
- C. Multiples ignore the industry entirely  
  _Rationale:_ Multiples are drawn from industry peers.
- D. A DCF never uses assumptions  
  _Rationale:_ A DCF is highly assumption-driven.

**MST-2489-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO factors increase a discounted cash flow valuation, all else equal? (Select TWO.)

- A. A lower discount rate **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a lower rate raises present value.
- B. Higher expected future cash flows **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: larger cash flows raise the valuation.
- C. A higher discount rate  
  _Rationale:_ A higher rate lowers present value.
- D. Lower expected growth in cash flows  
  _Rationale:_ Lower growth reduces the valuation.

**MST-2489-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A valuation is presented as a single exact figure to the board. The main risk is that it:

- A. Implies false precision and hides the assumptions and sensitivities behind it **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a range with drivers is more honest than a point estimate.
- B. Is illegal to present without an auditor  
  _Rationale:_ That is not the core issue here.
- C. Must always be rounded to the nearest million  
  _Rationale:_ Rounding convention is not the risk.
- D. Cannot be based on cash flows  
  _Rationale:_ Valuations are commonly based on cash flows.
