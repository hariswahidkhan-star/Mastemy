## Learning outcomes

1. Capture meeting outcomes into a structured set of commitments
2. Use Copilot to draft notes and actions without distorting what was agreed
3. Turn agreed actions into Planner tasks with owners and dates
4. Drive follow-through with Outlook communication and reminders
5. Operate the meeting-to-delivery loop with review and accountability

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Selection items cannot demonstrate production coding/operational ability; that is taught through instructor-built projects and walkthroughs.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-0786-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Copilot's summary lists an action item that no one actually agreed to in the meeting. What should you do?

- A. Remove it and keep the record to agreed commitments only **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the record must reflect what was agreed, not what the model inferred.
- B. Keep it because it sounds like a sensible task  
  _Rationale:_ Plausibility does not make it an agreed commitment.
- C. Assign it to whoever spoke last  
  _Rationale:_ Guessing an owner compounds the fabricated action.
- D. Leave it unassigned in the notes  
  _Rationale:_ An unagreed action does not belong in the record at all.

**MST-0786-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO attributes must every Planner task carry to support accountability? (Select TWO.)

- A. A single named owner **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a named owner is who is accountable for delivery.
- B. A due date **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a due date makes progress and lateness visible.
- C. A colour label matching the meeting theme  
  _Rationale:_ Colour is cosmetic and does not drive accountability.
- D. A copy of the full transcript  
  _Rationale:_ Attaching the transcript does not make the task actionable.

**MST-0786-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) When is a reminder before a due date proportionate?

- A. Timed to give the owner room to act, not daily nagging **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a well-timed reminder supports delivery without noise.
- B. Sent every day from the moment the task is created  
  _Rationale:_ Daily nagging trains people to ignore reminders.
- C. Only after the task is already overdue  
  _Rationale:_ A reminder only after lateness defeats its purpose.
- D. Sent to the whole team for each individual task  
  _Rationale:_ Broadcasting every reminder creates noise and dilutes ownership.
