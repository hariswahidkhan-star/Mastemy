## Learning outcomes

1. Explain how managerial accounting differs from financial accounting and supports internal decisions
2. Classify and analyse costs by behaviour, traceability and relevance
3. Apply cost-volume-profit and contribution analysis to short-term decisions
4. Build and interpret budgets and variance analysis
5. Use costing and performance information to support planning and control

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Integrative case

A regional food manufacturer's plant controller must advise on whether to accept a large discounted special order while a product line is underperforming. Classify the relevant and sunk costs, run a contribution and break-even analysis, flag the capacity constraint, and present an accept/reject recommendation with the risks named.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1697-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A machine bought last year for a now-cancelled project could be used for a new order. For the order decision, how should its original cost be treated?

- A. As a sunk cost that is irrelevant to the decision **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: past, unavoidable costs do not change with the decision.
- B. As a variable cost of the new order  
  _Rationale:_ The original purchase does not vary with the new order's volume.
- C. As an opportunity cost of accepting the order  
  _Rationale:_ Opportunity cost is the forgone alternative use, not the original purchase price.
- D. As a relevant fixed cost to recover in pricing  
  _Rationale:_ Recovering a sunk cost is not a reason to accept or reject the order.

**MST-1697-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) A product sells for 50, with variable cost 30 and total fixed costs of 40,000. Which TWO statements are correct? (Select TWO.)

- A. The contribution margin per unit is 20 **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: price 50 minus variable cost 30 equals 20.
- B. Break-even volume is 2,000 units **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: 40,000 fixed / 20 contribution = 2,000 units.
- C. The contribution margin per unit is 50  
  _Rationale:_ 50 is the selling price, not the contribution margin.
- D. Break-even volume is 800 units  
  _Rationale:_ That would require a contribution margin of 50, ignoring variable cost.

**MST-1697-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A flexible budget is most useful because it:

- A. Restates budgeted costs at the actual activity level for fair comparison **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: it isolates efficiency from volume effects.
- B. Guarantees costs will not exceed the original plan  
  _Rationale:_ It does not control spending; it adjusts the benchmark.
- C. Replaces the need for variance analysis  
  _Rationale:_ It is the basis for cleaner variance analysis, not a replacement.
- D. Fixes the budget regardless of output  
  _Rationale:_ That describes a static budget, not a flexible one.
