## Learning outcomes

1. Describe common AI governance operating models (centralised, federated, hub-and-spoke) and their trade-offs
2. Design accountability roles and decision rights for AI across an organisation
3. Write clear, enforceable AI policies and standards distinct from aspirational principles
4. Design intake, triage and approval workflows for AI use cases
5. Define governance metrics and reporting to leadership and the board
6. Plan the change management needed for governance to be adopted in practice

- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1883-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A company publishes a one-page 'AI Principles' document and calls it its AI policy. What is the gap?

- A. Principles state intent; a policy must set enforceable, testable requirements and consequences **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: enforceable policy differs from aspirational principles.
- B. The document should have been longer  
  _Rationale:_ Length is not the issue; enforceability is.
- C. Principles must be approved by regulators  
  _Rationale:_ Internal policy does not require regulator approval.
- D. Nothing, principles and policy are the same  
  _Rationale:_ They serve different functions.

**MST-1883-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO are strengths of a federated (business-unit-led) AI governance model? (Select TWO.)

- A. Decisions sit closer to domain context and move faster **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: proximity to context and speed are federated strengths.
- B. Business units own and are accountable for their AI risk **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: distributed ownership is a federated strength.
- C. It guarantees fully consistent standards everywhere  
  _Rationale:_ Consistency is a weakness, not a strength, of federation.
- D. It removes the need for any central policy  
  _Rationale:_ Federated models still need shared central policy.

**MST-1883-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Leadership wants one governance metric for the board. Which is most useful?

- A. Share of deployed AI use cases that passed the required review, with exceptions tracked **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: it shows governance coverage and surfaces exceptions.
- B. Total number of AI models built  
  _Rationale:_ A raw count says nothing about governance.
- C. Average model accuracy across the firm  
  _Rationale:_ Accuracy is not a governance coverage measure.
- D. Number of AI vendors contacted  
  _Rationale:_ Vendor contacts do not reflect governance.
