## Learning outcomes

1. Define current, potential difference and resistance with their units
2. Apply V = IR to series and parallel circuit problems
3. Compare how current and voltage behave in series and parallel circuits
4. Interpret and draw standard circuit diagrams using correct symbols
5. Explain energy transfer in a circuit and the role of resistance
6. Follow electrical safety: use only low-voltage cells, never mains

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance. Any experiment ideas are optional and require adult supervision; they are not assessed.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2855-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A component has 6 V across it and 2 A flowing through it. What is its resistance?

- A. 3 ohms **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: R = V/I = 6 V / 2 A = 3 ohms.
- B. 12 ohms  
  _Rationale:_ That multiplies V and I; resistance is V divided by I, giving 3 ohms.
- C. 8 ohms  
  _Rationale:_ That adds V and I; use R = V/I to get 3 ohms.
- D. 0.33 ohms  
  _Rationale:_ That is I/V; resistance is V/I = 3 ohms.

**MST-2855-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO statements about a simple SERIES circuit are correct? (Select TWO.)

- A. The current is the same at every point in the circuit **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: series current is the same throughout.
- B. The total resistance is the sum of the individual resistances **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: series resistances add together.
- C. Each component gets the full supply voltage  
  _Rationale:_ In series the voltage is shared between components.
- D. Removing one component leaves the others working  
  _Rationale:_ A break in series stops all current.

**MST-2855-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why must you never use a mains wall socket for a classroom electricity experiment?

- A. Mains voltage is high enough to cause serious or fatal electric shock **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: mains electricity is dangerous; only low-voltage cells should be used.
- B. Mains electricity does not obey Ohm's law  
  _Rationale:_ Ohm's law still applies; the reason is the danger of high voltage.
- C. Sockets do not provide enough current  
  _Rationale:_ The issue is danger, not lack of current.
- D. Batteries give a higher voltage than mains  
  _Rationale:_ Classroom cells are far lower voltage than mains.
