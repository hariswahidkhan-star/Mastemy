## Learning outcomes

1. Explain pharmacokinetic processes of absorption, distribution, metabolism and excretion
2. Explain pharmacodynamic principles of receptors, agonists, antagonists and dose-response
3. Describe major drug classes and their mechanisms, indications and common adverse effects
4. Apply principles of safe prescribing, interactions and medication administration

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every course outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Hands-on operation, the quality of tools and live outputs, and professional judgement are taught by video demonstration and model-answer analysis; the certificate assessment uses single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only and never requires uploads, recorded work, submitted code or peer review.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1765-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A drug has a half-life of 6 hours. Approximately how long until it reaches steady state on regular dosing?

- A. About 24-30 hours (4-5 half-lives) **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: steady state is reached after roughly four to five half-lives, so about 24-30 hours.
- B. About 6 hours (one half-life)  
  _Rationale:_ One half-life removes only about half the drug; steady state needs several half-lives.
- C. About 72 hours regardless of half-life  
  _Rationale:_ Time to steady state depends on half-life, not a fixed 72 hours.
- D. Immediately after the first dose  
  _Rationale:_ A single dose cannot establish steady state during repeated dosing.**MST-1765-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) A competitive antagonist is given with an agonist. What is the characteristic effect on the agonist dose-response curve?

- A. It shifts the curve to the right, and can be overcome by more agonist **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: competitive antagonism is surmountable, shifting the curve rightward without lowering the maximum.
- B. It lowers the maximum response and cannot be overcome  
  _Rationale:_ That describes a non-competitive (irreversible) antagonist, not a competitive one.
- C. It increases the agonist's potency  
  _Rationale:_ An antagonist reduces apparent potency; it does not increase it.
- D. It has no effect on the agonist  
  _Rationale:_ A competitive antagonist clearly reduces the agonist effect at a given dose.**MST-1765-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO factors would be expected to raise the plasma concentration of a renally cleared drug? (Select TWO)

- A. Reduced kidney function **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: impaired renal clearance reduces elimination and raises plasma levels.
- B. Displacement from plasma protein binding **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: displacement increases free drug in plasma.
- C. Induction of metabolising enzymes  
  _Rationale:_ Enzyme induction speeds metabolism and lowers concentration.
- D. Increased volume of distribution  
  _Rationale:_ A larger volume of distribution tends to lower plasma concentration.
