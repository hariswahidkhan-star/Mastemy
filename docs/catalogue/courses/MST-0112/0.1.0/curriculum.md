## Learning outcomes

1. Describe building and governing a compliance program across multiple jurisdictions
2. Apply global anti-corruption, trade and data requirements to operations
3. Evaluate third-party and cross-border risk and due diligence
4. Analyse harmonisation of a global program with conflicting local requirements

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-0112-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A global company's headquarters policy permits a business gift value that exceeds the stricter limit set by local law in one country. Which approach is correct?

- A. Apply the stricter local limit for activity in that country **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a global program must meet the stricter applicable local requirement, not override it.
- B. Apply the headquarters limit everywhere for consistency  
  _Rationale:_ Uniformity cannot override stricter local law.
- C. Ignore local law because headquarters sets global policy  
  _Rationale:_ Local law applies to local conduct.
- D. Let each employee choose which limit to follow  
  _Rationale:_ Discretionary choice of standard is not compliant.

**MST-0112-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why can anti-corruption obligations reach conduct outside a company's home country?

- A. Several major anti-corruption laws have extraterritorial reach over covered entities and persons **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: laws such as broad anti-bribery statutes can reach covered conduct abroad.
- B. Bribery abroad is always legal  
  _Rationale:_ That is false and the opposite of the rule.
- C. Only the host country's law ever applies  
  _Rationale:_ Home-country and other laws can also apply.
- D. Extraterritorial reach applies only to individuals, never companies  
  _Rationale:_ Companies are frequently covered.

**MST-0112-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Setting risk-based third-party due diligence for international agents, which TWO factors most raise the diligence tier? (Select TWO)

- A. Operating in a market with high perceived corruption risk **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: country corruption risk elevates diligence requirements.
- B. Interacting with government officials on the company's behalf **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: government-facing agents raise bribery exposure and diligence needs.
- C. Being a long-standing domestic office-supplies vendor  
  _Rationale:_ A low-risk domestic vendor does not warrant the highest tier.
- D. Having a publicly listed parent in a low-risk market  
  _Rationale:_ This tends to lower, not raise, risk.
- E. Supplying generic commodity goods with no official contact  
  _Rationale:_ Low official contact and commodity goods is lower risk.
