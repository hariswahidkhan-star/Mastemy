## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-0423-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A student spends an hour studying economics instead of working a job that pays $15. The opportunity cost of that hour of studying is best described as:

- A. The $15 of forgone wages (the value of the next-best alternative) **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: opportunity cost is the value of the best alternative given up.
- B. Zero, because studying is free  
  _Rationale:_ Studying has an opportunity cost equal to what was given up.
- C. The total of all possible alternatives combined  
  _Rationale:_ Opportunity cost is the single next-best alternative, not the sum of all.
- D. The money spent on tuition that semester  
  _Rationale:_ Sunk tuition is not the forgone alternative for this hour.

**MST-0423-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) Gross domestic product (GDP) is best defined as the market value of:

- A. All final goods and services produced within a country in a period **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: GDP counts final output produced domestically in a period.
- B. All goods ever produced in the country's history  
  _Rationale:_ GDP measures a single period, not cumulative history.
- C. Only exported goods  
  _Rationale:_ GDP includes domestic production, not only exports.
- D. Intermediate goods counted at every stage  
  _Rationale:_ Counting intermediate goods would double-count; GDP uses final goods.

**MST-0423-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO actions are expansionary fiscal policy?

- A. Increasing government spending **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: higher government spending raises aggregate demand.
- B. Cutting taxes to raise disposable income **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: lower taxes raise spending and aggregate demand.
- C. Raising the central bank's policy interest rate  
  _Rationale:_ That is contractionary monetary policy, not fiscal policy.
- D. Reducing government spending to balance the budget  
  _Rationale:_ Cutting spending is contractionary fiscal policy.
- E. Selling government bonds through open-market operations  
  _Rationale:_ That is a monetary-policy tool, not fiscal policy.
