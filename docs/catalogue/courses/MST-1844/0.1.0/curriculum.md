## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1844-Q0001** (single-answer-mcq, Select ONE) The hiragana は, when used as a topic-marking particle, is pronounced:

- A. 'wa' **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: as a topic particle, は is read 'wa' rather than 'ha'.
- B. 'ha'  
  _Rationale:_ 'ha' is the syllabic reading, but as the topic particle it is 'wa'.
- C. 'ba'  
  _Rationale:_ 'ba' is ば, a different kana with a dakuten.
- D. 'pa'  
  _Rationale:_ 'pa' is ぱ, with a handakuten, not は.

**MST-1844-Q0002** (single-answer-mcq, Select ONE) Choose the correct particle: 'わたし＿がくせいです。' (I am a student.)

- A. は **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: は marks 'わたし' as the topic of the sentence.
- B. を  
  _Rationale:_ を marks a direct object, which this is not.
- C. に  
  _Rationale:_ に marks a destination or time, not the topic here.
- D. へ  
  _Rationale:_ へ marks direction of movement, not a topic.

**MST-1844-Q0003** (multiple-answer-selection, Select TWO) Which TWO are hiragana (not katakana)?

- A. あ **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: あ is a hiragana character.
- B. き **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: き is a hiragana character.
- C. ア  
  _Rationale:_ ア is the katakana 'a'.
- D. カ  
  _Rationale:_ カ is the katakana 'ka'.
- E. 漢  
  _Rationale:_ 漢 is a kanji, not kana.
