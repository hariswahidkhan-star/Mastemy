## Learning outcomes

1. Describe the ancient and late-antique foundations of the Middle East
2. Explain the rise of Islam and the early caliphates
3. Analyse the great Islamic empires and their societies
4. Evaluate the impact of Ottoman rule and its long decline
5. Explain European imperialism, mandates and the emergence of modern states
6. Assess the major political and social dynamics of the region since 1945

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2103-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why is it inaccurate to say the Middle East was 'religiously uniform' before the rise of Islam?

- A. It contained Zoroastrian, Jewish, varied Christian and other communities across competing empires **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the late-antique region was religiously diverse and politically divided.
- B. Because no religions existed yet  
  _Rationale:_ Several established traditions were present.
- C. Because everyone was already Muslim  
  _Rationale:_ Islam emerged in the 7th century; this is anachronistic.
- D. Because records of religion do not survive  
  _Rationale:_ Abundant evidence of diverse communities survives.

**MST-2103-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO are associated with the intellectual flourishing under the early Abbasid caliphate? (Select TWO.)

- A. A translation movement rendering Greek and other works into Arabic **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: large-scale translation supported scholarship.
- B. Advances in fields such as mathematics, astronomy and medicine **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: these fields saw notable development.
- C. The invention of the printing press  
  _Rationale:_ Movable-type printing is a later European development.
- D. The construction of the Suez Canal  
  _Rationale:_ The canal is a 19th-century project.

**MST-2103-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A student writes that the Ottoman Empire 'declined steadily and inevitably from its peak'. Why do historians challenge this?

- A. The empire adapted and reformed over centuries; 'decline' oversimplifies a complex, uneven history **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the linear-decline model ignores adaptation, reform and regional variation.
- B. Because the empire never weakened at all  
  _Rationale:_ It faced real pressures; the issue is the simplistic narrative.
- C. Because it had no peak  
  _Rationale:_ It had powerful periods; the caution is about the framing of change.
- D. Because it still exists today  
  _Rationale:_ It ended after the First World War.
