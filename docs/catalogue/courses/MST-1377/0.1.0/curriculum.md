## Learning outcomes

1. Identify effective uses of AI in teaching and course design
2. Use AI to create and adapt learning materials responsibly
3. Support assessment and feedback while protecting integrity
4. Recognise accuracy, bias and equity risks in educational AI
5. Set classroom policies for responsible, transparent AI use

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Integrative case

A teacher wants to use AI to plan lessons, differentiate materials and give feedback for a mixed-ability class. Decide where AI genuinely helps learning, where it risks integrity or equity, and write a classroom AI policy that is fair and transparent.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1377-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A teacher runs student work through an AI-detection tool that flags an essay. What is the most responsible response?

- A. Treat the flag as one uncertain signal, not proof, and investigate fairly **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: AI detectors are unreliable and can produce false positives.
- B. Automatically fail the student based on the flag  
  _Rationale:_ Acting on an unreliable flag alone is unfair.
- C. Assume detectors are always correct  
  _Rationale:_ Detectors have significant error rates.
- D. Ignore all integrity concerns entirely  
  _Rationale:_ Integrity still matters; the issue is how to assess it fairly.

**MST-1377-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO practices help keep AI use in a classroom equitable? (Select TWO.)

- A. Provide an alternative for students without AI access **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: alternatives prevent access gaps from harming learners.
- B. Check AI materials for bias before sharing them **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: reviewing for bias protects fairness.
- C. Require every student to buy a paid AI subscription  
  _Rationale:_ Mandating paid tools creates an equity barrier.
- D. Assume all AI output is culturally neutral  
  _Rationale:_ AI output can carry bias and gaps.

**MST-1377-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) An AI tool writes a history explanation for a lesson. Before teaching it, the educator should mainly:

- A. Verify the facts and framing for accuracy and balance **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: AI can introduce factual errors or skewed framing.
- B. Teach it verbatim to save time  
  _Rationale:_ Unchecked content risks spreading errors.
- C. Assume historical facts are always correct in AI output  
  _Rationale:_ AI can misstate historical facts.
- D. Add more detail without checking any of it  
  _Rationale:_ More unchecked detail compounds the risk.
