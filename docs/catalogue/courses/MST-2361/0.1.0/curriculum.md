## Learning outcomes

1. Explain how wind turbines convert kinetic energy to electricity and the role of the power curve
2. Describe the main turbine components and onshore vs offshore configurations
3. Assess a wind resource from wind-speed distributions and the cube law
4. Interpret capacity factor, cut-in and cut-out speeds and the Betz limit
5. Outline wind-farm layout, wake effects and basic siting constraints
6. Identify grid-integration and environmental considerations for wind projects

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2361-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why does wind power available to a turbine rise with the cube of wind speed?

- A. Kinetic energy flux scales with velocity cubed, so small speed gains yield large power gains **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Power in the wind is proportional to air density, area and the cube of wind speed.
- B. Because turbine blades double in length as wind rises  
  _Rationale:_ Blade length is fixed; it does not change with wind speed.
- C. Because air density triples in strong wind  
  _Rationale:_ Air density changes little; the cube law comes from velocity.
- D. Because the generator adds energy proportional to speed  
  _Rationale:_ The generator converts, it does not add, energy.

**MST-2361-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO are correct about a turbine's cut-in and cut-out speeds? (Select TWO.)

- A. Below cut-in speed the turbine produces no useful power **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ There is too little energy to overcome losses below cut-in.
- B. Above cut-out speed the turbine shuts down to protect itself **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Cut-out protects the machine from excessive loads in high winds.
- C. Between cut-in and cut-out the turbine always runs at nameplate power  
  _Rationale:_ Output follows the power curve and only reaches rated power above rated speed.
- D. Cut-out speed is where efficiency first reaches the Betz limit  
  _Rationale:_ Cut-out is a protection threshold, unrelated to the Betz limit.

**MST-2361-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) What does the Betz limit tell us about a wind turbine?

- A. No turbine can extract more than about 59% of the kinetic energy in the wind **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ The Betz limit caps the theoretical fraction of wind energy any rotor can capture.
- B. Turbines can capture 100% of the wind's energy with perfect blades  
  _Rationale:_ That violates the Betz limit.
- C. Offshore turbines are exempt from the limit  
  _Rationale:_ The Betz limit applies regardless of location.
- D. It sets the maximum legal tip speed  
  _Rationale:_ It is a physical efficiency bound, not a regulation.
