## Learning outcomes

1. Map the main areas of AI and locate your own goals within them
2. Identify the foundational skills and mindset needed to begin learning AI
3. Explain the core concepts of data, models, training and evaluation
4. Build skills through structured practice and small, evaluated projects
5. Choose a specialisation and design a realistic learning path
6. Follow and assess frontier developments honestly and sustainably

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2675-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A beginner insists they cannot start learning AI until they have mastered advanced mathematics. Why is this a weak starting assumption?

- A. Much useful AI learning and tool use starts well before advanced maths; depth can be built as goals require **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a goal-driven path lets you learn maths as needed rather than front-loading all of it.
- B. Maths is never useful for AI  
  _Rationale:_ Overstated: maths matters for depth; it just need not block the start.
- C. Only people with degrees can learn AI  
  _Rationale:_ Inaccurate: self-directed learning is well-supported.
- D. Advanced maths guarantees AI expertise by itself  
  _Rationale:_ Inaccurate: practice and application are also needed.

**MST-2675-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO practices make a self-directed AI learning plan more effective? (Select TWO.)

- A. Building small projects and evaluating the results, not just consuming material **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: applied, evaluated practice builds durable skill.
- B. Tracing bold claims back to primary sources before trusting them **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: source-checking keeps your knowledge honest in a hype-heavy field.
- C. Memorising every new model name as it is announced  
  _Rationale:_ Low value: names change fast and rarely build skill.
- D. Avoiding all projects until you have finished every course  
  _Rationale:_ Counterproductive: practice should start early.

**MST-2675-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why should a learner treat a single tutorial as a starting point rather than the current state of the field?

- A. AI moves quickly, so any one resource can be partial or out of date and should be cross-checked **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: breadth and recency require consulting multiple, current sources.
- B. Tutorials are always wrong  
  _Rationale:_ Overstated: tutorials are useful but limited in scope and freshness.
- C. Only video tutorials are reliable  
  _Rationale:_ Inaccurate: format does not determine currency or accuracy.
- D. The newest tutorial is always complete  
  _Rationale:_ Inaccurate: recency does not guarantee completeness.
