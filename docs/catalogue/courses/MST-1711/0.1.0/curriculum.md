## Learning outcomes

1. What banks do
2. Deposits and lending
3. The balance sheet and capital
4. Risk and liquidity
5. Regulation and payments

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess operation of a real banking system or a regulated lending decision for a specific customer.

## Integrative case

Examine a small retail bank: trace how its deposits fund loans, compute its interest spread, place items on its balance sheet, identify the liquidity risk from borrowing short and lending long, and explain how capital and deposit insurance protect the system.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1711-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Banks earn their core income primarily from:

- A. The spread between interest earned on loans and paid on deposits **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: net interest spread is the core banking income.
- B. Charging customers to store their exact banknotes  
  _Rationale:_ Banks lend deposits out; they do not simply store cash.
- C. Selling shares to depositors  
  _Rationale:_ That is not the core income source.
- D. Government salary payments  
  _Rationale:_ Banks are not paid a government salary for operating.

**MST-1711-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select ALL that apply) Which two factors make a bank vulnerable to a liquidity crisis? (Select TWO) (Select TWO)

- A. Borrowing short-term while lending long-term **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: maturity mismatch is a core liquidity vulnerability.
- B. A sudden loss of depositor confidence **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: mass withdrawals can drain liquidity quickly.
- C. Holding a large buffer of cash and liquid assets  
  _Rationale:_ That reduces, not raises, liquidity risk.
- D. Having strong long-term profitability  
  _Rationale:_ Profit does not prevent a short-term liquidity squeeze, but it is not the vulnerability itself.

**MST-1711-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Deposit insurance primarily exists to:

- A. Reduce the incentive for depositors to run on a bank **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: insuring deposits calms depositors and curbs runs.
- B. Guarantee the bank will always be profitable  
  _Rationale:_ It protects depositors, not bank profits.
- C. Cover unlimited amounts for every account  
  _Rationale:_ Coverage is typically capped per depositor.
- D. Replace the need for bank capital  
  _Rationale:_ Capital and insurance are complementary, not substitutes.
