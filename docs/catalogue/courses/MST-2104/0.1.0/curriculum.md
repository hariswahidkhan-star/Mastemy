## Learning outcomes

1. Describe the ancient foundations of South Asian civilisation
2. Explain the classical empires and the development of major religious traditions
3. Analyse the medieval sultanates and the Mughal Empire
4. Evaluate the rise of British colonial rule and its economic and social effects
5. Explain nationalist movements and the path to independence and partition
6. Assess the major developments in the region since 1947

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2104-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why do historians avoid describing ancient South Asia as the story of a single religion or people?

- A. The region was home to many languages, communities and evolving traditions interacting over time **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: diversity and change, not a single lineage, characterise its history.
- B. Because nothing is known about the period  
  _Rationale:_ Much is known from varied evidence.
- C. Because only one empire ever existed  
  _Rationale:_ Many states and empires rose and fell.
- D. Because written sources do not exist  
  _Rationale:_ Extensive textual and material sources exist.

**MST-2104-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO features characterised Mughal imperial administration at its height? (Select TWO.)

- A. A structured land-revenue system supporting the state **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: revenue administration underpinned Mughal power.
- B. Integration of diverse elites into imperial service **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the Mughals incorporated varied regional and religious elites.
- C. Mass industrial factory production  
  _Rationale:_ Industrial factories are a later development.
- D. A transcontinental railway network  
  _Rationale:_ Railways arrived under colonial rule.

**MST-2104-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A summary states that colonial rule simply 'modernised' South Asia for everyone's benefit. What is the problem?

- A. Colonial policies also extracted wealth and disrupted local economies, with uneven and often harmful effects **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the impact was mixed and shaped by imperial interests, not uniformly beneficial.
- B. Colonial rule changed nothing at all  
  _Rationale:_ It brought major changes; the issue is the one-sided framing.
- C. There were no railways or institutions built  
  _Rationale:_ Infrastructure was built, but its purposes and effects were mixed.
- D. Modernisation is impossible to study  
  _Rationale:_ It can be studied; the caution is against a purely positive account.
