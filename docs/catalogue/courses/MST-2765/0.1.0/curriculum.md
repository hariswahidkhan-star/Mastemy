## Learning outcomes

1. Explain additive manufacturing and how it differs from subtractive methods
2. Describe the main 3D printing processes and their materials
3. Reason about the workflow from CAD model to printed part
4. Explain design-for-additive-manufacturing principles
5. Identify quality, tolerance and post-processing considerations
6. Match applications and assess when additive is the right choice

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2765-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) For producing one million identical simple plastic clips, why is additive manufacturing usually not the best choice?

- A. Additive is slow per part and has high unit cost at high volumes, where moulding or machining scale better **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: additive excels at complexity and low volume, not high-volume identical simple parts.
- B. Additive cannot print plastic at all  
  _Rationale:_ Additive commonly prints plastics; volume economics are the issue.
- C. Additive always produces stronger parts than any other method  
  _Rationale:_ Strength varies; this is not why additive loses at high volume.
- D. Additive requires no digital model  
  _Rationale:_ Additive needs a digital model; this is unrelated to the volume question.

**MST-2765-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO are genuine strengths of additive manufacturing? (Select TWO.)

- A. Producing complex geometries that are hard or impossible to machine **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: additive builds internal channels and lattices machining cannot.
- B. Economically making low-volume or customised parts **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: additive suits prototypes and customisation without tooling.
- C. Always being cheaper than every other process at any volume  
  _Rationale:_ Additive is not always cheapest, especially at high volume.
- D. Eliminating any need for design knowledge  
  _Rationale:_ Good results still require design-for-additive knowledge.

**MST-2765-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why does part orientation matter when planning a 3D print?

- A. Orientation affects strength, surface finish and how much support material is needed **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: orientation changes layer direction, supports and quality of the result.
- B. Orientation has no effect on a printed part  
  _Rationale:_ Orientation significantly affects strength and finish.
- C. Orientation only changes the part's colour  
  _Rationale:_ Colour is a material choice, not an orientation effect.
- D. Orientation determines the CAD software used  
  _Rationale:_ Orientation is a print-planning decision, not a software selector.
