## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1845-Q0001** (single-answer-mcq, Select ONE) The te-form is used to connect clauses. The te-form of 'たべる' (to eat) is:

- A. たべて **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: ichidan verb たべる drops る and adds て to form たべて.
- B. たべた  
  _Rationale:_ たべた is the plain past form, not the te-form.
- C. たべない  
  _Rationale:_ たべない is the negative form.
- D. たべろ  
  _Rationale:_ たべろ is an imperative form.

**MST-1845-Q0002** (single-answer-mcq, Select ONE) In a longer reading passage, a demonstrative それ most likely refers to:

- A. Something already mentioned earlier in the text **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: それ typically refers back to a previously mentioned item.
- B. Something the reader must guess with no context  
  _Rationale:_ Reference is recoverable from the text, not guessed.
- C. A brand-new topic never introduced  
  _Rationale:_ それ points to known information, not a new topic.
- D. The writer personally  
  _Rationale:_ それ is a thing-reference, not a reference to the writer.

**MST-1845-Q0003** (multiple-answer-selection, Select TWO) Which TWO conditional-type expressions can introduce a condition in Japanese?

- A. 〜たら **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: たら introduces a conditional 'if/when'.
- B. 〜ば **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: ば forms a conditional.
- C. 〜ながら  
  _Rationale:_ ながら means 'while doing', not a condition.
- D. 〜たい  
  _Rationale:_ たい expresses desire, not a condition.
- E. 〜ました  
  _Rationale:_ ました is a polite past form, not a conditional.
