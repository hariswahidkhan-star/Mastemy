## Learning outcomes

1. Explain what makes a city 'smart' and the systems involved
2. Describe sensing, connectivity and data platforms for urban systems
3. Reason about smart mobility, energy, water and buildings
4. Explain data-driven urban services and operations
5. Identify privacy, equity and governance considerations
6. Assess resilience, sustainability and realistic benefits

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2767-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A smart-city traffic project optimises only average vehicle speed and later finds pedestrian crossings became less safe. What lesson does this best illustrate?

- A. Optimising a narrow metric can harm overall outcomes; goals and equity must be considered together **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: single-metric optimisation can produce harmful side effects without a broader view.
- B. Smart cities should never measure anything  
  _Rationale:_ Measurement is useful; the issue is optimising too narrowly.
- C. Pedestrians are irrelevant to mobility planning  
  _Rationale:_ Pedestrian safety is central to mobility outcomes.
- D. Average speed is always the correct sole objective  
  _Rationale:_ A sole objective ignored safety and equity, which is the problem.

**MST-2767-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO are legitimate concerns when deploying city-wide sensing? (Select TWO.)

- A. Protecting residents' privacy and avoiding undue surveillance **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: pervasive sensing raises real privacy and surveillance concerns.
- B. Ensuring equitable access so benefits are not limited to wealthy areas **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: equity of access is a key smart-city consideration.
- C. Collecting as much personal data as possible with no purpose limit  
  _Rationale:_ Unlimited collection is a risk to avoid, not a goal.
- D. Ignoring governance because the technology is new  
  _Rationale:_ Governance is more important, not less, for new systems.

**MST-2767-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) What best distinguishes a genuinely 'smart' city system from simply installing technology?

- A. Integrating sensing, data and services to improve outcomes for residents, with governance **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: smartness comes from integration and improved outcomes, not gadgets alone.
- B. Having the largest number of cameras on every street  
  _Rationale:_ Counting devices is not what makes a city smart.
- C. Replacing all public services with apps regardless of need  
  _Rationale:_ Blanket app replacement is not the definition of smart.
- D. Keeping all city data secret from residents  
  _Rationale:_ Secrecy undermines engagement and trust, not a hallmark of smartness.
