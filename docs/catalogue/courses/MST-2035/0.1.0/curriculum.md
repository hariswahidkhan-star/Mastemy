## Learning outcomes

1. Explain how stars form, generate energy and evolve
2. Relate stellar properties using the Hertzsprung-Russell diagram
3. Describe the structure of galaxies and the cosmic distance ladder
4. Explain the evidence for an expanding universe and the Big Bang
5. Outline the roles of dark matter and dark energy
6. Interpret astronomical observations across the electromagnetic spectrum

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2035-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A distant galaxy's spectral lines are shifted toward longer (redder) wavelengths. This indicates that the galaxy is

- A. receding from us, consistent with an expanding universe **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: cosmological redshift indicates recession in an expanding universe.
- B. approaching us at high speed  
  _Rationale:_ Approach would produce a blueshift.
- C. made of different elements than the Sun  
  _Rationale:_ The shift is in wavelength of known lines, not composition.
- D. stationary but very hot  
  _Rationale:_ Temperature alone does not shift line wavelengths this way.

**MST-2035-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO observations are considered strong evidence for the Big Bang model? (Select TWO.)

- A. The cosmic microwave background radiation **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the CMB is relic radiation predicted by the Big Bang.
- B. The observed abundances of light elements such as hydrogen and helium **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: primordial nucleosynthesis predicts these abundances.
- C. The existence of black holes at galaxy centres  
  _Rationale:_ Supermassive black holes are not primary Big Bang evidence.
- D. The constancy of the speed of light  
  _Rationale:_ That is a relativity postulate, not Big Bang evidence.

**MST-2035-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Flat rotation curves of spiral galaxies, where outer stars orbit as fast as inner ones, are evidence for

- A. additional unseen mass, attributed to dark matter **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: visible mass cannot explain the flat curves, implying dark matter.
- B. the galaxies being much smaller than they appear  
  _Rationale:_ Size estimates do not resolve the rotation-curve discrepancy.
- C. a failure of the Doppler effect  
  _Rationale:_ The Doppler effect is well established and used to measure the curves.
- D. the expansion of the universe  
  _Rationale:_ Expansion governs large-scale recession, not internal galaxy rotation.
