## Learning outcomes

1. Identify AI use cases in nursing and allied health
2. Interpret AI outputs with clinical judgement
3. Protect patient data and privacy
4. Recognise bias, safety and equity risks in clinical AI
5. Apply oversight and accountability standards

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1403-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) An AI tool suggests a patient is low-risk. How should a clinician use this?

- A. As one input, combined with clinical judgement and the full picture **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: AI supports but does not replace clinical judgement.
- B. As the final decision with no review  
  _Rationale:_ Clinical judgement must remain in control.
- C. Ignore it completely  
  _Rationale:_ AI can add useful information when used carefully.
- D. Assume it is correct for every patient group  
  _Rationale:_ Tools may perform unevenly across groups.

**MST-1403-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO practices protect patients when using clinical AI? (Select TWO.)

- A. Check tools for bias across patient groups **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: bias checks protect equity and safety.
- B. Keep clinician oversight of AI-assisted decisions **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: human oversight protects patients.
- C. Use patient data without consent  
  _Rationale:_ Consent and privacy must be respected.
- D. Assume the tool performs equally for everyone  
  _Rationale:_ Performance can vary across groups.

**MST-1403-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Who holds accountability for an AI-assisted clinical decision?

- A. The responsible clinicians and organisation, not the AI tool **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: accountability stays with people and the organisation.
- B. The AI system itself  
  _Rationale:_ An AI tool cannot be accountable.
- C. No one, once AI is involved  
  _Rationale:_ Accountability does not disappear.
- D. Only the software vendor  
  _Rationale:_ Clinical accountability remains with the care team.
