## Learning outcomes

1. Plan and structure a short spoken presentation
2. Use clear voice, pace and volume when speaking
3. Use body language and eye contact effectively
4. Manage nerves before and during speaking
5. Use simple visual aids to support a talk
6. Give and receive helpful feedback on a presentation

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2884-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) What is the best way to make sure an audience can follow your talk?

- A. Organise it around a few clear points in order **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: clear structure helps the audience follow.
- B. Say as many facts as fast as you can  
  _Rationale:_ Rushing many facts confuses listeners.
- C. Read silently so nobody is disturbed  
  _Rationale:_ A talk must be spoken to be heard.
- D. Turn your back to the audience  
  _Rationale:_ Facing away makes you hard to hear and see.

**MST-2884-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why are pauses useful when speaking?

- A. They give the audience time to take in your point **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: pauses aid understanding and emphasis.
- B. They prove you have forgotten everything  
  _Rationale:_ A planned pause is a tool, not a mistake.
- C. They make the talk sound like a robot  
  _Rationale:_ Natural pauses sound human, not robotic.
- D. They are only for the ending  
  _Rationale:_ Pauses can be used throughout a talk.

**MST-2884-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO habits help you connect with your audience? (Select TWO.)

- A. Making eye contact with different people **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: eye contact builds connection.
- B. Speaking clearly and loudly enough to be heard **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: clear, audible speech reaches the audience.
- C. Mumbling into your notes  
  _Rationale:_ Mumbling makes you hard to understand.
- D. Hiding behind a desk the whole time  
  _Rationale:_ Hiding reduces connection with listeners.
