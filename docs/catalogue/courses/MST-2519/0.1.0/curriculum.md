## Learning outcomes

1. Explain the purpose of the statement of cash flows and the meaning of cash and cash equivalents
2. Classify cash flows into operating, investing and financing activities
3. Prepare operating cash flows using the direct and indirect methods
4. Account for interest, dividends and taxes within the statement
5. Deal with non-cash transactions and foreign-currency cash flows
6. Prepare the reconciliation of liabilities arising from financing activities

- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2519-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Into which three categories does IAS 7 require cash flows to be classified?

- A. Operating, investing and financing activities **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the three categories are operating, investing and financing.
- B. Fixed, variable and semi-variable activities  
  _Rationale:_ These are cost behaviours, not cash-flow categories.
- C. Current, non-current and contingent activities  
  _Rationale:_ These are balance-sheet classifications, not cash-flow categories.
- D. Domestic, foreign and intercompany activities  
  _Rationale:_ These are not the IAS 7 categories.

**MST-2519-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO adjustments are typically made to profit before tax when preparing operating cash flows under the indirect method? (Select TWO.)

- A. Adding back depreciation and amortisation **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: non-cash depreciation and amortisation are added back.
- B. Adjusting for changes in working capital such as receivables and payables **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: working-capital movements are adjustments under the indirect method.
- C. Adding the full cost of new property, plant and equipment purchased  
  _Rationale:_ Capital expenditure is an investing outflow, not an operating adjustment.
- D. Including proceeds from issuing shares  
  _Rationale:_ Share issues are financing, not operating adjustments.

**MST-2519-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A finance manager includes the purchase of a machine acquired under a lease (no cash paid at inception) as a cash outflow in investing activities. Why is this incorrect?

- A. It is a non-cash transaction at inception and is excluded from the statement of cash flows, with disclosure instead **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: non-cash transactions are excluded from the statement and disclosed separately.
- B. Leased assets are always operating outflows  
  _Rationale:_ The issue is that no cash moved at inception, not the category.
- C. All asset purchases are financing outflows  
  _Rationale:_ Asset purchases are investing, but here no cash moved.
- D. Leases can never appear in any disclosure  
  _Rationale:_ Non-cash transactions are disclosed, just not in the cash-flow lines.
