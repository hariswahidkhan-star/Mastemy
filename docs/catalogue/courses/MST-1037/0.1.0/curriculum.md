## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1037-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) What distinguishes a rolling forecast from a traditional annual budget?

- A. It always projects a fixed number of future periods, updated each period **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a rolling forecast continuously extends a constant horizon as time passes.
- B. It is prepared only once a year and never changed  
  _Rationale:_ That describes a static annual budget, not a rolling forecast.
- C. It contains no assumptions  
  _Rationale:_ Forecasts are built from assumptions and drivers.
- D. It replaces the need for actual results  
  _Rationale:_ Forecasts are compared against actuals, not a replacement for them.

**MST-1037-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) A sales shortfall is analysed. Which TWO variances help explain the revenue gap? (Select TWO)

- A. Sales price variance **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the price variance isolates the effect of selling at a different price.
- B. Sales volume variance **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the volume variance isolates the effect of selling a different quantity.
- C. Interest-rate variance  
  _Rationale:_ Interest rates relate to financing, not the revenue gap.
- D. Depreciation-method variance  
  _Rationale:_ Depreciation method does not explain a sales revenue gap.

**MST-1037-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why is a cash budget needed even when the profit budget shows a healthy surplus?

- A. Timing of receipts and payments can create cash shortfalls despite profit **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: profit and cash differ in timing, so a profitable plan can still run short of cash.
- B. A cash budget replaces the profit budget entirely  
  _Rationale:_ The two serve different purposes and are used together.
- C. Profit budgets include no revenue  
  _Rationale:_ Profit budgets do include revenue.
- D. Cash budgets ignore all outflows  
  _Rationale:_ Cash budgets include both inflows and outflows.
