## Learning outcomes

1. Apply critical-care assessment and management of cardiovascular and pulmonary conditions
2. Manage neurologic, renal, endocrine, gastrointestinal, hematologic and multisystem conditions
3. Apply monitoring, pharmacology and interventions used in adult critical care
4. Apply professional caring practices, safety, and the synergy of patient needs and nurse competencies

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1286-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A patient has a low mean arterial pressure, warm extremities, low systemic vascular resistance and high cardiac output after a severe infection. Which shock state best fits?

- A. Distributive (septic) shock **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: low SVR with high/normal cardiac output after infection is characteristic of distributive/septic shock.
- B. Cardiogenic shock  
  _Rationale:_ Cardiogenic shock typically shows low cardiac output and high SVR, not this pattern.
- C. Hypovolemic shock  
  _Rationale:_ Hypovolemic shock shows high SVR and low output, not warm extremities with low SVR.
- D. Obstructive shock  
  _Rationale:_ Obstructive shock arises from a mechanical obstruction and does not typically give low SVR with high output.

**MST-1286-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) An arterial blood gas shows low pH, high PaCO2, and a near-normal bicarbonate. This is best described as:

- A. Acute respiratory acidosis **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: low pH with elevated PaCO2 and little metabolic compensation indicates acute respiratory acidosis.
- B. Metabolic alkalosis  
  _Rationale:_ Metabolic alkalosis shows high pH and high bicarbonate, not this pattern.
- C. Fully compensated metabolic acidosis  
  _Rationale:_ A near-normal bicarbonate and high PaCO2 do not fit metabolic acidosis.
- D. Normal acid-base status  
  _Rationale:_ A low pH is not normal acid-base status.

**MST-1286-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Select TWO practices that reduce harm in a mechanically ventilated adult in critical care.

- A. Targeting a light, goal-directed sedation level and assessing daily readiness to wean **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: lighter, goal-directed sedation and daily readiness assessment reduce ventilator days and delirium.
- B. Elevating the head of the bed when not contraindicated **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: head-of-bed elevation helps reduce ventilator-associated pneumonia risk.
- C. Keeping the patient deeply sedated at all times  
  _Rationale:_ Routine deep sedation increases delirium and prolongs ventilation.
- D. Silencing all monitor alarms to reduce noise  
  _Rationale:_ Disabling alarms removes a key safety signal.
- E. Avoiding any assessment of weaning readiness  
  _Rationale:_ Skipping weaning assessment prolongs unnecessary ventilation.
