## Learning outcomes

1. Distinguish tactical purchasing from strategic sourcing and category management
2. Analyse spend and segment a supply base to prioritise sourcing effort
3. Develop and execute a sourcing strategy including make-versus-buy decisions
4. Evaluate suppliers and run a structured tender and negotiation
5. Manage supplier risk, contracts and value over the relationship lifecycle

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Hands-on operation of the tools and workflows taught, the quality of live outputs, and professional judgement on the job are taught by video demonstration and model-answer analysis; the certificate assessment uses single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only and never requires uploads, recorded work, graded prompts or peer review.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1162-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A category has high supply risk but low spend. Where does it sit on the Kraljic matrix and what is the usual aim?

- A. Bottleneck: secure supply continuity and reduce dependence **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: high risk/low spend is the bottleneck quadrant, where continuity matters more than price.
- B. Leverage: drive price down with competition  
  _Rationale:_ Leverage is high spend/low risk, not this profile.
- C. Non-critical: minimise transaction cost  
  _Rationale:_ Non-critical items are low risk and low spend.
- D. Strategic: build deep partnerships  
  _Rationale:_ Strategic items are high spend and high risk.

**MST-1162-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) You are comparing two suppliers on total cost of ownership rather than price. Which TWO costs belong in the TCO comparison? (Select TWO.)

- A. Inbound logistics and duties **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: landed cost components are part of TCO.
- B. Cost of quality failures and rework **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: quality and failure costs are a core TCO element.
- C. The competitor's list price  
  _Rationale:_ A third party's list price is not a cost you incur.
- D. Only the quoted unit price  
  _Rationale:_ Unit price alone is exactly what TCO is meant to go beyond.

**MST-1162-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) In negotiation planning, what does a strong BATNA most directly give the buyer?

- A. A credible walk-away alternative that anchors acceptable terms **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a strong BATNA sets the threshold below which no deal is better than this deal.
- B. A guarantee the supplier will lower price  
  _Rationale:_ A BATNA informs your position but guarantees nothing from the supplier.
- C. Permission to skip the evaluation  
  _Rationale:_ A BATNA does not replace structured evaluation.
- D. A longer contract term automatically  
  _Rationale:_ Term length is a separate negotiated variable.
