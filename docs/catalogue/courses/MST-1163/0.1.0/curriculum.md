## Learning outcomes

1. Define supplier requirements and build weighted evaluation criteria
2. Assess and compare suppliers, including total cost of ownership and risk
3. Prepare for and conduct principled supplier negotiations
4. Structure contracts and SLAs that align incentives
5. Manage ongoing supplier performance with scorecards and reviews

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1163-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Supplier A quotes a lower unit price than Supplier B, but A has higher defect rates, longer lead times and extra freight cost. What concept best captures the true comparison?

- A. Total cost of ownership **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: TCO adds quality, logistics and other downstream costs to the purchase price for a fair comparison.
- B. Gross margin  
  _Rationale:_ Gross margin is a seller's profitability measure, not the buyer's total cost.
- C. Economic order quantity  
  _Rationale:_ EOQ concerns order sizing, not supplier comparison on cost of ownership.
- D. Net present value  
  _Rationale:_ NPV discounts cash flows over time; here the issue is hidden current costs, not timing.

**MST-1163-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Before a supplier negotiation, which TWO preparations most strengthen your position? (Select TWO.)

- A. Know your BATNA (best alternative if no deal is reached) **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a strong, known alternative sets your walk-away and limits over-conceding.
- B. Estimate the ZOPA (zone of possible agreement) **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: knowing the likely overlap of acceptable terms guides realistic targets.
- C. Decide to accept the supplier's first offer to save time  
  _Rationale:_ Accepting the opening offer typically leaves value on the table.
- D. Hide all of your interests from the other side  
  _Rationale:_ Sharing some interests enables trades that expand value; total concealment blocks deals.

**MST-1163-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Which feature makes a service-level agreement enforceable rather than merely aspirational?

- A. Measurable metrics with agreed targets and defined remedies **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: an SLA needs objective measures, thresholds and consequences to be enforceable.
- B. A statement that the supplier will 'do its best'  
  _Rationale:_ Best-efforts language is not objectively measurable or enforceable.
- C. A longer contract term  
  _Rationale:_ Duration does not make performance measurable or enforceable.
- D. A higher contract value  
  _Rationale:_ Contract size does not create enforceable service levels.
