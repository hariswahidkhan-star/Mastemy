## Learning outcomes

1. Explain quality concepts and the quality technician's role
2. Apply inspection, test and measurement methods
3. Apply basic statistics, sampling and control charts
4. Explain quality systems, corrective action and safety

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-0323-Q0001** (single, Select ONE) Before trusting a micrometer reading, a technician should first confirm the instrument is:

- A. Within its calibration due date and traceable **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a gauge must be in calibration to give trustworthy readings.
- B. The most expensive available  
  _Rationale:_ Cost does not determine measurement validity.
- C. Stored in the toolroom  
  _Rationale:_ Storage location does not confirm accuracy.
- D. Painted a bright colour  
  _Rationale:_ Colour is irrelevant to measurement validity.

**MST-0323-Q0002** (single, Select ONE) An acceptance sampling plan lets a technician:

- A. Decide whether to accept or reject a lot from a sample **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: sampling plans base a lot decision on a defined sample.
- B. Guarantee zero defects in the lot  
  _Rationale:_ Sampling accepts some risk; it does not guarantee zero defects.
- C. Eliminate the need for specifications  
  _Rationale:_ Specifications are still required.
- D. Replace calibration  
  _Rationale:_ Sampling is unrelated to calibration.

**MST-0323-Q0003** (multiple, Select TWO) Select TWO items shown on a typical engineering drawing that a technician inspects to.

- A. Dimensional tolerances **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: tolerances define acceptable dimensions.
- B. Surface finish requirements **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: surface finish is a specified characteristic.
- C. The supplier's profit margin  
  _Rationale:_ Profit margin is not on a drawing.
- D. The machine operator's schedule  
  _Rationale:_ A schedule is not a drawing specification.
- E. The paint colour of the building  
  _Rationale:_ Irrelevant to inspection.
