## Learning outcomes

1. Explain the legacies of antiquity and the shape of medieval European society
2. Analyse the Renaissance, Reformation and the fracturing of Christendom
3. Trace the rise of states, absolutism and the Enlightenment
4. Evaluate the French Revolution, industrialisation and the age of nationalism
5. Explain the causes and consequences of the two world wars
6. Assess division, integration and change in Europe since 1945

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2102-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why do historians resist describing the medieval period as simply a 'dark age'?

- A. It saw significant intellectual, economic and institutional development, and the label reflects later polemic **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the term distorts a varied, productive era and originates in Renaissance and later rhetoric.
- B. Because no wars occurred  
  _Rationale:_ Conflict was common; the point is the dismissive label.
- C. Because records do not survive  
  _Rationale:_ Extensive records survive; the issue is the stereotype.
- D. Because it lasted only fifty years  
  _Rationale:_ It spanned roughly a thousand years.

**MST-2102-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO factors most directly help explain the rapid spread of the Reformation? (Select TWO.)

- A. The printing press, which circulated ideas and texts widely **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: print multiplied and spread reform writings quickly.
- B. The political interests of rulers who backed reform **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: princely support shaped where and how reform succeeded.
- C. The invention of the steam engine  
  _Rationale:_ That is an 18th-19th century development.
- D. The founding of the United Nations  
  _Rationale:_ A 20th-century institution, unrelated.

**MST-2102-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A textbook claims the First World War was 'inevitable because of one assassination'. What is the flaw?

- A. A single trigger cannot explain the war without alliances, militarism, rivalries and decisions by many actors **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the assassination was a trigger within a web of longer-term causes and choices.
- B. The assassination never happened  
  _Rationale:_ It did happen; the flaw is reducing cause to it alone.
- C. Wars are never caused by events  
  _Rationale:_ Events matter, but as part of wider causation.
- D. Only economics causes wars  
  _Rationale:_ No single factor suffices, including economics.
