## Learning outcomes

1. Record payroll transactions including gross pay, deductions and employer costs
2. Distinguish statutory deductions from voluntary and employer-borne costs
3. Reconcile payroll to the general ledger and to bank payments
4. Analyse the fully loaded cost of an employee
5. Design payroll controls that protect against error and fraud

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1048-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) The fully loaded cost of an employee is best described as:

- A. Gross salary plus all employer on-costs such as employer taxes, pension and benefits **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: fully loaded cost adds employer-borne costs to gross pay.
- B. The employee's net take-home pay  
  _Rationale:_ Net pay understates the cost to the employer.
- C. Gross salary only  
  _Rationale:_ This omits employer on-costs.
- D. Only the statutory deductions withheld  
  _Rationale:_ Deductions withheld are not the total cost.

**MST-1048-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO controls best reduce payroll fraud risk? (Select TWO.)

- A. Independent review and approval of all new starters and pay changes **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: approval of master-data changes blocks ghost employees and unauthorised raises.
- B. Segregating payroll preparation from bank payment authorisation **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: segregation stops one person creating and paying a fictitious employee.
- C. Letting the payroll clerk also open and close bank accounts  
  _Rationale:_ This concentrates incompatible duties and raises fraud risk.
- D. Skipping reconciliation to save time  
  _Rationale:_ Reconciliation is a key detective control.

**MST-1048-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Statutory payroll deductions withheld from employees represent, before remittance:

- A. A liability the employer owes to the relevant authority **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: withheld amounts are held in trust and owed onward, a liability.
- B. Revenue for the employer  
  _Rationale:_ Withholdings are not the employer's income.
- C. An expense of the employer  
  _Rationale:_ They are the employee's money withheld, not an employer expense.
- D. An asset the employer may keep  
  _Rationale:_ The employer must remit them; they are not retained.
