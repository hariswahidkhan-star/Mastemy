## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1847-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Which particle most typically marks the direct object of a transitive verb in Japanese?

- A. wo **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the particle wo marks the direct object of a transitive verb.
- B. ga  
  _Rationale:_ ga typically marks the grammatical subject, not the object.
- C. ni  
  _Rationale:_ ni marks destination, time or indirect object, not the direct object here.
- D. e  
  _Rationale:_ e marks direction toward, not the direct object.

**MST-1847-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) The kanji that most commonly carries the meaning 'sun' or 'day' is:

- A. hi/nichi (the 'sun/day' character) **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: that character's core meaning is sun or day.
- B. the character meaning 'moon/month'  
  _Rationale:_ That character means moon or month, not sun/day.
- C. the character meaning 'tree'  
  _Rationale:_ That character means tree, not sun/day.
- D. the character meaning 'water'  
  _Rationale:_ That character means water, not sun/day.

**MST-1847-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO statements about standard Japanese sentence structure are correct?

- A. The basic word order is subject-object-verb **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: Japanese is typically SOV.
- B. The main verb usually comes at the end of the clause **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the predicate verb is clause-final.
- C. Modifying adjectives follow the noun they modify  
  _Rationale:_ Japanese attributive modifiers precede the noun.
- D. Grammatical particles come before the word they mark  
  _Rationale:_ Particles follow the word they mark, not precede it.
- E. A definite article is required before every noun  
  _Rationale:_ Japanese has no articles.
