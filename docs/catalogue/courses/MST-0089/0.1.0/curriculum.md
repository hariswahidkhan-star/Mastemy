## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-0089-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Physical climate risk refers to:

- A. Risk from the physical impacts of climate change, such as floods and heatwaves **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: physical risk arises from acute and chronic physical climate impacts.
- B. Risk from policy changes that raise the cost of carbon  
  _Rationale:_ That is transition risk, not physical risk.
- C. Risk from reputational damage only  
  _Rationale:_ Reputation is one dimension, not the definition of physical risk.
- D. Risk that stock prices fall for any reason  
  _Rationale:_ That is general market risk, not physical climate risk.

**MST-0089-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) A carbon price introduced by regulation is an example of which climate risk category?

- A. Transition risk **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: policy-driven carbon pricing is a transition risk.
- B. Physical risk  
  _Rationale:_ Physical risk is about direct climate impacts, not policy.
- C. Liquidity risk  
  _Rationale:_ Liquidity risk concerns funding, not carbon policy.
- D. Operational error risk  
  _Rationale:_ This is unrelated to carbon pricing.

**MST-0089-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Select TWO elements commonly used in climate scenario analysis.

- A. A range of warming/emissions pathways **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: scenarios span different emissions and warming pathways.
- B. Assessment of both transition and physical risk drivers **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: scenario analysis considers transition and physical channels.
- C. A guarantee of the exact future temperature  
  _Rationale:_ Scenarios explore possibilities; they do not guarantee outcomes.
- D. Ignoring any policy changes  
  _Rationale:_ Policy pathways are central to scenario analysis.
- E. A single deterministic forecast only  
  _Rationale:_ Scenario analysis uses multiple pathways, not one forecast.
