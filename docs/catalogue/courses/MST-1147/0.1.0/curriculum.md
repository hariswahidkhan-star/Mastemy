## Learning outcomes

1. Explain delay types and their contractual treatment
2. Distinguish excusable, compensable and concurrent delay
3. Select an appropriate delay analysis method for the available records
4. Assemble and evaluate the records needed to prove an extension of time
5. Present a defensible extension-of-time and prolongation narrative

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Integrative case

A subcontractor on a commercial fit-out faces a client claim for liquidated damages. Using the baseline programme and progress records, classify the delays, choose a delay analysis method suited to the records held, and build an extension-of-time case that separates the contractor's own culpable delay from excusable events.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1147-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) During a three-week period, an employer-caused design delay and a contractor-caused labour shortage both independently delay completion. Under a typical analysis, what does true concurrent delay usually mean for entitlement?

- A. The contractor is generally entitled to time but not necessarily to prolongation cost  **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: concurrency commonly grants an extension of time while barring prolongation cost for the overlap.
- B. The contractor is entitled to both time and full prolongation cost  
  _Rationale:_ Its own concurrent culpable delay usually bars recovery of cost for the overlapping period.
- C. The contractor loses all entitlement to time  
  _Rationale:_ The excusable employer delay still supports time entitlement.
- D. Liquidated damages automatically apply for the whole period  
  _Rationale:_ The excusable delay means LADs do not simply run through the concurrent period.

**MST-1147-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO conditions make an as-planned-versus-as-built delay analysis a suitable choice? (Select TWO.)

- A. Reliable as-built progress records exist for the delayed work  **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the method compares planned to actual, so trustworthy as-built data is essential.
- B. A logic-linked baseline programme is available  **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a sound baseline gives the planned intent to compare against.
- C. No progress records were kept during the works  
  _Rationale:_ Without as-built data the comparison cannot be made.
- D. The analysis must be done before the works start  
  _Rationale:_ As-planned-vs-as-built is retrospective; it needs completed-work data.

**MST-1147-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A claim states only that 'the employer caused 40 days of delay overall' without linking events to effects. What is the main weakness?

- A. It is a global claim that fails to show cause and effect for each event  **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: tribunals expect each delay event particularised with its effect on completion.
- B. It claims too few days to be worth pursuing  
  _Rationale:_ The quantum is not the issue; the lack of particularisation is.
- C. Global claims are always accepted without evidence  
  _Rationale:_ Global claims are generally disfavoured and vulnerable to challenge.
- D. Employer delays cannot be claimed  
  _Rationale:_ Employer delays can found entitlement; they must be properly evidenced.
