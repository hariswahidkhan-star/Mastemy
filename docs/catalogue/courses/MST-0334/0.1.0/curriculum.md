## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-0334-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) An industrial hygienist compares a full-shift noise dose to an occupational exposure limit. Which quantity is most appropriate for assessing continuous and intermittent noise over the shift?

- A. Time-weighted average (TWA) sound level over the sampling period **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a TWA integrates varying levels over time and is compared to a full-shift limit.
- B. The single highest instantaneous peak reading  
  _Rationale:_ Peak readings assess impulsive-noise limits, not full-shift average exposure.
- C. The arithmetic mean of three spot readings  
  _Rationale:_ A few spot readings do not represent time-weighted exposure across a varying shift.
- D. The manufacturer's rated level for the loudest machine  
  _Rationale:_ Equipment ratings are not a measurement of the worker's actual exposure.

**MST-0334-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) When selecting controls for a solvent-vapor exposure, which option reflects the preferred engineering control over administrative measures?

- A. Installing local exhaust ventilation at the point of vapor generation **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: local exhaust ventilation is an engineering control that captures the contaminant at the source.
- B. Rotating workers to limit each person's exposure time  
  _Rationale:_ Job rotation is an administrative control that distributes rather than reduces the hazard.
- C. Requiring respirators for all exposed workers  
  _Rationale:_ Respirators are PPE, used when engineering controls are insufficient.
- D. Posting the safety data sheet near the work area  
  _Rationale:_ Providing information is administrative and does not reduce airborne concentration.

**MST-0334-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Select TWO factors that most directly affect a chemical's inhalation hazard in a workplace.

- A. The airborne concentration of the substance in the breathing zone **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: breathing-zone concentration is a primary determinant of inhalation dose.
- B. The vapor pressure (volatility) of the substance **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: higher volatility increases the tendency to generate airborne vapor.
- C. The color of the container the chemical is stored in  
  _Rationale:_ Container color has no bearing on inhalation hazard.
- D. The alphabetical order of the chemical's name  
  _Rationale:_ Naming has no effect on the physical hazard.
- E. The brand of the supplier  
  _Rationale:_ Supplier brand does not determine the inhalation hazard of the substance itself.
