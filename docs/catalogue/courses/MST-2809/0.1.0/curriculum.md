## Learning outcomes

1. Count forwards and backwards to 20 and say how many are in a group
2. Read, write and order numbers to 20
3. Recognise one more and one less than a given number
4. Match a numeral to the right quantity of objects
5. Compare two groups using more, fewer and the same
6. Begin counting in steps of two, five and ten with everyday objects

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Modules

### M01 Counting to 20 (25% (design weight), design weight)

- Worked applications: (1) Count 13 stickers one at a time without skipping any; (2) Count down from 5 before a jump
- Common misconception addressed: Thinking the last number said is only that object, not the total of the group
- Module check: 10 items / 10 min, threshold 75%

- Worked applications: (1) Write 17 when you hear 'seventeen'; (2) Order the cards 3, 1, 5, 2, 4
- Common misconception addressed: Writing numbers back to front, such as a reversed 3, and not noticing
- Module check: 10 items / 10 min, threshold 75%

### M03 More, fewer and the same (25% (design weight), design weight)

- Worked applications: (1) Decide if 6 bananas is more than 4 bananas; (2) Build matching towers of 7 cubes
- Common misconception addressed: Thinking the longer row always has more, even when it is just spread out
- Module check: 10 items / 10 min, threshold 75%

### M04 Counting in steps (25% (design weight), design weight)

- Worked applications: (1) Count 10 shoes in twos; (2) Count five hands in fives to reach 25

## Integrative case

Help a toy shop sort buttons, beads and blocks into labelled boxes by counting each group, writing how many are in each box, and deciding which box has the most.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2809-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) How many counters are in this group if you count '1, 2, 3, 4, 5' touching each one once?

- A. 5, because the last number you say when counting each object once is the total **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the final count word tells you how many are in the whole group.
- B. 1, because that is the first number you said  
  _Rationale:_ The first word is only the start of counting, not the total.
- C. 10, because counting always reaches ten  
  _Rationale:_ Counting stops at the last object, which here is the fifth.
- D. You cannot tell from counting  
  _Rationale:_ Counting each object once does tell you the total.

**MST-2809-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Select TWO numbers that are LESS than 8. (Select TWO.)

- A. 5 **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: 5 comes before 8 when counting, so it is less.
- B. 3 **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: 3 comes before 8 when counting, so it is less.
- C. 9  
  _Rationale:_ 9 comes after 8, so it is more than 8.
- D. 12  
  _Rationale:_ 12 comes after 8, so it is more than 8.

**MST-2809-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Which number is one more than 14?

- A. 15 **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: one more means the next number when counting up, which is 15.
- B. 13  
  _Rationale:_ 13 is one less than 14, not one more.
- C. 16  
  _Rationale:_ 16 is two more than 14.
- D. 10  
  _Rationale:_ 10 is less than 14, not one more.
