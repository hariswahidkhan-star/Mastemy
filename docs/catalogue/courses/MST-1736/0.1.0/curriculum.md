## Learning outcomes

1. What wealth management is
2. Understanding the client
3. Building the plan
4. Protection, tax and estate
5. Reviewing and behaviour

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses conceptual knowledge through selection items; this is general education, not personalised financial or investment advice, and it does not assess a specific client's wealth plan.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1736-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Risk tolerance and risk capacity differ in that risk capacity refers to:

- A. The client's financial ability to withstand losses **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: capacity is the objective ability to absorb losses.
- B. How the client emotionally feels about risk  
  _Rationale:_ That is risk tolerance, not capacity.
- C. The current level of the stock market  
  _Rationale:_ That is a market condition, not the client's capacity.
- D. The adviser's personal risk preference  
  _Rationale:_ The adviser's preference is irrelevant to the client's capacity.

**MST-1736-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select ALL that apply) Which two reflect good wealth-management practice? (Select TWO) (Select TWO)

- A. Planning around the client's goals and whole situation **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: holistic, goal-based planning is sound practice.
- B. Keeping costs and fees under control **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: controlling costs protects long-term wealth.
- C. Selling whichever product pays the adviser most  
  _Rationale:_ That is a conflict of interest, not good practice.
- D. Ignoring protection and estate needs  
  _Rationale:_ A complete plan addresses protection and estate matters.

**MST-1736-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) During a sharp market downturn, the most constructive thing a wealth manager usually does is:

- A. Coach the client to stick to the agreed long-term plan **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: steadying behaviour avoids locking in losses.
- B. Encourage the client to sell everything immediately  
  _Rationale:_ Panic-selling often crystallises losses at the worst time.
- C. Promise the market will recover by a fixed date  
  _Rationale:_ No one can guarantee timing of a recovery.
- D. Stop all communication with the client  
  _Rationale:_ Communication is most important during stress.
