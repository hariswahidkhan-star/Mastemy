## Learning outcomes

1. Explain emergency nursing scope, triage and assessment across body systems
2. Apply knowledge of cardiovascular, respiratory and neurological emergencies
3. Apply knowledge of trauma, shock, toxicologic and environmental emergencies
4. Describe psychosocial, medical, maternal and professional emergency-nursing practice

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1762-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Four patients arrive at once. Which principle should guide who is seen first?

- A. Acuity: the most clinically urgent patient is prioritized **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: triage prioritizes by acuity, not arrival time.
- B. Arrival order, strictly first-come first-served  
  _Rationale:_ Strict arrival order ignores life-threatening conditions.
- C. Whoever is loudest  
  _Rationale:_ Loudness does not indicate clinical urgency.
- D. Alphabetical by last name  
  _Rationale:_ Name order is irrelevant to clinical priority.

**MST-1762-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) A trauma patient has a rising heart rate, narrowing pulse pressure, and anxiety, but a still-normal blood pressure. What does this most likely indicate?

- A. Early (compensated) shock requiring prompt intervention **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: tachycardia and narrowing pulse pressure signal compensated shock before BP falls.
- B. A fully stable patient needing no action  
  _Rationale:_ These are warning signs, not reassurance.
- C. That vital signs can be ignored  
  _Rationale:_ Trending vital signs are exactly what reveals early shock.
- D. A reason to delay reassessment for an hour  
  _Rationale:_ Early shock requires prompt, frequent reassessment and action.

**MST-1762-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Select TWO actions appropriate when caring for a patient in a behavioral-health crisis.

- A. Ensure the safety of the patient, staff and others **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: safety is the first priority in a behavioral emergency.
- B. Assess for medical causes of the behavior change **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: medical conditions can present as behavioral emergencies and must be ruled out.
- C. Leave the patient unobserved to de-escalate alone  
  _Rationale:_ Unobserved patients at risk can harm themselves or others.
- D. Assume the behavior is never medically caused  
  _Rationale:_ Many behavioral presentations have medical causes.
- E. Restrain first and assess later as routine  
  _Rationale:_ Restraint is a last resort governed by policy, not a routine first step.
