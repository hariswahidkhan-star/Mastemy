## Learning outcomes

1. Explain common construction procurement routes and contract families
2. Interpret key contract clauses on payment, variations and time
3. Administer the variation and change process correctly
4. Operate the payment and valuation cycle under a standard form
5. Manage notices, claims and dispute-avoidance steps

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1148-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A contractor discovers a ground condition it believes entitles it to more time and money, but the contract requires written notice within 14 days of becoming aware. It serves notice after 30 days. What is the typical consequence?

- A. The entitlement may be lost or reduced because a condition precedent notice was missed  **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: where notice is a condition precedent, late service can bar the claim.
- B. Notice timing never affects entitlement  
  _Rationale:_ Many standard forms make timely notice a condition of recovery.
- C. The contractor is automatically entitled to double the claim  
  _Rationale:_ Late notice does not increase entitlement; it risks losing it.
- D. The employer must serve the notice instead  
  _Rationale:_ The burden to serve the entitlement notice is on the contractor.

**MST-1148-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO are typically true of a design-and-build procurement route? (Select TWO.)

- A. Design responsibility is transferred to the contractor  **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the contractor takes responsibility for completing and often developing the design.
- B. The employer usually has a single point of responsibility  **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a single contractor is responsible for both design and construction.
- C. The employer retains full control over detailed design throughout  
  _Rationale:_ That is more typical of a traditional route, not design-and-build.
- D. There is never any employer's requirements document  
  _Rationale:_ Employer's requirements are central to defining the brief under design-and-build.

**MST-1148-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A site engineer verbally tells a trade to add two extra doors. No written instruction follows. Under a standard form, what is the safest commercial position before proceeding?

- A. Obtain a compliant written variation instruction before carrying out the work  **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a compliant instruction protects valuation and payment entitlement.
- B. Proceed immediately; verbal instructions are always binding  
  _Rationale:_ Verbal instructions often need written confirmation to be valued as variations.
- C. Treat it as defect correction at no cost  
  _Rationale:_ Added scope is a variation, not correction of defective work.
- D. Ignore it because extra doors cannot be a variation  
  _Rationale:_ Added scope can be a variation; the issue is securing the instruction.
