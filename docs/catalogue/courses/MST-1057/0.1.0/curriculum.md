## Learning outcomes

1. Explain the purpose and scope of financial due diligence
2. Analyse quality of earnings and defensible adjustments
3. Assess net debt, debt-like items and a working-capital peg
4. Review cash generation and management forecasts
5. Translate DD findings into price and SPA implications
6. Report findings clearly and act with integrity

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1057-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why is a quality-of-earnings analysis central to financial due diligence?

- A. It distinguishes sustainable, normalised earnings from one-off or non-recurring items **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: QoE separates recurring earning power from noise.
- B. It re-audits the statutory accounts for an opinion  
  _Rationale:_ DD is not a statutory audit.
- C. It sets the legal structure of the deal  
  _Rationale:_ Structure is a legal, not a QoE, matter.
- D. It guarantees the forecast will be achieved  
  _Rationale:_ QoE assesses history; it does not guarantee forecasts.

**MST-1057-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO items are typically treated as debt-like when calculating net debt? (Select TWO.)

- A. Unpaid declared dividends **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a committed outflow behaves like debt.
- B. Overdue tax liabilities **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: overdue obligations are debt-like.
- C. Normal trade payables within terms  
  _Rationale:_ These sit in working capital, not net debt.
- D. Routine inventory held for sale  
  _Rationale:_ Inventory is a working-capital item, not debt-like.

**MST-1057-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A DD finding shows working capital was unusually low at the measurement date due to delayed supplier payments. What is the right response?

- A. Reflect it in a normalised working-capital peg so the buyer is not short-changed **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: normalising the peg prevents a value transfer from the buyer.
- B. Ignore it because it is only timing  
  _Rationale:_ Timing distortions directly affect value at completion.
- C. Automatically reduce the headline price by the same amount  
  _Rationale:_ The mechanism is the peg, not an automatic price cut.
- D. Treat it as additional EBITDA  
  _Rationale:_ A balance-sheet timing item is not earnings.
