## Learning outcomes

1. Scope a research question and plan the evidence needed to answer it
2. Gather evidence and attribute every claim to a traceable source
3. Produce an evidence-based synthesis that preserves attribution and conflict
4. Verify claims and represent uncertainty in a research deliverable
5. Deliver a sourced research brief and document the method used

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Selection items cannot demonstrate live tool use or writing quality; those are taught through instructor demonstrations and model-answer analysis.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-0515-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) You ask Claude to summarise research and it returns a tidy summary with reference-style citations. What must you do before relying on them?

- A. Check that each cited source exists and actually supports the claim **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: citations can be fabricated or mis-attributed and must be verified.
- B. Assume they are correct because they are formatted like citations  
  _Rationale:_ Citation-like formatting is not evidence of a real source.
- C. Delete the citations to keep the brief short  
  _Rationale:_ Removing attribution weakens the brief; verify instead.
- D. Ask Claude whether it is confident  
  _Rationale:_ Stated confidence is not verification.

**MST-0515-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO steps belong in a verification routine for a research brief? (Select TWO.)

- A. Trace each figure back to its original source **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: tracing figures to their origin is core verification.
- B. Label any claim that could not be confirmed **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: honest labelling of unconfirmed claims is part of verification.
- C. Increase the length of the brief  
  _Rationale:_ Length does not establish accuracy.
- D. Remove all caveats so the brief reads confidently  
  _Rationale:_ Stripping caveats hides genuine uncertainty.

**MST-0515-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Two credible sources give conflicting figures. What is the best way to handle this in the synthesis?

- A. Present both figures, attribute each, and note the disagreement **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: honest synthesis surfaces conflict rather than hiding it.
- B. Average them silently  
  _Rationale:_ Silent averaging misrepresents the evidence.
- C. Pick the one Claude wrote first  
  _Rationale:_ Order of generation is not a basis for selection.
- D. Omit both to avoid complexity  
  _Rationale:_ Dropping relevant evidence weakens the brief.
