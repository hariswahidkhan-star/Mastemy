## Learning outcomes

1. What forensic accounting is
2. The fraud triangle and schemes
3. Red flags and detection
4. Investigation and evidence
5. Reporting and prevention

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses conceptual knowledge through selection items; does not assess a real fraud investigation, evidence that would stand in court, or an expert-witness opinion.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1718-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) The three elements of the classic fraud triangle are:

- A. Pressure, opportunity and rationalisation **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: these three elements define the fraud triangle.
- B. Planning, fieldwork and reporting  
  _Rationale:_ Those are audit stages, not the fraud triangle.
- C. Placement, layering and integration  
  _Rationale:_ Those are money-laundering stages.
- D. Risk, control and assurance  
  _Rationale:_ Those are internal-control concepts, not the fraud triangle.

**MST-1718-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select ALL that apply) Which two practices help ensure evidence is credible in a forensic investigation? (Select TWO) (Select TWO)

- A. Maintaining a documented chain of custody **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: chain of custody preserves evidentiary integrity.
- B. Preserving original records and metadata **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: keeping originals intact protects reliability.
- C. Editing documents to make them clearer  
  _Rationale:_ Altering evidence destroys its integrity.
- D. Relying on the investigator's unrecorded memory  
  _Rationale:_ Undocumented recollection is weak and contestable.

**MST-1718-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A forensic accountant's report should present a finding by:

- A. Stating it objectively and supporting it with documented evidence **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: findings must be evidence-based and objective.
- B. Declaring the suspect guilty of a crime  
  _Rationale:_ Guilt is for the courts to decide, not the accountant.
- C. Omitting evidence that is inconvenient  
  _Rationale:_ Selective omission undermines objectivity.
- D. Relying mainly on personal opinion  
  _Rationale:_ Opinion must not replace documented evidence.
