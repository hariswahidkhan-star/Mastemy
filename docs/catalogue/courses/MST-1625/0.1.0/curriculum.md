## Learning outcomes

1. Read and reconcile the three core financial statements
2. Compute and interpret liquidity, profitability and leverage ratios
3. Analyse trends, variances and common-size statements
4. Build simple forecasts and sensitivity analyses
5. Apply time value of money to basic investment decisions

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1625-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A company reports a profit but negative operating cash flow. What is the most likely reason?

- A. Profit is accrual-based; cash can differ due to receivables, inventory and timing **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: accrual profit and cash flow differ because of working-capital timing.
- B. The income statement must be wrong  
  _Rationale:_ A gap between profit and cash is normal, not necessarily an error.
- C. Cash flow and profit are always equal  
  _Rationale:_ They are not equal under accrual accounting.
- D. The company has no revenue  
  _Rationale:_ Reporting a profit implies revenue exists.

**MST-1625-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO statements about financial ratios are correct? (Select TWO.)

- A. A ratio is most useful compared to a benchmark or trend **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: context from benchmarks and trends gives a ratio meaning.
- B. High return on equity can be driven by high leverage, not just performance **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: leverage inflates ROE and must be read with caution.
- C. The current ratio measures long-term profitability  
  _Rationale:_ The current ratio measures short-term liquidity, not profitability.
- D. A single ratio in isolation fully describes financial health  
  _Rationale:_ No single ratio captures overall health.

**MST-1625-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why must cash flows in different years be discounted before comparison?

- A. A sum received later is worth less today because of the time value of money **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: discounting reflects that money has a time value.
- B. Inflation is the only reason and it is negligible  
  _Rationale:_ The time value of money is broader than inflation and is not negligible.
- C. Future cash flows are always larger  
  _Rationale:_ Size is irrelevant; timing requires discounting.
- D. Accounting rules forbid adding cash flows  
  _Rationale:_ The issue is comparability across time, not a prohibition.
