## Learning outcomes

1. Apply English usage, mechanics and rhetorical-skills strategies
2. Apply reading comprehension strategies across prose and informational texts
3. Solve mathematics items across pre-algebra through trigonometry under timing
4. Interpret and reason about scientific data, research summaries and conflicting viewpoints

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess:
  - Knowledge-practice forms use MCQ/MR only; official item formats not reproducible in this format are listed in the exam-version record.
  - Course practice uses MCQ/MR only; the optional ACT Writing (essay) is a productive task not reproduced in this format.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-0411-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) In ACT Science, a data-representation item asks for a trend shown in a graph. The best approach is to:

- A. Read the trend directly from the axes and plotted data **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: ACT Science rewards reading the given data, not outside knowledge.
- B. Answer from memorised science facts  
  _Rationale:_ Outside facts can conflict with the passage data.
- C. Pick the option with technical vocabulary  
  _Rationale:_ Vocabulary is not evidence of a trend.
- D. Choose the first answer listed  
  _Rationale:_ Order is not a reliable cue.

**MST-0411-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) Which revision best follows ACT English conciseness principles: 'at this point in time'?

- A. 'now' **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: concise standard English prefers 'now' over the wordy phrase.
- B. 'at this current point in time presently'  
  _Rationale:_ More redundant, not less.
- C. 'at the time of this point'  
  _Rationale:_ Still wordy and awkward.
- D. 'in this point of time now'  
  _Rationale:_ Redundant and ungrammatical.

**MST-0411-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO strategies suit the ACT's tight timing?

- A. Maintain a steady pace and avoid over-dwelling on one item **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: pacing is critical given the time limits.
- B. Use elimination to remove clearly wrong options **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: elimination speeds accurate guessing.
- C. Read each science passage fully before any question  
  _Rationale:_ Going straight to data-based questions is often faster.
- D. Leave hard items blank with no guess  
  _Rationale:_ There is no wrong-answer penalty, so guessing is better than blanks.
- E. Recompute every math item twice by default  
  _Rationale:_ Double-checking everything wastes limited time.
