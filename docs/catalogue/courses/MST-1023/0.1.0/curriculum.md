## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1023-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why can a profitable company still run out of cash?

- A. Profit is accrual-based and can differ from cash due to receivables, inventory and timing **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: accrual profit recognises revenue and expenses independently of cash timing.
- B. Profit and cash are always identical by definition  
  _Rationale:_ They are not identical; accruals create timing differences.
- C. Because cash flow ignores all expenses  
  _Rationale:_ The cash flow statement does reflect cash expenses.
- D. Because the balance sheet has no cash line  
  _Rationale:_ The balance sheet reports cash; that is not the reason.

**MST-1023-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO ratios measure a company's short-term liquidity? (Select TWO)

- A. Current ratio **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the current ratio compares current assets to current liabilities.
- B. Quick ratio **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the quick ratio is a stricter liquidity measure excluding inventory.
- C. Debt-to-equity ratio  
  _Rationale:_ Debt-to-equity measures leverage, not short-term liquidity.
- D. Gross margin  
  _Rationale:_ Gross margin measures profitability, not liquidity.

**MST-1023-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) In the DuPont framework, return on equity is decomposed into net margin, asset turnover and which third factor?

- A. The equity (financial leverage) multiplier **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: DuPont multiplies net margin, asset turnover and the equity multiplier.
- B. The current ratio  
  _Rationale:_ The current ratio is not part of the DuPont decomposition.
- C. The dividend yield  
  _Rationale:_ Dividend yield is not a DuPont component.
- D. The quick ratio  
  _Rationale:_ The quick ratio is a liquidity measure, not a DuPont factor.
