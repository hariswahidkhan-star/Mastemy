## Integrative case

A domestic retailer wants to expand abroad and plans to copy its home formula and ownership model everywhere. Apply international-business thinking: assess cultural and institutional differences, account for trade barriers and currency risk, select an appropriate entry mode per market, and decide what to standardise versus adapt.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1832-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why is 'what works at home will work abroad unchanged' a risky assumption?

- A. Cultural, institutional and market differences often require adaptation **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: differences across countries frequently demand local adaptation.
- B. Foreign markets are always identical to the home market  
  _Rationale:_ They typically differ in important ways.
- C. Adaptation is never worthwhile  
  _Rationale:_ Adaptation is often essential to succeed abroad.
- D. Home strategies are always inferior abroad  
  _Rationale:_ The point is to assess fit, not to assume inferiority.

**MST-1832-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which statements about market-entry modes are correct? (Select TWO)

- A. Exporting generally involves lower risk than full foreign direct investment **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: exporting commits fewer resources and is more reversible.
- B. A joint venture shares both control and risk with a partner **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: JVs trade some control for shared risk and local knowledge.
- C. Full ownership always carries the least risk  
  _Rationale:_ FDI typically carries higher risk and commitment.
- D. Entry mode has no effect on risk or control  
  _Rationale:_ Entry mode directly shapes the risk-control trade-off.

**MST-1832-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A firm sells goods abroad priced in a foreign currency that then weakens against its home currency. What is the effect, all else equal?

- A. Its home-currency revenue from those sales falls **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a weaker foreign currency converts into less home currency.
- B. Its home-currency revenue is unaffected  
  _Rationale:_ Exchange-rate moves change the converted value.
- C. Currency movements never affect margins  
  _Rationale:_ They directly affect converted revenue and margins.
- D. The firm automatically profits from the move  
  _Rationale:_ A weakening sales currency reduces converted revenue.
