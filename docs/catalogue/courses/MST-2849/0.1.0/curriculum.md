## Learning outcomes

1. Identify the six simple machines and give an everyday example of each
2. Explain how a lever changes the force needed to move a load
3. Describe how pulleys and inclined planes make work easier
4. Explain the idea of mechanical advantage in simple terms
5. Recognise that machines trade a smaller force for a longer distance
6. Apply safe practice when building and testing machine models

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance. Any experiment ideas are optional and require adult supervision; they are not assessed.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2849-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) How does a ramp (inclined plane) make lifting a heavy box easier?

- A. It lets you use a smaller force over a longer distance **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a ramp trades a smaller push for a longer distance travelled.
- B. It reduces the total work to zero  
  _Rationale:_ The work is similar; the ramp only reduces the force needed.
- C. It makes the box lighter  
  _Rationale:_ The box's weight is unchanged; the effort force is reduced.
- D. It creates extra energy  
  _Rationale:_ Machines do not create energy; they change how force is applied.

**MST-2849-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO are examples of simple machines? (Select TWO.)

- A. A seesaw (a lever) **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a seesaw is a lever with a pivot.
- B. A ramp (an inclined plane) **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a ramp is an inclined plane.
- C. A battery  
  _Rationale:_ A battery stores chemical energy; it is not a simple machine.
- D. A thermometer  
  _Rationale:_ A thermometer measures temperature; it is not a simple machine.

**MST-2849-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A machine lets you lift a load with half the force. What is the trade-off?

- A. You must move the effort over about twice the distance **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: machines trade reduced force for increased distance.
- B. You get the lift for free with no trade-off  
  _Rationale:_ There is always a trade-off; work is roughly conserved.
- C. The load becomes twice as heavy  
  _Rationale:_ The load's weight does not change.
- D. The machine supplies extra energy  
  _Rationale:_ Machines do not create energy.
