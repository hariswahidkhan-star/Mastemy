## Learning outcomes

1. Explain cellular injury, adaptation, inflammation and healing
2. Explain the pathophysiology of immune, haematological and infectious disorders
3. Explain the pathophysiology of cardiovascular, respiratory and renal disease
4. Explain the pathophysiology of endocrine, neurological and neoplastic disease

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every course outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Hands-on operation, the quality of tools and live outputs, and professional judgement are taught by video demonstration and model-answer analysis; the certificate assessment uses single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only and never requires uploads, recorded work, submitted code or peer review.

## Integrative case

A patient with long-standing high blood pressure and diabetes develops chest pain, breathlessness and swollen legs. Trace the cellular and organ-level mechanisms linking the risk factors to the presenting disease and its complications.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1766-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A region of heart muscle dies after a coronary artery is blocked. This localised cell death is best described as:

- A. Necrosis **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: unregulated cell death from ischaemic injury is necrosis, here specifically infarction.
- B. Apoptosis  
  _Rationale:_ Apoptosis is programmed, energy-dependent cell death, not the result of acute ischaemic injury.
- C. Hypertrophy  
  _Rationale:_ Hypertrophy is an increase in cell size, an adaptation, not cell death.
- D. Metaplasia  
  _Rationale:_ Metaplasia is a reversible change of one mature cell type to another, not death.**MST-1766-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) Which change most directly explains the breathlessness of left-sided heart failure?

- A. Pulmonary congestion from blood backing up into the lungs **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a failing left ventricle raises pressure back into the pulmonary circulation, causing congestion and breathlessness.
- B. Peripheral oedema of the ankles  
  _Rationale:_ Ankle oedema is more typical of right-sided failure and does not directly cause breathlessness.
- C. Increased urine output  
  _Rationale:_ Heart failure tends to reduce renal perfusion and urine output, not increase it.
- D. Reduced blood volume  
  _Rationale:_ Heart failure is associated with fluid retention, not reduced blood volume.**MST-1766-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO features point toward a malignant rather than a benign tumour? (Select TWO)

- A. Invasion of surrounding tissue **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: invasion of adjacent tissue is a hallmark of malignancy.
- B. Metastasis to distant sites **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: spread to distant sites defines malignant behaviour.
- C. A well-defined capsule  
  _Rationale:_ A well-defined capsule is typical of a benign tumour.
- D. Slow, non-invasive growth  
  _Rationale:_ Slow, non-invasive growth is characteristic of benign tumours.
