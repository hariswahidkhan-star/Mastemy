## Learning outcomes

1. Explain why scientists classify living things
2. Describe the main kingdoms of life
3. Use features to group organisms
4. Explain how vertebrates and invertebrates differ
5. Use simple keys to identify organisms
6. Describe how classification can change with new evidence

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2869-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Animals WITHOUT a backbone are called:

- A. Invertebrates **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: invertebrates are animals without a backbone.
- B. Vertebrates  
  _Rationale:_ Vertebrates have a backbone.
- C. Mammals  
  _Rationale:_ Mammals are one group of vertebrates.
- D. Fungi  
  _Rationale:_ Fungi are not animals at all.

**MST-2869-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why do scientists classify living things?

- A. To organise and study the variety of life **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: classification brings order and helps study relationships.
- B. To make living things more colourful  
  _Rationale:_ Classification does not change how organisms look.
- C. To reduce the number of species  
  _Rationale:_ Classifying does not remove species.
- D. To stop animals from moving  
  _Rationale:_ Classification has nothing to do with movement.

**MST-2869-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO are examples of vertebrates? (Select TWO.)

- A. A dog **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a dog is a mammal with a backbone.
- B. A sparrow **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a bird has a backbone, so it is a vertebrate.
- C. An earthworm  
  _Rationale:_ An earthworm has no backbone; it is an invertebrate.
- D. A snail  
  _Rationale:_ A snail is an invertebrate.
