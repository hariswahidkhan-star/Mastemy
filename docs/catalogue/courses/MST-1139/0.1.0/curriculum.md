## Learning outcomes

1. Explain conversion-rate optimisation and its process
2. Research and diagnose conversion barriers
3. Design and prioritise experiments and hypotheses
4. Run A/B tests and interpret results responsibly

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Integrative case

A growth analyst must improve a landing page with high traffic but low conversion. They must diagnose barriers with analytics and qualitative input, write prioritised hypotheses, design a valid A/B test, and interpret the results without peeking, defending the decision to ship, iterate, or abandon the variant.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1139-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A good CRO hypothesis should state:

- A. A change, the expected effect, and why it should occur **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: hypotheses are testable and reasoned, not vague.
- B. Only that the page will 'look nicer'  
  _Rationale:_ That is not testable.
- C. The developer's lunch order  
  _Rationale:_ Irrelevant.
- D. A guaranteed revenue figure  
  _Rationale:_ Outcomes are estimated, not guaranteed.

**MST-1139-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO protect the validity of an A/B test? (Select TWO.)

- A. Deciding the sample size and duration in advance **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: pre-registration avoids peeking bias.
- B. Waiting for significance before calling a winner **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: early stopping inflates false positives.
- C. Stopping as soon as the variant is briefly ahead  
  _Rationale:_ That is the peeking error.
- D. Changing the success metric after seeing the data  
  _Rationale:_ That invalidates the test.

**MST-1139-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Combining analytics with session replays and surveys helps CRO by:

- A. Showing both where users drop off and why **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: quantitative finds where, qualitative explains why.
- B. Removing the need for any experiment  
  _Rationale:_ You still test the hypothesis.
- C. Guaranteeing a doubled conversion rate  
  _Rationale:_ No method guarantees results.
- D. Replacing the primary conversion metric  
  _Rationale:_ The metric still anchors the work.
