## Learning outcomes

1. Distinguish fixed, variable, direct, indirect and stepped costs
2. Calculate contribution, break-even and margin of safety
3. Compute and interpret per-unit economics for a product or customer
4. Choose a costing approach and understand overhead allocation
5. Use cost information to support pricing and make-or-buy decisions
6. Explain the assumptions and limits behind a unit-economics model

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2487-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Contribution margin per unit is best defined as:

- A. Selling price per unit minus variable cost per unit **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: contribution covers fixed costs then profit.
- B. Selling price minus all costs including fixed overhead  
  _Rationale:_ That is net profit per unit, not contribution.
- C. Total revenue minus total assets  
  _Rationale:_ Assets are not part of contribution.
- D. Fixed cost divided by units sold  
  _Rationale:_ That is fixed cost per unit, not contribution.

**MST-2487-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO raise a product's break-even volume, all else equal? (Select TWO.)

- A. An increase in fixed costs **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: higher fixed costs need more contribution to cover.
- B. A fall in contribution margin per unit **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: lower per-unit contribution raises units to break even.
- C. An increase in selling price with costs unchanged  
  _Rationale:_ That lowers break-even volume.
- D. A fall in variable cost per unit  
  _Rationale:_ That lowers break-even volume.

**MST-2487-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A one-time tooling cost was already spent and cannot be recovered. In a make-or-buy decision it should be:

- A. Ignored, because it is a sunk cost that does not change with the decision **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: only future, differential costs are relevant.
- B. Added to the 'make' option only  
  _Rationale:_ Sunk costs are irrelevant to the choice.
- C. Split evenly between the options  
  _Rationale:_ Allocating a sunk cost distorts the decision.
- D. Used as the deciding factor  
  _Rationale:_ It is irrelevant and should not decide anything.
