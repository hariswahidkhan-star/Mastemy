## Learning outcomes

1. Name and draw organic structures and identify functional groups
2. Explain hybridisation, resonance and stereochemistry
3. Classify reaction types and draw curly-arrow mechanisms
4. Rationalise reactivity of key functional-group families
5. Interpret basic spectroscopic data for structure elucidation
6. Describe how AI supports retrosynthesis and reaction prediction

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2005-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) In an SN2 reaction, how does the nucleophile approach the electrophilic carbon?

- A. From the side opposite the leaving group, causing inversion of configuration **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: backside attack gives the characteristic inversion.
- B. From the same side as the leaving group, with retention  
  _Rationale:_ That describes neither SN2 geometry nor its stereochemical outcome.
- C. Through a planar carbocation intermediate  
  _Rationale:_ That is SN1, not the concerted SN2 pathway.
- D. Only after the leaving group has fully departed  
  _Rationale:_ SN2 is concerted; bond making and breaking are simultaneous.

**MST-2005-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO features increase the rate of electrophilic aromatic substitution? (Select TWO.)

- A. An electron-donating activating substituent on the ring **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: activating groups raise electron density and speed substitution.
- B. A substituent that stabilises the arenium-ion intermediate **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: stabilising the intermediate lowers the activation barrier.
- C. A strongly electron-withdrawing nitro group  
  _Rationale:_ Nitro groups deactivate the ring and slow substitution.
- D. Removing all substituents to leave benzene  
  _Rationale:_ Benzene is the unactivated baseline, not faster.

**MST-2005-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why must an AI-proposed retrosynthetic step be checked before use?

- A. Suggested reagents or conditions may be infeasible or unselective in practice **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: predictions need experimental and expert feasibility checks.
- B. Retrosynthesis tools cannot propose any valid steps  
  _Rationale:_ They often propose valid steps; verification is still needed.
- C. Every proposed step is guaranteed to work  
  _Rationale:_ No such guarantee exists for predicted chemistry.
- D. Checking is only needed for inorganic reactions  
  _Rationale:_ Verification applies across chemistry.
