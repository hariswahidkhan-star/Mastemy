## Learning outcomes

1. Explain the nanoscale and why material properties change at that scale
2. Describe common nanomaterials and their structures
3. Outline top-down and bottom-up fabrication approaches conceptually
4. Describe characterisation tools used to see and measure nanostructures
5. Identify applications across electronics, medicine, energy and materials
6. Assess safety, environmental and ethical considerations of nanotechnology

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2763-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why can a material behave very differently at the nanoscale than in bulk form?

- A. At the nanoscale, a large fraction of atoms are at the surface and quantum effects can dominate, changing properties **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: high surface-to-volume ratio and quantum effects alter optical, chemical and electronic behaviour.
- B. Because nanoscale materials are simply lighter versions of the bulk  
  _Rationale:_ The change is qualitative (new properties), not just lighter.
- C. Because measuring tools make up the differences  
  _Rationale:_ The differences are real physical effects, not measurement artefacts.
- D. Because nanoscale materials contain different chemical elements by definition  
  _Rationale:_ Same element can show new behaviour at the nanoscale without changing composition.

**MST-2763-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO statements about nanotechnology fabrication are accurate? (Select TWO.)

- A. Top-down approaches carve or pattern structures from larger material **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: top-down methods start big and remove/pattern down to nanoscale.
- B. Bottom-up approaches build structures by assembling atoms or molecules **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: bottom-up methods grow or self-assemble nanostructures from components.
- C. Self-assembly requires no control over conditions  
  _Rationale:_ Self-assembly depends on carefully controlled chemistry and conditions.
- D. Top-down and bottom-up are the same process  
  _Rationale:_ They are distinct, complementary fabrication strategies.

**MST-2763-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why is nanosafety a legitimate consideration for products containing engineered nanoparticles?

- A. Their small size and novel properties can lead to biological and environmental behaviour not seen in the bulk material **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: nanoparticles may interact with cells and ecosystems differently, warranting careful assessment.
- B. Because nanoparticles are always more toxic than any other substance  
  _Rationale:_ Toxicity varies; the point is novel behaviour needing assessment, not blanket toxicity.
- C. Because nanoparticles cannot be measured so risk is unknowable  
  _Rationale:_ They can be characterised; risk assessment is possible, if careful.
- D. Because safety never differs from the bulk material  
  _Rationale:_ Nanoscale behaviour can differ from bulk, which is exactly why safety is considered.
