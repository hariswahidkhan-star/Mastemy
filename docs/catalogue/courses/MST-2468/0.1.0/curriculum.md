## Learning outcomes

1. Explain what current AI can and cannot reliably do for a business
2. Identify where AI can create genuine strategic value in the enterprise
3. Prioritise and sequence AI initiatives against value and feasibility
4. Understand the data, talent and technology foundations AI requires
5. Govern AI responsibly, managing risk, ethics and regulation
6. Lead the organisational change and build the capabilities AI demands

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2468-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A vendor claims its AI will make a bank's lending decisions with near-perfect accuracy and no oversight. What is the soundest executive response?

- A. Treat the certainty claim sceptically and insist on human oversight and responsible-AI controls **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: current AI is probabilistic and high-stakes decisions require oversight and governance.
- B. Accept the claim since vendors validate their own models  
  _Rationale:_ Vendor self-validation is not sufficient assurance.
- C. Deploy immediately to beat competitors  
  _Rationale:_ Speed without controls creates serious risk.
- D. Avoid AI entirely because no claim can be trusted  
  _Rationale:_ Blanket avoidance forfeits genuine value.

**MST-2468-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO factors should most shape how an executive prioritises AI use cases? (Select TWO.)

- A. The business value the use case would create **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: value is a primary prioritisation axis.
- B. The feasibility given data, talent and technology readiness **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: feasibility determines whether value can actually be captured.
- C. How impressive the demo looks to the board  
  _Rationale:_ Demo appeal is not a sound basis for prioritisation.
- D. Whether a competitor announced something similar  
  _Rationale:_ Competitor noise is not a value or feasibility measure.
- E. The novelty of the underlying algorithm  
  _Rationale:_ Novelty does not equal business value.

**MST-2468-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A bank launches 30 disconnected AI pilots with no prioritisation; few reach production. What is the core strategic failure?

- A. Absence of portfolio prioritisation and sequencing against value and feasibility **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: scattering effort without prioritisation and sequencing wastes resources and rarely scales.
- B. Too few pilots were launched  
  _Rationale:_ The problem is lack of focus, not too few pilots.
- C. AI simply cannot be deployed in banking  
  _Rationale:_ Banks deploy AI successfully with focus and governance.
- D. Pilots should never be used for AI  
  _Rationale:_ Pilots are useful when prioritised and sequenced.
