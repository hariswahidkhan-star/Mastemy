## Learning outcomes

1. Explain quality management principles and the process approach
2. Describe the structure and intent of a quality management system
3. Apply risk-based thinking and document control to QMS processes
4. Plan and conduct a process audit against defined criteria
5. Report findings and drive corrective action and continual improvement

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Hands-on operation of the tools and workflows taught, the quality of live outputs, and professional judgement on the job are taught by video demonstration and model-answer analysis; the certificate assessment uses single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only and never requires uploads, recorded work, graded prompts or peer review.

## Integrative case

An internal auditor must audit a purchasing process against a quality management system: plan the audit scope and criteria, gather objective evidence, classify findings, and present nonconformities and improvement opportunities with corrective-action requirements to process owners.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1170-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Which item is a record rather than a controlled document?

- A. A completed inspection log showing results for a batch **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a record is evidence of an activity performed; the completed log qualifies.
- B. A work instruction describing how to inspect  
  _Rationale:_ A work instruction is a controlled document, not a record.
- C. The quality policy  
  _Rationale:_ The policy is a controlled document.
- D. A blank inspection form template  
  _Rationale:_ A blank template is a controlled document until it is filled in.

**MST-1170-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) A well-written nonconformity statement should contain which TWO elements? (Select TWO.)

- A. The specific requirement or criterion not met **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a nonconformity cites the requirement it breaches.
- B. The objective evidence observed **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: objective evidence supports the finding.
- C. The auditor's personal opinion of the staff  
  _Rationale:_ Opinions about people are not valid audit evidence.
- D. A guess at who is to blame  
  _Rationale:_ Blame is not part of an objective nonconformity.

**MST-1170-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) During an audit, which approach best gathers objective evidence?

- A. Ask open questions and verify answers against records and observation **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: triangulating interview, records and observation yields objective evidence.
- B. Accept the process owner's verbal assurance alone  
  _Rationale:_ Unverified assurance is not objective evidence.
- C. Audit only the documents, never the activity  
  _Rationale:_ Documents alone do not confirm the process is followed.
- D. Rely on what the previous audit concluded  
  _Rationale:_ A new audit needs its own current evidence.
