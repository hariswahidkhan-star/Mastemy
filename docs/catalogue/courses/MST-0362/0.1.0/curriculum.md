## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-0362-Q0001** (single-answer-mcq, Select ONE) A responder finds a patient with noisy, partially obstructed breathing. The first priority is to:

- A. Open and maintain the airway **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: airway patency takes priority over other interventions.
- B. Apply a cardiac monitor  
  _Rationale:_ Monitoring is important but secondary to an airway threat.
- C. Obtain a full medical history  
  _Rationale:_ History follows management of immediate life threats.
- D. Document the scene  
  _Rationale:_ Documentation is not an immediate clinical priority.

**MST-0362-Q0002** (single-answer-mcq, Select ONE) In a described shock presentation with poor perfusion after significant bleeding, the shock state is best classified as:

- A. Hypovolaemic **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: blood loss reducing circulating volume produces hypovolaemic shock.
- B. Cardiogenic  
  _Rationale:_ Cardiogenic shock stems from pump failure, not volume loss.
- C. Neurogenic  
  _Rationale:_ Neurogenic shock follows loss of sympathetic tone, not haemorrhage.
- D. Anaphylactic  
  _Rationale:_ Anaphylactic shock is an allergic response, not described here.

**MST-0362-Q0003** (multiple-answer-selection, Select TWO) Which TWO reflect correct prehospital reasoning for a multi-system trauma patient?

- A. Address life threats before treating visible minor injuries **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: life threats take priority over cosmetic or minor injuries.
- B. Maintain scene safety throughout **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: responder and patient safety is a continuous priority.
- C. Transport only after every injury is fully treated on scene  
  _Rationale:_ Definitive care often requires transport; on-scene time should be limited for serious trauma.
- D. Treat a visible laceration before checking circulation  
  _Rationale:_ Circulation threats outrank a non-bleeding laceration.
- E. Ignore mechanism of injury  
  _Rationale:_ Mechanism informs the index of suspicion and must be considered.
