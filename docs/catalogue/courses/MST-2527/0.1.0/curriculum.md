## Learning outcomes

1. Compute tax bases for assets and liabilities in complex situations
2. Analyse temporary differences arising from revaluations, fair-value adjustments and leases
3. Account for deferred tax on business combinations and consolidation adjustments
4. Assess recoverability of deferred tax assets, including unused losses and credits
5. Account for deferred tax recognised in profit or loss, other comprehensive income and equity
6. Handle uncertain tax treatments and changes in tax rates

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Integrative case

A group completes an acquisition with fair-value uplifts, revalues property through OCI, carries forward tax losses in one subsidiary, holds unrealised intra-group profit in inventory, and faces an enacted tax-rate change and one uncertain tax position; work through the deferred tax computations, recoverability assessment, allocation between profit/OCI/equity, and the effect of the rate change.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2527-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) How is a taxable temporary difference best described?

- A. A difference between the carrying amount and the tax base that will result in taxable amounts in future periods **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a taxable temporary difference increases future taxable profit and gives rise to a deferred tax liability.
- B. A permanent difference that never reverses  
  _Rationale:_ Permanent differences do not create deferred tax.
- C. A difference that only affects the current tax charge  
  _Rationale:_ Temporary differences affect deferred, not current, tax.
- D. The difference between accounting profit and cash received  
  _Rationale:_ That is not the definition of a temporary difference.

**MST-2527-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO adjustments commonly create deferred tax in consolidated financial statements? (Select TWO.)

- A. Fair-value uplifts to the acquiree's identifiable assets on a business combination **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: fair-value uplifts with no change in tax base create temporary differences.
- B. Elimination of unrealised profit on intra-group inventory **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: eliminating unrealised intra-group profit creates a deferred tax effect.
- C. Recording a dividend received from a third party  
  _Rationale:_ A simple dividend receipt does not of itself create a consolidation deferred tax adjustment.
- D. Paying salaries to group staff  
  _Rationale:_ Routine salary payments do not create consolidation temporary differences.

**MST-2527-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A company revalues a building upward, recognising the gain in other comprehensive income, and the tax rate then changes. Where is the effect of the rate change on the related deferred tax recognised?

- A. In other comprehensive income, consistent with where the original item was recognised (backwards tracing) **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: tax follows the item; the rate-change effect is recognised in OCI.
- B. Entirely in profit or loss regardless of the original item  
  _Rationale:_ That ignores backwards tracing to OCI.
- C. Directly in share capital  
  _Rationale:_ It is not a share-capital movement.
- D. Not at all, because deferred tax is never remeasured  
  _Rationale:_ Deferred tax is remeasured for enacted rate changes.
