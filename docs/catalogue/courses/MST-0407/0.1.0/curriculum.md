## Learning outcomes

1. Apply listening comprehension strategies to workplace and everyday English
2. Apply reading comprehension strategies to business texts and correspondence
3. Analyse grammar and vocabulary in incomplete-sentence and text-completion tasks
4. Integrate listening and reading skills under test-like timing

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess:
  - Knowledge-practice forms use MCQ/MR only; official item formats not reproducible in this format are listed in the exam-version record.
  - The official TOEIC Listening & Reading test is receptive; speaking and writing productive skills are not in scope for this course (see the separate Speaking & Writing course).

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-0407-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) 'The report ___ by Friday.' Which completion is grammatically correct for a future expectation?

- A. will be finished **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: future passive fits an expected completion by a deadline.
- B. finishing  
  _Rationale:_ A bare -ing form cannot be the main verb here.
- C. has finish  
  _Rationale:_ Incorrect verb form after 'has'.
- D. finish  
  _Rationale:_ Lacks tense and voice agreement for the subject.

**MST-0407-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) In a short-talk listening item asking for the speaker's main purpose, the best approach is to:

- A. Listen for the overall goal, not just repeated words **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: purpose questions test gist, not word recognition.
- B. Pick the option sharing the most words with the audio  
  _Rationale:_ Word overlap is a common distractor, not evidence of purpose.
- C. Assume the purpose is always a complaint  
  _Rationale:_ Purpose varies; assuming one type is unreliable.
- D. Answer before listening to save time  
  _Rationale:_ Guessing without listening is unreliable for gist items.

**MST-0407-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO strategies help manage timing in the TOEIC Reading section?

- A. Locate answers rather than reading every word **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: targeted reading saves time on detail items.
- B. Budget time per part and move on from stuck items **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: pacing prevents running out of time.
- C. Read each passage three times before answering  
  _Rationale:_ Re-reading everything wastes limited time.
- D. Leave all hard items for an imaginary extra section  
  _Rationale:_ There is no extra time section; this risks blanks.
- E. Answer only the questions you find easy and skip the rest permanently  
  _Rationale:_ Unanswered items score nothing; at least an educated guess is better.
