## Learning outcomes

1. Explain how energy and electricity markets set prices and dispatch generation
2. Compute and interpret the levelised cost of energy and its limits
3. Describe policy instruments: carbon pricing, subsidies, standards and auctions
4. Analyse how externalities and market failures justify energy policy
5. Evaluate trade-offs between affordability, security and sustainability
6. Interpret an investment case for an energy project under uncertainty

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2367-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) In a merit-order electricity market, what sets the wholesale clearing price in each period?

- A. The marginal cost of the most expensive plant needed to meet demand **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ The marginal (last dispatched) plant sets the clearing price for all.
- B. The average cost of all plants running  
  _Rationale:_ Markets clear at the marginal, not the average, cost.
- C. The cheapest plant's cost  
  _Rationale:_ The cheapest sets the bottom of the stack, not the price.
- D. A fixed government tariff per kWh  
  _Rationale:_ Competitive wholesale markets are not set by a fixed tariff.

**MST-2367-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO are limitations of levelised cost of energy (LCOE) as a decision metric? (Select TWO.)

- A. It ignores system costs such as balancing and transmission **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ LCOE omits integration and system costs.
- B. It does not capture the value of when energy is delivered **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ LCOE treats all output as equal regardless of timing or value.
- C. It cannot be calculated for any renewable technology  
  _Rationale:_ LCOE is routinely calculated for renewables.
- D. It always equals the market clearing price  
  _Rationale:_ LCOE is a cost metric, not the market price.

**MST-2367-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why do economists argue a carbon price can improve market outcomes?

- A. It internalises the external cost of emissions that the market otherwise ignores **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Carbon pricing corrects an externality by making emitters pay its cost.
- B. It guarantees the lowest possible electricity bills immediately  
  _Rationale:_ A carbon price may raise some prices in the short run.
- C. It removes the need to measure emissions  
  _Rationale:_ Pricing requires measuring emissions, not removing the need.
- D. It eliminates all market failures at once  
  _Rationale:_ It addresses the emissions externality, not every failure.
