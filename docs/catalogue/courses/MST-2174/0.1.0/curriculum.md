## Learning outcomes

1. Explain systems engineering as applied to complex defense programs at a concept level
2. Describe the acquisition and development lifecycle conceptually
3. Explain requirements, architecture and integration in large systems
4. Describe verification, validation and test-and-evaluation concepts
5. Explain reliability, safety and configuration management at a high level
6. Communicate program trade-offs and risk to stakeholders

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2174-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) What is the primary aim of systems engineering on a large defense program?

- A. To ensure the whole system meets stakeholder needs through disciplined requirements, integration and verification **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: systems engineering integrates parts into a validated whole.
- B. To design a single component in isolation  
  _Rationale:_ Systems engineering spans the whole system, not one part.
- C. To avoid ever testing the system  
  _Rationale:_ Verification and testing are central to systems engineering.
- D. To skip stakeholder input to move faster  
  _Rationale:_ Stakeholder needs drive the whole process.

**MST-2174-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO activities distinguish verification from validation? (Select TWO.)

- A. Verification checks the system was built to its requirements **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: verification is 'built it right'.
- B. Validation checks the system meets the stakeholders' real needs **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: validation is 'built the right thing'.
- C. Verification means ignoring the requirements  
  _Rationale:_ Verification is based on the requirements.
- D. Validation means never consulting stakeholders  
  _Rationale:_ Validation centres on stakeholder needs.

**MST-2174-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why is interface management planned early in a complex program?

- A. Interfaces between subsystems are a common source of integration risk **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: early interface control reduces costly late surprises.
- B. Because interfaces never cause any problems  
  _Rationale:_ Interfaces are a leading integration risk.
- C. Because it lets teams ignore requirements  
  _Rationale:_ Interface management supports, not replaces, requirements.
- D. Because integration is never needed  
  _Rationale:_ Integration is essential for complex systems.
