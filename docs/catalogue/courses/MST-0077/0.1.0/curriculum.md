## Learning outcomes

1. Describe futures and options markets, contracts and market participants
2. Apply hedging, speculation and margin mechanics to trading scenarios
3. Evaluate order types, price analysis and risk-management techniques
4. Analyse the regulatory framework, disclosure and compliance duties for futures

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-0077-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A trader is told that initial margin on a futures contract is a 'down payment on ownership'. The correct characterisation is that margin is:

- A. A performance bond ensuring the trader can meet obligations **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: futures margin is a good-faith performance bond, not equity ownership.
- B. A loan from the clearinghouse  
  _Rationale:_ Margin is not a loan; it is a performance bond.
- C. A non-refundable fee  
  _Rationale:_ Margin can be returned when the position is closed in good standing.
- D. The full contract value  
  _Rationale:_ Margin is a fraction of contract value, not the whole.

**MST-0077-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) A commercial producer wants to lock in a selling price for a crop it will harvest later. The most appropriate strategy is to:

- A. Sell (short) futures contracts to hedge the expected output **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a short hedge locks in a selling price for future output.
- B. Buy call options with no underlying position  
  _Rationale:_ Buying calls does not lock in a selling price for output.
- C. Speculate on unrelated energy futures  
  _Rationale:_ Unrelated speculation does not hedge the crop.
- D. Do nothing and accept full price risk  
  _Rationale:_ Doing nothing leaves the producer fully exposed.

**MST-0077-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Before an associated person opens a futures account for a retail customer, which TWO are required or prohibited actions? (Select TWO)

- A. Provide the required risk-disclosure document **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: risk disclosure is required before trading.
- B. Avoid guaranteeing the customer against losses **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: guaranteeing against loss is a prohibited practice.
- C. Promise specific profits to win the account  
  _Rationale:_ Promising profits is a prohibited sales practice.
- D. Trade the account without authorisation  
  _Rationale:_ Unauthorised trading is prohibited.
- E. Hide commissions from the customer  
  _Rationale:_ Concealing costs breaches disclosure duties.
