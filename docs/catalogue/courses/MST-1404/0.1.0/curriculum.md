## Learning outcomes

1. Identify AI use cases in pharmacy practice
2. Interpret AI outputs with professional judgement
3. Use AI for interaction and dosing checks responsibly
4. Protect patient data and privacy
5. Recognise safety, bias and oversight requirements

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1404-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) An AI tool flags no interaction for a complex regimen. How should a pharmacist respond?

- A. Treat it as one input and apply professional judgement and checks **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: AI alerts support but do not replace pharmacist judgement.
- B. Accept it as final with no review  
  _Rationale:_ Professional judgement must remain in control.
- C. Assume the tool caught every possible issue  
  _Rationale:_ Tools can miss interactions.
- D. Ignore the regimen's complexity  
  _Rationale:_ Complexity warrants extra care.

**MST-1404-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO practices support safe AI use in pharmacy? (Select TWO.)

- A. Keep pharmacist oversight of AI-assisted checks **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: oversight protects patients.
- B. Verify AI-provided drug information before sharing it **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: verification guards against errors.
- C. Share patient data without consent  
  _Rationale:_ Consent and privacy must be respected.
- D. Follow every AI alert without judgement  
  _Rationale:_ Blind following ignores false alerts and context.

**MST-1404-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why verify AI-provided patient information before giving it out?

- A. AI can produce inaccurate or outdated information that could harm patients **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: unverified AI information can be wrong and unsafe.
- B. AI information is always correct  
  _Rationale:_ AI can be inaccurate or outdated.
- C. Verification is never needed  
  _Rationale:_ Verification protects patient safety.
- D. Only the dose ever matters  
  _Rationale:_ All information given to patients should be accurate.
