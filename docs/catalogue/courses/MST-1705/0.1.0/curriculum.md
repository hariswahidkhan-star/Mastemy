## Learning outcomes

1. Explain the main asset classes and how investment markets function
2. Describe the risk-return relationship and the role of diversification
3. Apply time value of money and basic valuation to investment decisions
4. Compare pooled vehicles, costs and tax wrappers at a conceptual level
5. Build a simple goal-based portfolio aligned to risk tolerance and horizon

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1705-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why does holding assets with low correlation reduce portfolio risk?

- A. Their returns do not move together, so swings partly offset **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: imperfect correlation dampens overall volatility.
- B. Low-correlation assets never lose value  
  _Rationale:_ They can still fall; correlation is about co-movement, not safety.
- C. It guarantees a higher return  
  _Rationale:_ Diversification targets risk, not guaranteed higher return.
- D. It removes market risk entirely  
  _Rationale:_ Systematic market risk cannot be diversified away.

**MST-1705-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) An investor compares two equity funds tracking the same index. Which TWO factors most justify choosing the cheaper one? (Select TWO.)

- A. A lower ongoing expense ratio **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: lower recurring fees compound to a large difference over time.
- B. Lower tracking difference to the index **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: closer tracking means the fund delivers the index return more faithfully.
- C. A higher past one-year return  
  _Rationale:_ Past return does not predict future results and may reflect noise.
- D. A more recognisable brand name  
  _Rationale:_ Brand does not improve net returns for an index fund.

**MST-1705-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) An investor with a 20-year horizon panics during a market fall and sells to cash. What is the main risk of this action?

- A. Locking in losses and missing the recovery **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: selling low realises the loss and risks missing rebounds.
- B. Paying lower fees on cash  
  _Rationale:_ Fee level is not the main issue; realised loss is.
- C. Becoming over-diversified  
  _Rationale:_ Moving to cash reduces, not increases, diversification.
- D. Increasing expected long-run return  
  _Rationale:_ Abandoning the plan typically lowers long-run outcomes.
