## Learning outcomes

1. Recognise a core set of high-frequency words instantly
2. Explain why some common words are learned by sight
3. Read sight words quickly within simple sentences
4. Spell frequently used sight words correctly
5. Combine sight words and decoding to read fluently
6. Build automatic recognition to free attention for meaning

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2876-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why do we learn words like 'the' and 'was' by sight?

- A. Because they do not follow regular sounding-out rules **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: these irregular words are learned by sight.
- B. Because they are very long words  
  _Rationale:_ They are short; the issue is irregular spelling.
- C. Because they are never used in books  
  _Rationale:_ They are among the most common words in books.
- D. Because they have no letters  
  _Rationale:_ They are made of letters like any word.

**MST-2876-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) Which word is the sight word in the sentence 'I can see the dog'?

- A. the **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: 'the' is a common sight word.
- B. dog  
  _Rationale:_ 'dog' is decodable by blending d-o-g.
- C. can  
  _Rationale:_ 'can' is decodable by blending c-a-n.
- D. see  
  _Rationale:_ 'see' can be decoded with the 'ee' sound.

**MST-2876-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO sight words are often mixed up because they look similar? (Select TWO.)

- A. was **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: 'was' is often confused with 'saw'.
- B. saw **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: 'saw' is often confused with 'was'.
- C. and  
  _Rationale:_ 'and' is not usually confused with a look-alike word here.
- D. it  
  _Rationale:_ 'it' is short and not a common mix-up word.
