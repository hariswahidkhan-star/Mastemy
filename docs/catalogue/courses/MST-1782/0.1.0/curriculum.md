## Learning outcomes

1. Explain the purpose of the clinical record and the principles of accurate documentation
2. Apply the characteristics of high-quality documentation: accurate, complete, timely and legible
3. Distinguish common documentation formats such as SOAP and structured EHR entries
4. Identify how documentation supports coding, billing, handoffs and continuity of care
5. Recognise legal, ethical and confidentiality obligations attached to the record

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Selection items cannot demonstrate production clinical-writing ability on real patient records; that is taught through instructor-built worked examples and model-note analysis.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1782-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A nurse realises an entry made two hours ago recorded the wrong medication dose. What is the correct way to fix the record?

- A. Add a dated, signed correction that marks the original as an error while keeping it visible **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the record is a legal document; errors are amended with a tracked, dated and signed correction, not erased.
- B. Delete the original entry and type the right dose in its place  
  _Rationale:_ Overwriting destroys the audit trail and can appear as concealment.
- C. Use correction fluid or black out the line on the printout  
  _Rationale:_ Obscuring the original removes evidence of what was recorded and when.
- D. Leave it unchanged because the next clinician will notice  
  _Rationale:_ Leaving a known error uncorrected risks patient harm and misrepresents care.

**MST-1782-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO features make a clinical note higher quality? (Select TWO.)

- A. It records the clinical reasoning behind the assessment and plan **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: documented reasoning lets other clinicians follow and safely continue care.
- B. It is completed close to the time of the encounter **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: timely entries are more accurate and support continuity.
- C. It is as long as possible to show thoroughness  
  _Rationale:_ Length is not quality; note bloat hides the clinically relevant detail.
- D. It copies the previous visit's note forward unchanged  
  _Rationale:_ Unedited copy-forward content misrepresents the current encounter.

**MST-1782-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) In a SOAP note, a patient's reported symptoms described in their own words belong in which section?

- A. Subjective **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the Subjective section captures what the patient reports, including symptoms in their own words.
- B. Objective  
  _Rationale:_ Objective holds measurable findings such as vital signs and exam results, not patient-reported symptoms.
- C. Assessment  
  _Rationale:_ Assessment records the clinician's interpretation and diagnosis, not the raw report.
- D. Plan  
  _Rationale:_ Plan documents next steps such as tests and treatment, not the patient's report.
