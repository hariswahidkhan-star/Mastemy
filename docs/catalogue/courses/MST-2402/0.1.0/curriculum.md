## Learning outcomes

1. Describe the main stages of vehicle manufacturing from stamping to final assembly
2. Explain lean production principles and the Toyota Production System
3. Analyse assembly-line balancing and takt time
4. Describe quality systems, inspection and defect prevention
5. Explain supply chain, logistics and just-in-time delivery
6. Apply continuous-improvement thinking to a production problem

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2402-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A line must build 480 vehicles in an 8-hour shift with no breaks. What is the approximate takt time?

- A. 1 second per vehicle  
  _Rationale:_ This ignores that there are 28,800 seconds in the shift for 480 units.
- B. 60 seconds per vehicle **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Takt = available time / demand = 28,800 s / 480 = 60 s per vehicle.
- C. 480 seconds per vehicle  
  _Rationale:_ This confuses demand count with time per unit.
- D. 8 hours per vehicle  
  _Rationale:_ That is the whole shift, not the per-unit pace.

**MST-2402-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) In the Toyota Production System, what does 'jidoka' (autonomation) primarily refer to?

- A. Running every machine at maximum speed regardless of defects  
  _Rationale:_ That contradicts the quality focus of jidoka.
- B. Giving machines and operators the ability to stop production automatically when an abnormality is detected **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Jidoka builds in the ability to halt when a defect or problem occurs, so quality issues are not passed downstream.
- C. Holding large buffer inventories between stations  
  _Rationale:_ That is the opposite of lean inventory practice.
- D. Eliminating all human workers from the plant  
  _Rationale:_ Jidoka is about human-machine quality cooperation, not removing people.

**MST-2402-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) A continuous-improvement team wants to reduce waste on an assembly line. Select TWO activities that lean production classifies as waste (muda) to be reduced.

- A. Overproduction of parts ahead of demand **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Overproduction is the foremost of the seven wastes in lean thinking.
- B. Operators waiting for parts that have not arrived **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Waiting is a recognised form of waste that adds no value.
- C. Value-adding assembly operations the customer pays for  
  _Rationale:_ Value-adding work is not waste; it is the purpose of the line.
- D. Standardised work that stabilises the process  
  _Rationale:_ Standardised work is a lean enabler, not a waste.
- E. Scheduled preventive maintenance that avoids breakdowns  
  _Rationale:_ Planned maintenance reduces losses rather than being waste itself.
