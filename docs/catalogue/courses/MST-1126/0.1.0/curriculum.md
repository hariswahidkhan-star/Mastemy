## Learning outcomes

1. Plan instructional video and define learning goals
2. Script and storyboard instructional content
3. Set up screen recording and capture
4. Record clear narration and on-camera segments
5. Edit instructional video for clarity
6. Export, caption and publish instructional video

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Integrative case

A subject expert must produce a short instructional video course on a software tool. They must define learning objectives, script and storyboard, set up clean screen capture and narration, edit for clarity with callouts, and export captioned, accessible video, defending choices that keep cognitive load low for learners.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1126-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) The first step in producing an instructional video should be to:

- A. Define what the learner should be able to do afterwards **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: objectives drive scripting, capture and editing.
- B. Buy the most expensive camera  
  _Rationale:_ Gear follows the learning design.
- C. Record a full take immediately  
  _Rationale:_ Recording follows planning.
- D. Choose background music  
  _Rationale:_ A late, minor decision.

**MST-1126-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO editing techniques reduce learner cognitive load? (Select TWO.)

- A. Zooms and callouts that direct attention to the relevant area **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: visual cueing focuses the learner.
- B. Trimming dead time and mis-clicks **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: tighter pacing keeps attention.
- C. Adding loud music over the narration  
  _Rationale:_ That competes with the explanation.
- D. Keeping every hesitation and pause  
  _Rationale:_ Increases load and loses viewers.

**MST-1126-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Captions on instructional video primarily:

- A. Improve accessibility and comprehension for many learners **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: captions aid accessibility and understanding.
- B. Increase the export resolution  
  _Rationale:_ Captions do not change resolution.
- C. Replace the need for a script  
  _Rationale:_ Captions derive from, not replace, the script.
- D. Light the on-camera subject  
  _Rationale:_ Unrelated to captions.
