## Learning outcomes

1. Recognise common coins and their values
2. Add a small number of coins to find a total
3. Work out simple change from a small amount
4. Read o'clock and half past on a clock face
5. Name the days of the week and months in order
6. Order events in a day using before, after and next

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Modules

### M01 Recognising coins (25% (design weight), design weight)

- Worked applications: (1) Sort coins into groups of the same value; (2) Say which coin is worth the most
- Common misconception addressed: Thinking the biggest coin is always worth the most
- Module check: 10 items / 10 min, threshold 75%

## Integrative case

Play shopkeeper and customer at a classroom stall: price items in coins, add up what a customer buys, give the right change, and note the time each 'shop' opens and closes using o'clock and half past.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2813-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Which coin is worth the most?

- A. A 10 coin **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: 10 is the largest value among these coins.
- B. A 1 coin  
  _Rationale:_ 1 is the smallest value here.
- C. A 2 coin  
  _Rationale:_ 2 is worth less than 10.
- D. A 5 coin  
  _Rationale:_ 5 is worth less than 10.

**MST-2813-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Select TWO pairs of coins that make 10 altogether. (Select TWO.)

- A. 5 and 5 **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: 5 + 5 = 10.
- B. 8 and 2 **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: 8 + 2 = 10.
- C. 6 and 2  
  _Rationale:_ 6 + 2 = 8, not 10.
- D. 4 and 4  
  _Rationale:_ 4 + 4 = 8, not 10.

**MST-2813-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) The clock shows the hour hand on 2 and the minute hand on 12. What time is it?

- A. 2 o'clock **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: minute hand on 12 means o'clock, and the hour hand points to 2.
- B. Half past 2  
  _Rationale:_ Half past has the minute hand on 6, not 12.
- C. 12 o'clock  
  _Rationale:_ The hour hand is on 2, so it is 2 o'clock.
- D. 2 minutes past  
  _Rationale:_ The minute hand on 12 means the start of the hour, not 2 minutes.
