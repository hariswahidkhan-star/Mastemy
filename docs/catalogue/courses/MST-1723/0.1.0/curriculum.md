## Learning outcomes

1. Why and when to value
2. Normalising the numbers
3. Multiples approach
4. Discounted cash flow
5. Adjustments and judgement

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses conceptual knowledge through selection items; this is general education, not a valuation opinion or investment advice, and it does not assess a defensible valuation of a specific business.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1723-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Normalising an SME's earnings before valuation typically involves:

- A. Adjusting for an owner's above-market salary and one-off items **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: normalisation restores sustainable, transferable earnings.
- B. Adding back all expenses to maximise the figure  
  _Rationale:_ Only non-recurring or owner-specific items are adjusted, not all expenses.
- C. Using the single best year ever recorded  
  _Rationale:_ A cherry-picked peak year is not sustainable earnings.
- D. Ignoring owner compensation entirely  
  _Rationale:_ Owner pay must be adjusted to a market rate, not ignored.

**MST-1723-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select ALL that apply) Which two adjustments are commonly applied when valuing a small, privately held business? (Select TWO) (Select TWO)

- A. A discount for lack of marketability **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: private stakes are harder to sell, warranting a discount.
- B. A discount for a minority (non-controlling) interest **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a minority stake lacks control and is often discounted.
- C. A premium because it is unlisted  
  _Rationale:_ Being unlisted usually reduces, not raises, value.
- D. Ignoring risk because it is a family firm  
  _Rationale:_ Family ownership does not remove risk.

**MST-1723-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Applying a large listed company's valuation multiple directly to a tiny SME tends to:

- A. Overstate the SME's value **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: small firms are riskier and less liquid, warranting lower multiples.
- B. Understate the SME's value  
  _Rationale:_ Large-company multiples are generally higher, overstating SME value.
- C. Have no effect on the valuation  
  _Rationale:_ The multiple directly drives the result.
- D. Guarantee an accurate valuation  
  _Rationale:_ Mismatched comparables reduce accuracy.
