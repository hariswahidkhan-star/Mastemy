## Learning outcomes

1. Explain the purpose and types of budgets
2. Build an operating budget from drivers and assumptions
3. Apply forecasting techniques including rolling forecasts
4. Perform variance analysis against budget and forecast
5. Communicate budget assumptions and risks clearly

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1699-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) The key difference between a budget and a forecast is that a budget:

- A. Is a fixed plan and target, while a forecast is an updated expectation of the likely outcome **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a budget is the plan; a forecast reflects the latest expected result.
- B. Is always more accurate than a forecast  
  _Rationale:_ Forecasts, updated with actuals, are often closer to reality.
- C. Is prepared after the year ends  
  _Rationale:_ Budgets are set before the period.
- D. Never contains assumptions  
  _Rationale:_ Budgets rest on explicit assumptions.

**MST-1699-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO practices improve the usefulness of a budget? (Select TWO.)

- A. Building it from measurable drivers and stated assumptions **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: driver-based budgets are transparent and testable.
- B. Providing a sensitivity range around key assumptions **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: sensitivities show how outcomes change if assumptions move.
- C. Copying last year and adding a flat percentage with no analysis  
  _Rationale:_ This ignores real drivers and change.
- D. Hiding the assumptions from decision-makers  
  _Rationale:_ Transparency of assumptions is essential.

**MST-1699-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A favourable sales variance caused entirely by higher selling prices, not more units, is best explained as:

- A. A price variance, with volume flat **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: isolating price from volume explains the true cause.
- B. A volume variance  
  _Rationale:_ Volume was flat in this case.
- C. Proof the budget was wrong  
  _Rationale:_ A variance reflects a difference, not necessarily a faulty budget.
- D. Irrelevant to management  
  _Rationale:_ Understanding the driver is useful to management.
