## Learning outcomes

1. Explain why security awareness matters and the role of people
2. Recognise everyday threats such as phishing and unsafe browsing
3. Apply good password and authentication habits
4. Handle data and devices safely in and out of the office
5. Report incidents promptly and correctly

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Integrative case

A new employee joins a company and needs to work safely from day one. Walk through the everyday risks they will face, the habits that keep accounts and data safe, how to spot a suspicious message, and exactly what to do if they think something has gone wrong.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1677-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Which is the safest response to an unexpected email asking you to urgently confirm your password via a link?

- A. Do not click; verify through a known official channel and report it **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: urgency and credential requests are classic phishing signs.
- B. Click the link and enter your password quickly  
  _Rationale:_ This is exactly what a phishing attack wants.
- C. Forward it to all colleagues to warn them by example  
  _Rationale:_ Forwarding the live link spreads the risk.
- D. Reply with your password to save time  
  _Rationale:_ Legitimate services never ask for your password by email.

**MST-1677-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO habits improve account security? (Select TWO.)

- A. Using a unique strong password per account, ideally via a manager **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: unique passwords limit the damage of any one breach.
- B. Enabling multi-factor authentication **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: MFA stops a stolen password from being enough.
- C. Reusing one memorable password everywhere  
  _Rationale:_ Reuse means one breach compromises many accounts.
- D. Writing passwords on a sticky note on the monitor  
  _Rationale:_ Visible written passwords are easily stolen.

**MST-1677-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) You realise you clicked a suspicious link yesterday. What should you do?

- A. Report it promptly so the team can check and contain any impact **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: prompt reporting enables quick containment.
- B. Say nothing and hope nothing happened  
  _Rationale:_ Silence delays containment and increases risk.
- C. Delete your browser history to hide it  
  _Rationale:_ Hiding it removes evidence and does not reduce risk.
- D. Wait a month to see if anything breaks  
  _Rationale:_ Delay gives an attacker more time.
