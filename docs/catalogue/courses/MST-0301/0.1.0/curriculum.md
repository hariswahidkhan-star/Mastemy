## Learning outcomes

1. Explain core payroll concepts and worker classification
2. Calculate gross-to-net pay including taxes and deductions
3. Describe payroll compliance, record-keeping and reporting obligations
4. Describe payroll systems, processes and professional administration

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-0301-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Under the FLSA, a non-exempt employee works 46 hours in a workweek. How are the extra hours generally compensated?

- A. At one-and-one-half times the regular rate for hours over 40 **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: non-exempt employees receive at least 1.5x the regular rate for hours worked over 40 in a workweek.
- B. At the regular rate with no premium  
  _Rationale:_ Hours over 40 for non-exempt workers require an overtime premium.
- C. At double the regular rate for all 46 hours  
  _Rationale:_ Overtime applies only to hours over 40, and the FLSA minimum premium is 1.5x, not 2x.
- D. Not at all, because salaried rules apply  
  _Rationale:_ The employee is non-exempt, so salaried-exempt rules do not apply.

**MST-0301-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) When computing taxable wages, how are qualifying pre-tax (Section 125) deductions treated?

- A. Subtracted from gross pay before income tax is withheld **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: qualifying pre-tax deductions reduce the wage base before applicable taxes are calculated.
- B. Added to gross pay before tax is withheld  
  _Rationale:_ Deductions reduce, not increase, the wage base.
- C. Withheld only after net pay is calculated  
  _Rationale:_ Pre-tax deductions apply before tax calculation, not after net pay.
- D. Ignored entirely for tax purposes  
  _Rationale:_ They are not ignored; they specifically reduce taxable wages.

**MST-0301-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Select TWO factors that typically indicate a worker should be classified as an employee rather than an independent contractor.

- A. The business controls how and when the work is performed **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: behavioural control over how/when work is done points to employee status.
- B. The worker is economically dependent on the business for ongoing work **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: economic dependence and an ongoing relationship point to employee status.
- C. The worker offers services to the general public and has multiple clients  
  _Rationale:_ Serving many clients points toward independent-contractor status.
- D. The worker supplies their own tools and bears profit/loss risk  
  _Rationale:_ Bearing business risk and supplying tools points toward contractor status.
- E. The worker sets their own business insurance and files as a business  
  _Rationale:_ Operating as an independent business points toward contractor status.
