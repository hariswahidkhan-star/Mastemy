## Learning outcomes

1. Explain why atoms bond to achieve stable electron arrangements
2. Describe ionic bonding as the transfer of electrons between atoms
3. Describe covalent bonding as the sharing of electrons between atoms
4. Relate bonding type to simple properties of substances
5. Represent simple molecules and ions with dot-and-cross diagrams
6. Appreciate the limits of simple bonding models

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance. Any experiment ideas are optional and require adult supervision; they are not assessed.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2858-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why do most atoms form chemical bonds?

- A. To achieve a stable, full outer electron shell **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: atoms bond to reach a stable electron arrangement like the noble gases.
- B. To become heavier  
  _Rationale:_ Mass change is not the driver of bonding.
- C. To change colour  
  _Rationale:_ Colour is not why atoms bond.
- D. To gain more neutrons  
  _Rationale:_ Bonding involves electrons, not neutrons.

**MST-2858-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO statements describe ionic bonding? (Select TWO.)

- A. Electrons are transferred from one atom to another **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: ionic bonding involves electron transfer.
- B. It forms positive and negative ions that attract each other **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: oppositely charged ions attract in ionic bonding.
- C. Electrons are shared between atoms  
  _Rationale:_ Sharing describes covalent bonding, not ionic.
- D. It only occurs between two non-metals  
  _Rationale:_ Ionic bonding is typically between a metal and a non-metal.

**MST-2858-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A substance has a low melting point and exists as small separate molecules. Which bonding is most likely?

- A. Covalent bonding within simple molecules **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: simple molecular (covalent) substances have low melting points.
- B. Ionic bonding  
  _Rationale:_ Ionic compounds have high melting points and are not small separate molecules.
- C. No bonding at all  
  _Rationale:_ The molecules are held together by covalent bonds.
- D. Bonding that makes it a metal  
  _Rationale:_ This describes a simple molecular covalent substance, not a metal.
