## Learning outcomes

1. Describe the structure of a standard clinical history as an educational framework
2. Explain the components of a systematic physical examination conceptually
3. Summarise principles of effective clinical communication and consent
4. Describe vital signs and what each conceptually indicates
5. Explain documentation principles and the purpose of structured notes
6. Describe infection-prevention and safety behaviours in clinical settings

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2133-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) In the inspect-palpate-percuss-auscultate framework, which step is performed FIRST?

- A. Inspection **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: inspection (observation) is performed first before touching the patient.
- B. Auscultation  
  _Rationale:_ Auscultation is typically later in the sequence.
- C. Percussion  
  _Rationale:_ Percussion follows palpation, not first.
- D. Palpation  
  _Rationale:_ Palpation follows inspection.

**MST-2133-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO behaviours support good clinical communication? (Select TWO.)

- A. Using open questions early in the history **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: open questions invite the patient to describe the problem in their own words.
- B. Active listening and summarising back **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: summarising confirms understanding and shows attention.
- C. Interrupting to speed up the interview  
  _Rationale:_ Interrupting undermines rapport and can miss information.
- D. Relying on technical jargon for precision  
  _Rationale:_ Jargon reduces patient understanding.
- E. Avoiding eye contact to stay neutral  
  _Rationale:_ Appropriate eye contact supports rapport.

**MST-2133-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) An SBAR-style framework is primarily used to:

- A. Structure a concise clinical handover or communication **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: SBAR (Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation) structures communication.
- B. Calculate a medication dose  
  _Rationale:_ SBAR is a communication tool, not a dosing method.
- C. Replace the physical examination  
  _Rationale:_ SBAR organises communication; it does not replace examination.
- D. Record billing information  
  _Rationale:_ SBAR is for clinical communication, not billing.
