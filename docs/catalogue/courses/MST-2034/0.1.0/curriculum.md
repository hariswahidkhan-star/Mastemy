## Learning outcomes

1. Explain special relativity's postulates and their kinematic consequences
2. Relate mass and energy and apply relativistic momentum qualitatively
3. Describe nuclear structure, binding energy, fission and fusion
4. Classify fundamental particles and the four interactions
5. Explain the role of conservation laws in particle reactions
6. Outline the Standard Model and its open questions

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2034-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A muon travelling near light speed is observed to live far longer than its rest-frame lifetime. This is explained by

- A. time dilation: moving clocks run slow in the lab frame **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: special relativity predicts time dilation for fast-moving particles.
- B. the muon gaining mass and becoming stable  
  _Rationale:_ Increased relativistic energy does not make the muon stable.
- C. a slower chemical reaction rate  
  _Rationale:_ Muon decay is not a chemical process.
- D. measurement error in the detector  
  _Rationale:_ The effect is a real, repeatable relativistic prediction.

**MST-2034-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO quantities must be conserved in any particle interaction? (Select TWO.)

- A. Electric charge **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: charge conservation holds in all interactions.
- B. Total energy **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: energy is conserved in every interaction.
- C. The number of quarks of each flavour individually  
  _Rationale:_ Flavour can change in weak interactions.
- D. The number of photons  
  _Rationale:_ Photon number is not conserved; photons can be created and absorbed.

**MST-2034-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why does nuclear fusion of light nuclei release energy?

- A. The product nucleus has a higher binding energy per nucleon, so mass is converted to energy **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: moving toward the binding-energy peak releases energy via E = mc^2.
- B. Light nuclei are radioactive and decay  
  _Rationale:_ Fusion is not radioactive decay.
- C. Energy is created from nothing  
  _Rationale:_ Energy is conserved; it comes from the mass defect.
- D. The nuclei are pushed apart by the strong force  
  _Rationale:_ The strong force binds nucleons together.
