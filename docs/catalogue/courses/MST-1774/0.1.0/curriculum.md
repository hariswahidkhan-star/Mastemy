## Learning outcomes

1. Explain the principles and functions of public health
2. Use measures of disease frequency and association in epidemiology
3. Describe epidemiological study designs and sources of error
4. Apply public health approaches to prevention, screening and outbreaks

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every course outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Hands-on operation, the quality of tools and live outputs, and professional judgement are taught by video demonstration and model-answer analysis; the certificate assessment uses single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only and never requires uploads, recorded work, submitted code or peer review.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1774-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) In a town of 10,000 people, 200 already have a chronic condition and 50 new cases arise during the year. The number that best represents incidence is:

- A. 50 new cases in the year **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: incidence counts new cases arising over a period; prevalence would be the 200 existing cases.
- B. 200 existing cases  
  _Rationale:_ That is prevalence, the existing cases, not incidence.
- C. 250 cases total  
  _Rationale:_ That mixes new and existing cases and is neither a clean incidence nor prevalence figure.
- D. 10,000 people  
  _Rationale:_ That is the population at risk, the denominator, not the incidence count.**MST-1774-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) To investigate a rare disease by comparing past exposures of people with and without the disease, the most efficient design is:

- A. Case-control study **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: case-control studies are efficient for rare diseases, comparing exposures retrospectively.
- B. Prospective cohort study  
  _Rationale:_ A cohort following people forward is inefficient for a rare disease because few would develop it.
- C. Randomised controlled trial  
  _Rationale:_ An RCT assigns exposure and is not used to study a naturally occurring rare disease this way.
- D. Ecological study  
  _Rationale:_ An ecological study uses population-level data and cannot link individual exposure to individual disease.**MST-1774-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO conditions must hold for herd immunity to protect an unvaccinated person? (Select TWO)

- A. A high enough proportion of the population is immune **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: once immunity passes the threshold, transmission chains break and the susceptible are indirectly protected.
- B. The disease spreads from person to person **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: herd immunity applies to communicable diseases transmitted between people.
- C. The disease is non-infectious and genetic  
  _Rationale:_ Herd immunity does not apply to non-communicable, non-transmissible conditions.
- D. Immunity lasts only a single day  
  _Rationale:_ Very short-lived immunity would not sustain the population-level protection herd immunity requires.
