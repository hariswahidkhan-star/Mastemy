## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1119-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Customer success is best measured by whether the customer:

- A. Logs in as often as possible  
  _Rationale:_ Logins are activity, not necessarily the desired outcome.
- B. Achieves the business outcome they bought the product for **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: success is defined by the customer's intended outcome.
- C. Files the fewest support tickets  
  _Rationale:_ Low tickets can also mean low usage or hidden struggle.
- D. Uses every feature available  
  _Rationale:_ Using all features is not the goal if outcomes are met.

**MST-1119-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) Reducing 'time-to-first-value' in onboarding matters because it:

- A. Lets the vendor stop supporting the customer sooner  
  _Rationale:_ Early value is about the customer's benefit, not ending support.
- B. Builds early confidence and reduces the risk of early churn **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: reaching value quickly builds momentum and lowers churn risk.
- C. Removes the need for success criteria  
  _Rationale:_ Success criteria remain essential.
- D. Guarantees expansion revenue immediately  
  _Rationale:_ Fast value helps but does not guarantee expansion.

**MST-1119-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO are useful early signals in a customer health score? (Select TWO)

- A. Declining usage of the core workflow **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a drop in core usage is an early churn-risk signal.
- B. No executive sponsor engaged on the account **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a missing sponsor is a known risk indicator.
- C. The renewal date being far in the future  
  _Rationale:_ A distant renewal is not itself a health signal.
- D. A high number of completed onboarding steps early on  
  _Rationale:_ Strong early onboarding is a positive, not a risk signal.
