## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1082-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Which sentence is the clearest business English?

- A. Please be advised that the aforementioned deliverable has been duly completed.  
  _Rationale:_ It is padded with legalistic filler that adds no meaning.
- B. We have finished the report. **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: it is plain, precise and immediately clear.
- C. The completion of the report is a thing that has now occurred.  
  _Rationale:_ It is wordy and buries a simple fact.
- D. Report = done, FYI.  
  _Rationale:_ Too informal and fragmentary for most business writing.

**MST-1082-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) The main purpose of an executive summary is to:

- A. Repeat every detail of the report  
  _Rationale:_ It summarises; it does not reproduce the whole report.
- B. Give a busy reader the key findings and recommendation up front **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: it lets decision-makers grasp the essentials without reading everything.
- C. List the authors and their titles  
  _Rationale:_ That is front matter, not the summary's purpose.
- D. Hide the conclusion until the end  
  _Rationale:_ An executive summary states the conclusion early, not late.

**MST-1082-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO phrases use appropriate conditional negotiation language? (Select TWO)

- A. If you can commit to a larger order, we could offer a discount. **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a conditional offer links a concession to something in return.
- B. We would be willing to extend the deadline provided the scope stays fixed. **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: 'provided that' sets a clear condition on the concession.
- C. Take it or leave it, no changes.  
  _Rationale:_ An ultimatum is not conditional bargaining language.
- D. We will give you everything you want for free.  
  _Rationale:_ Unconditional surrender is poor negotiation, not conditional language.
