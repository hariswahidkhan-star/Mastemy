## Learning outcomes

1. Describe what magnets do and which everyday materials are magnetic
2. Explain that magnets have two poles and that like poles repel while unlike poles attract
3. Describe a simple complete electric circuit with a battery, wires and a bulb
4. Explain why a circuit must be complete for current to flow
5. Sort materials into electrical conductors and insulators
6. Follow electrical safety: use only batteries, never mains sockets

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance. Any experiment ideas are optional and require adult supervision; they are not assessed.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2847-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why does a bulb light only when the circuit is complete?

- A. Electric current can flow all the way around the loop **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: current needs a complete, unbroken loop to flow and light the bulb.
- B. The bulb stores its own electricity  
  _Rationale:_ The bulb needs current from the battery through a complete circuit.
- C. Light is made by the plastic case  
  _Rationale:_ The glowing part needs current flowing through it.
- D. Only one wire is ever needed  
  _Rationale:_ A complete loop needs the current path to return to the battery.

**MST-2847-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO materials are good electrical conductors? (Select TWO.)

- A. A copper wire **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: copper metal conducts electricity well.
- B. A steel paperclip **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: steel is a metal and conducts electricity.
- C. A rubber band  
  _Rationale:_ Rubber is an insulator and does not conduct.
- D. A plastic ruler  
  _Rationale:_ Plastic is an insulator and does not conduct.

**MST-2847-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Two magnets push away from each other when you bring them close. Why?

- A. Two like poles are facing each other **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: like poles (N-N or S-S) repel each other.
- B. The magnets are out of battery  
  _Rationale:_ Magnets do not use batteries.
- C. Unlike poles are facing  
  _Rationale:_ Unlike poles attract; it is like poles that repel.
- D. Magnets can only ever attract  
  _Rationale:_ Magnets can also repel when like poles meet.
