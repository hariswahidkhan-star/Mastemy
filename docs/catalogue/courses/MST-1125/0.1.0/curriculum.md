## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1125-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) When picture looks good but audio is poor, audiences generally:

- A. Do not notice or mind the audio  
  _Rationale:_ Audiences are highly sensitive to bad audio.
- B. Disengage quickly because poor audio is hard to tolerate **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: viewers forgive imperfect picture far more than bad sound.
- C. Prefer the video because it feels authentic  
  _Rationale:_ Poor audio reads as low quality, not authentic.
- D. Watch longer to work out what is said  
  _Rationale:_ Straining to hear increases drop-off, not watch time.

**MST-1125-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) The 'rule of thirds' is a guideline for:

- A. How long each clip should last  
  _Rationale:_ That concerns pacing, not the rule of thirds.
- B. Placing key subjects off-centre for a balanced frame **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the rule of thirds positions subjects along thirds for stronger composition.
- C. How many lights to use  
  _Rationale:_ Lighting count is unrelated to the rule of thirds.
- D. Which export format to choose  
  _Rationale:_ Export format is a post-production setting.

**MST-1125-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO belong in solid pre-production? (Select TWO)

- A. A script or outline with a clear hook **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a script gives the shoot direction and a strong opening.
- B. A shot list covering the footage you need **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a shot list ensures you capture the required coverage.
- C. Deciding to improvise everything on the day  
  _Rationale:_ Pure improvisation is the opposite of pre-production.
- D. Leaving audio and lighting entirely to chance  
  _Rationale:_ Planning audio and lighting is part of good pre-production.
