## Learning outcomes

1. Describe the primary energy sources and how they are converted into useful energy carriers
2. Distinguish energy, power and capacity and use consistent units across electricity, heat and fuels
3. Explain how electricity, heat and transport demand vary over time and by sector
4. Trace energy flows from primary supply to end use and identify conversion losses
5. Interpret a national or regional energy balance and basic supply-demand indicators
6. Relate energy choices to cost, reliability and emissions trade-offs

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2359-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A 2 kW heater runs for 3 hours. How much energy does it consume?

- A. 6 kWh **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Energy = power x time = 2 kW x 3 h = 6 kWh.
- B. 2 kWh  
  _Rationale:_ That ignores the 3-hour duration; it is the power rating only.
- C. 6 kW  
  _Rationale:_ kW is a unit of power, not of energy consumed over time.
- D. 0.67 kWh  
  _Rationale:_ That divides power by time, which has no physical meaning here.

**MST-2359-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO statements about capacity factor are correct? (Select TWO.)

- A. It is the ratio of actual energy output to the output at full nameplate capacity over a period **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ That is the definition of capacity factor.
- B. A higher capacity factor means the plant delivered more of its theoretical maximum **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: it measures how fully the capacity was used.
- C. It is always equal to the plant's thermal efficiency  
  _Rationale:_ Capacity factor and thermal efficiency are different quantities.
- D. It can exceed 100% for renewable plants  
  _Rationale:_ Output cannot exceed the nameplate maximum over the same period.

**MST-2359-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A country's total primary energy use is flat but peak electricity demand is climbing. What best explains this?

- A. Electrification and shifting daily usage patterns raise peaks even when total energy is steady **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Peak power depends on simultaneous demand, which can rise independently of annual totals.
- B. The country is using more energy overall  
  _Rationale:_ The premise states total energy use is flat.
- C. Capacity factors of all plants have fallen to zero  
  _Rationale:_ That would cut output, not raise demand peaks.
- D. Energy and power are the same quantity  
  _Rationale:_ They are distinct; peaks are a power measure, totals an energy measure.
