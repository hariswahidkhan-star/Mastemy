## Learning outcomes

1. Determine whether a transaction is a business combination and identify the acquirer
2. Apply the acquisition method and determine the acquisition date
3. Recognise and measure identifiable assets acquired and liabilities assumed
4. Calculate goodwill or a bargain purchase gain, including the choice of measuring non-controlling interest
5. Account for contingent consideration and acquisition-related costs
6. Apply the measurement period and account for subsequent adjustments

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Integrative case

A listed group acquires a controlling stake in a target with recognised and unrecognised intangibles, an earn-out contingent on future profits, and significant advisory fees; apply the acquisition method to identify the acquirer, fair-value the net assets, choose how to measure the non-controlling interest, and compute goodwill, then explain the measurement-period adjustments made after year-end.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2513-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Which method does IFRS 3 require for accounting for a business combination?

- A. The acquisition method **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: IFRS 3 requires the acquisition method.
- B. The pooling-of-interests method  
  _Rationale:_ Pooling of interests is not permitted for business combinations in scope.
- C. The equity method  
  _Rationale:_ The equity method is for associates, not for consolidating an acquired business.
- D. The proportionate consolidation method  
  _Rationale:_ Proportionate consolidation is not the required method here.

**MST-2513-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO items are generally NOT included in the cost of a business combination and are instead expensed or accounted for separately? (Select TWO.)

- A. Acquisition-related costs such as advisory and legal fees **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: acquisition-related costs are expensed as incurred.
- B. Costs to issue debt or equity used as consideration **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: debt and equity issue costs are accounted for under other standards, not added to goodwill.
- C. The fair value of shares issued as consideration  
  _Rationale:_ Shares issued as consideration are part of the consideration transferred.
- D. The fair value of cash paid to the former owners  
  _Rationale:_ Cash consideration is part of the consideration transferred.

**MST-2513-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) An acquirer measures goodwill and finds the fair value of identifiable net assets exceeds the consideration plus non-controlling interest. After reassessing the amounts, what is the required treatment of the excess?

- A. Recognise it immediately as a gain on a bargain purchase in profit or loss **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a confirmed excess is a bargain purchase gain recognised in profit or loss.
- B. Defer it as a liability and release it over time  
  _Rationale:_ Negative goodwill is not deferred and released.
- C. Add it to goodwill as a positive balance  
  _Rationale:_ An excess over cost is not positive goodwill.
- D. Credit it directly to share capital  
  _Rationale:_ It is recognised in profit or loss, not in equity capital.
