## Integrative case

A manufacturer competing on fast, reliable delivery is instead chasing the lowest possible cost everywhere, creating bottlenecks and excess inventory. Apply operations strategy: link operations to the competitive goal, choose the right performance objectives and order-winners, manage process, capacity and inventory, and improve in a way that stays aligned with strategy.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1842-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A firm competes by offering the fastest, most reliable delivery but manages operations purely to minimise cost. What is the strategic error?

- A. Operations are not aligned with the firm's order-winning objectives **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: operations should support speed and dependability, its order-winners.
- B. Cost should never be considered in operations  
  _Rationale:_ Cost matters, but not at the expense of the chosen order-winners.
- C. Delivery speed is irrelevant to operations  
  _Rationale:_ Speed is a core operations performance objective here.
- D. Operations have no link to strategy  
  _Rationale:_ Operations strategy is precisely about that link.

**MST-1842-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which statements about operations performance objectives are correct? (Select TWO)

- A. Firms usually must make trade-offs between objectives **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: excelling everywhere at once is rarely feasible.
- B. Order-winners are the objectives that most influence customer choice **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: order-winners directly win business in a given market.
- C. A firm can be best-in-class at cost, quality, speed and flexibility simultaneously  
  _Rationale:_ Trade-offs usually prevent dominating every dimension at once.
- D. Performance objectives are unrelated to competitive strategy  
  _Rationale:_ They are how operations delivers the competitive strategy.

**MST-1842-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A plant adds capacity to every workstation to raise output but throughput barely improves. What was overlooked?

- A. Output is limited by the bottleneck, so only relieving the constraint helps **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: adding capacity away from the constraint does not raise throughput.
- B. Capacity never affects output  
  _Rationale:_ Capacity matters, but specifically at the bottleneck.
- C. Every workstation is always a bottleneck  
  _Rationale:_ Typically only the constraint limits throughput.
- D. Throughput depends only on inventory levels  
  _Rationale:_ Throughput is governed by the constraint, not inventory alone.
