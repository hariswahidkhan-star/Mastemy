## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-0382-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) For steady incompressible flow in a pipe that narrows to a smaller diameter, the continuity equation requires that the average velocity:

- A. Increase as the cross-sectional area decreases **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: with Q = VA constant, smaller area means higher velocity.
- B. Decrease as the cross-sectional area decreases  
  _Rationale:_ Velocity rises, not falls, where the pipe narrows at constant flow.
- C. Stay constant regardless of area  
  _Rationale:_ Continuity ties velocity to area at constant discharge.
- D. Depend only on the pipe material  
  _Rationale:_ Velocity is set by discharge and area, not pipe material.

**MST-0382-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) Manning's equation is used primarily to analyze:

- A. Uniform flow in an open channel **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: Manning's equation relates open-channel velocity to slope and roughness.
- B. Compressible gas flow in a nozzle  
  _Rationale:_ Manning's equation is for open-channel liquid flow, not gas nozzles.
- C. Heat conduction through a wall  
  _Rationale:_ That is Fourier's law, not Manning's equation.
- D. Electrical current in a conductor  
  _Rationale:_ That is Ohm's law, unrelated to Manning's equation.

**MST-0382-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO statements about applying Bernoulli's equation to real pipe flow are correct?

- A. It expresses conservation of energy along a streamline **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: Bernoulli is an energy statement along a streamline.
- B. Real pipe flow requires adding head-loss terms **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: friction losses must be added for real flow.
- C. It applies unchanged to viscous, lossy flow  
  _Rationale:_ Ideal Bernoulli ignores losses, so it does not apply unchanged.
- D. It requires the fluid to be compressible  
  _Rationale:_ Standard Bernoulli assumes incompressible flow.
- E. It states that velocity has no effect on pressure  
  _Rationale:_ Bernoulli explicitly links velocity and pressure.
