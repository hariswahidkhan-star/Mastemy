## Learning outcomes

1. What executive presence is and why it matters
2. Gravitas, credibility and composure
3. Communicating with clarity and structure
4. Tailoring the message to the audience
5. Influence, persuasion and storytelling
6. Presence in meetings and difficult conversations
7. Managing stakeholders and building trust
8. Presence under pressure and feedback

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Knowledge-practice forms use MCQ/MR only; real behavioural performance, live stakeholder interaction and delivery are not assessed in this format.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1070-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) When giving a status update to a time-pressed executive audience, how should the message be structured?

- A. Lead with the headline and the so-what, then provide supporting detail only as needed **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: top-down structure gives executives the conclusion first and detail on demand.
- B. Walk through the full chronology before reaching any conclusion  
  _Rationale:_ Chronological build-up buries the point for a busy audience.
- C. Open with technical methodology to establish credibility  
  _Rationale:_ Leading with methodology delays the information the audience needs.
- D. Avoid stating a recommendation to stay neutral  
  _Rationale:_ Executives generally want a clear recommendation, not neutrality for its own sake.

**MST-1070-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select ALL that apply) Which behaviours strengthen executive presence? (Select TWO)

- A. Staying composed and measured when challenged **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: composure under challenge projects gravitas and credibility.
- B. Tailoring the level of detail to the audience's priorities **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: adapting the message to the audience is central to effective presence.
- C. Using the same dense detail for every audience  
  _Rationale:_ One-size-fits-all detail undermines clarity and presence.
- D. Becoming defensive when questioned  
  _Rationale:_ Defensiveness erodes presence rather than strengthening it.

**MST-1070-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A senior stakeholder asks a pointed, slightly hostile question in a meeting. What is the most effective response?

- A. Acknowledge the concern, answer calmly and directly, and stay composed **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: composed acknowledgement and a direct answer preserve credibility and defuse hostility.
- B. Become defensive and dismiss the question  
  _Rationale:_ Defensiveness damages presence and trust.
- C. Avoid the question and change the subject  
  _Rationale:_ Evading the question undermines credibility.
- D. Match the hostility to show strength  
  _Rationale:_ Escalating hostility reduces, rather than demonstrates, presence.
