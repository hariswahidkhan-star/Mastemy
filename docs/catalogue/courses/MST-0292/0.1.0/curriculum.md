## Learning outcomes

1. Apply HR leadership and strategy competencies to organisational decisions
2. Evaluate talent acquisition, development and total-rewards strategy
3. Analyse employee relations, engagement and organisational effectiveness
4. Apply HR knowledge within legal, ethical and global workplace contexts

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: official item formats not reproducible as MCQ/MR (for example performance, speaking, essay, hands-on or simulation tasks) are not reproduced here; see the exam-version record.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-0292-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A senior leader asks HR to quietly overlook a policy breach by a top performer. Which SHRM competency most directly guides the response?

- A. Ethical Practice **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: upholding integrity and applying standards consistently is the Ethical Practice competency.
- B. Analytical Aptitude  
  _Rationale:_ Analytical aptitude concerns data-driven decisions, not the integrity question here.
- C. Global Mindset  
  _Rationale:_ Global mindset concerns cross-cultural effectiveness, not this ethics issue.
- D. Technology Management  
  _Rationale:_ Technology management is unrelated to the ethical decision.

**MST-0292-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) An HR leader wants a development programme to directly support a goal of entering a new market. What makes the programme strategically aligned?

- A. Its objectives are tied to the capabilities the new-market goal requires **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: strategic alignment means HR initiatives build the capabilities the business strategy needs.
- B. It uses the lowest-cost training vendor available  
  _Rationale:_ Cost alone does not create strategic alignment to the market goal.
- C. It is delivered to all employees equally regardless of role  
  _Rationale:_ Uniform delivery is not what makes a programme strategically aligned.
- D. It matches what a competitor recently did  
  _Rationale:_ Copying a competitor is not alignment to this organisation's own strategy.

**MST-0292-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Select TWO practices that strengthen pay equity in a total-rewards strategy.

- A. Conducting regular pay-equity analyses across comparable roles **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: periodic analysis of comparable roles surfaces and helps close unjustified gaps.
- B. Documenting objective, job-related criteria for pay decisions **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: job-related, documented criteria reduce bias and support equitable pay.
- C. Setting pay solely on each candidate's prior salary  
  _Rationale:_ Relying on salary history can carry forward historical inequities.
- D. Keeping all compensation criteria undisclosed from managers  
  _Rationale:_ Opacity for decision-makers tends to entrench inconsistency, not equity.
- E. Rewarding negotiation skill rather than role and performance  
  _Rationale:_ Paying for negotiation skill introduces inequity unrelated to the job.
