## Learning outcomes

1. Explain concepts of mental health, wellbeing and the biopsychosocial model
2. Describe common mental health conditions and their features
3. Apply principles of assessment, risk and recovery-oriented support
4. Describe treatments, stigma reduction and promoting mental wellbeing

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every course outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Hands-on operation, the quality of tools and live outputs, and professional judgement are taught by video demonstration and model-answer analysis; the certificate assessment uses single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only and never requires uploads, recorded work, submitted code or peer review.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1776-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) The biopsychosocial model of mental health holds that mental health is best understood as:

- A. The interaction of biological, psychological and social factors **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the model integrates biological, psychological and social influences rather than a single cause.
- B. Purely a chemical imbalance in the brain  
  _Rationale:_ That reduces mental health to biology alone, ignoring psychological and social factors.
- C. Only a matter of personal willpower  
  _Rationale:_ Framing it as willpower ignores biological and social determinants and is stigmatising.
- D. Entirely determined by childhood events  
  _Rationale:_ Early experience matters, but the model includes biological and current social factors too.**MST-1776-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) When supporting someone you are worried about, the safest approach to suicidal thoughts is to:

- A. Ask directly and calmly whether they are thinking of suicide **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: asking directly does not plant the idea and allows risk to be understood and support offered.
- B. Avoid the topic so you do not give them the idea  
  _Rationale:_ Evidence shows asking directly does not increase risk; avoiding it leaves risk unassessed.
- C. Immediately tell them they have nothing to worry about  
  _Rationale:_ Dismissing their feelings shuts down disclosure and misses risk.
- D. Change the subject to something positive  
  _Rationale:_ Deflecting avoids assessing risk and can feel dismissive.**MST-1776-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO are principles of recovery-oriented mental health practice? (Select TWO)

- A. Hope and the belief that recovery is possible **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: instilling hope is central to recovery-oriented practice.
- B. Working in partnership and respecting the person's own goals **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: co-production and self-determination are core recovery principles.
- C. The clinician alone decides all goals  
  _Rationale:_ Recovery practice is collaborative, not clinician-directed in isolation.
- D. Focusing only on eliminating symptoms  
  _Rationale:_ Recovery emphasises a meaningful life, not symptom elimination alone.
