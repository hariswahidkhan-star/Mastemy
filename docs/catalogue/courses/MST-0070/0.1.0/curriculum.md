## Learning outcomes

1. Describe the securities trader role and the market structure in which equity and convertible orders are executed
2. Apply order-handling, display and best-execution obligations to customer and proprietary orders
3. Evaluate trading activity against manipulation, marking and front-running prohibitions
4. Analyse reporting, clearance, settlement and recordkeeping duties for trades

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-0070-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A trader holds a customer limit order to buy 500 shares at $20.00 while the firm displays a bid of $19.98. The market is quiet and the order is marketable against the firm's quote. What does the limit-order display obligation generally require?

- A. Display the customer's better-priced limit order (or route it) so it is reflected in the quote **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a customer limit order priced better than the firm's quote must generally be displayed or routed promptly.
- B. Hold the order hidden until the firm can trade ahead of it  
  _Rationale:_ Trading ahead of a displayable customer limit order is prohibited.
- C. Cancel the order because it improves the firm's quote  
  _Rationale:_ Improving the quote is not a basis to cancel a customer order.
- D. Delay display until end of day  
  _Rationale:_ Display must be prompt, not deferred to end of day.

**MST-0070-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) During a single-stock trading pause under limit-up/limit-down, a trader wants to keep sending marketable orders. What is the correct understanding of the pause?

- A. Trading in that security is halted for the pause period; orders cannot execute until trading resumes **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: an LULD pause halts trading in the security until the re-opening process completes.
- B. Only customer orders are paused; proprietary orders execute normally  
  _Rationale:_ The pause applies to the security, not to a class of orders.
- C. Orders execute but at the prior day's close  
  _Rationale:_ Executions do not occur at a stale close during the pause.
- D. The pause only affects options on the stock  
  _Rationale:_ The pause affects trading in the underlying security.

**MST-0070-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) A compliance review flags a trader's activity near the close. Which TWO patterns are most consistent with prohibited manipulation rather than legitimate trading? (Select TWO)

- A. Entering a series of small aggressive buy orders in the final seconds solely to set a higher closing print **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: trading designed to set an artificial closing price is marking-the-close manipulation.
- B. Executing a large customer sell order worked throughout the day to minimise impact  
  _Rationale:_ Working an order to reduce impact is legitimate execution practice.
- C. Buying ahead of a known large customer order to profit from its expected impact **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: front-running a customer order is prohibited.
- D. Posting a displayed limit order that improves the NBBO  
  _Rationale:_ Improving the quote with a genuine order is ordinary market-making.
- E. Reporting an executed trade within the required time window  
  _Rationale:_ Timely trade reporting is a compliance obligation, not manipulation.
