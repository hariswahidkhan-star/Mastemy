## Learning outcomes

1. Generate and plan story ideas before writing
2. Create characters and simple settings
3. Structure a story with a beginning, middle and end
4. Use descriptive words to make writing vivid
5. Write dialogue between characters
6. Revise a draft to improve clarity and detail

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Integrative case

A learner invents a brave character, maps a short adventure with a problem and solution, writes it with vivid describing words and a line of dialogue, then revises one paragraph to make it clearer.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2880-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) What are the three main parts of a simple story?

- A. Beginning, middle and end **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: stories are shaped as beginning, middle and end.
- B. Title, title and title  
  _Rationale:_ A story has one title, not three parts of titles.
- C. Only an ending  
  _Rationale:_ A story needs a beginning and middle too.
- D. A list of spellings  
  _Rationale:_ Spellings are not the parts of a story.

**MST-2880-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) Which sentence uses the most vivid, descriptive language?

- A. The huge, shaggy dog bounded across the muddy field. **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: strong adjectives and a vivid verb paint a clear picture.
- B. The dog went in the field.  
  _Rationale:_ This is plain with no description.
- C. There was a dog.  
  _Rationale:_ This gives no detail at all.
- D. A dog.  
  _Rationale:_ This is not even a full descriptive sentence.

**MST-2880-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO things should a story plan include before you start writing? (Select TWO.)

- A. Who the main character is **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: knowing your character helps you plan.
- B. What problem the character faces **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a problem drives the story.
- C. The exact number of letters in the title  
  _Rationale:_ Counting letters does not help plan a story.
- D. The colour of the paper you write on  
  _Rationale:_ Paper colour is not part of story planning.
