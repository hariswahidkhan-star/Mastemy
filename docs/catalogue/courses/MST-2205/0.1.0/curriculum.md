## Learning outcomes

1. Explain why health and safety is central to construction and who is responsible
2. Identify the major hazards on a construction site and their controls
3. Apply the hierarchy of controls to reduce risk
4. Describe the purpose of risk assessments, method statements and permits
5. Recognise the role of PPE, welfare, training and competence
6. Understand that this course teaches general principles and is not a substitute for certified safety training or local legal codes

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2205-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Under the hierarchy of controls, which approach should be preferred first when managing a hazard?

- A. Eliminating the hazard where reasonably practicable **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: elimination is the most effective, top of the hierarchy.
- B. Issuing personal protective equipment  
  _Rationale:_ PPE is the last resort, not the first choice.
- C. Putting up a warning sign  
  _Rationale:_ Administrative signage ranks low in the hierarchy.
- D. Telling workers to be more careful  
  _Rationale:_ Relying on behaviour alone is the weakest control.

**MST-2205-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO are examples of engineering controls rather than PPE? (Select TWO.)

- A. Edge protection and guardrails at a leading edge **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: guardrails are an engineering control.
- B. Local exhaust ventilation to capture dust **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: ventilation is an engineering control.
- C. A hard hat worn by a worker  
  _Rationale:_ A hard hat is PPE, not an engineering control.
- D. Safety boots issued to the crew  
  _Rationale:_ Safety boots are PPE, not an engineering control.

**MST-2205-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why should near-misses be reported even when no one was hurt?

- A. They reveal weaknesses in the system that can be fixed before an injury occurs **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: near-misses are early warnings for prevention.
- B. So an individual worker can be punished  
  _Rationale:_ Punitive responses discourage reporting and harm safety culture.
- C. Because reporting is only needed after injuries  
  _Rationale:_ Waiting for injuries defeats the purpose of prevention.
- D. To increase paperwork for its own sake  
  _Rationale:_ The value is learning and prevention, not paperwork.
