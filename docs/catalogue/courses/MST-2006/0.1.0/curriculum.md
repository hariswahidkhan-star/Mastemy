## Learning outcomes

1. Explain periodicity and the chemistry of the main-group elements
2. Describe transition-metal and coordination chemistry
3. Apply crystal-field ideas to colour and magnetism
4. Explain solid-state structures and bonding models
5. Interpret descriptive chemistry of representative compounds
6. Describe how AI accelerates inorganic materials discovery

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2006-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why is a [Co(NH3)6]3+ complex typically low-spin?

- A. Ammonia is a strong-field ligand that produces a large d-orbital splitting **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a large splitting favours pairing, giving low spin.
- B. Ammonia is a weak-field ligand with small splitting  
  _Rationale:_ Weak-field ligands favour high spin, which is not observed here.
- C. Cobalt cannot form low-spin complexes  
  _Rationale:_ Co(III) commonly forms low-spin complexes.
- D. The charge alone determines spin state regardless of ligand  
  _Rationale:_ Ligand-field strength, not charge alone, sets the spin state.

**MST-2006-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO factors raise the lattice energy of an ionic solid? (Select TWO.)

- A. Higher ionic charges on the constituent ions **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: lattice energy scales with the product of the charges.
- B. Smaller interionic distance between the ions **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: smaller separation increases electrostatic attraction.
- C. Larger ionic radii for both ions  
  _Rationale:_ Larger ions increase separation and lower lattice energy.
- D. Lower ionic charges on the ions  
  _Rationale:_ Lower charges reduce the attraction and lattice energy.

**MST-2006-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) What is the main risk of acting on an AI-screened materials shortlist without validation?

- A. Predicted properties may not hold under real synthesis and testing conditions **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: predictions require experimental confirmation.
- B. Screening can never suggest a useful candidate  
  _Rationale:_ Screening is useful; it still needs validation.
- C. Inorganic materials cannot be characterised  
  _Rationale:_ They are routinely characterised experimentally.
- D. A shortlist hit is a finished product  
  _Rationale:_ A hit is a hypothesis, not a validated material.
