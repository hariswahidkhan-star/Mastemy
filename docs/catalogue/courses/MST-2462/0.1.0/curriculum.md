## Learning outcomes

1. Diagnose the type and scale of a change and the readiness for it
2. Build a compelling case for change and a guiding coalition
3. Design a change approach that fits the context and culture
4. Anticipate and work with resistance rather than against it
5. Sustain change through structures, incentives and reinforcement
6. Lead people through the emotional transition that change demands

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2462-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Clinicians strongly resist a new records system. What is the most effective first leadership response?

- A. Surface and understand the specific roots of their resistance before responding **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: understanding the roots lets leaders address real concerns and separate valid objections.
- B. Mandate compliance and discipline non-adopters immediately  
  _Rationale:_ Coercion first usually deepens resistance.
- C. Ignore the resisters and focus on willing adopters  
  _Rationale:_ Ignoring influential sceptics risks the whole change.
- D. Cancel the change at the first sign of resistance  
  _Rationale:_ Abandoning at first resistance wastes the effort.

**MST-2462-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO practices most help a change actually stick after launch? (Select TWO.)

- A. Aligning incentives and performance measures with the new behaviour **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: incentives that reward the new behaviour reinforce it.
- B. Reinforcing and celebrating early wins **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: visible early wins build momentum and belief.
- C. Declaring victory immediately at go-live  
  _Rationale:_ Premature victory undermines sustainment.
- D. Removing the sponsor once launch is done  
  _Rationale:_ Withdrawing sponsorship too early lets change fade.
- E. Keeping the old incentives unchanged  
  _Rationale:_ Unchanged old incentives pull people back.

**MST-2462-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A change programme created intense urgency but people are now exhausted and disengaged. What does this most likely indicate?

- A. Urgency was generated without pacing and reinforcement, exceeding change capacity **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: urgency without regard for capacity and pacing produces fatigue and disengagement.
- B. The vision was too modest  
  _Rationale:_ Fatigue points to overload, not modest ambition.
- C. Resistance was fully eliminated  
  _Rationale:_ Exhaustion is not the same as acceptance.
- D. The change needed no coalition  
  _Rationale:_ This does not follow from fatigue.
