## Learning outcomes

1. Apply listening for gist and for specific detail to spoken passages
2. Recognise connected speech, weak forms and signpost language in audio
3. Infer speaker purpose, attitude and relationships from spoken cues

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning (vocabulary, rules, comprehension) through selection items (MCQ/MR) only; does not assess live speaking, extended writing production or any hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1860-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Before listening to an announcement only to find the departure time, the most efficient approach is to:

- A. listen for that specific detail and ignore unrelated content **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: listening for specific information targets the needed detail.
- B. transcribe the whole announcement word for word  
  _Rationale:_ Full transcription is unnecessary for one detail.
- C. translate each sentence into your first language  
  _Rationale:_ Translation is slow and not the listening goal here.
- D. memorise the speaker's name  
  _Rationale:_ The speaker's name is not the information required.

**MST-1860-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO are features of connected speech that can make listening harder for learners? (Select TWO.)

- A. Elision, where a sound is dropped **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: elision removes sounds, so words sound different from their citation form.
- B. Linking, where a final sound joins the next word **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: linking blurs word boundaries in natural speech.
- C. Spelling out each word letter by letter  
  _Rationale:_ Incorrect: spelling out is not a feature of natural connected speech.
- D. Pausing fully between every word  
  _Rationale:_ Incorrect: full pauses between words are not typical connected speech.

**MST-1860-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A speaker says 'Oh, that's just GREAT' with heavy stress and a flat tone after bad news. The most likely intended attitude is:

- A. sarcasm or frustration **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: heavy stress and flat tone after bad news typically signal sarcasm.
- B. genuine delight  
  _Rationale:_ Genuine delight would usually carry a warmer, rising tone.
- C. polite agreement  
  _Rationale:_ Polite agreement would not use marked stress after bad news.
- D. confusion  
  _Rationale:_ Confusion is usually signalled by rising, questioning intonation.
