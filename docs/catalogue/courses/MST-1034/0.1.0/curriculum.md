## Learning outcomes

1. The accounts receivable cycle and working capital
2. Credit policy and customer risk assessment
3. Billing, invoicing and revenue recognition basics
4. Cash application and remittance
5. Collections strategy and dunning
6. Dispute and deduction management
7. Bad debt, provisions and write-offs
8. AR metrics, forecasting and close

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Knowledge-practice forms use MCQ/MR only; hands-on system operation, judgement under real transactions and professional sign-off are not assessed in this format.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1034-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) What does Days Sales Outstanding (DSO) measure?

- A. The average number of days it takes to collect cash after a credit sale **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: DSO estimates how long receivables take to convert to cash.
- B. The number of days inventory sits before sale  
  _Rationale:_ That is days inventory outstanding, not DSO.
- C. The number of days to pay suppliers  
  _Rationale:_ That is days payable outstanding, an AP metric.
- D. The total value of overdue invoices  
  _Rationale:_ DSO is a duration measure, not a balance.

**MST-1034-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select ALL that apply) Which statements about managing accounts receivable are correct? (Select TWO)

- A. A bad-debt provision estimates expected uncollectible amounts before any specific write-off **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the allowance recognises expected losses in advance of confirming a specific write-off.
- B. Prioritising collections by balance and risk is more effective than treating all overdue accounts equally **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: risk-and-value-based prioritisation focuses effort where it recovers the most cash.
- C. Unapplied cash should be left unmatched to keep the ledger simple  
  _Rationale:_ Unapplied cash should be matched to invoices for accurate records.
- D. A disputed deduction should be written off immediately as bad debt  
  _Rationale:_ Disputes should be investigated and resolved, not written off as bad debt by default.

**MST-1034-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A customer pays less than the invoice amount and cites a pricing disagreement. How should AR treat this first?

- A. Log it as a dispute or deduction and investigate before deciding on resolution **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: short payments tied to disputes are logged and investigated, not assumed uncollectible.
- B. Immediately write off the shortfall as bad debt  
  _Rationale:_ A disputed amount is not automatically bad debt.
- C. Apply the full invoice as paid and ignore the gap  
  _Rationale:_ Ignoring the shortfall misstates the receivable.
- D. Cancel the customer's credit limit without review  
  _Rationale:_ A knee-jerk credit cut is not the correct first step for a dispute.
