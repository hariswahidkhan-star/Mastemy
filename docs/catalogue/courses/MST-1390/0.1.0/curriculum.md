## Learning outcomes

1. Use AI tools responsibly to support learning without replacing it
2. Apply AI for comprehension, summarisation and note-making
3. Apply AI for practice, self-testing and feedback
4. Recognise academic-integrity, accuracy and privacy limits of AI study tools

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)


**MST-1390-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Which use of AI best respects academic integrity?

- A. Submitting AI-written answers as your own  
  _Rationale:_ Passing AI work off as your own breaches integrity.
- B. Using AI to explain a concept you then learn and write yourself **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: AI supports understanding without replacing your work.
- C. Having AI take an online test for you  
  _Rationale:_ This is academic misconduct.
- D. Copying AI text verbatim into an essay  
  _Rationale:_ Verbatim copying without attribution breaches integrity.

**MST-1390-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO habits make AI practice questions effective for learning? (Select TWO.)

- A. Attempting each question before revealing the answer **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: retrieval practice strengthens memory.
- B. Reading only the answers without attempting them  
  _Rationale:_ Reading answers alone gives weak recall.
- C. Reviewing and re-attempting the questions you got wrong **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: targeted review fixes gaps.
- D. Generating questions but never taking them  
  _Rationale:_ Unused questions do not help learning.

**MST-1390-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) An AI tool gives a confident but unfamiliar historical date. What should you do first?

- A. Verify the date against a trusted source before using it **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: AI confidence is not proof of accuracy.
- B. Trust it because the tool sounded certain  
  _Rationale:_ Confidence does not guarantee correctness.
- C. Assume it is wrong and ignore it  
  _Rationale:_ Reflexively ignoring it is as unjustified as blind trust.
- D. Ask the same tool to confirm itself  
  _Rationale:_ Self-confirmation does not establish accuracy.
