## Learning outcomes

1. Describe airway, respiration and ventilation assessment and management
2. Apply assessment and management for cardiology and resuscitation at the EMT level
3. Describe trauma assessment and management
4. Describe medical, obstetric and EMS operations knowledge

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: official item formats not reproducible as MCQ/MR (for example performance, speaking, essay, hands-on or simulation tasks) are not reproduced here; see the exam-version record.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-0361-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) On arrival at an emergency scene, what must the EMT confirm before approaching the patient?

- A. That the scene is safe **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: scene safety is confirmed first to protect the responder and others.
- B. The patient's insurance status  
  _Rationale:_ Insurance is irrelevant to immediate emergency care and comes much later, if at all.
- C. The patient's full medical history  
  _Rationale:_ A full history is gathered during assessment, not before ensuring safety.
- D. The nearest pharmacy location  
  _Rationale:_ Pharmacy location is not a scene-arrival priority.

**MST-0361-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) An unresponsive adult has loud snoring respirations. What is the most appropriate first action?

- A. Open the airway with a manual manoeuvre **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: snoring suggests airway obstruction by the tongue; a manual airway manoeuvre is the first step.
- B. Apply the AED immediately  
  _Rationale:_ The AED is for pulseless cardiac arrest, not an initial airway concern with respirations present.
- C. Begin chest compressions  
  _Rationale:_ Compressions are for a pulseless patient; this patient is breathing.
- D. Administer oral medication  
  _Rationale:_ An unresponsive patient should not be given anything by mouth.

**MST-0361-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Select TWO signs that suggest a patient may be developing shock.

- A. Pale, cool and clammy skin **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: pale, cool, clammy skin is a classic sign of compensating shock.
- B. Rapid, weak pulse **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: tachycardia with a weak pulse is a common early sign of shock.
- C. Slow, strong pulse with warm dry skin  
  _Rationale:_ These describe a stable patient, not shock.
- D. Pupils equal and reactive with normal colour  
  _Rationale:_ Normal findings do not indicate shock.
- E. Steady blood pressure with calm demeanour  
  _Rationale:_ A steady BP and calm state are not signs of developing shock.
