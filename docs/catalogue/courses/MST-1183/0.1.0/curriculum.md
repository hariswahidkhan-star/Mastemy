## Learning outcomes

1. Identify where AI can support audit and assurance work and where it cannot
2. Use AI to assist risk assessment, sampling and analytical procedures responsibly
3. Apply AI to document review and evidence summarisation with human verification
4. Recognise bias, hallucination and data-confidentiality risks in audit contexts
5. Maintain audit evidence, documentation and professional scepticism when using AI

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Hands-on operation of the tools and workflows taught, the quality of live outputs, and professional judgement on the job are taught by video demonstration and model-answer analysis; the certificate assessment uses single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only and never requires uploads, recorded work, graded prompts or peer review.

## Integrative case

A senior auditor must decide how to use AI on an engagement: pick two tasks where AI adds value and one where it must not be relied on, design the human-review controls, protect client confidentiality, and document the AI use so the file withstands review.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1183-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) An audit senior wants to use an AI summary of a contract as audit evidence. What is the correct position?

- A. The AI summary is a starting point only; evidence requires the auditor to verify the source **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: AI output must be corroborated; it is not audit evidence on its own.
- B. The AI summary is sufficient evidence if the tool is reputable  
  _Rationale:_ Tool reputation does not substitute for verification against the source.
- C. AI output can replace the auditor's judgement  
  _Rationale:_ Professional judgement and scepticism remain the auditor's responsibility.
- D. No verification is needed if the client agrees  
  _Rationale:_ Client agreement does not establish audit evidence.

**MST-1183-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO practices protect client confidentiality when using AI on an engagement? (Select TWO.)

- A. Use a tool with contractual data-protection terms and no training on your inputs **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: controlled tools with clear data terms reduce confidentiality risk.
- B. Remove or avoid entering identifying client data where not required **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: minimising sensitive data entered reduces exposure.
- C. Paste full client financials into a free public chatbot  
  _Rationale:_ Uncontrolled public tools create confidentiality and independence risks.
- D. Share the client's data with the tool vendor for marketing  
  _Rationale:_ Sharing client data for marketing breaches confidentiality.

**MST-1183-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) To keep the audit file reviewable when AI assisted a procedure, what should be documented?

- A. The tool used, the prompt or query, and the reviewer's verification of the output **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: documenting tool, input and human review keeps the work transparent and reviewable.
- B. Nothing, because AI use is routine  
  _Rationale:_ Undocumented AI use cannot be reviewed or relied upon.
- C. Only that 'AI was used' with no detail  
  _Rationale:_ A bare note does not let a reviewer assess the work.
- D. Only the final conclusion  
  _Rationale:_ The conclusion without the basis is not reviewable.
