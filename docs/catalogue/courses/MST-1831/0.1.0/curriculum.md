## Integrative case

A small manufacturer faces rising input prices, a new low-cost competitor, and a central-bank interest-rate rise. Apply economic reasoning: weigh opportunity costs and incentives, analyse the supply-and-demand effects, judge the competitive and market-failure issues, and anticipate how macro indicators and policy will shape the firm's choices.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1831-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A factory could produce either tables or chairs with the same wood and hours. The cost of making tables, expressed economically, is best described as:

- A. The chairs given up to make the tables (opportunity cost) **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: opportunity cost is the value of the next-best forgone option.
- B. Only the money spent on wood  
  _Rationale:_ That ignores the forgone alternative, which is the economic cost.
- C. Zero, because the wood was already owned  
  _Rationale:_ Owning the input does not eliminate opportunity cost.
- D. The total revenue from tables  
  _Rationale:_ Revenue is not the cost of the choice.

**MST-1831-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which statements about supply and demand are correct? (Select TWO)

- A. A fall in consumer income can shift the demand curve **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: income is a determinant that shifts demand, not a movement along it.
- B. A change in the good's own price causes a movement along the curve **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: price changes move along the curve rather than shifting it.
- C. A change in price shifts the entire demand curve  
  _Rationale:_ A price change is a movement along the curve, not a shift.
- D. Supply and demand never affect market price  
  _Rationale:_ Their interaction is precisely what sets market price.

**MST-1831-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A pollution cost borne by a nearby community but not paid by the factory is an example of:

- A. A negative externality **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a cost imposed on third parties outside the market transaction.
- B. A perfectly efficient market outcome  
  _Rationale:_ Externalities are a form of market failure, not efficiency.
- C. An opportunity cost to the factory  
  _Rationale:_ The cost falls on others, not as a forgone option for the firm.
- D. Inflation  
  _Rationale:_ Inflation is a general rise in prices, unrelated here.
