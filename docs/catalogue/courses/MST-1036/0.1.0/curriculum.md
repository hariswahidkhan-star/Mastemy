## Learning outcomes

1. Explain working capital and the cash conversion cycle
2. Analyse receivables and collection performance
3. Analyse payables and supplier-payment strategy
4. Manage inventory investment and turnover
5. Forecast short-term cash and liquidity needs
6. Build a working-capital improvement programme

- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Integrative case

A manufacturer with a 95-day cash conversion cycle is drawing heavily on its overdraft. The analyst must diagnose where cash is trapped across receivables, payables and inventory, build a 13-week forecast, and propose a prioritised programme to release cash without harming supplier or customer relationships.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1036-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) The cash conversion cycle is best expressed as:

- A. DSO + DIO - DPO **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: CCC = days sales outstanding + days inventory outstanding - days payables outstanding.
- B. DSO + DPO - DIO  
  _Rationale:_ This misplaces payables and inventory.
- C. DIO - DSO - DPO  
  _Rationale:_ Incorrect arrangement of the three drivers.
- D. DPO - DSO + DIO  
  _Rationale:_ Incorrect arrangement of the three drivers.

**MST-1036-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO actions shorten the cash conversion cycle? (Select TWO.)

- A. Collect receivables faster (reduce DSO) **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: faster collection releases cash.
- B. Negotiate longer supplier payment terms (increase DPO) **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: paying later, within terms, frees cash.
- C. Hold more inventory (increase DIO)  
  _Rationale:_ That lengthens the cycle.
- D. Offer longer customer credit terms  
  _Rationale:_ That increases DSO and lengthens the cycle.

**MST-1036-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A supplier offers 2/10 net 30. Taking the discount is usually attractive because the implied annualised cost of NOT taking it is:

- A. Very high (roughly 37% annualised) **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: forgoing 2% to delay 20 days implies about a 37% annual rate.
- B. About 2% per year  
  _Rationale:_ That ignores the short 20-day period.
- C. Zero, since terms are optional  
  _Rationale:_ There is a real economic cost to forgoing the discount.
- D. Equal to the overdraft rate exactly  
  _Rationale:_ The implied rate is typically far above overdraft cost.
