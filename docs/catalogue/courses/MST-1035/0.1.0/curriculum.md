## Learning outcomes

1. Treasury function and its objectives
2. Cash management and bank account structures
3. Cash forecasting and liquidity planning
4. Short-term funding and investment of surplus
5. Foreign exchange exposure and hedging basics
6. Interest-rate risk and debt management
7. Bank relationships, controls and payments security
8. Treasury technology, reporting and policy

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Knowledge-practice forms use MCQ/MR only; hands-on system operation, judgement under real transactions and professional sign-off are not assessed in this format.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1035-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) What is the primary purpose of a short-term (for example 13-week) cash forecast in treasury?

- A. To anticipate the timing of cash inflows and outflows so liquidity shortfalls or surpluses can be managed in advance **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a rolling cash forecast reveals timing gaps so funding or investment can be arranged ahead of time.
- B. To replace the statutory financial statements  
  _Rationale:_ A cash forecast supports treasury decisions; it does not replace statutory reporting.
- C. To calculate taxable profit for the period  
  _Rationale:_ Taxable profit is an accounting computation, not the forecast's purpose.
- D. To set employee bonus accruals  
  _Rationale:_ That is unrelated to the liquidity purpose of the forecast.

**MST-1035-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select ALL that apply) Which statements about managing financial risk in treasury are correct? (Select TWO)

- A. A forward contract can fix the rate on a future foreign-currency payment to reduce FX risk **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a forward locks the exchange rate, hedging the exposure.
- B. Floating-rate debt exposes the borrower to rising interest costs if rates increase **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: floating-rate debt reprices with market rates, so cost rises when rates rise.
- C. FX exposure disappears once an invoice is recorded in the accounts  
  _Rationale:_ Recording the invoice does not remove the currency exposure until settled or hedged.
- D. Fixed-rate debt always costs less than floating-rate debt  
  _Rationale:_ Neither is universally cheaper; it depends on the rate environment.

**MST-1035-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why does treasury concentrate cash from many accounts into a central structure?

- A. To improve visibility and use of cash, reducing idle balances and borrowing needs **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: concentration and pooling put cash where it is needed and cut idle balances and external borrowing.
- B. To hide cash from the external auditors  
  _Rationale:_ Concentration is an efficiency measure, not a way to conceal cash.
- C. To increase the number of bank fees paid  
  _Rationale:_ Pooling typically reduces, not increases, costs.
- D. Because regulations forbid more than one bank account  
  _Rationale:_ No such rule exists; pooling is a choice for efficiency.
