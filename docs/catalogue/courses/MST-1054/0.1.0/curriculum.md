## Learning outcomes

1. Explain the arm's-length principle and why transfer pricing matters
2. Identify controlled transactions between related parties
3. Describe the main transfer-pricing methods at a conceptual level
4. Outline functional analysis and comparability
5. Describe transfer-pricing documentation requirements and risk

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1054-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) The arm's-length principle requires that related-party transactions be priced as if:

- A. They had occurred between independent parties under comparable conditions **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the arm's-length principle benchmarks to independent-party pricing.
- B. The group wished to minimise tax regardless of market terms  
  _Rationale:_ Pricing to minimise tax is precisely what the principle prevents.
- C. No price were charged at all  
  _Rationale:_ Controlled transactions still need arm's-length pricing.
- D. The parent dictated any convenient figure  
  _Rationale:_ Arbitrary intercompany prices fail the principle.

**MST-1054-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) A functional analysis in transfer pricing examines which TWO of the following? (Select TWO.)

- A. The functions each entity performs **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: functions performed are central to characterising entities.
- B. The risks each entity bears **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: risk assumption drives where profit should sit.
- C. The personal preferences of the group's auditors  
  _Rationale:_ Auditor preferences are not part of functional analysis.
- D. The colour of the group's branding  
  _Rationale:_ Branding colour is irrelevant to the analysis.

**MST-1054-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Transfer-pricing documentation is best prepared:

- A. Contemporaneously, to support positions before a tax authority asks **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: contemporaneous documentation reduces risk and penalties.
- B. Only after a tax audit has started  
  _Rationale:_ Late documentation weakens the defence and raises penalty risk.
- C. Never, since intercompany prices are private  
  _Rationale:_ Many jurisdictions require documentation by law.
- D. By copying another group's files verbatim  
  _Rationale:_ Documentation must reflect the group's own facts.
