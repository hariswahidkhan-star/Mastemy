## Learning outcomes

1. Explain the core principles and frameworks of medical ethics
2. Apply the requirements of valid consent and capacity
3. Explain confidentiality, data protection and their lawful limits
4. Apply ethical and legal reasoning to end-of-life and resource dilemmas

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every course outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Hands-on operation, the quality of tools and live outputs, and professional judgement are taught by video demonstration and model-answer analysis; the certificate assessment uses single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only and never requires uploads, recorded work, submitted code or peer review.

## Integrative case

A seriously ill adult refuses a recommended, life-prolonging treatment. Work through capacity, consent, confidentiality and the clinician's legal duties to decide what may and may not be done, and justify the reasoning.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1769-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) For consent to treatment to be valid, which set of elements must all be present?

- A. The person is informed, has capacity, and gives it voluntarily **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: valid consent requires sufficient information, capacity and freedom from coercion.
- B. The consent is written and witnessed  
  _Rationale:_ A signature is evidence but is neither necessary nor sufficient; the substantive elements matter.
- C. A relative agrees on the patient's behalf  
  _Rationale:_ A competent adult consents for themselves; a relative cannot override that.
- D. The clinician judges the decision to be wise  
  _Rationale:_ A capable adult may make a decision others consider unwise; that does not invalidate consent.**MST-1769-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) A competent adult refuses a treatment that clinicians believe would save their life. The ethically and legally correct action is to:

- A. Respect the refusal after ensuring the decision is informed and voluntary **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a capable, informed adult has the right to refuse treatment, even life-sustaining treatment.
- B. Treat anyway under beneficence  
  _Rationale:_ Beneficence does not permit overriding a capable adult's informed refusal.
- C. Ask a family member to consent instead  
  _Rationale:_ No one can consent on behalf of a capable adult who has refused.
- D. Declare the patient to lack capacity because the choice is unwise  
  _Rationale:_ An unwise choice does not by itself indicate lack of capacity.**MST-1769-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO situations can justify disclosing confidential patient information without consent? (Select TWO)

- A. A legal requirement or court order to disclose **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: disclosure required by law is a recognised lawful exception.
- B. A serious risk of harm to others that disclosure would prevent **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: an overriding public-interest risk of serious harm can justify disclosure.
- C. A colleague is curious about the case  
  _Rationale:_ Curiosity is never a lawful basis for disclosure.
- D. The information would be interesting for teaching, using identifiable details  
  _Rationale:_ Teaching uses require anonymisation or consent, not identifiable disclosure without consent.
