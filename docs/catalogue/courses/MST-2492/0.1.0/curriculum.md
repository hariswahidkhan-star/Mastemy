## Learning outcomes

1. Compare cost-plus, value-based and competition-based pricing approaches
2. Analyse how price changes affect volume, contribution and profit
3. Apply price elasticity thinking to a pricing decision
4. Design simple discount, bundle and tiering structures responsibly
5. Measure profitability by product, customer and channel
6. Avoid common pricing traps and communicate pricing changes fairly

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2492-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A product earns a 40% contribution margin. If price is cut by 10%, roughly how much must volume rise just to hold contribution?

- A. More than proportionally, because the margin cushion shrinks when price falls **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a lower margin requires a disproportionate volume gain to break even.
- B. Exactly 10%, matching the price cut  
  _Rationale:_ Volume must rise more than 10% because margin falls.
- C. Volume can fall and contribution still holds  
  _Rationale:_ Falling volume cannot offset a price cut.
- D. No change in volume is needed  
  _Rationale:_ Contribution would drop without a volume rise.

**MST-2492-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO are risks of habitual deep discounting? (Select TWO.)

- A. Customers learn to delay purchases until the next discount **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: discounting can train buying behaviour.
- B. The reference price in customers' minds is eroded **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: frequent discounts lower perceived value.
- C. Contribution margin per unit automatically rises  
  _Rationale:_ Discounting lowers, not raises, unit margin.
- D. Brand premium is strengthened  
  _Rationale:_ Deep discounting tends to weaken a premium.

**MST-2492-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A large customer generates the most revenue but demands heavy service and discounts. The best profitability view is to:

- A. Measure contribution after cost to serve, not revenue alone **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: cost to serve can turn a big account unprofitable.
- B. Rank them top because revenue is highest  
  _Rationale:_ Revenue ignores the cost of serving them.
- C. Ignore service costs as overhead  
  _Rationale:_ Cost to serve is central to account profitability.
- D. Raise prices for every other customer instead  
  _Rationale:_ That does not address this account's economics.
