## Learning outcomes

1. Describe English vowel and consonant sounds using phonemic symbols
2. Explain word stress, sentence stress and connected-speech features
3. Analyse intonation patterns and their meaning in spoken English

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning (vocabulary, rules, comprehension) through selection items (MCQ/MR) only; does not assess live speaking, extended writing production or any hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1858-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A 'minimal pair' such as 'ship' and 'sheep' differs by exactly one of which linguistic unit?

- A. phoneme **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a minimal pair differs by a single phoneme in the same position.
- B. syllable  
  _Rationale:_ Both words have one syllable; the difference is not a whole syllable.
- C. morpheme  
  _Rationale:_ The difference is a sound, not a unit of meaning.
- D. grapheme  
  _Rationale:_ A grapheme is a written symbol; minimal pairs are defined by sound.

**MST-1858-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO statements about English word stress are correct? (Select TWO.)

- A. Unstressed vowels often reduce to the schwa sound **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: weak, unstressed syllables commonly reduce to schwa.
- B. Stress can distinguish a noun from a verb (e.g. 'REcord' vs 're-CORD') **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: stress placement can mark the noun/verb difference.
- C. Every syllable in a word carries equal stress  
  _Rationale:_ Incorrect: English words have one primary stress, not equal stress.
- D. Word stress never affects meaning  
  _Rationale:_ Incorrect: stress can change both meaning and word class.

**MST-1858-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A speaker answers a yes/no question with a clear falling tone on 'yes'. This most typically signals that the answer is:

- A. definite and complete **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a falling tone typically signals a definite, complete statement.
- B. uncertain and open to change  
  _Rationale:_ Uncertainty is more often signalled by a rising or fall-rise tune.
- C. a sarcastic refusal  
  _Rationale:_ Sarcasm relies on marked patterns, not a plain fall.
- D. an unfinished list  
  _Rationale:_ An unfinished list typically takes a non-falling, continuing tone.
