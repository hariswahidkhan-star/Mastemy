## Learning outcomes

1. Use units, conversions and the fundamentals of safe calculation
2. Calculate oral and injectable medication doses accurately
3. Calculate intravenous flow rates and infusion problems
4. Apply weight-based, paediatric and advanced dosing with safety checks

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every course outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Hands-on operation, the quality of tools and live outputs, and professional judgement are taught by video demonstration and model-answer analysis; the certificate assessment uses single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only and never requires uploads, recorded work, submitted code or peer review.

## Integrative case

On a busy shift you must prepare several medicines: a tablet dose, an injection from a stock concentration, a weight-based paediatric dose and an IV infusion rate. Perform each calculation with independent safety checks and explain the reasoning.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1772-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A prescription is for 500 mg. The stock is 250 mg tablets. How many tablets are needed?

- A. 2 tablets **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: 500 mg divided by 250 mg per tablet equals 2 tablets.
- B. 1 tablet  
  _Rationale:_ One 250 mg tablet gives only half the prescribed 500 mg.
- C. 0.5 tablet  
  _Rationale:_ That would give 125 mg, a quarter of the prescribed dose.
- D. 5 tablets  
  _Rationale:_ Five tablets would give 1250 mg, far above the prescription.**MST-1772-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) 500 mL of fluid is to be infused over 4 hours on a volumetric pump. What rate in mL/hour should be set?

- A. 125 mL/hour **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: 500 mL divided by 4 hours equals 125 mL/hour.
- B. 200 mL/hour  
  _Rationale:_ 200 mL/hour would deliver the 500 mL in 2.5 hours, too fast.
- C. 2000 mL/hour  
  _Rationale:_ That multiplies instead of divides; it would empty the bag in 15 minutes.
- D. 100 mL/hour  
  _Rationale:_ 100 mL/hour would take 5 hours, not the prescribed 4.**MST-1772-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO are sound safety practices when performing a high-risk dose calculation? (Select TWO)

- A. Check that units match before applying the dose formula **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: mismatched units are a common source of large errors; matching them first is essential.
- B. Have a second competent person independently recalculate the dose **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: an independent double-check catches calculation and decimal errors.
- C. Round to whatever makes the number look neat  
  _Rationale:_ Rounding must be clinically appropriate and to a measurable amount, not cosmetic.
- D. Trust the first answer without an estimate or sense-check  
  _Rationale:_ A rough estimate and sense-check guard against order-of-magnitude errors.
