## Learning outcomes

1. Frame decisions clearly and identify the real choice to be made
2. Recognise and counter common cognitive biases in executive judgement
3. Use evidence and structured analysis proportionate to the decision
4. Decide well under uncertainty using probabilistic thinking
5. Design decision processes that improve group judgement
6. Balance speed and quality and know when each matters

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2463-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) An executive judges a past decision as bad purely because it lost money. What is the flaw in this reasoning?

- A. It conflates decision quality with outcome, ignoring the role of uncertainty and luck **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a sound decision can still have a poor outcome under uncertainty.
- B. Losing money always means the decision was good  
  _Rationale:_ The opposite claim is equally flawed.
- C. Outcomes are the only valid measure of decisions  
  _Rationale:_ Outcomes alone ignore process quality and luck.
- D. Decision quality cannot be assessed at all  
  _Rationale:_ It can, by examining the process and information at the time.

**MST-2463-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO techniques most directly counter overconfidence in an executive forecast? (Select TWO.)

- A. Reference-class forecasting using base rates from similar cases **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: base rates anchor estimates in real comparable outcomes.
- B. Running a pre-mortem imagining the decision has failed **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: pre-mortems surface overlooked failure modes.
- C. Asking the most confident person to decide alone  
  _Rationale:_ This amplifies overconfidence.
- D. Removing all dissent to decide faster  
  _Rationale:_ Suppressing dissent worsens judgement.
- E. Rounding the forecast to a single optimistic number  
  _Rationale:_ A single optimistic point ignores uncertainty.

**MST-2463-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A reversible, low-stakes decision has stalled for weeks awaiting more analysis. What is the best executive guidance?

- A. Decide quickly and adjust later, reserving deep analysis for high-stakes irreversible choices **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: reversible low-stakes decisions reward speed and learning over exhaustive analysis.
- B. Commission a full analysis regardless of stakes  
  _Rationale:_ Disproportionate analysis wastes time.
- C. Escalate every such decision to the CEO  
  _Rationale:_ Escalation is unnecessary for low-stakes reversible calls.
- D. Never decide without complete certainty  
  _Rationale:_ Certainty is rarely available and not required here.
