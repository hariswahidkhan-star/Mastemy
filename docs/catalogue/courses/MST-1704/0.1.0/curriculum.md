## Learning outcomes

1. Money basics and budgeting
2. Saving and emergency funds
3. Debt and credit
4. Investing fundamentals
5. Protection and planning

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: This course is general financial education, not personalised financial advice; it does not assess suitability of any specific product for an individual.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1704-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) You carry a balance on a credit card at 22% APR and have spare cash. What usually gives the best guaranteed return?

- A. Paying down the high-interest card balance **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: eliminating 22% interest is a guaranteed return few investments beat.
- B. Buying a lottery ticket  
  _Rationale:_ Lotteries have a negative expected return and are not a strategy.
- C. Leaving the cash in a checking account  
  _Rationale:_ A checking account earns little while the 22% interest keeps accruing.
- D. Making only the minimum card payment  
  _Rationale:_ Minimum payments let the high interest keep compounding against you.

**MST-1704-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select ALL that apply) Which statements about an emergency fund are sound? (Select TWO)

- A. It should be kept somewhere safe and quickly accessible **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: an emergency fund must be available on short notice.
- B. A common target is several months of essential expenses **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a few months of essential spending is a widely used guideline.
- C. It should be fully invested in volatile stocks for growth  
  _Rationale:_ Volatile assets can fall just when you need the cash, defeating its purpose.
- D. It is unnecessary if you have a credit card  
  _Rationale:_ A credit card is debt, not savings, and may be unavailable in a crisis.

**MST-1704-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why does starting to invest for retirement earlier generally help, even with small amounts?

- A. Compound growth has more time to work on contributions **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: more time lets returns compound on earlier returns.
- B. Markets always rise every single year  
  _Rationale:_ Markets fluctuate; the benefit comes from time and compounding, not a guarantee.
- C. Early investors pay no fees  
  _Rationale:_ Fees apply regardless of when you start; starting early does not remove them.
- D. Later contributions are not allowed  
  _Rationale:_ You can contribute later too; starting early simply adds more compounding time.
