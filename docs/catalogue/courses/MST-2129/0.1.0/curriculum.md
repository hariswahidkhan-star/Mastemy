## Learning outcomes

1. Describe the hierarchical organisation of the human body from cells to organ systems and apply standard anatomical terminology
2. Explain homeostasis and common feedback mechanisms that maintain the body's internal environment
3. Summarise the structure and core functions of the major organ systems
4. Relate tissue types and their properties to the organs they build
5. Trace the flow of blood, air and nerve signals through their respective systems
6. Use correct directional, regional and sectional terms to locate structures

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2129-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Which anatomical plane divides the body into left and right portions?

- A. Sagittal plane **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the sagittal plane runs vertically and separates left from right.
- B. Transverse plane  
  _Rationale:_ The transverse plane divides the body into superior and inferior parts.
- C. Frontal (coronal) plane  
  _Rationale:_ The frontal plane divides anterior from posterior.
- D. Oblique plane  
  _Rationale:_ An oblique plane is any slanted plane, not the standard left-right divider.

**MST-2129-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Body temperature rising above its set point triggers sweating, which cools the body back toward the set point. Which TWO statements describe this mechanism? (Select TWO.)

- A. It is an example of negative feedback **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the response opposes the change and returns the variable toward its set point.
- B. It is an example of positive feedback  
  _Rationale:_ Positive feedback amplifies a change rather than reversing it.
- C. A receptor, control centre and effector are involved **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: feedback loops use a receptor, a control centre and an effector.
- D. It permanently resets the body's set point higher  
  _Rationale:_ The set point is unchanged; the loop restores the original value.
- E. It requires no effector  
  _Rationale:_ An effector (sweat glands) carries out the corrective response.

**MST-2129-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Which tissue type is specialised to contract and generate movement?

- A. Muscle tissue **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: muscle tissue contracts to produce movement.
- B. Epithelial tissue  
  _Rationale:_ Epithelial tissue covers surfaces and lines cavities.
- C. Connective tissue  
  _Rationale:_ Connective tissue supports and binds other tissues.
- D. Nervous tissue  
  _Rationale:_ Nervous tissue transmits signals but does not contract.
