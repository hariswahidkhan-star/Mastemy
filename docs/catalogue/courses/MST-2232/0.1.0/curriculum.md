## Learning outcomes

1. Describe the stage-gate product development process end to end
2. Translate user and market needs into prioritised requirements and specifications
3. Generate and select concepts using structured decision methods
4. Plan prototyping and testing to retire the biggest risks first
5. Apply design for manufacture, assembly and cost thinking
6. Plan a product launch and incorporate feedback into iterations

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2232-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) What is the main purpose of a stage gate in product development?

- A. A decision point to continue, pivot or stop based on evidence **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: gates force an evidence-based go/no-go before more is invested.
- B. A place to finalise the marketing logo  
  _Rationale:_ Branding is one input, not the purpose of a gate.
- C. A step where engineering is forbidden  
  _Rationale:_ Gates govern decisions; they do not ban work.
- D. A legal requirement for all products  
  _Rationale:_ Stage gates are a management practice, not a legal mandate.

**MST-2232-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO are signs of a well-written product requirement? (Select TWO.)

- A. It is measurable and testable **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: a good requirement can be verified objectively.
- B. It states the need, not a specific solution **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: solution-free requirements leave room for better concepts.
- C. It names the exact supplier part number  
  _Rationale:_ That over-specifies a solution too early.
- D. It is intentionally vague to stay flexible  
  _Rationale:_ Vague requirements cannot be tested or agreed.

**MST-2232-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) You have limited time and one very uncertain assumption about user behaviour. What should your first prototype do?

- A. Test the riskiest assumption as cheaply as possible **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: prototyping retires the biggest risk first with least cost.
- B. Perfect the final finish and packaging  
  _Rationale:_ Finish work is wasted until the core risk is retired.
- C. Build the full product to production quality  
  _Rationale:_ That is slow and expensive before the risk is understood.
- D. Skip prototyping and go straight to launch  
  _Rationale:_ That leaves the biggest uncertainty untested.
