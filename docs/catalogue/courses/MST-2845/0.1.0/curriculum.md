## Learning outcomes

1. Explain that we see things when light from a source reaches our eyes
2. Identify light sources and sort materials as transparent, translucent or opaque
3. Describe how a shadow forms when an opaque object blocks light
4. Investigate how a shadow's size and shape change with the light's position
5. Describe how light reflects off shiny surfaces like mirrors
6. Follow safe habits: never look at the Sun or shine bright lights into eyes

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance. Any experiment ideas are optional and require adult supervision; they are not assessed.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2845-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) Why can you NOT see anything in a completely dark, sealed room?

- A. There is no light reaching your eyes from the objects **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: we see only when light reaches our eyes; with no light there is nothing to see.
- B. Your eyes stop working in the dark  
  _Rationale:_ Your eyes still work; there is simply no light to detect.
- C. The objects disappear in the dark  
  _Rationale:_ Objects are still there; they just are not lit.
- D. Dark rooms absorb your eyesight  
  _Rationale:_ Eyesight is not absorbed; seeing just needs light.

**MST-2845-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO materials would make a clear, dark shadow because they are opaque? (Select TWO.)

- A. A thick cardboard box **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: cardboard is opaque and blocks the light.
- B. A wooden spoon **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: wood is opaque and blocks the light.
- C. A clear glass cup  
  _Rationale:_ Clear glass is transparent, so light passes through and the shadow is faint.
- D. A sheet of tracing paper  
  _Rationale:_ Tracing paper is translucent; it lets some light through.

**MST-2845-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) You move a torch closer to an object in front of a wall. What happens to the shadow?

- A. The shadow gets bigger **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: moving the light closer makes the shadow larger.
- B. The shadow disappears  
  _Rationale:_ The shadow stays; it just changes size.
- C. The shadow turns into a mirror image  
  _Rationale:_ Shadows are blocked light, not reflections.
- D. The shadow gets smaller  
  _Rationale:_ Moving the light closer enlarges the shadow, it does not shrink it.
