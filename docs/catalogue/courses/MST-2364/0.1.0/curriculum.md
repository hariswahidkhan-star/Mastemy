## Learning outcomes

1. Distinguish energy efficiency from energy conservation and demand reduction
2. Identify the largest energy end uses in homes, buildings and industry
3. Apply a structured energy audit to find and prioritise savings
4. Evaluate efficiency measures by cost, payback and energy saved
5. Explain behavioural and operational conservation measures and their limits
6. Avoid common pitfalls such as the rebound effect and false economies

- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Integrative case

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2364-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) What is the key difference between energy efficiency and energy conservation?

- A. Efficiency delivers the same service with less energy; conservation reduces the service or usage itself **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Efficiency is doing the same with less input; conservation is using less of the service.
- B. They are identical terms with no distinction  
  _Rationale:_ They describe different approaches to reducing energy use.
- C. Conservation always costs more than efficiency  
  _Rationale:_ Cost is not what defines the distinction.
- D. Efficiency only applies to transport  
  _Rationale:_ Efficiency applies across all end uses.

**MST-2364-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO measures would a structured energy audit rely on to prioritise savings? (Select TWO.)

- A. A measured baseline of current consumption **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ A baseline lets savings be quantified and prioritised.
- B. Cost of saved energy for each candidate measure **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Ranking by cost of saved energy targets the best value first.
- C. The colour of the building's exterior  
  _Rationale:_ Exterior colour is not an audit input for prioritisation.
- D. The number of staff who prefer a measure  
  _Rationale:_ Preference is not a basis for prioritising energy savings.

**MST-2364-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) After a lighting retrofit, measured savings are smaller than predicted because occupants leave lights on longer. What is this called?

- A. The rebound effect **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Cheaper-to-run service often increases usage, eroding part of the saving.
- B. Simple payback  
  _Rationale:_ Payback is a financial metric, not this behaviour.
- C. The Betz limit  
  _Rationale:_ That is a wind-turbine concept, unrelated here.
- D. Round-trip efficiency  
  _Rationale:_ That is a storage metric, not this effect.
