## Learning outcomes

1. ESG foundations
2. ESG integration and investing
3. Sustainable instruments
4. Reporting and disclosure
5. Greenwashing and integrity

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses conceptual knowledge through selection items; this is general education, not investment advice, and it does not assess a specific ESG rating methodology or regulatory compliance determination.

## Integrative case

Review a company's sustainability bond and ESG report: classify its ESG issues, judge whether the bond is use-of-proceeds or KPI-linked, test its targets for credibility, and flag any greenwashing risks before an investor relies on the claims.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1715-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) A bond whose interest rate steps up if the issuer misses a published emissions-reduction target is a:

- A. Sustainability-linked (KPI-linked) bond **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: the coupon is tied to meeting a sustainability KPI.
- B. Use-of-proceeds green bond  
  _Rationale:_ Use-of-proceeds bonds ring-fence spending, not coupon to KPIs.
- C. Plain government bond  
  _Rationale:_ A plain bond has no sustainability mechanism.
- D. Convertible bond  
  _Rationale:_ Convertibility relates to equity conversion, not ESG targets.

**MST-1715-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select ALL that apply) Which two reasons explain why two providers can give the same company very different ESG scores? (Select TWO) (Select TWO)

- A. They weight E, S and G factors differently **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: differing weightings drive score divergence.
- B. They use different data sources and definitions **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: data and definitions differ across providers.
- C. ESG scores are set by a single global regulator  
  _Rationale:_ There is no single mandated global ESG score.
- D. Scores are legally required to be identical  
  _Rationale:_ No law mandates identical scores.

**MST-1715-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) A marketing claim says a fund is 'green' but gives no evidence, metrics or definition. This is a red flag for:

- A. Greenwashing **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: unsubstantiated green claims are classic greenwashing.
- B. Double materiality  
  _Rationale:_ Double materiality is a disclosure concept, not this issue.
- C. Negative screening  
  _Rationale:_ Screening is a strategy, not a misleading-claim problem.
- D. A sustainability-linked structure  
  _Rationale:_ That is a bond structure, unrelated to this vague claim.
