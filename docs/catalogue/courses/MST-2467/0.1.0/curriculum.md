## Learning outcomes

1. Communicate complex ideas with clarity and executive brevity
2. Structure messages top-down so the point lands first
3. Adapt communication to different stakeholders and their concerns
4. Influence and persuade without relying on formal authority
5. Present and tell stories that move decisions forward
6. Communicate credibly under pressure, scrutiny and in crises

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Assesses knowledge and applied reasoning through selection items; does not assess hands-on performance.

## Integrative case

A functional leader must win board approval and cross-functional support for a major investment with no direct authority over the other teams: craft an answer-first message, tailor it to the board and to peers, build a supporting coalition, and present a decision-moving story that holds up under tough questioning.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-2467-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) An executive opens a board proposal with 15 minutes of background before the recommendation. What is the main communication flaw?

- A. Burying the recommendation violates answer-first structure and loses the audience **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: executives expect the point first, then support; leading with background loses attention.
- B. Background should always come first in any setting  
  _Rationale:_ Answer-first structure is preferred for executives.
- C. The proposal had too few slides  
  _Rationale:_ Slide count is not the issue.
- D. Boards prefer long build-ups  
  _Rationale:_ Boards generally want the recommendation up front.

**MST-2467-Q0002** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO approaches best build influence when you have no formal authority? (Select TWO.)

- A. Understanding and addressing the other parties' interests and concerns **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: addressing others' interests creates willing support.
- B. Building a coalition of supporters before the decision **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: coalitions supply influence that authority cannot.
- C. Repeatedly asserting that you are correct  
  _Rationale:_ Being right is not the same as being persuasive.
- D. Escalating to force a decision through hierarchy  
  _Rationale:_ Force substitutes for, and undermines, genuine influence.
- E. Withholding information to gain leverage  
  _Rationale:_ Manipulation erodes trust and durable influence.

**MST-2467-Q0003** (single-answer, Select ONE) Under hostile questioning, an executive becomes defensive and over-explains every point. What is the most credible alternative?

- A. Acknowledge the concern directly, answer concisely, and stay composed **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: composed, direct, concise answers project credibility under scrutiny.
- B. Match the hostility to show strength  
  _Rationale:_ Matching hostility undermines credibility.
- C. Avoid answering the difficult questions  
  _Rationale:_ Evasion damages trust.
- D. Over-explain until the questioner gives up  
  _Rationale:_ Over-explaining signals insecurity and loses the room.
