## Learning outcomes

1. Explain occupational safety management systems, roles and regulatory context
2. Identify hazards and assess and control risk across common workplace exposures
3. Describe programs for emergency preparedness, fire, and environmental and health controls
4. Apply incident investigation, measurement and safety-program management practices

## What this course assesses / does not assess

- Assesses: knowledge and applied reasoning on every in-scope outcome, using single-answer MCQ and multiple-answer selection only.
- Does not assess: Knowledge-practice forms use MCQ/MR only; official item formats not reproducible in this format are listed in the exam-version record.

## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-0328-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) According to the hierarchy of controls, which option is the MOST effective way to address a hazard?

- A. Eliminating the hazard at its source **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: elimination is the most effective control because the hazard no longer exists.
- B. Providing personal protective equipment  
  _Rationale:_ PPE is the least effective control and the last resort.
- C. Posting a warning sign  
  _Rationale:_ Administrative signage ranks below engineering controls.
- D. Writing a safe-work procedure  
  _Rationale:_ Procedures are administrative controls, less effective than elimination.

**MST-0328-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) A noise survey shows exposure above the limit. Applying the hierarchy of controls, what should be considered BEFORE issuing hearing protection?

- A. Engineering controls such as enclosing or dampening the noise source **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: engineering controls rank above PPE and should be considered first.
- B. Immediately issuing earplugs and taking no other action  
  _Rationale:_ PPE alone skips higher controls in the hierarchy.
- C. Ignoring the result because workers are used to the noise  
  _Rationale:_ Habituation does not reduce the exposure or the risk.
- D. Reducing break times to limit complaints  
  _Rationale:_ This does not reduce noise exposure and harms workers.

**MST-0328-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Select TWO leading indicators of safety-program performance.

- A. Percentage of hazard reports closed on time **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: this measures proactive activity before incidents occur.
- B. Number of safety inspections completed as planned **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: completed inspections are a proactive, leading measure.
- C. Total recordable injuries last year  
  _Rationale:_ Injury counts are lagging indicators, measured after harm.
- D. Days since the last fatality  
  _Rationale:_ This is a lagging outcome measure.
- E. Workers' compensation costs paid  
  _Rationale:_ Costs paid reflect past incidents, a lagging indicator.
