## Sample items (original, draft, unreviewed)

**MST-1078-Q0001** (single-answer, Select ONE) In a high-context culture, much of a message's meaning is carried by:

- A. Explicit, spelled-out words only  
  _Rationale:_ That describes a low-context style, where meaning is stated directly.
- B. Context, relationship and nonverbal cues around the words **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: high-context communication relies heavily on shared context and indirect cues.
- C. Written contracts exclusively  
  _Rationale:_ Formality of documents is not what high- versus low-context describes.
- D. The speaker's job title  
  _Rationale:_ Hierarchy matters in some cultures but is not what 'context' refers to here.

**MST-1078-Q0002** (single-answer, Select ONE) The fairest way to handle recurring meetings for a team split across distant time zones is to:

- A. Always use the headquarters time zone  
  _Rationale:_ This repeatedly burdens the same regions and signals they matter less.
- B. Rotate inconvenient meeting times so the burden is shared **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: rotating the awkward slot spreads the cost of distance across everyone.
- C. Cancel all synchronous meetings  
  _Rationale:_ Some discussions genuinely need real-time conversation.
- D. Let the most senior person always pick  
  _Rationale:_ Convenience for one person is not the same as fairness for the team.

**MST-1078-Q0003** (multiple-answer, Select TWO) Which TWO practices help when giving feedback across cultures? (Select TWO)

- A. Learn whether the person's culture expects direct or indirect feedback **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: matching directness to expectation makes feedback usable.
- B. Protect the person's dignity and avoid public criticism where face matters **(key)**  
  _Rationale:_ Correct: attention to face keeps feedback from causing withdrawal.
- C. Always deliver feedback as bluntly as possible  
  _Rationale:_ Bluntness can destroy trust in cultures that value indirectness and face.
- D. Assume your own feedback norms are universal  
  _Rationale:_ Projecting your norms is the core cross-cultural feedback error.
